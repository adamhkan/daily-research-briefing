Executive Summary

  • Stanford and Berkeley contribute strong advances in data-efficient learning for long-horizon manipulation (TANDEM, TP-Flow, DemoDPO/LeWAM, TP-Flow, etc.), including selective demonstrations, few-shot adaptation, and world-action models.
  • Dexterous and dual-arm manipulation see notable progress in both hardware (Berkeley QUAD Hand) and control (LiMA, DEAL-Grasp, BrickCraft-Duo), pushing toward real-time, long-horizon, and geometry-aware dexterity.
  • World models and WAM/VLA-style policies are central across many papers, supporting generalizable insertion, contact-rich manipulation, and memory-aware control with improved latency, temporal alignment, and evaluation protocols.
  • Navigation and locomotion work spans multi-robot relational navigation, off-road traversability, GNSS-denied UAV localization, dense monocular-inertial SLAM, amphibious and humanoid locomotion, and social/group-aware goal prediction.
  • Safety, robustness, and reliability cut across topics: contact detection on low-cost arms, safety filters and safety-focused RL, uncertainty-aware local navigation, adversarial robustness, and backdoor/security analysis in industrial arms.
  • Benchmarks and meta-infrastructure (X2Real, BranchDrive, RegenHarness, EmbodiedSWE, CMA, Kairos, NaviScale) aim to make evaluation, data generation, and long-horizon embodied reasoning more faithful and scalable.

TANDEM: Task and Motion Planning with As-Needed Demonstrations for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Model Fine-tuningStanford UniversityTANDEM combines task and motion planning with selective human teleoperation, treating human assistance as an on-demand planning capability to fill gaps in a fixed TAMP domain. It uses VLMs to extend predicates and operators and aligns planner motions with a VLA pretraining distribution, yielding 2.9× more useful demonstrations per unit human time and boosting π_{0.5}-DROID success from 0% to 60% on challenging long-horizon tasks.Link
A Quasi-Direct-Drive Underactuated Asymmetric Hand for Dexterous and Efficient Grasping and ManipulationUC BerkeleyThe Berkeley QUAD hand is a 4-finger anthropomorphic design with QDD actuation and bio-inspired asymmetry, dedicating dexterity to radial fingers and strength via an underactuated ulnar transmission. It achieves 29/33 Feix grasps, very low backdrive forces (~50 g), and up to 96× heat reduction under sustained loading, enabling both delicate and forceful manipulation in a compact, thermally efficient form factor.Link
Banana Kick: Response-Informed Skill Evolution for Humanoid SoccerCarnegie Mellon UniversityThis work introduces Response-Informed Skill Evolution (RISE) to evolve a standard humanoid kick into a high-spin 'banana kick' by focusing RL updates on policy responses that actually change the ball trajectory, overcoming first-order learning starvation. Under calibrated aerodynamics and sim-to-real transfer, the method achieves an 11.55 rad/s mean ball spin and 19.8% score improvement, with motion-capture validation on hardware.Link
Vision-Based Control of a Tether-Suspended Aerial Radiation Sensing PayloadUC BerkeleyThe authors control a passive radiation sensor suspended on a tether beneath a heavy-lift drone, regulating the payload rather than vehicle trajectory using vision-based estimation of tether swing. A Kalman filter infers tether angles from a downward-facing camera, and an LQR with integral action achieves 20% lower payload tracking error versus a vehicle-referenced baseline in windy outdoor tests.Link
HiRE: Hindsight Reward Editing for Policy FinetuningUC BerkeleyHiRE is a training-free framework that retrofits foundation representation models into control-aware reward functions by contrasting successful and failed trajectories to identify and penalize 'trap states'. It produces dense, calibrated rewards that avoid value collapse and reward hacking, enabling RL finetuning that achieves up to 3× base-policy performance with improved sample efficiency and stability.Link
Action-Directed Information for Distributed Control and Agentic InteractionUC San DiegoThis conceptual study proposes measuring information at the point where messages change actions, then connecting it to function via interventions and disturbances in a limb-coordinated walker. Comparing self-sensing vs peer-sensing controllers, peer information improves tracking under failures, and the analysis clarifies how action-relevant information can be hidden in aggregate performance metrics.Link
Surgical Kinematics from Monocular Video with Learned Articulated Motion ConstraintsNew York UniversityThis paper reconstructs surgical instrument kinematics (position, orientation, jaw angle) from monocular video by fusing DINOv3 features, SAM masks, monocular depth, and temporal encoders with a differentiable least-squares trajectory fit. On 2,802 Open-H episodes it reduces path-length MAE from 0.45 to 0.34 cm and boosts motion-segmentation mAP from 44.5% to 54.4% over prior video-based methods.Link
Fine Wrist Control as a Marker of Surgical Teleoperation ExpertiseStanford UniversityAnalyzing upper-limb motion of 9 expert and 27 novice users on a peg-transfer task, the study finds experts stabilize wrist motion more while preserving shoulder/elbow range, completing tasks significantly faster. These biomechanics suggest that fine wrist control is a consistent marker of teleoperation expertise and may warrant explicit training attention.Link

LiMA: Bridging Long-term Imagination to Real-time Dexterous Manipulation via Asynchronous DiffusionKey Laboratory of Multimedia Information Processing, School of Computer ScienceLiMA is an asynchronous dual-system diffusion framework that decouples slow long-horizon intent planning from fast reactive motion refinement for dexterous bimanual manipulation. Using a latent Schrödinger bridge coupling between sparse intents and dense actions, it cuts inference latency by ~46% over Cosmos-Policy while achieving 70.8% overall success across six dexterous tasks, including generalization to unseen scenarios.Link
PointCast: One World Model for Rigid, Articulated, and Deformable Object ManipulationPointCast is a diffusion-transformer world model that predicts future 3D point trajectories for objects and end-effector, treating rigid, articulated, cloth, and rope settings under a unified point-set representation. Trained from randomized simulation and teleoperation data, it outperforms baselines on 3/4 regimes and, when embedded in MPC, enables competitive or superior performance on four manipulation tasks with one network evaluation per horizon window.Link
Contact-Implicit Stein Projected ADMM for Discovery of Diverse Contact-Rich Manipulation StrategiesThis work augments contact-implicit trajectory optimization with Stein variational ideas, applying the repulsive force in ADMM's split variable space to obtain multiple distinct solutions in one run. Across pushing, grasping, and multi-robot handover, the method discovers diverse contact modes and robust strategies more simply than prior multi-solution solvers.Link
BrickCraft-Duo: Efficient Dual-Arm Skill Learning and Refinement for Compositional Long-Horizon AssemblyTsinghua UniversityBrickCraft-Duo learns reusable uni- and bimanual brick assembly skills from few demonstrations, sharing across symmetric arms via bilateral alignment and role assignments. Stability-aware reasoning composes skills into long-horizon dual-arm assembly with human-in-the-loop refinement, achieving ≥60% full-task and ≥95% step-level success on real 9-step assemblies with partial support and tight tolerances.Link
Generalizable Robotic Insertion with World ModelsThe authors train a single vision+proprioception world model from up to 90 diverse insertion tasks and use it for model-based planning that generalizes to unseen, geometrically novel parts. The approach attains 56% zero-shot success on unknown objects vs 7% for a model-free baseline, with performance scaling positively as more training objects are added and efficient finetuning on new parts.Link
DEAL-Grasp: Decoupled Alignment Representation for Geometry-Aware Dexterous Grasp GenerationDEAL-Grasp reformulates dexterous grasp synthesis in an alignment space that separates geometric anchors from articulation parameters, recovering rigid transforms via Procrustes alignment. Using heterogeneous-state flow matching with adaptive physical regularization, it generates diverse, stable grasps with low penetration and high force-perturbation robustness on MultiDex and zero-shot RealDex, with markedly lower latency than optimization-heavy baselines.Link
Distillation for Efficient Multitask Manipulation Policies via Conditional Flow MatchingThis paper distills many single-task Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) expert policies into a single multi-task manipulation model by transferring their learned velocity fields while retaining the original CFM objective. On RLBench, the multi-task policy matches or exceeds naïve multi-task CFM training at fixed model size, improving performance without per-task models.Link
AeRSoM: An Aerial Rigid-Soft Integrated Manipulator for Contact-Rich ManipulationAeRSoM is a fully actuated aerial robot with an integrated rigid-soft manipulator and variable stiffness, distributing compliance to absorb contact disturbances while maintaining precision. A composite control scheme enables robust, accurate execution of contact-rich tasks such as transmission-line grasping, turbine-blade interaction, peg-in-hole, and screwing in real-world tests.Link
Task-Prototype Guided Flow Matching for Few-Shot Generalization in Vision-Language Robot ManipulationTP-Flow builds structured phase-level 'task prototypes' from a small support set and injects them into a flow-matching manipulation policy via adaptive priors and gated normalization. Trained episodically, it reaches 66.8%, 79.6%, and 82.1% success in 1-, 4-, and 6-shot settings on LEROBOT-ARM-SO101, substantially outperforming CFM variants while running in real time at 10 Hz.Link
CoRelNav: Collaborative Relational Navigation for Multi-Robot Spatially Constrained Semantic NavigationCoRelNav couples task-conditioned multi-robot exploration with collaborative relation verification for navigation goals specified by object relations (e.g., 'left of the sofa'). A spatial-semantic field drives exploration and robot reallocation, while distributed observations are fused for instance-consistent relation hypotheses, yielding consistent gains over single-robot and non-relational baselines in simulation and deployment on two real robots.Link
