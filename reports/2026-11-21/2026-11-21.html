Executive Summary

  • Several works advance autonomous navigation and spatial decision-making: ROAM combines a pre-trained vision-language model, open-vocabulary segmentation, and two RL policies to build dense risk maps that enable kilometer-scale autonomous legged navigation in alpine and urban environments, including sign interpretation and safe foothold selection despite residual VLM and spatial-reasoning limitations; CHAC augments frozen VL navigation policies with training-free, memory-guided action chunking to cut model calls by 33.4–64.7% on HM3D-OVON and R2R-CE with modest success-rate changes and achieves more task completions in real-world runs while halving compute; passage-conditioned navigation for wheeled-legged robots stores each stair or ledge crossing as a passage event and enforces an entry gate during replanning, boosting disturbed ledge-crossing accuracy from 24.1% to 88.6%, preventing simulated falls in 180 disturbed stair climbs, and enabling fully autonomous completion of a 271 m multi-obstacle competition course; EAGLE trains a VLM-conditioned Swin teacher and a distilled context-free student to build explicit spatial-belief tensors for a compact PPO agent, then runs without any VLM at inference, achieving state-of-the-art active geo-localization success (78.1% on MASA, 76.4% on xBD zero-shot, 76.8% on MM-GAG); and a physics-informed probabilistic framework for gas source localization incrementally reconstructs indoor airflow fields from onboard wind and gas sensing to update a belief map and guide informative path planning, improving robustness and exploration efficiency in high-fidelity simulations and indoor robot experiments compared with less physically structured baselines. (arXiv:2611.25215; arXiv:2611.25587; arXiv:2611.25788; arXiv:2611.25866; arXiv:2611.25067)
  • New world-model and foundation frameworks support whole-body and embodied control: WEVO post-trains humanoid vision-language-action policies inside an action-conditioned video world model using simulated play plus a few autonomous real rollouts, where RL in the learned world model raises mean success from 23.3% to 75.0% on three real loco-manipulation tasks and outperforms real-robot RL (48.3%) under the same data budget; ECHO is an in-context humanoid behavior foundation model that learns a general behavior prior and dynamics-specific residuals from interaction histories without test-time parameter updates, surpassing prior BFMs across diverse motions and physical conditions and enabling extreme acrobatics, terrain interaction, and payload-adaptive whole-body teleoperation on Unitree G1 hardware; LocoMuJoCo offers an open-source benchmark and library unifying CPU MuJoCo with GPU MJX/MjWarp across 9 humanoid, 3 musculoskeletal, and 4 quadruped embodiments, with modular observation/reward/terrain plugins, curated motion-capture retargeting, and compact JAX RL/IL implementations plus a sim-to-real companion; O²LaF reuses a single video diffusion transformer as a shared prior for inverse and forward dynamics via a compact latent-action bottleneck, learning transferable actions from unlabeled videos that yield state-of-the-art few-shot action probing and strong zero-shot action transfer across navigation and manipulation settings; and TuringPAI introduces on-device Physical AI models built on a HybridViT-MLQ backbone—where a 0.65B-parameter TuringPAI-VL improves geometric perception over Qwen2-VL-2B, TuringPAI-Drive maintains driving ADE/FDE within 1.2% of a larger baseline at 12 Hz on an Orin-X SoC, and TuringPAI-Embodied achieves 96.8% average success across four LIBERO suites. (arXiv:2611.25251; arXiv:2611.25778; arXiv:2611.26293; arXiv:2611.26617; arXiv:2611.25474)
  • Multi-agent coordination, tool use, and safety are addressed by several modeling advances: MAWAM extends a single-robot world-action backbone into a multi-agent world-action model using cross-agent attention and team-stage future-video supervision to jointly generate future views and action chunks, achieving 67.8% mean success on three real multi-arm manipulation tasks (18.9 percentage points over a MA-VLA baseline) and 90.3% mean success on eight simulated tasks, with ablations showing large performance drops when either cross-agent attention or team-stage supervision is removed; A²-VLA discovers and verifies tool affordances by reconstructing teleoperated demos in simulation and running autonomous experiments to build a reusable affordance library that conditions a π₀.₅ backbone, attaining 86.1% simulated cross-tool trajectory retargeting success and improving real-robot tool-use performance by 30.7 percentage points over the unaugmented baseline; SAFI enhances safety of vision-language-action policies by injecting fallback demonstrations that command stationary arms and consistent gripper states during prohibited tasks and jointly training on them, which with OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO and BadLIBERO cuts aggregate target success after unsafe-policy takeover to 1.3% with no observed physical incidents while preserving performance on allowed tasks; and AgentRoboCritic introduces an intent-grounded multimodal critic that analyzes action-level outcomes and failure modes from frontier agents across 12 manipulation benchmarks, with fine-tuning of Muse-Glimmer-30B on 12,844 annotated actions improving failure-mode classification accuracy from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling lower-cost, systematic action-level evaluation. (arXiv:2611.25105; arXiv:2611.25656; arXiv:2611.26239; arXiv:2611.26613)
  • Several works improve manipulation robustness, correction, and trajectory repair: VG-Steer derives visual-geometric guidance from object-relational distance pairs plus semantics from large vision and VLM models to steer existing manipulation policies under distribution shift, improving simulated flow-matching policies under pose and goal shifts and more than doubling success one grid step outside training positions for a cable-placement policy while largely eliminating object confusion when steering to unseen targets; TipDex-VTLA combines Visual Action Sensitivity Estimation with Tactile Anticipation and Correction during Execution to gate bounded tactile corrections based on predicted vs. measured signals, achieving 87.5% average success on four real dexterous tasks under normal lighting and 73.8% under challenging unseen lighting, outperforming several baselines under visual perturbations; Hindsight Self-Distillation trains a teacher with access to current and future visual frames and a student with only current frames to match denoised actions and visual summaries, then deploys the student alone with single-pass encoding and reused summaries, attaining average success rates of 98.50% on LIBERO and 92.31% on RoboTwin 2.0; and Close&Safe repairs trajectories for redundant manipulators by following the nominal end-effector path exactly where feasible and computing minimal-deviation configuration-space detours near unforeseen obstacles, recovering 52.4–78% of nominal trajectories while avoiding collisions in diverse simulations and Franka hardware experiments. (arXiv:2611.25199; arXiv:2611.25683; arXiv:2611.25777; arXiv:2611.26031)
  • Perception and 3D reconstruction methods target challenging sensing regimes: MIR-Depth performs single-view RGB-D depth completion for transparent objects by constructing coordinate-aware multi-range hypotheses (a global inverse-depth volume plus local depth-filtered volumes) and applying Selective Recurrent Refinement with ConvGRUs and Spatially Gated Correction, achieving lower depth errors than ReMake, DFNet, BP-Net, and PSD across multiple transparent-object benchmarks and supporting accurate 6-DoF grasp pose prediction in real-robot tests; Infer3D frames 3D reconstruction as inverse generative modeling that optimizes latent variables of a generative image model under constraints from a frozen 3D lifting network to enforce multi-view-consistent, non-degenerate geometry, yielding higher out-of-distribution reconstruction accuracy than feedforward networks on synthetic and real data—especially under extreme viewpoints—and introducing an adaptive inference strategy that allocates more test-time computation to ambiguous OOD inputs while routing in-distribution inputs more efficiently. (arXiv:2611.25417; arXiv:2611.25061)
  • Additional control and locomotion contributions span driving, legged motion, ferrofluids, and exoskeleton assistance: a differentiable MPC module is used to replace the action head of pre-trained PPO driving policies while enforcing geometric constraints (road-boundary half-planes and agent ellipses), with DAgger distillation from the PPO teacher yielding constrained DiffMPC students that match or improve teacher performance across nuPlan, CARLA, and OOD Waymo benchmarks while using at most 30% of the original training time; deep RL with networks of abstract oscillators modeled on spinal central pattern generators learns 11 quadrupedal gaits and styles plus a meta-policy that selects gaits to optimize terrain- and velocity-specific metrics, with each gait optimal under at least one terrain–metric–velocity combination and zero-shot adaptation demonstrated to unseen gait couplings, OOD terrains, heavier quadrupeds, and even bipedal-mode locomotion under a pitch-tracking reward; a deep model-based RL framework learns to control ferrofluid shapes directly from physical interaction by mapping visual observations to electromagnet currents, repeatedly realizing transient target geometries such as polygons, asterisks, and pentominoes with a single fixed electromagnet; and a sim-to-real pipeline that combines motion imitation with RL on a muscle-driven neuromusculoskeletal model trains an ankle-exoskeleton controller using motion capture from a single virtual human, which when deployed on five unseen subjects increases tolerated backward-translation perturbations by 11.8% and reduces peak center-of-mass displacement and velocity by 13.5% and 28.2%, respectively, while highlighting inter-user variability and the potential benefits of more diverse virtual training populations. (arXiv:2611.25197; arXiv:2611.25958; arXiv:2611.25232; arXiv:2611.25480)

Bridging vision-language reasoning and learned control for legged navigation in the wildETH ZurichThe paper proposes ROAM, a legged navigation system that combines a pre-trained vision-language model for semantic scene understanding and risk assessment with an open-vocabulary segmentation module and two reinforcement-learned locomotion and local navigation policies, producing a dense risk map that conditions low-level control. In real-world alpine and urban experiments, ROAM enables kilometer-scale autonomous legged navigation with minimal human intervention, including interpreting signs to avoid danger and selecting safe footholds, while still exhibiting limitations due to imperfect VLM reliability and spatial reasoning.Link
WEVO: World Model-Enabled VLA Optimization for Whole-Body Loco-ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper introduces WEVO, a teleoperation-free framework that post-trains whole-body humanoid vision-language-action policies inside an action-conditioned video world model, using simulated play data plus a small number of autonomous real rollouts to align the world model with deployment observations and form a closed-loop RL environment with dense rewards. Across three real-robot loco-manipulation tasks, adding simulated play data improves all evaluated world-model metrics and downstream policy performance, and reinforcement learning inside the video world model increases mean success rate from 23.3% to 75.0%, outperforming real-robot RL which reaches 48.3% under the same setting.Link
From Self-Play RL to Constrained Control: Distilling DiffMPC Students with DAggerUC San DiegoThe authors propose replacing the action head of a pre-trained PPO driving policy with a differentiable MPC module that enforces explicit geometric constraints (road boundary half-planes and agent ellipses), then distilling this constrained student from the original teacher via DAgger in the teacher’s self-play simulator. Across 9 seeds and multiple driving benchmarks (nuPlan, CARLA, and out-of-distribution Waymo scenes), the constrained DiffMPC students generally match or improve over their PPO teachers—matching on nuPlan and outperforming on CARLA and Waymo for a smaller teacher, and reducing failures on nuPlan and Waymo while maintaining CARLA performance for a stronger teacher—while requiring at most 30% of the teacher’s original training time.Link
LocoMuJoCo: A Comprehensive Imitation Learning Benchmark for Whole-Body ControlCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents LocoMuJoCo, an open-source benchmark and software library that unifies CPU-based MuJoCo and GPU-based MJX/MjWarp under a single API, providing 9 humanoid, 3 musculoskeletal, and 4 quadruped embodiments plus curated motion-capture datasets and retargeting from AMASS and LAFAN1 for whole-body control research. LocoMuJoCo offers a modular plug-in design for interchangeable observations, rewards, control modes, terrains, and domain randomization, along with compact JAX implementations of many RL and IL algorithms for JIT-compiled training and a companion repository for sim-to-real deployment, illustrated through a usage example.Link
MAWAM: A Multi-Agent World-Action Model for Coordinated ManipulationMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe authors introduce MAWAM, a multi-agent world-action model that extends a single-robot world-action backbone to jointly generate future views and executable action chunks for multiple robots via cross-agent attention, while preserving separate control interfaces for each robot. On three real-robot coordinated manipulation tasks (laundry-bag collection, bath-towel folding, umbrella handover), MAWAM achieves a 67.8% mean success rate, improving by 18.9 percentage points over a MA-VLA baseline trained on the same data, and in simulation attains 90.3% mean success on eight tasks with up to four arms. Ablation studies show that removing cross-agent attention or team-stage future-video supervision substantially degrades performance—for example, four-arm relay success drops from 95.0% to 39.0% without attention, and Place Food success falls from 78.0% to 47.0% without team-stage future-video supervision—indicating both components are important for coordinated control.Link
MIR-Depth: Multi-Range Refinement for Single-View RGB-D Depth Completion of Transparent ObjectsETH ZurichThe paper proposes MIR-Depth, a single-view RGB-D depth completion method for transparent objects that constructs coordinate-aware multi-range hypotheses (a global inverse-depth volume plus local depth-axis-filtered volumes) and refines them via Selective Recurrent Refinement with ConvGRUs and Spatially Gated Correction to recover accurate visible-surface depth without treating raw sensor readings as hard constraints. On multiple transparent-object benchmarks (TransCG, TODD, TRansPose, ClearPose, and HouseCat6D), MIR-Depth achieves lower depth error than ReMake, DFNet, BP-Net, and PSD, and real-robot experiments demonstrate that its reconstructions enable accurate 6-DoF grasp pose prediction for transparent objects.Link
Learning optimal gaits and styles for terrain-adaptive quadruped locomotionEPFLThe authors use deep reinforcement learning with networks of abstract oscillators, representing spinal central pattern generators, to learn 11 distinct quadrupedal gaits and associated locomotion styles (base height, ground clearance, foot offsets) across diverse challenging terrains, and a second policy that selects among these gaits to optimize user-specified criteria such as cost of transport, base stability, or joint smoothness. Within the learned repertoire and across different terrains, performance metrics, and velocities, each gait is optimal under at least one terrain-metric-velocity combination, and the framework adapts zero-shot to unseen gait couplings and styles, out-of-distribution terrains, and larger quadrupeds up to five times the training mass; with a simple pitch-tracking reward extension, the same gait couplings also support bipedal-mode locomotion.Link
Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models to Stop SafelyMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces State-Aware Fallback Imitation (SAFI), which augments vision-language-action policies for safety by constructing fallback targets that command stationary arms and consistent gripper states throughout prohibited-task demonstrations, and jointly adapting the policy so it learns these alternative responses alongside original behaviors. Experiments with OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO and BadLIBERO benchmarks show that SAFI enables policies to stop safely mid-execution: on evaluated LIBERO tasks, aggregate target success after unsafe-policy takeover is reduced to 1.3%, with no observed physical incidents or action saturation, while retaining high success on allowed tasks.Link
One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action LearningStanford UniversityThe authors propose O²LaF, a latent-action learning framework that repurposes a single pretrained video diffusion transformer as a shared prior for both inverse dynamics (via a feed-forward transition encoder with learnable action queries) and forward dynamics (via a causal, action-conditioned video generator), training them end-to-end through a compact action bottleneck while carefully controlling information access. Across navigation and robotic manipulation settings, O²LaF learns transferable latent actions from unlabeled videos, achieving state-of-the-art few-shot action probing performance and stronger zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments compared to prior methods, and an extension using predictive representation priors such as V-JEPA2 attains competitive results under the same framework.Link
VG-Steer: Visual-Geometric Policy Steering from In-Context Aligned AbstractionsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents VG-Steer, a test-time policy steering method that transfers nuanced motion from successful in-distribution rollouts using object-relational distance pairs, combining these geometric abstractions with semantic cues from large vision and vision-language models to form a visual-geometric guidance signal that corrects existing manipulation policies under distribution shift. In simulation, VG-Steer effectively corrects flow-matching policies under pose and task-goal shifts, and in real-robot experiments it expands the region of the workspace where a frozen cable-placement policy succeeds—more than doubling success one grid step outside the training positions—and steers a single-task policy toward unseen target objects while largely eliminating object confusion.Link
Ferrofluid Shape Control with Deep Reinforcement LearningETH ZurichThe paper proposes a deep model-based reinforcement learning framework that learns ferrofluid shape control policies directly from physical interaction, using visual observations to command currents to a single fixed electromagnet and drive the fluid toward prescribed transient geometries that may not be sustainable at equilibrium. In hardware experiments with a single electromagnet, the learned policies repeatedly achieve a range of target shapes, including polygons, asterisks, and pentominoes, demonstrating reliable control of transient ferrofluid configurations under limited actuation.Link
AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot ControlCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which a frontier multimodal agent supplies action-level intents and a critic evaluates outcomes and failure modes against those intents to enable systematic, action-level analysis of direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. Action-level analysis using AgentRoboCritic finds that frontier agents can exhibit closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, robust semantic grounding in diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation, while still suffering from task-relevant physical bottlenecks such as grasping, alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Using a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions, the authors fine-tune the open-source critic Muse-Glimmer-30B, improving its failure-mode classification accuracy from 32.58% to 74.24%, thereby enabling lower-cost action-level evaluation.Link
Learning Ankle Exoskeleton Control for Reactive Standing Balance Through Neuromusculoskeletal SimulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes a simulation-to-real framework that combines motion imitation and reinforcement learning on a muscle-driven neuromusculoskeletal model, trained from limited motion-capture data of a single subject, to develop an ankle exoskeleton controller that provides bilateral balance assistance using only inertial sensor measurements without explicit perturbation detection. When deployed onboard a bilateral ankle exoskeleton and tested on five healthy subjects unseen in training, the learned controller increased the maximum backward-translation perturbation magnitude tolerated without stepping by 11.8% and reduced peak center-of-mass displacement and velocity by 13.5% and 28.2%, respectively. Experimental results indicate that a controller trained using a single virtual human model can transfer measurable balance benefits to multiple real users, with individual benefits depending on baseline recovery strategies and body mass, suggesting that more diverse virtual humans could further improve effectiveness.Link
Infer3D: Inverse Generative Modeling Enables Robust 3D ReconstructionMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces Infer3D, an inverse generative modeling framework that infers latent variables of a generative image model to match monocular inputs, while constraining optimization with a frozen 3D lifting network to ensure multi-view-consistent, non-degenerate 3D reconstructions. Across controlled synthetic and real-world tasks, Infer3D achieves higher out-of-distribution 3D reconstruction accuracy than feedforward networks trained on equal or larger datasets, particularly under unfamiliar or extreme viewpoints. The authors propose an adaptive inference strategy that allocates more test-time computation to ambiguous out-of-distribution inputs and routes in-distribution inputs more efficiently, analogously to longer human processing times for confusing stimuli.Link

Mapping the Wind to Find the Source: Online Physics-Informed Gas Source Localization with Mobile RobotsUnknown affiliationThe paper presents a lightweight physics-informed probabilistic framework for gas source localization in indoor environments, in which a mobile robot incrementally reconstructs a spatially varying airflow field from onboard wind and gas measurements, then uses this field in a forward dispersion model to update a probabilistic belief over source locations. The framework explicitly accounts for spatial variation in the reliability of the reconstructed wind field and incorporates this uncertainty into the gas source belief update, using the resulting belief map to drive informative path planning. High-fidelity simulations and physical indoor experiments show that incorporating lightweight physical airflow models improves robustness of localization under spatially complex indoor airflow and enables more efficient exploration compared with approaches lacking such physical structure.Link
TuringPAI: A Foundation for On-Device Physical AIXPeng IncThe paper introduces TuringPAI, a family of on-device Physical AI models including TuringPAI-VL for shared vision-language understanding, TuringPAI-Drive for autonomous driving, and TuringPAI-Embodied for robotic manipulation, built on a HybridViT-MLQ backbone with multi-scale latent queries that integrate spatial features and temporal history in a compact interface to language models. TuringPAI-VL, a 0.65B-parameter model trained with general vision-language data plus geometric, cross-view, and temporal supervision, supports multimodal understanding and yields improved quantitative geometric perception relative to Qwen2-VL-2B, while serving as a shared initializer for downstream driving and manipulation policies. Under practical on-device constraints, TuringPAI-Drive maintains all evaluated ADE/FDE autonomous driving metrics within 1.2% of a full-token HybridViT baseline and runs at 12 Hz on an NVIDIA Orin-X SoC, while TuringPAI-Embodied attains a 96.8% average success rate across four LIBERO suites, indicating strong task performance with compact models.Link
Cognitive-Horizon Action Chunking: Memory-Guided Adaptive Reasoning for Embodied NavigationNanjing University of Posts and Telecommunications; Southeast University; Tsinghua UniversityThe paper proposes Cognitive-Horizon Action Chunking (CHAC), a training-free framework that augments a frozen navigation policy with memory-guided adaptive reasoning by using retained visual context, completion cues, and controller feedback to determine how many cached waypoints to execute before requesting a new prediction, plus a bounded local correction step at termination without extra model calls. On the HM3D-OVON and R2R-CE embodied navigation benchmarks, CHAC reduces vision-language model calls by 33.4% and 64.7%, respectively, with success-rate decreases of 0.63 and 10.82 percentage points, and improves both success rate and success weighted by path length when normalized per model call. Across four real-world navigation tasks with 40 runs per method, CHAC achieves 18 task successes versus 14 for native execution while cutting model calls by 54.9% and GPU inference time by 49.7%, demonstrating improved compute efficiency without retraining the underlying policy.Link
A²-VLA: Adapting Vision-Language-Action Policies to New Tools through Agentic Affordance DiscoveryShanghai Jiao Tong UniversityThe paper presents A²-VLA, a tool-adaptation framework that couples an embodied agent with a vision-language-action policy to autonomously discover, represent, and verify task-relevant tool affordances—functional regions and key geometric properties—by reconstructing teleoperated demonstrations in simulation, conducting autonomous experiments, and building a reusable library of affordance-extraction skills. During downstream training and inference, A²-VLA conditions a $\\pi_{0.5}$ backbone on encoded functional-region features, enabling cross-tool trajectory retargeting for unseen tools based on the discovered affordances. In evaluation, A²-VLA achieves 86.1% success in simulated cross-tool trajectory retargeting and improves real-robot task success on three tool-use tasks by 30.7 percentage points over the unaugmented $\\pi_{0.5}$ baseline.Link
TipDex-VTLA: Learning When to Refine Actions with Touch for Dexterous ManipulationWuhan Huaweike Intelligent Technology Co., Ltd.; Wuhan UniversityThe paper introduces TipDex-VTLA, a vision–tactile–language–action policy that regulates execution-time action refinement by estimating visual action reliability via Visual Action Sensitivity Estimation (VASE) and combining it with Tactile Anticipation and Correction during Execution (TACE), which compares predicted and measured tactile signals and gates bounded corrections to future actions. TipDex-VTLA is trained using data collected under normal lighting yet, when evaluated on four real-world dexterous manipulation tasks and the UniVTAC simulation benchmark, attains average full-task success rates of 87.5% under normal lighting and 73.8% under challenging unseen lighting conditions, outperforming several existing methods under visual perturbations.Link
Hindsight Self-Distillation: Teaching Robots to Act from the PresentInstitute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Nanjing University; Primotion; Shenzhen University; Shenzhen University of Advanced Technology; Southern University of Science and TechnologyThe paper proposes Hindsight Self-Distillation (HSD), a training method in which a teacher branch with access to both current and future camera frames and a student branch with only current frames share a visual encoder and action predictor, summarize images into compact feature sets, and train the student to match both the teacher’s denoised action predictions and visual summaries from noisy demonstrations. At deployment, HSD uses the student alone, encoding current images once and reusing their summaries across denoising iterations, and achieves average success rates of 98.50% on the LIBERO benchmark and 92.31% on RoboTwin 2.0, indicating strong performance in visuomotor manipulation tasks.Link
ECHO: In-Context Behavior foundation model for Humanoid Whole-Body ControlGamma Robotics; Institute of Artificial Intelligence (TeleAI), China Telecom; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; ShanghaiTech University; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper proposes ECHO, an in-context humanoid behavior foundation model that first learns a general behavior prior via Mixed and Annealed Sampling, then uses Context Representation and Residual Adaptation to derive dynamics-specific residual corrections from interaction histories without test-time parameter updates. In extensive experiments across diverse motions and physical conditions, ECHO outperforms all baseline methods, indicating improved environment-specific precision over prior BFMs that do not explicitly separate behavioral capability from dynamics-specific execution. On Unitree G1 hardware, ECHO enables whole-body teleoperation for extreme acrobatics and terrain interaction, as well as payload-adaptive manipulation and whole-body control, demonstrating transfer of its learned control strategy to a real humanoid platform.Link
Collision-Free Is Not Enough: Passage-Conditioned Navigation for Wheeled-Legged RobotsColumbia University; Tsinghua UniversityThe paper introduces passage-conditioned navigation for wheeled-legged robots, which stores each taught stair or ledge crossing as an event and, during replanning, enforces an entry gate that makes the robot turn or back up and retry if its approach deviates by more than 10 cm or 10° from the taught crossing, without requiring a robot model or additional training. In experiments with localization noise and unchanged path, gait schedule, and sensing, passage-conditioned navigation increases the share of disturbed ledge crossings that end within 10 cm and 10° of the taught crossing from 24.1% to 88.6%, and in contact simulation prevents any falls in 180 disturbed stair climbs (versus 9 falls when stored approach direction is ignored) while enabling a robot pushed 1.2 m off course to rejoin the route and finish with an average endpoint offset of 0.14 m (1.10 m without rejoining). Deployed on a real wheeled-legged robot in competition over 271 m of multi-obstacle terrain with 7.0 m elevation change, the method completes all six runs fully autonomously and secures third place at the 2026 Global Open-source AI Challenge, being the only finalist in its task to finish without human intervention.Link
From Sequence to Space: Explicit Spatial Belief with Vision-Language Distillation for Active Geo-LocalizationNational Space Science Center, Chinese Academy of Sciences; University of Chinese Academy of SciencesThe paper presents EAGLE, an Explicit spatial-belief Agent for Geo-Localization and Exploration, which offline-trains a teacher-conditioned Swin branch and a context-free Swin student on randomly masked aerial mosaics using reconstruction and uncertainty supervision; the student additionally distills stop-gradient predictions from a Qwen2.5-VL-7B-conditioned teacher, then alone predicts cell-level aerial embeddings and uncertainty from partial observations to build a spatial belief tensor for a CNN actor-critic policy. After distillation, EAGLE discards the VLM, cached context, and teacher-attention branch, and uses a frozen student plus a compact CNN trained with PPO to perform active geo-localization from partial aerial observations and aligned goal embeddings without any vision-language model or cached visual tokens at inference. EAGLE achieves state-of-the-art average success rates of 78.1% on MASA, 76.4% on xBD zero-shot transfer, and 76.8% on the MM-GAG zero-shot aerial-goal split for active geo-localization tasks involving aerial, ground, and text goals.Link
Close&Safe: Maximal Task Preservation for Redundant Manipulators via C-Space Boundary DetourIndian Institute of Science (IISc); Indian Institute of Technology Ropar; New York University Abu DhabiThe paper proposes Close&Safe, a trajectory-repair method for redundant manipulators that decouples tracking from avoidance by following the nominal end-effector trajectory exactly wherever feasible and only altering the motion over intervals obstructed by unforeseen obstacles, sampling the configuration-space obstacle boundary in a corridor around the nominal and finding a minimal-deviation detour that rejoins the nominal at the earliest feasible configuration. Across diverse simulated obstacle configurations, physical experiments on a Franka manipulator, and ablations of algorithmic components, Close&Safe produces collision-free trajectory repairs with localized task modification, recovering 52.4–78% of the original nominal trajectory in both simulation and hardware evaluations.Link
