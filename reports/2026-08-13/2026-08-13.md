## Executive Summary

- Stanford and ETH Zurich contribute strong navigation and aerial-interaction methods, including tunable human-following in crowds and millimeter-accurate ceiling marking with a compliant aerial end-effector.
- Multiple works advance risk- and safety-aware navigation and planning, from CVaR-based kinodynamic planning in uncertain planetary terrains to dual-space MPC safety monitors and robust safety filtering with control barrier functions.
- Mobile manipulation and whole-body control see notable progress via semantic 3D Gaussian splatting for open-vocabulary manipulation and volume-informed whole-body planning for humanoids in confined environments.
- Dexterous and deformable manipulation capabilities are extended with real-world cooperative bimanual grasping of large objects and competition-grade autonomous T-shirt handling.
- World-action models and skill abstractions continue to mature, emphasizing failure-aware training, multi-stream geometry/semantics, and stage-level futures for robust, data-efficient manipulation policies.
- Several works strengthen long-horizon autonomy infrastructure, including persistent world models for service robots, human-centric spatio-temporal memories, and fabric-specific perception tailored for deformable manipulation.

| Navigating the Proximity-Safety Balance: Constraint Decomposition for Human Following in Pedestrian Crowds | Stanford University | Introduces a multi-constraint RL formulation for human-following in dense crowds that decomposes proximity and safety into separate cost constraints with interpretable thresholds. | Aerial Layouting: Design and Control of a Compliant and Actuated End-Effector for Precise In-flight Marking on Ceilings | ETH Zurich | Presents an aerial system with a novel smart end-effector based on a stability-optimized Gough-Stewart platform to achieve millimeter-level precision in ceiling marking tasks. | Protection Levels for Vision-Based Pose Estimation | Stanford University | Derives protection levels—probabilistic bounds on pose error—for a nonlinear PnP-based, runway-relative vision navigation pipeline, even under undetected measurement faults. The work quantifies how redundancy, pixel uncertainty, and runway distance shape these bounds, offering a path toward certifiable integrity for vision-based aircraft navigation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10023 |
| Lost in Reconstruction: Aligning Action Representations with Language in Vision-Language-Action Models | Carnegie Mellon University | Shows that reconstruction-only action tokenization in VLAs discards verb-level semantics relevant for language-conditioned control. Proposes SALT, an action tokenizer that aligns discrete action codes with language via an auxiliary instruction-reconstruction loss, yielding substantially higher success rates and verb-specialized codes while preserving action fidelity. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10484 |
