Executive Summary

  • Multiple systems advance dexterous, contact-rich manipulation: a five-finger tendon-driven Soft-Rigid Hybrid Hand with 12 active DoF achieves 28/33 (84.8%) human-range grasp postures and up to 1432 deg/s joint extension in teleoperated soft and delicate manipulation tasks; ViTacLab couples a tri-modal visuo-tactile simulator, benchmark, and policy, reaching 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS with 256 environments and cutting normal/tangential force RMSE from 2.31→0.42 N and 1.85→0.53 N, though success on purely tactile-dominant tasks remains low; FabricBench provides six bimanual fabric tasks with 10,000 assets and a Newton-based simulator whose solver succeeds in 45/45 grasp–hold–release trials where baselines fail, yet best learned policies still struggle on long-horizon and highly deformed T-shirt configurations; HiMoDex pretrains a hierarchical multimodal (vision, tactile, force-torque, audio) representation then a shared world-action transformer, achieving 78.7% average success ( +32.0 points over the strongest baseline) on five real bimanual dexterous tasks; Ego2Real leverages egocentric video plus tactile-glove data for bimanual skill transfer, using tactile-guided reconstruction and contact-informed RL, and reports higher manipulation success when tactile supervision is used in both stages than in ablated variants. (arXiv:2611.32582; arXiv:2611.32476; arXiv:2611.31577; arXiv:2611.31782; arXiv:2611.32101)
  • New policy architectures broaden generality across embodiments and arms while improving dynamic control: OmniAct conditions on each robot’s kinematic graph and uses flow matching over joint deltas, trained on 1M simulated embodiments to achieve 63.0% success on 2,000 held-out embodiment–scene episodes (vs. 17.8% for π₀.₅ and 57.9% for the best baseline), 64.7% success on GPT-6 Astra task families (vs. 41.1%), and nearly 2× the success of MolmoAct2 on a previously unseen real robot; Duo treats each arm as an action token within a shared head with cross-arm attention, yielding RoboTwin 2.0 gains of 12.55 and 9.02 percentage points for FLOWER (0.89B) and π₀.₅ (3.62B), and enabling compact FLOWER-Duo to attain top randomized-generalization, precision, and memory on RoboDojo and better real-world success and speed than π₀.₅; Co-DRIS + SuperMimic co-designs perception and control for reactive catching with a physics-informed world-frame model, dual-stream causal GRU belief encoder, and phase-aware variable-impedance control, achieving 89.6 ± 0.4% success on standard benchmarks (70.5% at 4σ noise) and 66.6 ± 0.5% across 10 stress scenarios, with large gains over DRIS and improved catching and reduced impact forces in transfer tasks. (arXiv:2611.33158; arXiv:2611.32108; arXiv:2611.31843)
  • World(-action) modeling methods target more efficient and interpretable control: DexWAC jointly trains an action-conditioned world model, actor, and bootstrapped critic using an image-editing backbone that predicts endpoint visual latents, proprioception, reward, and continuation per action chunk, then improves the actor via PPO or critic-guided best-of-N over imagined rollouts, achieving 75% average success on two real dexterous tasks vs. 45% for π₀.₅ and reporting faster, more accurate dynamics than video-based baselines; iWAM conditions a policy on intermediate features from a single evaluation of a pretrained video model by inverting futures into latent noise with flow matching and learning a bidirectional flow map, matching the generalization of full video-prediction WAMs while cutting inference cost for real-time control; SimPWM represents scenes with an explicit 3D robot model and point-based objects evolved by a rule-based simulator and rendered by a video model, improving robot IoU to 0.75–0.78 on unseen DROID setups (vs. ≤0.69 end-to-end) and reducing missed pick-ups from 54% to 38% when adding simulated objects, while analyses show that explicit simulated state remains reliable even when rendered video disagrees; PILOT augments World Action Models with a Representational Deduction mechanism that introduces motion chain-of-thought via explicit state-transition tokens, which the authors report improves success and generalization on complex manipulation, enhances physical interpretability, and enables more data-efficient real-robot adaptation under reduced demonstrations. (arXiv:2611.32654; arXiv:2611.33141; arXiv:2611.32786; arXiv:2611.32241)
  • Several works address temporal dynamics, latency, and tactile/force inference in perception–action loops: FoveaWAM is a 4D world-action model that allocates precision to the target object’s SE(3) trajectory and sparse 3D flow for surroundings while reusing the current frame for static context, achieving 15.1 ms action latency on a single RTX 5090 (≈10× faster than video-based WAMs) and 82.1% success on OnTimeBench (20.8 points above the strongest baseline) plus leading results on DOMINO and real robots; VidForce learns shared force-level and temporal prototypes from limited instrumented data to jointly infer interaction trajectories and force profiles from third-person RGB, reaching 6.6 cm ATE, peak-force MAE/RMSE of 4.81/5.92 N, and force-profile RMSE of 5.52 N on held-out Hoi! interactions, and enabling Spot to manipulate articulated objects using inferred trajectories and forces. (arXiv:2611.31705; arXiv:2611.32967)
  • Training recipes, datasets, and benchmarks expose gaps and improve data efficiency: DRIFT trains sim-to-real visual RL policies in a single phase by freezing foundation vision encoders and learning only the control layer, yielding a 58.8% relative gain in average real-world success over matched two-phase distillation methods and larger simulated advantages under out-of-distribution shifts (>61% relative) than in-distribution (~9%); β-Data collects adversarial teleoperation where one human perturbs the environment while another completes the task and relabels segments with local language outcomes, and midtraining on this dataset with recent-action conditioning produces policies with better data scaling, zero-shot generalization, extended-horizon benefits, and more sample-efficient online finetuning than standard demonstrations across simulated and three real-robot platforms; RoboClock defines 60 manipulation tasks with explicit temporal constraints and latency-aligned evaluation, and finds that the best trained VLA attains only 8.3% temporal success even on Easy tasks, indicating pronounced deficits in temporal reasoning and transfer. (arXiv:2611.31588; arXiv:2611.33130; arXiv:2611.32233)
  • Additional contributions span multi-agent optimization, adaptation, and higher-level reasoning and assistance: DS-TO is a fully decentralized distributed sampling-based trajectory optimizer that has each agent sample only in its own control space while communicating with neighbors, and experiments on 2D/3D tasks with up to 1024 agents report improved exploration, constraint satisfaction, and scalability over derivative-based and sampling baselines; R2R’s deployment-localization plus Human-Anchored RL adapts released autonomous-driving planners and perception to new vehicles and sensors, raising a diffusion planner’s closed-loop success from 82.2% to 95.2% and boosting a released multi-camera detector’s mAP from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera rig without target labels; Ada-BFM adds lightweight adapters to Behavior Foundation Models for humanoid scene interaction, enabling shared-domain models that handle diverse terrains and object contacts in sim and real while accelerating learning and largely preserving prior skills; an offline RL method learns a help-seeking policy that tailors human-intervention alerts to past helpfulness and autonomous progress, improving exploration and reducing unnecessary alerts in simulation and enhancing novice support without harming expert performance in a physical user study; Embodied Neuro-Symbolic AI is proposed as a deployment-oriented paradigm that combines learned perception and control with explicit background knowledge and formal constraints to enable robots that can reason about environments, adapt to new tasks, and provide auditable compliance. (arXiv:2611.31561; arXiv:2611.31814; arXiv:2611.31863; arXiv:2611.31925; arXiv:2611.31488)

Design, Control, and Teleoperation of a Dexterous Robotic Hand with Soft-Rigid Hybrid FingersMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes a five-fingered tendon-driven Soft-Rigid Hybrid Hand with 12 active DoF, combining structural compliance with a rigid skeleton to enable linear control for soft grasping and teleoperated manipulation. In evaluation of grasp postures within the human range, the proposed hand successfully achieves 28 of 33 (84.8%) grasp postures and demonstrates high-bandwidth motion, reaching up to 1432 deg/s extension speed compared to about 700 deg/s for the equivalent human joint. Teleoperation experiments on various grasping and non-rigid manipulation tasks (e.g., picking up thin deformable objects and interacting with soft or delicate targets) indicate that the hand’s mechanical design and control strategy are effective for compliant, dexterous manipulation.Link
OmniAct: One Policy, One Million EmbodimentsUniversity of WashingtonThe paper introduces OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy that conditions directly on each robot’s kinematic graph (joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity to vision) and uses flow matching to predict relative joint motions for robots with diverse degrees of freedom and configurations. Trained on procedurally generated trajectories for one million simulated robot embodiments across diverse manipulation tasks, OmniAct achieves 63.0% success on 2,000 embodiment-scene-held-out evaluation episodes, outperforming pi 0.5 (17.8%) and the strongest baseline (57.9%). On the task families used to evaluate GPT-6 Astra, OmniAct reaches 64.7% success versus 41.1% for GPT-6 Astra, and on a previously unseen real-world robot it attains nearly twice the success rate of the next best method (MolmoAct2), while exhibiting distinct morphology-dependent manipulation strategies.Link
Sampling at Scale: Distributed Multi-Agent Trajectory OptimizationMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes DS-TO (Distributed Sampling-based Trajectory Optimization), a distributed architecture that splits multi-agent trajectory optimization so each agent samples only in its own control space, independent of neighborhood size, while still incorporating constraint-induced non-convexity and neighbors’ decisions. DS-TO is fully decentralized, relying only on neighborhood communication with parallel local updates, and includes efficient variants such as parallel Jacobi updates within each subproblem and single-step constraint linearization to reduce computation. Experiments on 2D and 3D multi-agent tasks with up to 1024 agents show that DS-TO improves exploration and constraint satisfaction compared to state-of-the-art derivative-based distributed optimization methods, and scales better than existing distributed sampling approaches.Link
Duo: Towards Spatiotemporal Action Coordination in Bimanual PoliciesETH ZurichThe paper introduces Duo, a bimanual manipulation policy architecture that represents each arm as a separate action token per timestep within a shared action head, enabling cross-arm attention during flow matching to model both spatial and temporal dependencies. On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, Duo improves overall success for all four evaluated base policies (0.45B–3.62B parameters), yielding gains of 12.55 and 9.02 percentage points for FLOWER (0.89B) and π₀.₅ (3.62B), respectively. Despite its compact size, FLOWER-Duo attains the best randomized-generalization, precision, and memory scores on RoboDojo, and in real-world tasks it outperforms π₀.₅ in both success rate and execution speed; ablations indicate that cross-arm attention and arm-wise interaction are key to the accuracy-compute trade-off.Link
ViTacLab: Physically Consistent Visuo-Tactile Simulation, Benchmarking, and Representation Learning for Robotic ManipulationUC BerkeleyThe paper presents ViTacLab, consisting of ViTacSim (a tri-modal visuo-tactile simulator), ViTacBench (a benchmark suite), and ViTacPolicy (a visuo-tactile policy architecture) to support physically consistent visuo-tactile simulation and learning for contact-rich robotic manipulation across grippers, dexterous hands, and bimanual systems. ViTacSim combines a global sparse contact-force lattice, a local dense force array, and tactile images anchored to PhysX contact outputs, and uses local stiffness alignment, decoupled slip–stick reconstruction, and joint projection to correct normal/tangential inconsistencies while maintaining GPU-parallel throughput of 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS with 256 parallel environments. In controlled Hertzian indentation and real-sensor sliding tests, ViTacSim reduces normal and tangential force RMSE from 2.31 N to 0.42 N and from 1.85 N to 0.53 N, respectively; simulation and real-world experiments show performance gains on several contact-rich tasks, though absolute success remains low on tactile-dominant tasks, underscoring the difficulty of purely tactile control.Link
DexWAC: A Unified World-Actor-Critic Model for Dexterous Robot ManipulationUC San DiegoThe paper proposes DexWAC, a unified world-actor-critic model that jointly learns an action-conditioned world model, an actor, and a bootstrapped critic in a single pretraining stage, using visual prediction for control-relevant features and value prediction for task-relevant representations. Built on an image-editing backbone, DexWAC predicts for each action chunk the endpoint visual latent, proprioception, reward, and continuation, enabling efficient imagined transitions without generating intermediate frames, and subsequently improves the actor via PPO on imagined rollouts or critic-guided best-of-N selection after world-model and critic adaptation on real rollouts. In simulation and on two real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, DexWAC outperforms large-scale pretrained VLA and WAM baselines, achieving 75% average real-world success compared to 45% for π₀.₅, with a world model that is both more accurate and faster than video-based baselines; ablations support the benefit of jointly learning dynamics and value.Link
VidForce: Predicting Force-Aware Articulation by Observing HumansETH ZurichThe paper introduces VidForce, a method that estimates both an interaction trajectory and a force profile from third-person RGB videos of human interactions, optionally using 2D contact clicks and text object–action prompts, by leveraging learned physical prototypes from limited instrumented force measurements. VidForce uses shared force-level and temporal-profile prototypes as priors across interactions of similar effort, refines them with geometric and vision-language features, and jointly predicts trajectories and forces via a conditional flow model; a separate torque model shows extensibility to other physical quantities. On held-out interactions from the Hoi! dataset, VidForce achieves 6.6 cm ATE, peak-force MAE/RMSE of 4.81 N/5.92 N, and force-profile RMSE of 5.52 N; it produces consistent motion and force estimates on real-world videos and enables Spot robot manipulation of articulated objects using trajectories and force profiles inferred from human demonstrations.Link
Exploratory Midtraining for Robot PoliciesCarnegie Mellon University; University of WashingtonThe paper studies midtraining for robot policies and introduces β-Data, a dataset collected via adversarial teleoperation in which a human adversary reversibly perturbs the environment while another operator continues toward task completion, producing trajectories that richly cover low-level behaviors without resets. Trajectory segments in β-Data are relabeled with local natural-language outcomes, and a pretrained policy is finetuned on this data while being conditioned on recent action history, yielding policies that exhibit more sustained use of diverse low-level behaviors. Across simulated manipulation tasks and three real-robot platforms, midtraining with β-Data leads to more favorable data scaling, better zero-shot generalization, improved benefits from extended rollout time, and more sample-efficient online finetuning (fewer rollouts and higher eventual success) compared to training on standard demonstration data.Link
iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future PredictionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes iWAM, an efficient world-action-model variant that conditions a policy on intermediate features from a single evaluation of a pretrained video model, avoiding explicit high-resolution future prediction while retaining the benefits of world models for action prediction. iWAM inverts each training future frame into a latent noise vector using flow-matching dynamics and learns a bidirectional flow map that amortizes future-generation computation so that a single velocity evaluation at inference (from a sampled prior latent) yields informative conditioning features for the policy. Across simulated benchmarks and real-robot experiments, iWAM matches the generalization benefits of full video-prediction WAMs while substantially reducing inference cost for real-time control.Link
Simulator-as-Proxy World Model for Robot ManipulationUC San DiegoThe paper introduces SimPWM (Simulator-as-Proxy World Model), which represents a scene from the first frame using a calibrated 3D robot model and point-based object representations, applies a parameter-free rule-based simulator to evolve their state under actions, and uses a video model to render realistic future frames while exposing the explicit simulated state. On DROID episodes from unseen setups, SimPWM shows that video models strongly follow the simulated robot arm proxy (robot IoU 0.75–0.78, exceeding at most 0.69 for end-to-end models), and that adding simulated objects primarily affects predicted object state, reducing missed pick-ups from 54% to 38% relative to a robot-only proxy. By freezing simulated objects while replaying recorded arm motions, the study reveals that some video models (e.g., MiniMax-H3) faithfully track the simulated object state (rigid objects’ lift rate drops from 42% to 10%), whereas others (e.g., LTX-2.3) often infer grasps from arm motion alone (lift rate only drops from 57% to 49%), and that for all base models the explicit simulated state reliably encodes what the simulator did even when rendered video disagrees under perturbed actions.Link
One Phase Is Enough: Sim-to-Real Transfer with Direct Visual Reinforcement LearningMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes DRIFT, a one-phase sim-to-real visual reinforcement learning recipe that trains control policies directly on image observations by freezing pretrained foundation vision encoders so RL only learns the control layer. In real-world robotic manipulation across three tasks, DRIFT outperforms two-phase distillation methods under matched architectures, observations, and compute, achieving a 58.8% relative improvement in average real-world success rate. Simulation studies show DRIFT’s relative advantage grows from roughly 9% on in-distribution conditions to over 61% under out-of-distribution shifts, indicating improved generalization that supports superior sim-to-real transfer.Link
Roboclock: A Unified Benchmark for Time-Aware Robotic IntelligenceUC San DiegoThe paper introduces RoboClock, a unified time-aware robotic manipulation benchmark with 60 tasks across five domains, structured around three temporal constraint types: Temporal Window, Event-Reactive, and Throughput, and evaluated with a Latency-Aligned protocol and time-sensitive metrics. Across more than 30 evaluated methods, the best-performing trained vision-language-action (VLA) approach achieves only 8.3% temporal success even on Easy-level tasks under RoboClock, revealing substantial deficits in temporal reasoning, generalization, and transfer in current robotic systems.Link
Embodied Neuro-symbolic AI: Making Robot Behavior Auditable, Safe, and ScalableCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes Embodied Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) AI, a deployment-oriented paradigm that integrates learned perception, planning, and control with explicit machine-readable background knowledge and formal constraints for robotic systems. By combining knowledge-guided perception, constraint-aware symbolic planning, and hybrid learning, Embodied NeSy AI is presented as enabling robots to reason about environments, adapt to new tasks, and provide auditable, machine-checkable compliance with safety and operational requirements.Link

Hi3R-VLA: Grounding Semantics and Actions in 3DCNRS; INRIA; PSL Research University; École normale supérieureThe paper introduces Hi3R-VLA, a 3D vision-language-action policy that grounds visual, linguistic, and action modalities in explicit 3D geometry by fusing spatially aligned visual features and language into point cloud representations and anchoring low-level control commands in the same 3D frame with 3D-RoPE-enhanced action-to-point attention. Hi3R-VLA, with fewer than 250M trainable parameters, achieves state-of-the-art performance on RoboCasa, RLBench, RoboTwin2, DexJoCo, and DexSuite across diverse robotic manipulation tasks and embodiments, and demonstrates effectiveness in real-robot evaluations.Link
FabricBench: A Standardized Policy Benchmark for Bimanual Fabric Manipulation with a Contact-Driven Deformable SimulatorHorizon Robotics; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Style3D ResearchThe paper presents FabricBench, a standardized benchmark for bimanual fabric manipulation featuring six tasks (including garments, silk, and bags), 10,000 simulation-ready assets, and controlled configuration and appearance generalization tests, built on an open-source Newton-based deformable simulator with stable contact-driven bimanual interactions. In a controlled grasp–hold–release evaluation, the proposed solver succeeds in all 45 trials while tested baseline configurations achieve no successes, indicating improved modeling of cloth–rigid and multi-body contact dynamics. Across six evaluated policies, including VLA and world-action models, the best policy attains 77% average success but remains below 36% on long-horizon T-shirt folding and drops to 6–14% success under highly deformed initial T-shirt configurations, highlighting configuration generalization as a key open challenge; zero-shot real-robot deployment of π_{0.5} preserves task difficulty ordering with Spearman ρ=0.94.Link
FoveaWAM: Role-Aware 4D World-Action Modeling for Dynamic ManipulationBeijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence; Beijing Zhongguancun Academy; Peking UniversityThe paper proposes FoveaWAM, a 4D world-action model for dynamic manipulation that allocates prediction precision by role: modeling the target object as a metric SE(3) trajectory, the surroundings as sparse 3D flow, and relying on the current frame for static context to enable fast, targeted forecasting. FoveaWAM achieves a 15.1 ms action latency on a single RTX 5090, about 10× faster than video-based world-action baselines, thereby reducing scene evolution during inference. On the OnTimeBench dynamic manipulation benchmark with latency-aware evaluation, FoveaWAM attains 82.1% success, outperforming the strongest baseline by 20.8 percentage points, and also leads on DOMINO and real-robot tasks.Link
Many Senses, One World: Learning Hierarchical Multimodal World Representations for Bimanual Dexterous ManipulationNational Key Laboratory of Human-Machine Hybrid Augmented Intelligence; Xi'an Jiaotong UniversityThe paper introduces HiMoDex, a two-stage framework for bimanual dexterous manipulation that first pretrains a hierarchical multimodal representation network on 1.19M synchronized timesteps to fuse vision, tactile, force-torque, and audio into world-state embeddings, then freezes this network and trains a shared world-action transformer for action generation and action-conditioned future-state prediction. On five real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks, HiMoDex achieves an average success rate of 78.7%, representing an absolute improvement of 32.0 percentage points over the strongest evaluated baseline. Ablation and failure analyses indicate that hierarchical representation learning and the two-stage training procedure are key contributors to the observed performance gains across tasks.Link
Beyond the Benchmark: Localizing Released Autonomous Driving Models to Real VehiclesToyota North America R&D; University of North TexasThe paper studies the deployment gap between released autonomous-driving models and new real-vehicle platforms and introduces Released-to-Real (R2R), a deployment-localization methodology that identifies components coupled to source-system assumptions and corrects only those while preserving the rest of the released model. For data and behavior adaptation, the authors propose Human-Anchored Reinforcement Learning (HA-RL), which addresses mismatch between trajectory-level planning and receding-horizon execution by correcting ego-state handling using recorded target driving, avoiding costly closed-loop RL rollouts; for sensor adaptation, R2R adjusts spatial and temporal representations with frozen perception weights and no target annotations or retraining. Applied to a released diffusion-based planner, HA-RL improves closed-loop success rate from 82.2% to 95.2%, while sensor adaptation across multiple released detectors and three real sensor rigs increases vehicle mAP of a released multi-camera detector from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera target rig.Link
Co-DRIS: Physics-Informed Predictive Belief and Variable-Impedance Learning for Reactive Catching under Coupled UncertaintyHarbin Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes Co-DRIS + SuperMimic, a perception–action co-design framework for reactive catching under coupled uncertainties, combining a physics-informed inertial world-frame model with ballistic kinematics and zero-initialized contact residuals, an action-conditioned dual-stream causal GRU belief encoder, and phase-aware variable-impedance soft-catching control for a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 arm. Under unbiased three-seed tail evaluation on unseen throws (15,360 throws per scenario), the method achieves 89.6 ± 0.4% success on standard benchmarks (including an increase to 70.5% at 4-sigma noise) and 66.6 ± 0.5% across 10 coupled/dropout stress scenarios, improving performance by 39.0 percentage points over DRIS and by 30.4 percentage points under 50% observation dropout. Additional experiments validate impedance decoupling, causal contact triggering under +0.65 s flight-time shifts, and cross-task transfer, improving success from 87.5% to 100.0% on steel-plate ping-pong bouncing and achieving +8.4% catching success under 4× noise on FR3+Hand with 6–13% lower peak impact forces.Link
Ada-BFM: Adapting Behavior Foundation Models for Humanoid Scene InteractionFudan University; Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Southeast University; The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper introduces Ada-BFM, a post-training framework that augments pretrained Behavior Foundation Models with lightweight adapters to enable humanoid scene interaction, allowing scene-dependent geometric constraints and contact dynamics to be handled while leveraging existing whole-body control capabilities. Ada-BFM yields a shared model per interaction domain rather than per task, supporting diverse terrain and object interaction behaviors in simulation and real-world settings, and is reported to accelerate learning compared with training controllers from scratch while largely preserving pretrained behavioral competence.Link
Learning When a Robot Should Ask For Help: Adaptive Intervention Requests in Human-Robot TeamingCSIRO Technology; Monash UniversityThe paper proposes an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that learns a help-seeking policy for robots, enabling adaptive human intervention requests based on past intervention helpfulness and autonomous progress during human-robot teaming. In simulation across different task environments and user expertise levels, the learned alert policy selectively requests human help, improving exploration efficiency and reducing unnecessary alerts compared to non-adaptive alert strategies. In a physical user study, the learned policy enhances support for novice users without degrading expert user performance in real-time human-robot teaming missions.Link
Ego2Real: Learning Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Visuo-Tactile DemonstrationsAGILINK; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper introduces Ego2Real, a framework that uses egocentric visual demonstrations and tactile glove data to transfer bimanual dexterous manipulation skills to anthropomorphic robotic hands. Ego2Real employs tactile-guided hand-object interaction reconstruction to refine human hand poses and contact geometry, and contact-informed reinforcement learning to convert human contact patterns into robot contact rewards during policy training. Experiments on bimanual dexterous manipulation show that incorporating tactile supervision in both reconstruction and policy learning stages increases manipulation success rates relative to variants without such tactile supervision.Link
Motion Chain of Thought: Disentangling Motion Semantics from Visual Appearance for Robotic ManipulationHong Kong University of Science and Technology; Joy Future Academy, JD; Nanjing University; The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen; Tsinghua UniversityThe paper proposes PILOT (Physical Inference for Latent Optimized Trajectories), which introduces a Representational Deduction mechanism to integrate motion chain-of-thought guidance, encouraging the action branch of World Action Models to explicitly model potential state transition tokens that guide fine-grained motion trajectories. By disentangling high-level motion semantics from low-level trajectory details, the proposed RD mechanism improves success rates and generalization of World Action Models on complex robotic manipulation tasks while enhancing the physical interpretability of the learned models. The additional state-transition supervision signals provided by RD alleviate sparse supervision in action generation, enabling more data-efficient adaptation to real robots with reduced demonstration sets.Link
