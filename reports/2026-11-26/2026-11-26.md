## Executive Summary

- Multiple systems advance dexterous, contact-rich manipulation: a five-finger tendon-driven Soft-Rigid Hybrid Hand with 12 active DoF achieves 28/33 (84.8%) human-range grasp postures and up to 1432 deg/s joint extension in teleoperated soft and delicate manipulation tasks; ViTacLab couples a tri-modal visuo-tactile simulator, benchmark, and policy, reaching 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS with 256 environments and cutting normal/tangential force RMSE from 2.31→0.42 N and 1.85→0.53 N, though success on purely tactile-dominant tasks remains low; FabricBench provides six bimanual fabric tasks with 10,000 assets and a Newton-based simulator whose solver succeeds in 45/45 grasp–hold–release trials where baselines fail, yet best learned policies still struggle on long-horizon and highly deformed T-shirt configurations; HiMoDex pretrains a hierarchical multimodal (vision, tactile, force-torque, audio) representation then a shared world-action transformer, achieving 78.7% average success ( +32.0 points over the strongest baseline) on five real bimanual dexterous tasks; Ego2Real leverages egocentric video plus tactile-glove data for bimanual skill transfer, using tactile-guided reconstruction and contact-informed RL, and reports higher manipulation success when tactile supervision is used in both stages than in ablated variants.  ([arXiv:2611.32582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32582); [arXiv:2611.32476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32476); [arXiv:2611.31577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31577); [arXiv:2611.31782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31782); [arXiv:2611.32101](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32101))
- New policy architectures broaden generality across embodiments and arms while improving dynamic control: OmniAct conditions on each robot’s kinematic graph and uses flow matching over joint deltas, trained on 1M simulated embodiments to achieve 63.0% success on 2,000 held-out embodiment–scene episodes (vs. 17.8% for π₀.₅ and 57.9% for the best baseline), 64.7% success on GPT-6 Astra task families (vs. 41.1%), and nearly 2× the success of MolmoAct2 on a previously unseen real robot; Duo treats each arm as an action token within a shared head with cross-arm attention, yielding RoboTwin 2.0 gains of 12.55 and 9.02 percentage points for FLOWER (0.89B) and π₀.₅ (3.62B), and enabling compact FLOWER-Duo to attain top randomized-generalization, precision, and memory on RoboDojo and better real-world success and speed than π₀.₅; Co-DRIS + SuperMimic co-designs perception and control for reactive catching with a physics-informed world-frame model, dual-stream causal GRU belief encoder, and phase-aware variable-impedance control, achieving 89.6 ± 0.4% success on standard benchmarks (70.5% at 4σ noise) and 66.6 ± 0.5% across 10 stress scenarios, with large gains over DRIS and improved catching and reduced impact forces in transfer tasks.  ([arXiv:2611.33158](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158); [arXiv:2611.32108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32108); [arXiv:2611.31843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31843))
- World(-action) modeling methods target more efficient and interpretable control: DexWAC jointly trains an action-conditioned world model, actor, and bootstrapped critic using an image-editing backbone that predicts endpoint visual latents, proprioception, reward, and continuation per action chunk, then improves the actor via PPO or critic-guided best-of-N over imagined rollouts, achieving 75% average success on two real dexterous tasks vs. 45% for π₀.₅ and reporting faster, more accurate dynamics than video-based baselines; iWAM conditions a policy on intermediate features from a single evaluation of a pretrained video model by inverting futures into latent noise with flow matching and learning a bidirectional flow map, matching the generalization of full video-prediction WAMs while cutting inference cost for real-time control; SimPWM represents scenes with an explicit 3D robot model and point-based objects evolved by a rule-based simulator and rendered by a video model, improving robot IoU to 0.75–0.78 on unseen DROID setups (vs. ≤0.69 end-to-end) and reducing missed pick-ups from 54% to 38% when adding simulated objects, while analyses show that explicit simulated state remains reliable even when rendered video disagrees; PILOT augments World Action Models with a Representational Deduction mechanism that introduces motion chain-of-thought via explicit state-transition tokens, which the authors report improves success and generalization on complex manipulation, enhances physical interpretability, and enables more data-efficient real-robot adaptation under reduced demonstrations.  ([arXiv:2611.32654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32654); [arXiv:2611.33141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141); [arXiv:2611.32786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32786); [arXiv:2611.32241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32241))
- Several works address temporal dynamics, latency, and tactile/force inference in perception–action loops: FoveaWAM is a 4D world-action model that allocates precision to the target object’s SE(3) trajectory and sparse 3D flow for surroundings while reusing the current frame for static context, achieving 15.1 ms action latency on a single RTX 5090 (≈10× faster than video-based WAMs) and 82.1% success on OnTimeBench (20.8 points above the strongest baseline) plus leading results on DOMINO and real robots; VidForce learns shared force-level and temporal prototypes from limited instrumented data to jointly infer interaction trajectories and force profiles from third-person RGB, reaching 6.6 cm ATE, peak-force MAE/RMSE of 4.81/5.92 N, and force-profile RMSE of 5.52 N on held-out Hoi! interactions, and enabling Spot to manipulate articulated objects using inferred trajectories and forces.  ([arXiv:2611.31705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31705); [arXiv:2611.32967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32967))
- Training recipes, datasets, and benchmarks expose gaps and improve data efficiency: DRIFT trains sim-to-real visual RL policies in a single phase by freezing foundation vision encoders and learning only the control layer, yielding a 58.8% relative gain in average real-world success over matched two-phase distillation methods and larger simulated advantages under out-of-distribution shifts (>61% relative) than in-distribution (~9%); β-Data collects adversarial teleoperation where one human perturbs the environment while another completes the task and relabels segments with local language outcomes, and midtraining on this dataset with recent-action conditioning produces policies with better data scaling, zero-shot generalization, extended-horizon benefits, and more sample-efficient online finetuning than standard demonstrations across simulated and three real-robot platforms; RoboClock defines 60 manipulation tasks with explicit temporal constraints and latency-aligned evaluation, and finds that the best trained VLA attains only 8.3% temporal success even on Easy tasks, indicating pronounced deficits in temporal reasoning and transfer.  ([arXiv:2611.31588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31588); [arXiv:2611.33130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33130); [arXiv:2611.32233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32233))
- Additional contributions span multi-agent optimization, adaptation, and higher-level reasoning and assistance: DS-TO is a fully decentralized distributed sampling-based trajectory optimizer that has each agent sample only in its own control space while communicating with neighbors, and experiments on 2D/3D tasks with up to 1024 agents report improved exploration, constraint satisfaction, and scalability over derivative-based and sampling baselines; R2R’s deployment-localization plus Human-Anchored RL adapts released autonomous-driving planners and perception to new vehicles and sensors, raising a diffusion planner’s closed-loop success from 82.2% to 95.2% and boosting a released multi-camera detector’s mAP from 0.041 to 0.420 on a five-camera rig without target labels; Ada-BFM adds lightweight adapters to Behavior Foundation Models for humanoid scene interaction, enabling shared-domain models that handle diverse terrains and object contacts in sim and real while accelerating learning and largely preserving prior skills; an offline RL method learns a help-seeking policy that tailors human-intervention alerts to past helpfulness and autonomous progress, improving exploration and reducing unnecessary alerts in simulation and enhancing novice support without harming expert performance in a physical user study; Embodied Neuro-Symbolic AI is proposed as a deployment-oriented paradigm that combines learned perception and control with explicit background knowledge and formal constraints to enable robots that can reason about environments, adapt to new tasks, and provide auditable compliance.  ([arXiv:2611.31561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31561); [arXiv:2611.31814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31814); [arXiv:2611.31863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31863); [arXiv:2611.31925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31925); [arXiv:2611.31488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31488))

| Design, Control, and Teleoperation of a Dexterous Robotic Hand with Soft-Rigid Hybrid Fingers | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper proposes a five-fingered tendon-driven Soft-Rigid Hybrid Hand with 12 active DoF, combining structural compliance with a rigid skeleton to enable linear control for soft grasping and teleoperated manipulation. In evaluation of grasp postures within the human range, the proposed hand successfully achieves 28 of 33 (84.8%) grasp postures and demonstrates high-bandwidth motion, reaching up to 1432 deg/s extension speed compared to about 700 deg/s for the equivalent human joint. Teleoperation experiments on various grasping and non-rigid manipulation tasks (e.g., picking up thin deformable objects and interacting with soft or delicate targets) indicate that the hand’s mechanical design and control strategy are effective for compliant, dexterous manipulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32582 |
| OmniAct: One Policy, One Million Embodiments | University of Washington | The paper introduces OmniAct, a cross-embodiment manipulation policy that conditions directly on each robot’s kinematic graph (joint positions, motion axes, and connectivity to vision) and uses flow matching to predict relative joint motions for robots with diverse degrees of freedom and configurations. Trained on procedurally generated trajectories for one million simulated robot embodiments across diverse manipulation tasks, OmniAct achieves 63.0% success on 2,000 embodiment-scene-held-out evaluation episodes, outperforming pi 0.5 (17.8%) and the strongest baseline (57.9%). On the task families used to evaluate GPT-6 Astra, OmniAct reaches 64.7% success versus 41.1% for GPT-6 Astra, and on a previously unseen real-world robot it attains nearly twice the success rate of the next best method (MolmoAct2), while exhibiting distinct morphology-dependent manipulation strategies. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33158 |
| Sampling at Scale: Distributed Multi-Agent Trajectory Optimization | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper proposes DS-TO (Distributed Sampling-based Trajectory Optimization), a distributed architecture that splits multi-agent trajectory optimization so each agent samples only in its own control space, independent of neighborhood size, while still incorporating constraint-induced non-convexity and neighbors’ decisions. DS-TO is fully decentralized, relying only on neighborhood communication with parallel local updates, and includes efficient variants such as parallel Jacobi updates within each subproblem and single-step constraint linearization to reduce computation. Experiments on 2D and 3D multi-agent tasks with up to 1024 agents show that DS-TO improves exploration and constraint satisfaction compared to state-of-the-art derivative-based distributed optimization methods, and scales better than existing distributed sampling approaches. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31561 |
| Duo: Towards Spatiotemporal Action Coordination in Bimanual Policies | ETH Zurich | The paper introduces Duo, a bimanual manipulation policy architecture that represents each arm as a separate action token per timestep within a shared action head, enabling cross-arm attention during flow matching to model both spatial and temporal dependencies. On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, Duo improves overall success for all four evaluated base policies (0.45B–3.62B parameters), yielding gains of 12.55 and 9.02 percentage points for FLOWER (0.89B) and π₀.₅ (3.62B), respectively. Despite its compact size, FLOWER-Duo attains the best randomized-generalization, precision, and memory scores on RoboDojo, and in real-world tasks it outperforms π₀.₅ in both success rate and execution speed; ablations indicate that cross-arm attention and arm-wise interaction are key to the accuracy-compute trade-off. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32108 |
| ViTacLab: Physically Consistent Visuo-Tactile Simulation, Benchmarking, and Representation Learning for Robotic Manipulation | UC Berkeley | The paper presents ViTacLab, consisting of ViTacSim (a tri-modal visuo-tactile simulator), ViTacBench (a benchmark suite), and ViTacPolicy (a visuo-tactile policy architecture) to support physically consistent visuo-tactile simulation and learning for contact-rich robotic manipulation across grippers, dexterous hands, and bimanual systems. ViTacSim combines a global sparse contact-force lattice, a local dense force array, and tactile images anchored to PhysX contact outputs, and uses local stiffness alignment, decoupled slip–stick reconstruction, and joint projection to correct normal/tangential inconsistencies while maintaining GPU-parallel throughput of 2,482.9 tactile-pipeline FPS with 256 parallel environments. In controlled Hertzian indentation and real-sensor sliding tests, ViTacSim reduces normal and tangential force RMSE from 2.31 N to 0.42 N and from 1.85 N to 0.53 N, respectively; simulation and real-world experiments show performance gains on several contact-rich tasks, though absolute success remains low on tactile-dominant tasks, underscoring the difficulty of purely tactile control. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32476 |
| DexWAC: A Unified World-Actor-Critic Model for Dexterous Robot Manipulation | UC San Diego | The paper proposes DexWAC, a unified world-actor-critic model that jointly learns an action-conditioned world model, an actor, and a bootstrapped critic in a single pretraining stage, using visual prediction for control-relevant features and value prediction for task-relevant representations. Built on an image-editing backbone, DexWAC predicts for each action chunk the endpoint visual latent, proprioception, reward, and continuation, enabling efficient imagined transitions without generating intermediate frames, and subsequently improves the actor via PPO on imagined rollouts or critic-guided best-of-N selection after world-model and critic adaptation on real rollouts. In simulation and on two real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, DexWAC outperforms large-scale pretrained VLA and WAM baselines, achieving 75% average real-world success compared to 45% for π₀.₅, with a world model that is both more accurate and faster than video-based baselines; ablations support the benefit of jointly learning dynamics and value. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32654 |
| VidForce: Predicting Force-Aware Articulation by Observing Humans | ETH Zurich | The paper introduces VidForce, a method that estimates both an interaction trajectory and a force profile from third-person RGB videos of human interactions, optionally using 2D contact clicks and text object–action prompts, by leveraging learned physical prototypes from limited instrumented force measurements. VidForce uses shared force-level and temporal-profile prototypes as priors across interactions of similar effort, refines them with geometric and vision-language features, and jointly predicts trajectories and forces via a conditional flow model; a separate torque model shows extensibility to other physical quantities. On held-out interactions from the Hoi! dataset, VidForce achieves 6.6 cm ATE, peak-force MAE/RMSE of 4.81 N/5.92 N, and force-profile RMSE of 5.52 N; it produces consistent motion and force estimates on real-world videos and enables Spot robot manipulation of articulated objects using trajectories and force profiles inferred from human demonstrations. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32967 |
| Exploratory Midtraining for Robot Policies | Carnegie Mellon University; University of Washington | The paper studies midtraining for robot policies and introduces β-Data, a dataset collected via adversarial teleoperation in which a human adversary reversibly perturbs the environment while another operator continues toward task completion, producing trajectories that richly cover low-level behaviors without resets. Trajectory segments in β-Data are relabeled with local natural-language outcomes, and a pretrained policy is finetuned on this data while being conditioned on recent action history, yielding policies that exhibit more sustained use of diverse low-level behaviors. Across simulated manipulation tasks and three real-robot platforms, midtraining with β-Data leads to more favorable data scaling, better zero-shot generalization, improved benefits from extended rollout time, and more sample-efficient online finetuning (fewer rollouts and higher eventual success) compared to training on standard demonstration data. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33130 |
| iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper proposes iWAM, an efficient world-action-model variant that conditions a policy on intermediate features from a single evaluation of a pretrained video model, avoiding explicit high-resolution future prediction while retaining the benefits of world models for action prediction. iWAM inverts each training future frame into a latent noise vector using flow-matching dynamics and learns a bidirectional flow map that amortizes future-generation computation so that a single velocity evaluation at inference (from a sampled prior latent) yields informative conditioning features for the policy. Across simulated benchmarks and real-robot experiments, iWAM matches the generalization benefits of full video-prediction WAMs while substantially reducing inference cost for real-time control. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141 |
| Simulator-as-Proxy World Model for Robot Manipulation | UC San Diego | The paper introduces SimPWM (Simulator-as-Proxy World Model), which represents a scene from the first frame using a calibrated 3D robot model and point-based object representations, applies a parameter-free rule-based simulator to evolve their state under actions, and uses a video model to render realistic future frames while exposing the explicit simulated state. On DROID episodes from unseen setups, SimPWM shows that video models strongly follow the simulated robot arm proxy (robot IoU 0.75–0.78, exceeding at most 0.69 for end-to-end models), and that adding simulated objects primarily affects predicted object state, reducing missed pick-ups from 54% to 38% relative to a robot-only proxy. By freezing simulated objects while replaying recorded arm motions, the study reveals that some video models (e.g., MiniMax-H3) faithfully track the simulated object state (rigid objects’ lift rate drops from 42% to 10%), whereas others (e.g., LTX-2.3) often infer grasps from arm motion alone (lift rate only drops from 57% to 49%), and that for all base models the explicit simulated state reliably encodes what the simulator did even when rendered video disagrees under perturbed actions. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32786 |
| One Phase Is Enough: Sim-to-Real Transfer with Direct Visual Reinforcement Learning | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper proposes DRIFT, a one-phase sim-to-real visual reinforcement learning recipe that trains control policies directly on image observations by freezing pretrained foundation vision encoders so RL only learns the control layer. In real-world robotic manipulation across three tasks, DRIFT outperforms two-phase distillation methods under matched architectures, observations, and compute, achieving a 58.8% relative improvement in average real-world success rate. Simulation studies show DRIFT’s relative advantage grows from roughly 9% on in-distribution conditions to over 61% under out-of-distribution shifts, indicating improved generalization that supports superior sim-to-real transfer. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31588 |
| Roboclock: A Unified Benchmark for Time-Aware Robotic Intelligence | UC San Diego | The paper introduces RoboClock, a unified time-aware robotic manipulation benchmark with 60 tasks across five domains, structured around three temporal constraint types: Temporal Window, Event-Reactive, and Throughput, and evaluated with a Latency-Aligned protocol and time-sensitive metrics. | Embodied Neuro-symbolic AI: Making Robot Behavior Auditable, Safe, and Scalable | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper proposes Embodied Neuro-Symbolic (NeSy) AI, a deployment-oriented paradigm that integrates learned perception, planning, and control with explicit machine-readable background knowledge and formal constraints for robotic systems. By combining knowledge-guided perception, constraint-aware symbolic planning, and hybrid learning, Embodied NeSy AI is presented as enabling robots to reason about environments, adapt to new tasks, and provide auditable, machine-checkable compliance with safety and operational requirements. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31488 |
