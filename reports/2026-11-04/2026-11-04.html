Submission date covered: 2026-11-03

Executive Summary

  • Several works advance whole-body humanoid and mobile manipulation: an interactive object search system fusing vision-language semantic plausibility with geometric occupancy in a 3D voxel belief field for active searching on a bimanual humanoid, outperforming geometry-only and LLM-planning baselines; a dodgeball framework that learns monocular perception plus whole-body dodge and recovery policies, achieving up to 97.8–100.0% dodge success in simulation and >85% on a Unitree G1; HuG, an affordance-aware grasping framework with belief-driven active perception and kinodynamically filtered training data that attains 87.5% real-world success on multi-level shelf grasps; Pixel-UMI and Pixel, which respectively introduce an environment-aware human-to-robot retargeting and masking pipeline achieving 87.0% average task progress across five mobile-manipulation tasks, and a low-cost wheeled humanoid plus unified language-conditioned control stack capable of dexterous whole-body mobile manipulation; Anchor 1, a humanoid controller using sparse calibrated motion references and decoupled error feedback that reduces tracking error by 30–80% under strict real-world scoring; CompliantBot, which trains compliant whole-body responses from task inputs and proprioception alone and shows greater yielding, lower interaction forces, and lower joint power than baselines; and a supervised autonomous bimanual surgical system that coordinates suction and cauterization for splenic trauma, reaching 82.9% average cauterization coverage on an ex vivo hemorrhage model. (arXiv:2611.00499; arXiv:2611.01197; arXiv:2611.01659; arXiv:2611.03647; arXiv:2611.03648; arXiv:2611.00193; arXiv:2611.01748; arXiv:2611.00237)
  • New navigation and perception systems target open-world, social, and safety-critical scenarios: SNAP learns a reinforcement-learning navigation policy over a semantic bird’s-eye-view map that jointly reasons about terrain and pedestrians, outperforming crowd-navigation and semantic baselines and transferring zero-shot to a legged robot in crowded environments; OpenNWM pretrains a generalist navigation world model on 17.5M action-free observations to learn latent actions, then aligns them with controls for improved visual prediction and navigation; MoSoLoco reuses pose-detector features with 2D skeletons for monocular 3D human localization, cutting JRDB error by about 23% versus a keypoint-only baseline and benefiting downstream forecasting while remaining Jetson-deployable; RESONATE provides a standardized socially aware navigation benchmark where, across 320 experiments, DWA+Gaussian social costmaps and SFM-MPPI emerge as strong baselines under different social–navigation trade-offs; a decentralized reachability-based runtime safety checker is deployed on outdoor UAV swarms, successfully verifying 4 s of safety per agent in roughly 500–520 ms over 140 flights; a Real2Sim2Real agent, CUE, actively probes contact dynamics to infer hidden physical parameters, reducing billiards ball-position error by 72% versus the strongest baseline and transferring to a real pool table; and a desert-ant-inspired learning-walk model, when implemented on a mobile robot, reproduces key exploratory-to-foraging behavioral transitions, supporting the plausibility of PI–visual-mismatch-driven active visual learning. (arXiv:2611.03498; arXiv:2611.01844; arXiv:2611.03189; arXiv:2611.02937; arXiv:2611.01081; arXiv:2611.02691; arXiv:2611.00392)
  • Tactile sensing and contact-rich manipulation are advanced by new hardware and policy designs: PanoTac introduces a <$50-per-fingertip compliant sensor with dense 12×32×3 normal-and-shear measurements at 60 Hz, and in a visuo-tactile pipeline boosts manipulation performance by an average of 46.7 percentage points over vision-only baselines, with ablations showing both spatial contact and shear signals are important; TF^2-Steering learns residual tactile steering over a flow-matching action expert via a tactile mixture-of-experts and three-stage training, improving success by 10.7 percentage points in simulation and real-world task-progress success by 9.1 points over the strongest baselines; and a systematic integration of tactile foundation models into Diffusion Policy reports empirical conditions under which pretrained tactile encoders and adaptation strategies (fine-tuning, mid-training) help end-to-end imitation learning on over 5,000 real-world contact-rich rollouts. (arXiv:2611.03520; arXiv:2611.01483; arXiv:2611.00957)
  • Several works introduce benchmarks and evaluation frameworks for complex robot behavior: DexArena defines a unified long-horizon bimanual dexterous-manipulation benchmark with 10 full tasks and 30 stage-level tasks that reveal difficulties in precision, generalization, and skill composition; RoboColosseum standardizes large-scale policy evaluation across 124 sites and 5,699 pairwise trials, showing frontier agents perform similarly to specialized VLAs yet all struggle on open-world tasks and that fine-tuned rankings can differ sharply from open-track results; KlaskTron offers an open, low-cost physical adversarial multi-agent RL testbed with a GPU-native digital twin and demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real policies that exhibit emergent adversarial behaviors; VLALagBench characterizes how inference latency affects task success, finding that even 100 ms delay can nearly halve moving-target performance and providing an empirical model to predict success vs. latency; MOST introduces a policy-internal memory over object states and subtask progress that raises success on RoboCasa365 from 65.9% to 72.1% and real-world mobile manipulation success from 6.66% to 43.33% versus XR-1; behavior graphs compress rollout datasets into interpretable graphs that users prefer over raw videos and that improve progress prediction error by 53.5% over the strongest baseline; uni-robot-agent standardizes interfaces for robot-use agents and shows an open-weight Qwen3.8-Flash-Next surpassing a reported GPT-6 baseline on RoboDojo (31.39 vs. 28.97) while remaining competitive on 19 real-world tasks; and RESONATE supplies a reproducible real-world benchmark for socially aware navigation with joint navigation–social metrics and human feedback. (arXiv:2611.02629; arXiv:2611.02497; arXiv:2611.03138; arXiv:2611.03103; arXiv:2611.00734; arXiv:2611.00405; arXiv:2611.01560; arXiv:2611.02937)
  • New learning algorithms and orchestration methods target planning, safety, and representation: EPO frames policy orchestration as choosing both a low-level policy and its goal, learning energy models to score policy–goal pairs and showing in simulation that refining goals before switching improves the precision–robustness trade-off and that the same frozen components transfer to real humanoid loco-manipulation; Hi-CoLA combines importance sampling, concentration bounds, and a differentiable surrogate to directly optimize a high-confidence performance lower bound, outperforming offline and safe RL baselines and offering certified guarantees in flex-route transit control; SPARK adds a semantic critic, targeted revisiting, and selective KB merging to embodied RAG, yielding up to 2.7× EQA gains in AirSim with fewer stored objects and surpassing undegraded references after multiple corrective episodes; CUE’s agentic Real2Sim2Real pipeline (also noted above) chooses experiments to reduce simulation–reality gaps in contact-rich dynamics; and complex-nerfacto trains a complex-valued NeRF of steady-state surface wavefields from pose-labeled LDV scans, roughly doubling Z-normalized cross-correlation (0.60 vs. 0.28) over an RGB nerfacto baseline on simulated welded structures and enabling defect-related analyses. (arXiv:2611.02546; arXiv:2611.01071; arXiv:2611.00344; arXiv:2611.02691; arXiv:2611.00527)
  • Several studies analyze and steer foundation-style robot policies and training dynamics: a behavioral-steering method freezes an OpenVLA-OFT 7B model and trains TinyLoRA adapters with as few as 8–384 parameters via GRPO or CMA-ES, achieving a 120 mm carry-height increase at 92% success on trained tasks and 74 mm at 80% success on a held-out task with only 256 parameters, and enabling post hoc rescaling of the style–performance trade-off; an investigation of closed-loop pass@k for generative robot policies shows large-k pass@k differentiates checkpoints with similar pass@1, reveals a coverage–reliability tradeoff, and motivates an offline checkpoint-selection rule and a generative data-augmentation scheme that preserves or improves pass@1 and pass@k; D&A (Describe & Assess) uses text descriptions plus rubric-based assessment to calibrate VLM progress estimates on robot videos, matching or exceeding trained reward models while generalizing across tasks and supporting RL without extra VLM training; TF²-Steering (also noted above) uses tactile residuals to refine vision-based actions and improves average success; and a study of inertia mismatch finds behavior cloning degrades when heavy objects are manipulated by higher-reflected-inertia robots and shows simulated mitigations via collecting demonstrations in lower-inertia workspaces or steering demonstrators toward low-inertia strategies. (arXiv:2611.00522; arXiv:2611.01565; arXiv:2611.03622; arXiv:2611.01483; arXiv:2611.03501)

Knowing Where to Look: Interactive Object Search via Spatial-Semantic FusionMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes an interactive object search system that fuses semantic plausibility from a vision-language model with geometric occupancy evidence into an explicit 3D voxel belief field using Bayesian log-odds filtering, and uses high-belief regions to drive view changes, exploration, and physical interactions (e.g., opening cabinets). Deployed on a bimanual mobile humanoid, the combined spatial-semantic belief system outperforms geometry-only search methods and LLM-based planning methods for object search, according to the reported experiments.Link
Learning Agile Humanoid Dodgeball with Onboard Monocular VisionETH ZurichThe authors develop an integrated dodgeball system for humanoid robots that combines onboard monocular perception, temporal ball-state and threat-flag estimation, and learned whole-body dodge/recovery and search/track policies trained via a three-stage curriculum without image rendering (using analytic projection and perception randomization). In simulation, the learned policies achieve dodge success rates of 97.8–100.0% for balls launched from 5.0 m at 6 m/s and 33.2–40.8% for 2.5 m launches at 10 m/s over 1000 throws per condition; on a Unitree G1 robot, controlled single-throw dodge success exceeds 85% over more than 15 trials, including sequences with full-azimuth target reacquisition and subsequent dodging.Link
HuG: Learning Humanoid Whole-Body Affordance-Aware Grasping with Active PerceptionUC San DiegoThe paper presents HuG, a whole-body affordance-aware grasping framework that couples belief-driven active perception (treating camera viewpoint as a decision variable), a point-cloud-conditioned affordance policy, and an affordance-aware alignment stage to maintain object visibility, reachability, and base stability during grasping with a floating-base humanoid. HuG curates training data using controller-in-the-loop dynamics simulation to filter out 73% of kinematically valid but dynamically destabilizing grasps, leading to a policy that learns kinodynamically feasible affordances and achieves 87.5% real-world success on a multi-level shelf task, with at least 73% success at every tested height and simulation evidence of spatial extrapolation to unseen table heights.Link
GoalDex: Learning Image-Goal-Conditioned Policies for Dexterous In-Hand ManipulationETH ZurichThe authors propose an end-to-end visuomotor framework (GoalDex) for dexterous in-hand reorientation that operates on RGB images and proprioception without explicit pose estimation, learning visual representations and control policies via behavior distillation from privileged-state teacher policies and auxiliary reconstruction losses, and enabling image-goal-conditioned reorientation where goals are specified by images rather than canonical poses. In simulation across diverse object geometries, the proposed image-goal-conditioned visuomotor policies consistently outperform pose-based methods and policies using pretrained visual encoders, and a single such policy transfers directly from simulation to a real robot to reorient five real-world objects accurately without explicit pose tracking.Link
Energy-Based Policy OrchestrationCarnegie Mellon University; UC BerkeleyThe paper formulates policy orchestration as selecting both a low-level policy and its goal and introduces EPO, an energy-based orchestrator that learns, for each goal-conditioned policy, a composite energy over candidate goals such that lower energy indicates higher feasibility and better tracking of high-level planner targets; at runtime, EPO refines each policy’s goal and executes the lowest-energy policy-goal pair. Simulation results show that EPO improves the empirical precision–robustness trade-off by refining policy-specific goals before selectively switching among policies based on learned energy scores, and the same set of frozen policies and energy models is demonstrated to transfer from simulation to real humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.Link
SNAP: Semantic Navigation Among PedestriansETH ZurichThe authors propose SNAP, a reinforcement learning navigation policy that jointly reasons about semantic terrain and dynamic pedestrians using a single semantically annotated bird’s-eye-view map, avoiding explicit human-state estimation while aiming for socially compliant navigation (e.g., staying on sidewalks). In experiments, the SNAP policy outperforms established crowd-navigation and semantic-aware baselines and transfers zero-shot to a legged robot, where it demonstrates socially compliant navigation in highly dynamic real-world crowded environments.Link
Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human DemonstrationsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces Pixel-UMI, a pipeline for learning environment-aware whole-body mobile manipulation policies from sensorized human demonstrations, featuring Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR) that uses reconstructed scene geometry plus demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to generate collision-aware joint and base targets, and delay-calibrated action assignment to convert these trajectories into robot actions. To handle residual pose and viewpoint mismatches, Pixel-UMI introduces Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM) that down-weights mismatched visual regions during training and uses DAgger-style on-policy human corrections for contact-rich behaviors; evaluated across five challenging tasks, the learned policies achieve an average of 87.0% task progress.Link
Pixel: A Co-Designed Full-Stack Robot System for Learning General-Purpose Mobile ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors present Pixel, a co-designed full-stack system for general-purpose mobile manipulation that integrates a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics and compliant grippers, multiple teleoperation and robot-free human data interfaces, a unified whole-body controller, and a shared policy-learning pipeline across human and robot demonstrations within a hierarchical, language-conditioned visuomotor control stack. Using this integrated hardware–software–data–learning design, the Pixel system is experimentally shown to execute mobile-manipulation behaviors requiring dexterity, active perception, navigation, and whole-body coordination, with evaluations reported across hardware capabilities, data collection, and policy learning components.Link
SPARK: Self-Correcting Embodied Memory via Physical Re-ObservationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes SPARK, a corrective multi-episode framework for embodied retrieval-augmented generation where a semantic critic flags unreliable regions of an environment knowledge base, a targeted planner schedules revisits, and a selective merge procedure adds, removes, or replaces stored information based on new physical observations to correct accumulated errors in labels, positions, and spatial relationships. On four outdoor AirSim maps under two degraded-viewpoint conditions and a fixed set of 158 embodied QA queries, a single corrective episode with SPARK improves EQA performance on every map–condition pair while storing 13–41% fewer objects than append-only updating, with up to 2.7× EQA gains in one case; extending correction to four episodes yields EQA performance exceeding that of the corresponding undegraded reference, and ablations show that critic signals, targeted revisits, and selective merging each contribute to the improvements.Link
OpenNWM: A Generalist Navigation World Model with Latent Action PretrainingUC BerkeleyThe authors introduce OpenNWM, a generalist navigation world model that learns universal latent action representations from large-scale action-free navigation videos via visual prediction pretraining and then aligns these latent actions with physical motions through action-conditioned post-training, enabling action-conditioned future observation prediction without requiring action labels during pretraining. To support latent-action pretraining, the paper curates NavAnywhere, a dataset of 17.5 million observations from navigation videos spanning robots, humans, and drones across five broad scene categories; experiments show that OpenNWM improves visual prediction on in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks and yields better navigation performance, indicating that latent-action pretraining on action-free videos enhances dynamics learning and generalization for open-world navigation.Link
DexArena: A Unified Benchmark for Long-Horizon Dexterous ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes DexArena, a unified benchmark for long-horizon bimanual dexterous manipulation comprising 10 long-horizon tasks and 30 stage-level tasks with physically grounded success conditions and standardized simulation, scene randomization, and task-level evaluation. Using baseline policy-learning experiments in simulation, the authors show that DexArena tasks expose substantial challenges in precise manipulation, generalization to scene variations, and composition of multiple skills over long horizons.Link
PanoTac: A Dense Three-Axis Tactile Sensor for Robust Touch-Centric ManipulationNew York UniversityThe paper introduces PanoTac, a low-cost (<$50 per fingertip), compliant tactile fingertip that provides dense 12×32×3 three-axis (normal and two shear) tactile sensing at 60 Hz, and characterizes its repeatability and consistency across sensors and contact locations. Integrated into a visuo-tactile manipulation pipeline and evaluated on test-tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, PanoTac-based policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 46.7 percentage points in task performance in hardware experiments. Ablation studies indicate that removing either spatial contact information or shear feedback from PanoTac reduces overall performance across the evaluated manipulation tasks, suggesting both sensing components are important for robust contact-rich control.Link
MoSoLoco: Monocular 3D Human Localization through Visual Latent Features and Social ContextEPFLThe paper proposes MoSoLoco, a per-frame monocular 3D human localizer that combines a pose detector’s internal visual latent features with detected 2D skeletons, optionally augmented with sparse same-frame multi-person context, without requiring an additional RGB or depth encoder. On JRDB and NuScenes datasets, reusing pose-detector representations with MoSoLoco reduces localization error, including an approximately 23% error reduction on JRDB compared to the strongest evaluated keypoint-only baseline. Controlled trajectory-forecasting experiments show that MoSoLoco’s improved 3D localization provides downstream benefits while remaining computationally efficient enough for deployment on Jetson AGX Xavier.Link
3D Neural Rendering for Automated Ultrasonic Laser ScansCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents a 3D neural rendering method, complex-nerfacto, that uses pose-labeled laser Doppler vibrometer scans acquired via a 6-DOF robot arm to learn a complex-valued NeRF representation of steady-state surface wavefields on 3D parts, outputting real and imaginary velocity components instead of RGB. In simulation-based evaluations on welded aluminum cylinder and welded T-bracket components, complex-nerfacto achieves a mean Z-normalized cross-correlation of 0.60 at held-out viewing angles, substantially outperforming an RGB nerfacto baseline trained on rendered images of the same data (ZNCC 0.28). The reconstructed complex surface wavefields support defect detection and characterization on complex geometries, with geometry reconstruction from sparse LDV scans identified as a key remaining obstacle for practical deployment.Link
Democratizing Robot-Use Agents: A Unified Harness with Open ModelsUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces uni-robot-agent, a unified harness for robot-use agents that standardizes interfaces for open-weight models and supports further training without relying on proprietary frontier models or privileged inputs. Within this harness, an off-the-shelf Qwen3.8-Flash-Next model achieves a RoboDojo score of 31.39, surpassing the reported GPT-6 Astra-RoboProbe score of 28.97 and outperforming many VLA/WAM baselines, while also performing competitively on 19 real-world robot tasks. Analysis across model generations, families, and sizes suggests that effective robot use correlates with four general underlying capabilities, motivating joint development of these capabilities in future RUA training.Link
RoboColosseum: Evaluating Physical Intelligence at Scale In-The-WildStanford University; University of WashingtonThe paper presents RoboColosseum, a distributed evaluation framework that unifies policy serving and standardizes protocols across a large network of locally operated robots in diverse real-world environments, enabling scalable, reproducible assessment of general-purpose robot policies. In an initial study over 5,699 pairwise Open Track comparisons at 124 sites, frontier AI agents perform comparably to specialized VLA policies but all systems struggle on open-world tasks; after fine-tuning with 100 demonstrations per task on 20 tasks, GR00T N1.7 leads on SO-101 while pi0.5 leads on Franka and Bimanual-YAM, and Open Track rankings are found to be poor predictors of fine-tuned performance with substantial rank reversals. Analysis of partial task progress reveals differences obscured by success/failure metrics (e.g., on the G1 Humanoid, no policy completes any of five tasks but average progress ranges from 13.3% to 23.2%), and active matchmaking is shown to stabilize rankings with fewer trials than uniform sampling.Link
KlaskTron: An Open-Source Platform for Physical Adversarial Multi-Agent RLETH ZurichThe paper introduces KlaskTron, an open-source, low-cost (<$2500), desktop-scale robotic testbed for physical adversarial multi-agent reinforcement learning based on the KLASK tabletop game, featuring dual CoreXY gantries with high-torque brushless motors for fast, precise motion. A complete ecosystem is provided, including hardware designs and documentation, a GPU-native digital twin in NVIDIA Isaac Lab, and a sim-to-real baseline with a learned neural actuator model that enables zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to the physical platform. Experiments on the platform demonstrate that transferred agents can exhibit emergent adversarial behaviors in physical play, supporting KlaskTron as a reproducible benchmark for multi-agent RL on real robots.Link
Beyond Validation Loss: Coverage and Reliability Dynamics in Robot Policy PretrainingCarnegie Mellon University; Stanford UniversityThe paper studies closed-loop pass@k as a pretraining metric for generative robot policies, where pass@1 captures mean task success and large-k pass@k measures whether success remains accessible under repeated sampling from the same initial condition. Across various posttraining recipes, the authors find that large-k pass@k distinguishes policy checkpoints with similar pass@1 but different downstream posttraining performance, revealing a coverage–reliability tradeoff and showing that pass@k peaks at different times during supervised pretraining. Leveraging these insights, the paper proposes (1) an offline checkpoint-selection criterion to balance coverage and reliability without closed-loop evaluation, and (2) a generative data augmentation strategy that preserves large-k pass@k while maintaining or improving pass@1 and empirically improving the observed coverage–reliability Pareto frontier.Link
VLALagBench: Understanding When and Why VLA Inference Latency MattersStanford UniversityThe paper introduces VLALagBench, a benchmark suite to systematically study how vision-language-action policy inference latency affects task success across different task types, object motion speeds, execution modes (synchronous vs. asynchronous), and VLA policies. Using VLALagBench, the authors find that even a modest 100 ms inference latency can nearly halve success rates on moving-target tasks, with sensitivity strongly depending on task precision and continuity of motion, while asynchronous execution can partially mitigate these losses at the cost of more frequent inference calls. The paper further presents an empirical model that predicts task success as a function of latency using limited calibration data, achieving root-mean-square prediction errors of 5.7–7.8% across VLA policies, and shows how these predictions can guide latency-aware selection of VLA configurations under system throughput constraints.Link
When Robots Outweigh Objects: How Inertia Mismatch Shapes Robot Policy LearningMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper investigates how inertia mismatch between heavy robot arms and lighter manipulated objects affects robot policy learning, using controlled simulations that vary robot and object masses as well as reflected motor inertia. Results indicate that while reinforcement learning succeeds across a wide range of mass combinations, behavior cloning produces more reliable policies when a lighter robot manipulates a heavier object, and increasing data narrows but does not eliminate this performance gap; behavior cloning performance also degrades as reflected motor inertia increases even when total robot mass is fixed. The authors explore practical mitigations that do not require new hardware, showing in simulation that collecting demonstrations in lower-inertia workspaces or steering demonstrators toward lower-inertia task solutions yields demonstrations that behavior cloning imitates more reliably.Link
D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward ModelsUniversity of WashingtonThe paper proposes D&A (Describe & Assess), a purely prompt-based framework that decouples video task-progress estimation into first generating an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics and then assessing progress using explicit rubrics, anchoring evaluation to an absolute success definition rather than a video’s internal timeline. Empirical results on robotic tasks indicate that D&A calibrates zero-shot progress estimates from vision-language models, mitigating confirmation bias by providing fine-grained partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions and correctly interpolate successful runs, matching or surpassing post-trained reward models while generalizing across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks. The reward estimates produced by D&A are shown to be suitable for downstream reinforcement learning, enabling efficient training without additional post-training of the base VLM or collection of expensive real-world robotics datasets.Link
Neuro Inspired Robotics: What Robot Learning Can Borrow from the Sensorimotor BrainMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper surveys failures of current robot policies in everyday manipulation despite large-scale pretraining and argues that these limitations stem from design rather than data alone. It proposes a set of robot model designs inspired by the sensorimotor nervous system: compliant control that co-commands stiffness, a dedicated fast vision pathway for motor behavior, a cerebellum-like predictor for forecasting and recalibration, and a motor-cortex-like architecture that composes and consolidates action primitives into fluent skills. The survey frames each neuro-inspired design as a testable hypothesis for enabling robots that act on their own initiative and can recursively improve their skills over time.Link
Behavior Graphs: Unsupervised Graph Structures for Interpreting Robot Deployment DataStanford UniversityThe paper proposes behavior graphs, an unsupervised method that compresses high-dimensional robot policy rollouts into a single interpretable directed graph by clustering similar behavior modes across rollouts, connecting them temporally, and linking them to outcomes. In a user study over three real-world tasks, behavior graphs were preferred by 16 of 18 participants over raw rollout videos for choosing data collection strategies, while enabling comparable conclusions with 40.3% lower perceived temporal demand and 37.6% lower effort. Across 43 datasets, behavior graphs serve as capable progress predictors, improving rank correlation between predicted progress and true temporal order by 22.9% and reducing mean absolute error by 53.5% relative to the strongest baseline, thereby structuring deployment data for easier interpretation and decision-making.Link
Closing the Dynamics Gap through Self-Directed Robotic InteractionETH Zurich; Stanford UniversityThe paper introduces CUE, an agentic Real2Sim2Real system that actively interacts with the environment to close the dynamics gap by building its own perception, inferring physical parameters such as friction and restitution, and targeting interactions where its simulation is least accurate, given only a high-level instruction and no resets. In a billiards domain with 25 hidden-physics conditions in simulation, CUE reduces held-out ball-position error by 72% relative to the strongest baseline, and is further deployed on a real pool table, demonstrating its applicability to real-world contact-rich dynamics.Link
Behavioral Steering of Vision-Language-Action Models in the Low-Parameter RegimeCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper studies behavioral steering of pretrained vision-language-action models by freezing an OpenVLA-OFT 7B policy and adapting it with TinyLoRA adapters containing only 8–384 trainable parameters, optimized via policy-gradient RL (GRPO) or evolutionary search (CMA-ES) to modify execution style (e.g., carry height) while retaining capability. On a carrying-height preference task, the method achieves a 120 mm increase in carry height on trained tasks at 92% success and a 74 mm increase on a held-out task at 80% success using just 256 trainable parameters, and the learned update can be rescaled post hoc to adjust the trade-off between style change and performance retention without retraining. Experiments show that higher adapter capacity generally improves the trade-off between style gains and capability retention, with CMA-ES competitive at very low parameter counts and GRPO providing better trade-offs as capacity grows, indicating that extremely low-dimensional updates suffice for effective behavioral steering of VLAs.Link
Hi-CoLA: High Confidence Lower Bound Approximation Based Reinforcement Learning for Flex-Route Transit Operation ControlMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes Hi-CoLA, a High Confidence Lower Bound Approximation reinforcement learning framework that combines importance sampling, concentration inequalities, and a differentiable surrogate network to efficiently estimate and directly optimize a high-confidence performance lower bound via policy gradients for safe policy improvement. On a flex-route transit real-time operation control problem, Hi-CoLA outperforms state-of-the-art offline and safe RL methods in both average performance and tail-risk measures, while providing statistically certified performance guarantees before deployment. A case study using an actual flex-route in Chicago within the SUMO simulator demonstrates Hi-CoLA as a proof of concept for enhancing real-time FRT system control and indicates its broader applicability to other safety-critical decision-making domains.Link

Anchor 1: Precise and Stable Humanoid Control for Whole-Body ManipulationArchon RoboticsThe paper presents Anchor 1, a humanoid whole-body controller for precise and stable manipulation that uses calibrated sparse motion references to preserve task-relevant human motion structure without online joint-space retargeting, while curating paired robot motions offline and adding explicit error feedback decoupled from motion encoding to guide precise, physically plausible coordination. Emergent Control of Ant Learning Walks in a Mobile RobotUniversity of SussexThe paper implements a previously proposed model of desert ant learning walks—based on angular mismatch between path integration (PI) and a mushroom-body-inspired visual compass (VC)—on a four-wheeled outdoor robot equipped with a panoramic camera, IMU, and wheel encoders, to test its robustness under real-world sensory and motor noise. On a replicated desert-ant experimental setup, the PI–VC mismatch in the robot regulates speed, turning, pirouettes, and nest returns while PI provides a teaching signal for visual learning, enabling the robot to autonomously decide when to acquire visual memories and to gradually shift behavior from exploration to foraging. The robot reproduces key features of desert ant learning walks, including expanding and straightening trajectories, decreasing pirouette frequency, and improved visual homing, supporting the plausibility of the error-driven mechanism as an explanation for active visual learning in desert ants and illustrating the use of physical robots to test biological models.Link
MOST: Memory of Object States and Task Progress for Long-Horizon Mobile ManipulationBeijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence; Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications; East China Normal University; Northwestern Polytechnical UniversityThe paper proposes MOST (Memory of Object States and Task Progress), a policy-internal memory framework for long-horizon mobile manipulation that compresses multi-view observations into temporal tokens explicitly supervised with object states and subtask progress, so that memory focuses on world-state changes and completed subtasks rather than raw visual history, and conditions action generation within a single end-to-end policy without external planners or retrieval. A new benchmark, RoboCasa-Long, is introduced for multi-minute, multi-stage mobile manipulation tasks, complementing evaluations on RoboCasa365 and physical robot experiments. Across RoboCasa365, RoboCasa-Long, and real-world experiments, MOST outperforms the previous state-of-the-art Xiaomi-Robotics-1 (XR-1), increasing average success from 65.9% to 72.1% on RoboCasa365, boosting average maximum task progress from 20.83% to 42.22% on RoboCasa-Long, and improving real-world success from 6.66% to 43.33%, and also outperforms a matched visual-history memory variant, indicating that structuring memory around task-relevant progress and object states is more effective than retaining more unstructured experience.Link
Evaluating Tactile Foundation Models for End-to-End Imitation Learning in Contact-Rich ManipulationCNRS-AIST JRL (Joint Robotics Laboratory), IRL; National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST)The paper integrates several tactile foundation models (TFMs) as tactile encoders within Diffusion Policy and systematically evaluates them for end-to-end imitation learning in contact-rich manipulation across both simulation and real-world settings.
