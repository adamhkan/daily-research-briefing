## Executive Summary

- University-linked work spans mega-scale RL for control, microscopic-language-to-motion mapping, execution safety gates, cross-embodiment world models, and high-dimensional safe RL.
- Mega-scale parallel RL with adaptive sampling (SGS) enables previously unsolved quadruped locomotion and contact-rich manipulation, with sim-to-real transfer via RGB policies.
- Microscale task-conditioned trajectory integration from language substantially improves geometric accuracy and coverage for microscopic robots with minimal new labels.
- Safety and reliability at the control interface are addressed through execution gates (PIER) and feasibility-aware safety filtering (FAITH) that separate task performance from safety constraints.
- Cross-embodiment latent action spaces (LAC-WM) and world models unify control priors across heterogeneous robots, improving adaptation to unseen embodiments without action-space redesign.

| A Balanced Data Diet: Addressing the Exploration Bottleneck in Mega-Scale RL for Robot Control | University of Washington | Introduces Success Guided Sampling (SGS), an adaptive reset-sampling strategy that concentrates mega-scale parallel RL on task configurations at the frontier of current capability. | From Language to Motion: Task-Conditioned Focal-Stack Trajectory Integration for Microscopic Robots | Carnegie Mellon University | Maps natural-language instructions for microscopic robots into geometric operators and integrates multiple focal-plane trajectories via confidence-weighted dynamic programming. The method cuts trajectory RMSE by over 50% versus image-first fusion and improves physical coverage in dispensing experiments while requiring no new part-specific labels beyond an existing open-vocabulary perception stack. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12241 |
| PIER: An Evidence-Gated Execution Interface for Robotic Manipulation | University of Washington | Defines PIER, a deterministic execution-authorization gate that separates evidence checks and provenance tracking from low-level hardware control in manipulation tasks. Through synthetic traces it characterizes how re-observation budgets and thresholding affect rates of valid denials and unsafe proceeds, exposing contracts and limitations of evidence-based gating without claiming superiority over calibrated rule logic. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12123 |
| FAITH: Feasibility-Aware Safety-Filtered RL for High-Dimensional Systems | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | Proposes FAITH, a model-free safety filter that learns a state–action safety value function and amortizes minimal-intervention filtering via a neural network, removing safety terms from the task-policy gradient. It achieves high safety rates with near-unfiltered return on a 29-DoF humanoid and handles infeasible states by steering toward actions with minimum predicted peak harm, with policies validated on a real Unitree G1. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12432 |
| Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World Models with Latent Actions | Stanford University | Introduces LAC-WM, a robot world model that operates in a unified latent action space shared across heterogeneous embodiments to support cross-robot generalization. Compared to explicit action conditioning, LAC-WM improves downstream performance by up to 46.7% on dexterous manipulation and scales positively with the number of pretraining embodiments instead of degrading. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10846 |
| Scalable LEO Conjunction Screening using Adaptive Synthetic-Covariance Thresholds | Stanford University | Presents an uncertainty-aware conjunction-screening sieve for large LEO catalogs, using object-specific, time-varying altitude thresholds derived from propagated radial covariance plus a binary-search altitude sweep. On 33k tracked objects it reduces candidate pairs by ~29% over a fixed-threshold filter and >70% over brute-force all-pairs, while retaining all close approaches in reference sets. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11645 |
