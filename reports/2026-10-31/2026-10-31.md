## Executive Summary

- Several works advance whole-body and navigation capabilities for legged and wheeled platforms: a hierarchical off-road framework uses a low-level RL value function as an implicit cost map within an MPPI planner to achieve safer, more effective goal-directed driving in simulation without explicit cost maps; FORGE learns a reduced-order reference generator plus a whole-body tracking policy to realize agile humanoid locomotion on a Unitree G1 over −2.0–2.8 m/s longitudinal speeds, lateral motion, turning, hopping, and smooth command transitions; Metatron couples a promptable generative base policy with programmable skill prompts so a fixed checkpoint on G1 can execute walking, recovery, teleoperated pickup, sitting, and simulated climbing/acrobatics purely via code-level skills; TACOS defines terrain-stability scores from legged state-estimation residuals to self-supervise visual foothold prediction, improving instability severity ranking over baselines by 28% and terrain-stability rank correlation by 58% in outdoor tests; and a Multi-Layer Navigation System for smart wheelchairs combines virtual-sensor potential fields, MPC, open-vocabulary perception, and priority-based target selection to robustly execute users’ high-level intents—even with faulty inputs—in real-world trials with 10 participants. ([arXiv:2610.38731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38731); [arXiv:2610.37974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37974); [arXiv:2610.38027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38027); [arXiv:2610.37806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37806); [arXiv:2610.38569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38569))
- New hardware, datasets, and interfaces target richer, safer manipulation: Catch It All! trains sim-to-real humanoid catching policies from a single egocentric camera, using grasp-focused rewards and safety curricula to operate near torque limits and zero-shot catch 9 unseen objects of diverse geometry and deformability on a Unitree G1; DynamicVLA models trained on 1,616 underwater episodes achieve up to 93.3% success (including partials) across three underwater manipulation tasks, while a single-task ACT policy attains 47.9% pooled success (up to 66.7% on one task) in real marine sites, outperforming a SmolVLA baseline; EgoContact provides 22.5K labeled egocentric interaction events to benchmark future contact prediction and, when used as a memory bank, boosts grasp success from 14.2% to 31.2% in simulation and 16% to 58% on hardware; UMI-Live offers an open-source, self-contained bimanual teleoperation and data-capture system whose datasets enable fine-tuned π₀.₅ and MolmoAct2 policies to achieve 10/10 and 8/10 success on bimanual pick-and-place; and SwapBot introduces modular, tactilely instrumented tool adapters and fast tool-swapping that reduce teleoperation time and workload and yield imitation policies where tactile-augmented demonstrations improve contact-rich tool skills by 15–25 percentage points over RGB-only training. ([arXiv:2610.39044](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39044); [arXiv:2610.38199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38199); [arXiv:2610.39167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39167); [arXiv:2610.39385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39385); [arXiv:2610.40646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40646))
- Multiple works focus on safety, force estimation, and precision in constrained manipulation: an optimization-based active-constraint framework for handheld micromanipulators explicitly models limited workspaces and relative actuation, reducing safety violations by over two orders of magnitude and recovering a threefold accuracy loss seen when enforcing safety alone; FEEL combines a virtual-spring model with an MDN residual to estimate per-finger forces from proprioception with 0–20 N contacts, cutting RMSE by 92.8% versus the spring baseline and by 9.4% versus an end-to-end MDN, and improving damage-free reliability from 7/10 to 10/10 in box pick-and-place; and a straight-skeleton-based auto-labeling pipeline plus SkelGG-CNN produce scalable, geometry-aware grasp labels and predictions, reaching >95% grasp accuracy on Jacquard/Cornell, AP 18.33 on GraspNet novel split, and >95% real-robot success on single and cluttered household-object grasps, outperforming state of the art without domain adaptation. ([arXiv:2610.37981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37981); [arXiv:2610.38351](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38351); [arXiv:2610.38443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38443))
- Several papers propose new learning and transfer mechanisms for control policies: a meta-learned graph neural network leverages semantic, geometric, and relational object histories to rapidly adapt and predict when and how objects move, transferring from simulation to real robots without fine-tuning, outperforming a vision-language model and a class-frequency baseline and enabling near-fastest collision-free navigation in closed-loop simulation; Self-Labeled Preference Transfer jointly trains a shared backbone for preference-conditioned prediction and recognition, then self-labels target demonstrations to reach 96.4% simulation preference-recognition accuracy, 84.8% preference following, and large real-world gains (e.g., 52.5%→92.5% preference following) without extra annotations; a cross-controller transfer framework encodes controller–motion interactions via a transformer context, improving average success by 33 points over naive data pooling and achieving ~50% success on unseen controllers/gains while adapting high-level strategies on contact-rich tasks; Data Inoculation perturbs human teleoperation with an online-trained residual to gather error-and-recovery rich data, yielding policies that match or improve success over standard behavioral-cloning and offline-RL data collection on three real tasks; and ELEVATE lets coding agents reformulate RL problems to learn missing whole-body controllers for a quadrupedal manipulator, achieving 98.4–100% simulated success on tasks beyond the initial controller’s workspace and transferring to hardware with 18/20 and 16/20 successes on rack retrieval and floor-to-rack placement without real-robot training. - New environments, benchmarks, and representational tools support complex perception, navigation, and orbital reasoning: OrbitalGym is a JAX-based multi-agent orbital-game simulator where experiments show propellant ratios dominate outcomes in pursuit–evasion, whereas guard-policy choice strongly affects defended-asset survival in a three-player lady-bandit-guard game; Gym-Anything for Robotics automates economically grounded robotics-task creation and auditing to build RoboWorld (25 simulations; 25 test and 175 training RL environments) where frontier models achieve only 0.06 average reward, and demonstrates that in-context repetition, privileged teachers, self-assisted learning, and code-generated demonstrations all substantially improve performance; MATCH introduces a hierarchical RL agent for VLN-CE that jointly learns a language-conditioned planner and goal-conditioned executor with execution-aware graphs and macro rewards, achieving state-of-the-art results among 0.5B-parameter methods on R2R-CE and RxR-CE and showing preliminary real-robot executability; and a zero-shot VLN system with structured task, temporal, and spatial memories—implemented with 8B observer and reasoner models—achieves the best R2R-CE val-unseen performance among Qwen3-based zero-shot baselines, with ablations revealing large path-length and success degradations when temporal or spatial memories are removed and real-robot tests showing higher success than a post-trained baseline. - Several contributions advance perception and SLAM for robotics: GOFish extends 3D Gaussian Splatting to fisheye cameras beyond 180° FoV with calibrated densification, mip filtering, and wide-angle appearance modeling, improving PSNR by 2.28 dB and 1.97 dB on 200° FIORD and 205° FullCircle, respectively, and yielding top mesh F-scores on FIORD and ScanNet++ plus a real-time LiDAR-free digital twin with collision geometry; SymAnt is a neuro-symbolic long-horizon action-anticipation framework that jointly searches scene and knowledge graphs from short video to create symbolic context, then iteratively refines action sequences, achieving strong performance on five diverse datasets and enabling user-editable knowledge without retraining; SKiD-SLAM is a distributed multi-robot LiDAR SLAM system tuned to planetary mission resource budgets, validated in simulation, public planetary-like datasets, and field deployments in lava-tube caves and crater/rock terrains under a 250 kbps inter-robot bandwidth; a detailed empirical study of action interfaces in contact-rich Factory tasks shows that seemingly minor changes—like physical action scaling or EMA filtering within the same position-feedback law—can swing sample-median success by up to 1.000, and that even gain-matched velocity vs. position interfaces yield residual performance gaps, motivating full reporting of executed action pathways; and a policy paper contends that securing the robotics supply chain requires dedicated, long-term federal investment distinct from general AI funding, highlighting gaps in component verification, underfunded hardware research, large-scale test infrastructure, and workforce development.
