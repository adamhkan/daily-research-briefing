Executive Summary

  • Several works advance autonomous navigation: Frontier Voltage Boost Laplacian path planning uses piecewise harmonic potentials to retain smooth, local-minima-free paths while scaling to larger maps and traversing narrow passages faster than prior Harmonic Potential Field planners; AELF introduces a lightweight 2.5D map for UAV exploration that avoids full 3D voxels, reduces unnecessary altitude changes, and in simulation and real flights efficiently explores >2000 m² of cluttered forest and sloped terrain (up to 10 m elevation change) at speeds up to 5 m/s, outperforming existing methods. (arXiv:2611.27308; arXiv:2611.27263)
  • Mission-level robustness is addressed by staleness deadlines for language-model-based planning, which use calibrated pedestrian prediction regions and robot deviation tubes to allocate onboard reasoning time; in 81,000 simulated missions, full LM reasoning cut wrong decisions by ~20× and executing plans until staleness deadlines reduced late arrivals by up to 64%, with hardware tests on a Jetson AGX Orin showing viable tradeoffs between wrong-decision and lateness targets; a portable stereo visual-inertial recovery module for quadrotors, attachable without controller retraining, achieved 88.5% [81.2, 94.8] worst-condition mission completion on a 48-scene, 1,920-flight benchmark—+29.2 percentage points [17.7, 40.6] over a DATT-L1 controller—and raised SimpleFlight worst-condition completion to 86.5%, with analyses indicating survival is dominated by visual-inertial re-acquisition asymmetry at vision return; NavRSI, a harness-centric recursive self-improvement system around a frozen LLM, reached 85.64% navigation success on R2R-CE val-unseen with GPT-6-Astra (+4.13 points over prior best) and generalized its harness self-improvement across multiple VLN benchmarks, LLM vendors, and to a real humanoid robot using only RGB input and primitive actions. (arXiv:2611.26858; arXiv:2611.27121; arXiv:2611.27499)
  • For robust and expressive robot control, PACE (Policy Augmentation and Compensation with Explicit disturbance estimation) treats payload shifts, CoM changes, actuator degradation, and external forces as joint-space disturbances estimated online without joint acceleration sensing, feeding both torque compensation and disturbance-aware policy inputs plus an analytical contact estimator; in simulation over 11 dynamics conditions it reduced quadruped velocity-tracking error by 18.1–57.0% versus state-of-the-art baselines and maintained gains under out-of-distribution commands, while real-robot tests showed a policy trained only on flat terrain with ≤3 kg extra mass could carry an 11.5 kg payload, manage unseen 16 cm step-downs, and traverse five outdoor terrains; S-BFM, a behavioral foundation model for humanoids, generates whole-body motion from sparse spatiotemporal goals using Spatial Grounding, Time-Conditioned Distillation, and Generative Fine-Tuning, reducing global joint-position error by >32% versus the best baseline, raising fixed-goal reach from 43.5% to 74.6%, cutting terminal position error from 1.67 m to 0.26 m, and running on the full-size AGIBOT A3 to produce natural, well-timed motions from sparse goals. (arXiv:2611.27061; arXiv:2611.27552)
  • Learning-based policy design for manipulation and surgery is advanced by FlowScope, which pretrains the trunk of an Action Chunking Transformer on unlabeled stereo surgical video via metric 3D scene-flow forecasting and then discards the flow head, yielding on-robot gains in needle pickup success from 54%→90% and first-handover needle regrasp from 4%→52% (full task 2%→14%) over training from scratch in 50-trial evaluations, with ablations indicating metric 3D scene flow outperforms kinematics, 2D tracks, depth alone, or scale-free 3D flow as a pretraining signal and encodes needle depth more accurately; GenPA directly fine-tunes an image-generation backbone into a control policy using a 19M-parameter action-regression adapter jointly trained with the backbone’s generative objective, requiring only a single forward pass at inference and achieving 99.10% success on LIBERO and 83.79% on LIBERO-Plus in simulation without extra large-scale robot data, running at 20.9 ms per action chunk on a single H100 (1.7–3.4× faster than strong VLA/WAM baselines) and showing depth supervision and visual-generation-based perception are particularly effective within the unified architecture. (arXiv:2611.26865; arXiv:2611.27446)
  • Language and interaction modeling are targeted by a System-One-based NL-to-LTL pipeline that replaces free-form text generation with structured question answering, using an NL classifier to emit LTL formulas and on three benchmarks matching or exceeding the accuracy of the best locally runnable LLMs while cutting runtime and energy by up to an order of magnitude and generalizing to unseen atomic propositions and temporal patterns; Embodied Interaction Policy (EIP) unifies audio and motion via layer-wise joint attention with chunk-wise causal masking and vocal tokens encoding speaking state and emotion, trained on a new EI-Data corpus to generate state-adaptive, emotion-conditioned robot motions that outperform gesture baselines in motion quality, support continuous motion during proactive replies and interruption-aware turn-taking, and in user and physical deployment studies improve perceived naturalness and interaction comfort while reducing warm-start latency by 64% versus a cascaded system. (arXiv:2611.27489; arXiv:2611.27327)
  • Safety, testing, and coverage for complex robots are addressed by a manifold-centered testing framework for RCM-constrained surgical robots that builds a feasible task manifold decoupled from low-level commands, samples tests directly in this manifold, and maps them to joint trajectories assessed by safety oracles and a spatial reference grid; across random, systematic, and genetic search, manifold-based test generation improves efficiency and enables systematic reference-cell coverage compared with Cartesian and PCA-based spaces, with random/genetic manifold sampling revealing diverse failure patterns beyond what cell coverage alone captures, and differential testing plus a cross-platform Kinova Gen3 study showing that manifold-based evolutionary search can detect aggregate shifts in failure rates from software repairs and that the workflow transfers to kinematically redundant manipulators. (arXiv:2611.27144)

Frontier Voltage Boost Laplacian Path PlanningUniversity of WashingtonThe paper proposes Frontier Voltage Boost Laplacian path planning, a piecewise harmonic-potential-based method that preserves the absence of local minima and smooth paths of prior Harmonic Potential Field approaches while improving scalability and handling of narrow passages. Experimental evaluations on maps of varying sizes indicate that the proposed method solves larger maps and navigates narrow passages more quickly than earlier Harmonic Potential Field planners, while maintaining path smoothness.Link
Efficient Natural Language to Temporal Logic Translation for Real-Time Physical AIETH ZurichThe authors propose an NL-to-LTL translation pipeline that replaces free-form text generation with answering a fixed set of structured questions, implemented via a System-One natural-language-based classifier to produce LTL formulas from natural-language instructions. On three benchmarks, the proposed translator matches or exceeds the accuracy of the best locally runnable LLMs for NL-to-LTL translation while achieving up to an order-of-magnitude reduction in runtime and energy consumption, and it generalizes to unseen atomic propositions and temporal patterns.Link

Staleness Deadlines for Motion Plans during Onboard ReasoningThe University of Texas at AustinThe paper introduces staleness deadlines, a planning framework in which a robot uses calibrated pedestrian prediction regions and robot deviation tubes to set time budgets for onboard language-model reasoning, rebuilding and adopting new routes only after standard clearance checks. Across 81,000 simulated missions using recorded Qwen3-4B outputs, full language-model reasoning reduces wrong decisions by about twentyfold, and executing the current plan until the staleness deadline reduces late arrivals by up to 64%, with no collisions while the robot is moving though pedestrians sometimes walk into the stopped robot. On a Jetson AGX Orin under perception load, fixed and per-query deadline policies reveal a tradeoff where short reasoning budgets violate wrong-decision targets and full reasoning violates lateness targets, with intermediate budgets satisfying task-specific loss targets and live runs matching offline replay losses in 24/32 missions with any recorded loss.Link
FlowScope: 3D Scene Flow Pretraining Improves Surgical Robot PoliciesIntuitive Surgical Inc.; Vanderbilt UniversityFlowScope is a pretraining framework that trains the trunk of an Action Chunking Transformer on unlabeled stereo clinical video by forecasting metric 3D scene flow derived from depth and optical flow, then discards the flow head so the final policy remains a standard ACT at inference time. On a real surgical robot, FlowScope pretraining improves needle pickup success from 54% to 90% and first-handover success on needle regrasp from 4% to 52% (full task: 2% to 14%) compared with training from scratch over 50 trials per condition, and these gains persist when using a generative flow-matching action head. Ablation and representation analyses indicate that metric 3D scene flow is a stronger pretraining signal than kinematics, 2D tracks, depth alone, or scale-free 3D flow (with kinematics), and that scene-flow pretraining encodes metric needle depth more accurately while kinematics pretraining increases reliance on robot state.Link
PACE: Policy Augmentation and Compensation With Explicit Disturbance Estimation for Robust LocomotionBeihang University; Nanyang Technological UniversityThe authors propose PACE (Policy Augmentation and Compensation with Explicit disturbance estimation), which models payload changes, CoM shifts, actuator degradation, and external forces as a joint-space disturbance estimated online by a nonlinear observer without joint acceleration measurements, providing both feedforward torque compensation and disturbance-aware policy inputs; an analytical contact estimator from joint and inertial data further handles fast-varying contact effects without force sensors. In simulation across eleven different dynamics conditions, PACE reduces quadruped velocity-tracking error by 18.1% to 57.0% relative to state-of-the-art baselines and maintains improved tracking performance under out-of-distribution command profiles. Real-robot experiments show that a PACE-trained policy, learned only on flat terrain with at most 3 kg added mass, can carry an 11.5 kg payload (nearly equal to the robot’s mass), negotiate unseen 16 cm step-downs, and traverse five outdoor terrains.Link
Portable Active Recovery under Timed and Position-Dependent Visual OutagesHubei UniversityThe paper formulates visual-outage recovery for stereo visual-inertial quadrotors as a worst-condition active-observability problem and introduces a portable recovery module, attachable at the reference interface of existing controllers without retraining, that orchestrates visual-loss detection, jerk-limited braking, bounded backward probing, settled rejoining, height-integral trim, and an optional prior-map branch using only deployable telemetry. On a preregistered 48-scene benchmark totaling 1,920 flights, the full MPPI-based configuration of the recovery module achieves 88.5% [81.2, 94.8] worst-condition mission completion, outperforming the development-selected DATT-L1 controller by +29.2 percentage points [17.7, 40.6] and surpassing six external baselines under Bonferroni correction. When attached to the SimpleFlight stack without retraining, the module increases worst-condition completion by +17.7 percentage points [7.3, 28.1] to 86.5% (90% over 1.0x/1.25x speeds, 0/80 hard failures), and trace diagnostics show that survival primarily depends on visual-inertial re-acquisition asymmetry at vision return rather than on IMU drift suppression, corroborated by a bias-drift crossover observed on 72 held-out EuRoC windows.Link
Simulation-Based Testing of RCM-Constrained Robotic Software via Task ManifoldsHangzhou Institute for Advanced Study, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences; Institute of Software Chinese Academy of Sciences; Sun Yat-sen UniversityThe authors present a manifold-centered testing framework for RCM-constrained surgical robots that constructs a feasible task manifold decoupled from low-level commands, samples candidate test inputs directly within this manifold, and maps them to joint trajectories for simulation, with trajectory safety oracles and a spatial reference grid used to assess failure yield and coverage. Across random testing, systematic traversal, and genetic search, the task-manifold representation improves test-generation efficiency and enables systematic reference-cell coverage compared with Cartesian XYZ and PCA-based representations, while random and genetic strategies on the manifold detect a broader variety of failure patterns, indicating that full reference-cell coverage does not capture intra-cell behavioral diversity. Differential testing between defective and patched controller versions, as well as a cross-platform study on a 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 arm in MoveIt 2, show that manifold-based evolutionary search can detect aggregate changes in failure rates from software repairs and that the workflow transfers to kinematically redundant manipulators.Link
AELF: Active Terrain-Aware UAV Exploration Using a Lightweight FrameworkThe University of Hong KongThe paper introduces AELF, a lightweight 2.5D map-based framework for autonomous UAV exploration that avoids full 3D voxel grids, preserves key topographic features, and systematically eliminates blind voxels caused by occlusions and limited visibility. AELF performs 2D path planning on the 2.5D map and then lifts paths to 3D by incorporating terrain traversability into viewpoint cost evaluation, allowing altitude adaptation to unknown terrain without prior height information and reducing unnecessary altitude changes for smoother exploration. Experiments in simulation and real-world environments show that AELF efficiently explores more than 2000 m^2 of cluttered forest and sloped mountainous terrain with up to 10 m elevation changes, achieving UAV speeds up to 5 m/s and outperforming existing exploration methods.Link
Embodied Interaction Policy: A Unified Model for Full-Duplex Human-Robot InteractionAstribot; International Digital Economy Academy (IDEA); Sichuan UniversityThe authors propose Embodied Interaction Policy (EIP), a unified streaming model that couples an Audio-Interaction expert and a motion expert via layer-wise joint attention with a chunk-wise causal mask and vocal tokens encoding speaking state and emotional content, enabling motion generation directly conditioned on evolving interaction context. Trained on the constructed Embodied Interaction Dataset (EI-Data) using a two-stage procedure, EIP produces state-adaptive, emotion-conditioned robot behavior that achieves higher motion quality compared with representative gesture-generation baselines and supports continuous motion during proactive responses and interruption-aware turn-taking. In comparative evaluations against a cascaded interaction-plus-motion system, EIP attains higher interaction success and reduces warm-start latency by 64%, with physical deployments and user studies indicating improvements in perceived motion naturalness and interaction comfort.Link
GenPA: Image Generators as Efficient Robot PoliciesBeijing Zhongguancun Academy; Delft University of Technology; Fudan University; SunRising AI Ltd.; Tsinghua UniversityGenPA is a control framework that directly fine-tunes an image generation backbone into a robot policy by adding a lightweight 19M-parameter action-regression adapter and jointly training with the backbone’s native generative objective for perception, leveraging its image-editing interface to handle different visual targets without task-specific perception heads. At inference time, GenPA requires only a single pass through the visual generation backbone plus action regression (no visual generation or iterative denoising), and on simulation benchmarks it achieves 99.10% success on LIBERO and 83.79% on LIBERO-Plus without additional large-scale robot-data pretraining, while running in 20.9 ms per action chunk on a single H100 GPU and providing 1.7–3.4× speedups over strong VLA and WAM baselines under compiled inference. Ablation studies within the unified architecture show that supervision with current-scene depth is a particularly effective training signal, and comparisons of perception variants (current-scene, future prediction, and combinations) highlight architectural advantages of visual generation backbones for efficient and robust robot control.Link
NavRSI: Recursive Harness Self-Improvement for Vision-and-Language NavigationFudan University; Nanjing University of Science and Technology; Renmin University of China; Singapore Management UniversityThe paper proposes NavRSI, a harness-centric recursive self-improvement framework that iteratively rewrites and refines the interaction harness around a frozen LLM for vision-and-language navigation by collecting navigation trajectories, using simulator-based failure diagnostics accessible only to a separate designer agent, and retaining effective revisions. In simulation experiments on the R2R-CE val-unseen split, NavRSI achieves an 85.64% navigation success rate with GPT-6-Astra, surpassing the previously reported best result by 4.13 percentage points. Evaluations on RxR-CE, NavRAG-CE, and a real humanoid robot indicate that NavRSI’s harness self-improvement mechanism generalizes across multiple VLN benchmarks, LLMs from four vendors, and from simulation to hardware using only a single RGB camera and primitive actions without task-specific model training or additional navigation tools.Link
S-BFM: A Behavioral Foundation Model for Humanoid Control from Sparse Spatiotemporal GoalsAGIBOT; Nanyang Technological University; Shenzhen MSU-BIT University; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)The paper proposes S-BFM, a behavioral foundation model for humanoid control that generates whole-body motion from sparse spatiotemporal goals (target postures/poses and arrival times) using three components: Spatial Grounding to embed world-frame position and heading into a forward-backward behavior representation, Time-Conditioned Distillation to learn transitions and pace via closed-loop imitation of a dense-reference teacher, and Generative Fine-Tuning to extend the range of distances and arrival times. In simulation, S-BFM reduces global joint-position error by over 32% compared to the best baseline under both fixed and mixed horizons, increases fixed-goal reach rate from 43.5% to 74.6%, and lowers terminal position error from 1.67 m to 0.26 m when controlling humanoid motion from sparse goals. The authors demonstrate hardware deployment of S-BFM on the full-size AGIBOT A3 humanoid, showing that sparse spatiotemporal goals can suffice to produce natural, well-timed whole-body motion on a real robot.Link
