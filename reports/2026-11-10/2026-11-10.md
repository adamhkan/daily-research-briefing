## Executive Summary

- Several vision-language-action foundations and training recipes are introduced: Open-HumanWAM scales egocentric human video pretraining to a 50,000-hour Open-HumanWAM-50K corpus and reports log-linear scaling between pretraining hours and downstream robot success, with HumanWAM-Goal joint human–robot pretraining achieving state-of-the-art RoboTwin 2.0 post-trained success (92.70% Clean2Clean, 84.60% Clean2Random) and showing in-context adaptation to unseen visual goals on physical robots; MR0 presents a three-stage VLA foundation model trained on 147M vision-language samples and 15,060 hours of trajectories across 12 embodiments, reaching 93.6% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and strong Ethernet insertion performance (70% success, 0.90 progress) with Rollback-based CFG-DAgger; A2-MEAS introduces an embodied multi-agent framework where correction codes train and adapt A2-WLA, which attains a 0.6411 Q-score and 45.40% task success on BEHAVIOR 2026, 93.98–95.05% RoboTwin 2.0 success, and 57.92% success on 12 real-robot household tasks versus 26.67% for π₀.₅; and VICTR proposes an in-context value function estimator for progress-aware demonstration retrieval, improving a post-trained π₀.₅ policy on out-of-domain real-world manipulation from 6.2% to 23.8% success and from 28.9% to 55.5% average task progress relative to a fine-tuned baseline. ([arXiv:2611.06817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06817); [arXiv:2611.08577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08577); [arXiv:2611.09124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09124); [arXiv:2611.08698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08698))
- New methods target safer or more efficient policy adaptation from structured signals: CS-Pi combines projection and rejection for constraint-satisfying sampling in flow-matching policies, increasing safe success on a 7-DoF shelf pick-and-place from 55% (pure projection) to 81% and zero-shot Franka hardware safe success from 40% to 55% while maintaining policy plausibility; Q-OIL models empirically observed progress-based human interventions and adds optimistic bonuses on intervention transitions with decoupled critics, yielding 10–50% higher success than baselines in simulation and real-robot experiments and improving pretrained vision-language-action agents on two bimanual tasks; and a weight-space programming approach has GPT-6 Astra edit 65 token-embedding rows of a robot policy to repair lexical grounding without gradients or new demonstrations, raising π₀.₅ success on 765 paraphrased instructions from 86.3% to 92.0% in the best run (48 repairs, 4 regressions) and from 74.3% to 87.5% on instructions containing edited tokens, with controls suggesting targeted lexical repair rather than broad new capabilities. ([arXiv:2611.08654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08654); [arXiv:2611.08576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08576); [arXiv:2611.09376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09376))
- Several works contribute data, benchmarks, and augmentation pipelines for embodied intelligence: Open-HumanWAM releases an open, densely annotated 50,000-hour egocentric human video corpus with hand motion and language labels plus curation and pretraining pipelines; Augmentron provides a geometry-aware visual augmentation system that re-renders 3D scene representations along original camera trajectories, and on three physical manipulation tasks shows that replacing half the real data with Augmentron data improves success under in-distribution and out-of-distribution distractors and workspace changes, outperforming alternative augmentations in pooled success; ToolDex offers a large-scale egocentric–exocentric human tool-use dataset with multi-view sensing and detailed hand/tool/object/deformation annotations, defining benchmarks for tool-use reasoning, sparse-view 3D/4D reconstruction, and robot policy learning; Flex-RSI defines a recursive self-improvement benchmark where an improver edits the agent harness under a fixed budget, and analyses indicate that self-editing prompts/tools/memory/control loops substantially contribute to performance gains and that open-weight models struggle when only success/failure feedback or strict physical/exactness checks are present; and Seeing What Matters introduces a task-conditioned actionable spatial-belief benchmark where a diagnostic pipeline reaches ~99% success while seven vision-language models achieve only 26–43%, highlighting the importance of functional information and command binding and that view selection should be judged by task-relevant evidence coverage rather than scene completeness. ([arXiv:2611.06817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06817); [arXiv:2611.09145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09145); [arXiv:2611.09036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09036); [arXiv:2611.08946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08946); [arXiv:2611.09794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09794))
- Planning, navigation, and perception for challenging environments are advanced by several methods: TPS-A* plans with certified collision checking using a trajectory-parameter lattice for exact, possibly non-convex footprints, solving all 300 cluttered-world queries in a median 3 ms while an exact checker finds no collisions and hybrid A* incurs 45% obstacle overlap, and achieving up to 89× speedups on position-only goals over hybrid A*; Execution-Grounded Traversability learns a flow-matching model over terrain and vehicle-contact features to estimate the probability of successful path execution, reducing safe-path rejection to 2.2% and unsafe-path acceptance to 7.7% in 650 simulated trials and lowering navigation failure rates by 24–44 percentage points and goal time by up to 45% when embedded in a planner, with zero-shot outdoor transfers; FATHOM couples a multimodal recurrent state-space world model with diffusion-based trajectory selection for AUV tracking of sparse light markers, with offline studies and real-AUV experiments indicating accurate belief prediction and robust, real-time tracking under occlusions and visual degradation without motion capture; a reinforcement-learned camera controller for PIV uses only reconstructed velocity fields and uncertainty to position a manipulator-mounted camera, transferring zero-shot from photorealistic simulation to a water tunnel where it frames wakes more accurately and faster than fixed views while using far fewer image pairs; and a learned inertial odometry method for quadrotors augments IMU fusion with body-frame torque dynamics in an EKF, improving pose and velocity estimation over prior learned inertial odometry on unseen trajectories and four held-out public datasets, with ablations indicating rotor-derived torque inputs are most informative. ([arXiv:2611.09210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09210); [arXiv:2611.09270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09270); [arXiv:2611.08898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08898); [arXiv:2611.09679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09679); [arXiv:2611.09584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09584))
- Whole-body locomotion and manipulation control across diverse robot morphologies are addressed via hybrid model-based/learning frameworks: ForPC trains a locomotion prior and a residual force-aware policy to coordinate humanoid cart pushing using only onboard observations, reducing cart-velocity RMSE by up to 68.2% and cross-track RMSE by up to 74.2% versus a locomotion-only baseline in sim-to-sim tests and transferring to a Unitree G1 that pushes carts with up to 30 kg payload at 0.5 m/s without real-world tuning; DKWBC decouples humanoid motion tracking and balancing by pairing inverse-dynamics plus PD tracking with a learned residual torque policy, and experiments show a single balancing policy maintains accurate tracking under varying surfaces, payloads, and interactions on both simulation and hardware; SUGARv2 synthesizes interaction references from task templates to train privileged teachers and then distills to visual loco-manipulation policies with shared imitation rewards and domain randomization, reaching a mean 91.0% success across five humanoid loco-manipulation tasks in simulation and demonstrating sim-to-real transfer; and a loco-manipulation framework for the COBRA snake robot uses contact-implicit sidewinding trajectory optimization over full dynamics, validated in hardware for pushing objects to goal locations and further tested in simulation for complex robot–object–ground contact handling. ([arXiv:2611.08664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08664); [arXiv:2611.09605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09605); [arXiv:2611.09266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09266); [arXiv:2611.09777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09777))
- Broader studies examine agentic robotics and generalized embodied agents: a large-scale simulation study of coding agents iteratively writing robot policies over nine manipulation tasks (631 configurations, 28,000 rollouts) finds that self-learning is often limited less by feedback availability than by the agent’s ability to interpret feedback into targeted code updates, with successful loops depending on accurate failure attribution, strong spatial/geometric reasoning, and structured policy refactoring; a quadrotor design-optimization framework jointly tunes geometry and the torque–deflection response of passively folding arms via contact-based simulation of narrow-gap traversal, yielding a prototype that compresses from 31.3 cm to 14.1 cm width, passes a 15 cm opening at 2 m/s with up to 30° approach-angle error and through a 40 cm passage, while adding only 2 mm position RMSE and <1% extra power versus a rigid twin in free flight; and a computer-use agent that operates via screen, keyboard, and mouse is shown to solve both standard software tasks and screen-rendered embodied tasks without competition-specific tuning, achieving placements from first to sixth in four ECCV workshop competitions and motivating CUAs as potential embodied generalists whose learned skills (visual grounding, precise clicking, exploration) may transfer broadly. ([arXiv:2611.09030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09030); [arXiv:2611.09272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09272); [arXiv:2611.09847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09847))
