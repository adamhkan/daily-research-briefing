## Executive Summary

- Humanoid control sees rapid advances in whole-body dexterity, interactive skills from video, and reactive sports-like behaviors, with multiple groups deploying on Unitree G1 hardware.
- Generalist manipulation and VLA/WAM-style policies are being scaled and specialized through new representations (effects, optical flow, action tokens), world models, and efficient edge deployments.
- Navigation and motion planning progress includes anticipatory 3D reconstruction from a single image, mapless ESDF queries for fast collision avoidance, GPU-parallel constrained RRT, and cross-embodiment/mapless nav frameworks.
- New benchmarks and supervisory layers target long-horizon, memory-intensive, and failure-prone manipulation, including dexterous lab tasks, revisable embodied memory, motion-quality replay, and spatial grounding on real robots.
- Adaptation and continual learning methods (e.g., retrieval-anchored fine-tuning, noise-space steering with world-model rehearsal) focus on safely reusing priors while avoiding representation drift and catastrophic forgetting.
- Institutional teams (Stanford, UC Berkeley, UW, CMU, ETH, UCSD, NYU) contribute strongly across humanoids, world models, timing-robust policies, and memory/agent benchmarks.

| VICAR: Video Imitation with Contact-Aware Retargeting for Humanoid Interactive Skills | UC Berkeley | VICAR builds a pipeline that converts third-person human videos into contact-rich humanoid interactive skills by explicitly prioritizing world-frame contact poses, timing, and collision clearance in the retargeting optimization. | Reflex: Visually Grounded Reactive Humanoid Control | Stanford University; University of Washington | Reflex factors visually grounded whole-body catching into three learned stages: an RL-trained state-based catching policy, dynamics inference from partial observations, and an egocentric RGB-D encoder that recovers object dynamics. | AlphaNIST: Emergent Assembly Skills from Binary Task Rewards | University of Washington | AlphaNIST trains a single RL policy on all 20 NIST Assembly Task Board #1 operations using only sparse binary completion rewards, without demonstrations or shaped progress signals. The policy achieves over 98% training success and discovers strategies such as grasp selection for future assembly and opportunistic sequencing, revealing that large-scale RL can learn complex assembly planning and dexterous manipulation from task-agnostic rewards. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13354 |
| Chronos Policy: Learning Robot Policies on Physical Time | Stanford University | Chronos Policy conditions diffusion-transformer robot policies on explicit observation and desired action execution times, using time-aware rotary positional encodings to bind attention to physical time rather than sequence index. Temporal randomization during training yields policies that handle varying sensor rates, delays, and asynchronous multimodal inputs, improving robustness on dynamic and multisensory tasks without degrading quasi-static performance. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13929 |
| BT-Agent: Deploying Onboard Robot Agents with Behavior Trees and Multi-Fidelity Evaluation | Stanford University | BT-Agent couples an onboard LLM with a library of behavior-tree skills whose preconditions and failure codes provide structured execution feedback. Running fully on a 9B model, the system improves task success over ReAct-style baselines on a quadruped and a multi-fidelity benchmark shows that structured tool interfaces and feedback are key to robust, interruptible navigation-style task execution. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13862 |
| LeWAM: End-to-End World and Action Modeling with JEPAs | ETH Zurich; UC San Diego | LeWAM integrates a JEPA-style latent world model with action flow matching in a single end-to-end objective, constraining the latent space to an isotropic Gaussian to support both direct policy execution and efficient planning. Compared to prior JEPA planners it dramatically improves success on contact-rich and long-horizon control tasks while being over 30× faster in planning, and remains competitive on behavior cloning benchmarks and real robots. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12818 |
| REMM: Revisable Embodied Memory for Manipulation | Carnegie Mellon University | REMM adds a symbolic memory layer atop a frozen G0.5 VLA executor, separating append-only evidence from revisable task state and maintaining explicit dependency links. When new observations invalidate prior facts, REMM retracts derived conditions and triggers targeted recovery at skill boundaries, substantially improving progress and recall on long-horizon physical manipulation tasks without retraining the underlying policy. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12710 |

The model leverages human signals and scene context to outperform state-of-the-art baselines on minADE/FDE in complex, changing work-zone layouts. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12747 |
| What Should Embodied Agents Remember? Experiments show that storing visual frames outperforms purely textual logs at lower write-time cost, and augmenting frames with scene graphs yields the best overall performance across diverse indoor tasks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12561 |
