Executive Summary

  • Several works advance complex robot coordination and contact-rich manipulation: cuArms introduces a GPU-parallel, conflict-based planner with reusable multi-modal roadmaps and lazy conflict resolution plus high-level scheduling, reporting real-time initial solutions and anytime refinements that outperform prior multi-arm task allocation and coordination methods on simulated and real industrial tasks; an extended PPO framework with phase-conditioned critics, phase-gated rewards, a multi-phase reset curriculum, and hierarchical control learns a single loco-manipulation policy that executes multi-stage behaviors on both Unitree G1 and Boston Dynamics Spot hardware without human motion data; a contact-planning method for bimanual non-prehensile tilting uses signed friction distance to derive task-adaptive friction margins within sequential SOCPs, improving robustness to object variations compared with fixed or no margins in simulation and hardware; and a sanding-focused study of lightweight mobile manipulators shows that low base–ground resistance yields only 54–70% of a commanded 100 N contact force and up to 100 mm tool error, arguing that joint consideration of force, motion, and vibration is needed to characterize non-rigid bases under contact. (arXiv:2610.36250; arXiv:2610.36270; arXiv:2610.36961; arXiv:2610.36725)
  • Multiple humanoid control and motion-generation frameworks target scalable whole-body skills: a stochastic motion tokenizer conditions tracking policies on discrete motion tokens from behavior labels or text, outperforming FSQ and VQ-VAE in G1 motion reconstruction while enabling 16.8 s dance/fight/walk clips and a 35 s fight-to-dance sequence on hardware; GRAFT adds a lightweight, terrain-aware residual adapter on top of frozen humanoid behavior foundation models, using depth-based terrain cues and regularization to enable simulated stair/gap/platform locomotion and real-world uneven-terrain traversal on Unitree G1 without retraining the base tracker; REACH combines RL-based bipedal and crawling policies with a LiDAR planner so a G1 can track body heights from 1.27–0.50 m (bipedal) and 0.50–0.20 m (crawling) for confined-space navigation with robust mode transitions; and HumiTransfer converts in-the-wild human–human interaction videos into physically plausible human–humanoid interactions, producing the ~500k-frame HumiMimic dataset for Unitree R1 Edu and HighTorque Mini-hi and demonstrating improved motion fidelity and real-robot execution of partner-following behaviors over baselines. (arXiv:2610.36452; arXiv:2610.36507; arXiv:2610.36546; arXiv:2610.36615)
  • Several works focus on dexterous manipulation interfaces, retargeting, and hardware: CARD unifies video, motion capture, and tactile glove data via an object-free contact-anchored hand representation, preserving contact in 43–78% of frames (vs <10% and 22.5% for purely kinematic and object-aware baselines) and raising strict task success on GRAB from 12% to 32% when integrated into physics-based retargeting, with hardware tests showing its tactile tracker is needed to reproduce contact forces; a LEAP-hand training recipe with diverse resets, goal generation from grasp datasets, and an adaptive friction curriculum plus a tunable residual safety filter yields zero-shot sim-to-real in-hand pose reaching with 95.2% goal success within 2 cm/0.4 rad (91.5% with safety filtering) over 13.3 hours and 4,800 unseen goals while reducing object drops by 75%; EgoThumb offers a lightweight supernumerary robotic thumb with forearm-mounted actuation and an egocentric visual imitation policy that maps palm-camera images to joint positions for autonomous grasp/hold assistance that generalizes across users and environments; Torch-Hand introduces a 21-DoF anthropomorphic hand with palm-integrated screw-linkage actuation and decoupled DoFs, experimentally validated for workspace, thumb opposition, 33-grasp coverage, grasp force, payload, and task-level manipulation; and ActiveAlign’s Real-to-Sim-to-Real alignment—via ActiveAlign-Waving for hand–eye calibration and ActiveAlign-Touching for tactile object pose refinement—supports curating uncalibrated datasets and enables millimeter-scale tasks such as button pressing and slider manipulation on a Unitree G1. (arXiv:2610.36539; arXiv:2610.37652; arXiv:2610.37582; arXiv:2610.36789; arXiv:2610.36859)
  • Perception, odometry, and navigation for mobile robots are advanced through several frameworks: RESPLE2 models 6-DoF motion with non-uniform B-splines in an iterated EKF with inertial-informed control-point prediction, motion-adaptive knot placement, and continuous-time fusion of LiDAR, cameras, IMUs, and leg kinematics, achieving real-time, competitive-or-better accuracy and robustness on handheld, tracked, legged, and aerial-legged platforms, especially in aggressive maneuvers; Omni-IGN uses quad-fisheye RGB, an attention-based RL policy, and a collision-aware safety layer for fully onboard image-goal drone navigation, outperforming continuous-action baselines on Habitat ImageNav, improving collision avoidance with learned depth in IsaacSim, and transferring zero-shot to real drones without external localization; RAVEN unifies frontier and loop-closure planning for quadrotor active SLAM using a utility vector to trade off exploration vs localization and a perception-aware planner that minimizes visual estimation error, outperforming existing active SLAM baselines and running fully onboard in challenging field environments; and VLA-Stem wraps vision-language-action policies with a learned execution-level safety layer that converts action chunks into motion references and RL-trained trajectory adjustments, roughly doubling constrained-scenario collision-free success (52.8% vs 23.7% for the strongest baseline) in simulation and reducing collisions in real robots while preserving unconstrained performance. (arXiv:2610.37602; arXiv:2610.36968; arXiv:2610.37111; arXiv:2610.37378)
  • New datasets and theoretical structures support embodied intelligence research: an egocentric tennis dataset with synchronized ego/allocentric views, 3D positions, and annotated strokes enables seven spatiotemporal prediction tasks where foundation models achieve 0.14 m ego-player and 0.54 m ball localization error, 0.34/0.91 m future trajectory error over 1.1 s, and 0.14 s time-to-contact error, with analyses indicating that added image history does not aid ball forecasting and that auxiliary depth helps but leaves 3D ball localization challenging; and a formal study of a bi-level regulatory regime where a regulator publishes seed agents defines “future-proof” compatibility as the closure of the seed set, proves this closure is monotone in the seed, and examines executable tests that rely only on seed interactions to distinguish future-proof from non-future-proof agents within a general multi-agent compatibility framework. (arXiv:2610.37619; arXiv:2610.36289)
  • Additional domain-specific hardware and motor designs extend actuation capabilities: a macro-scale electric-field induction motor with a rotor built from discrete circuit elements between conductive vanes allows tailoring rotor resistivity, permittivity, and circuitry, and demonstrates a deep-bar-like rotor effect that improves torque across operating speeds via frequency-dependent rotor dynamics. (arXiv:2610.37452)

cuArms: GPU-Accelerated Conflict-Based Planning for Multi-Arm ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes cuArms, a conflict-based multi-arm manipulation planner co-designed with GPU parallelism that decomposes search using reusable multi-modal roadmaps for individual robots and resolves conflicts and deadlocks via a lazy search, integrated with a high-level scheduler that jointly optimizes task assignment, schedules, motions, and manipulation skills. In experiments on simulated and real-world industrial tasks, cuArms achieves real-time initial solutions with anytime improvements in motion quality and scheduling, establishing a new state of the art compared to prior task-allocation, joint motion-planning, and learning-based multi-arm coordination methods that can require days of domain-specific training.Link
Learning Multi-Phase Robot Loco-Manipulation via Phase-Conditioned Policy OptimizationETH ZurichThe paper extends proximal policy optimization (PPO) to multi-phase loco-manipulation by introducing a phase-conditioned critic, phase-gated reward functions, and a multi-phase reset curriculum, combined with a decoupled hierarchical control architecture that separates low-level balancing from high-level phase execution to support diverse robot hardware. Using only environment interaction and no human motion data, the proposed method trains a single policy that executes complex multi-stage loco-manipulation skills on hardware for both the Unitree G1 humanoid and a Boston Dynamics Spot with a manipulator arm, with ablations validating the contributions of the phase-conditioning and curriculum design.Link
CARD: Contact-Anchored Retargeting for Dexterous ManipulationCarnegie Mellon University; UC BerkeleyThe paper introduces CARD, a simulator and object-free contact-anchored retargeting framework that unifies human data sources (video, motion capture, tactile gloves) via a shared hand–contact representation to generate both kinematic trajectories and explicit contact force targets for dexterous robot manipulation, including a tactile tracking controller for hardware open-loop replay and a video-to-contact model (Vid-HACO). Across video, glove, motion-capture data, and the GRAB dataset, CARD preserves contact on 43–78% of contact frames, whereas purely kinematic and object-aware retargeting methods maintain contact on less than 10% and 22.5% of frames respectively, and integrating CARD into physics-based retargeting increases strict task success on GRAB from 12% to 32%. Hardware experiments show that the proposed tactile tracker is necessary to reproduce demonstrated contact forces during replay, supporting the claim that explicit contact preservation via CARD is an effective interface for transferring human dexterity to robots.Link
ActiveAlign: Aligning Egocentric Manipulation via Active PerceptionUC San DiegoThe paper proposes ActiveAlign, a Real-to-Sim-to-Real alignment framework that uses active perception via two modules: ActiveAlign-Waving for hand-eye calibration through deliberate hand motions and differentiable rendering, and ActiveAlign-Touching for refining object alignment using tactile feedback to achieve millimeter-level accuracy. ActiveAlign-Waving is shown to curate simulation environments for open-sourced, uncalibrated robot manipulation datasets, and real-robot experiments on a Unitree G1 demonstrate that ActiveAlign enables fine manipulation of millimeter-scale objects, such as pressing buttons and moving sliders, by improving real2sim alignment.Link
Pushing Dexterity and Safety for In-Hand Object Pose Reaching That Works Remarkably WellUC BerkeleyThe paper presents a training recipe for in-hand object pose reaching with a LEAP hand in simulation, using diverse resets and goal generation from grasp datasets and an adaptive friction curriculum, and augments the learned task policy with a learned residual safety filter whose conservativeness can be tuned to trade off performance and safety. With system identification and either motion capture or a single wrist-mounted depth camera, the learned policies transfer zero-shot to a real LEAP hand; over 13.3 hours of real-robot evaluation on 4 objects and 4,800 unseen goals, the state-based policy reaches 95.2% of goals within 2 cm and 0.4 rad, and the safety filter reduces object drops by 75% while still reaching 91.5% of goals. The resulting in-hand manipulation policies exhibit robustness to out-of-distribution goals, fast arm motions, and perturbations, and can perform tasks such as spinning a lightbulb in arbitrary poses using only a wrist camera, illustrating portability of the learned dexterous skill.Link
EgoThumb: A Vision-Driven Supernumerary Robotic Thumb for Autonomous Grasp and Holding AssistanceUC San DiegoThe paper introduces EgoThumb, an open-source supernumerary robotic thumb system with a compact dual tendon-driven finger whose actuation is relocated to the forearm, reducing distal weight while allowing precise active joint control for assisting hold-and-manipulate tasks. EgoThumb employs a visual imitation learning policy that maps egocentric images from a palm-facing camera directly to continuous joint positions, enabling autonomous grasp and holding assistance that infers user intent from visual context and generalizes across different users and environments.Link
RESPLE2: Inertial-Informed Recursive Non-Uniform Spline Estimation for Multi-Sensor Odometry with Global RefinementETH ZurichThe paper presents RESPLE2, a recursive multi-sensor odometry framework that represents 6-DoF motion with non-uniform B-splines and uses knot-clamping properties to derive an inertial-informed control-point prediction within an iterated extended Kalman filter, with motion-adaptive knot placement and a reset step to maintain trajectory consistency. RESPLE2 incorporates heterogeneous sensor measurements (LiDAR, cameras, IMUs, leg kinematics) at native timestamps through continuous-time observation models, and includes a lightweight global optimization module that refines spline control points using LiDAR-visual loop-closure constraints. Across public and experimental datasets on handheld, tracked, legged, and hybrid aerial-legged platforms undergoing highly dynamic maneuvers, RESPLE2 achieves competitive or superior accuracy and robustness relative to state-of-the-art multi-sensor odometry systems under corresponding sensor configurations, while maintaining real-time performance, particularly in aggressive motions.Link
Perceiving and Predicting Dynamic Sports: Spatiotemporal Intelligence in Vision Models for Egocentric Tennis VideoMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces an egocentric tennis dataset with synchronized egocentric and five allocentric videos, estimated global positions, labeled contact times, and annotated stroke types, and uses it to train and evaluate foundation vision models on seven spatiotemporal prediction tasks including 2D ego player and 3D ball localization, future trajectories, stroke anticipation, and contact-time and position prediction. On these tasks, the best models achieve mean current-frame localization errors of 0.14 m for the ego player and 0.54 m for the ball, future forecasting errors of 0.34 m for the ego player and 0.91 m for the ball over 1.1 s, and a mean prediction error of 0.14 s for time to next ball contact, while auxiliary depth significantly improves 3D ball localization, indicating metric depth inference remains challenging. Analysis shows that extending image history length does not improve ball forecasting, suggesting that foundation models leverage scene context rather than solely inter-frame motion, and a proposed conditional diffusion model can generate whole-body tennis motion from egocentric video at real-time rates, adapting movements as vision-based predictions are refined.Link
Compatibility and future-proof testing for multi-agent systemsETH ZurichThe paper studies a bi-level regulatory structure for multi-agent systems in which a regulator publishes seed agents and developers independently test their agents for compatibility against these seeds, defining a notion of future-proofness where any seed-compatible agents are guaranteed to be mutually compatible without direct coordination. Within a general formulation of multi-agent compatibility, the authors characterize future-proof agents as the closure of the seed set and prove that this closure is monotone in the seed, then analyze a class of executable tests that rely only on interaction with the seed to assess how effectively such tests can distinguish future-proof agents from non-future-proof ones.Link
Electric-Field Induction Motor with Discrete Circuit Elements and Improved Rotor DynamicsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes an electric-field induction motor design whose rotor surface is constructed from discrete circuit elements between a finite number of conductive vanes, enabling rotor properties such as resistivity, permittivity, and circuitry to be tailored beyond what is possible with single continuum materials. Inspired by deep-bar rotors in magnetic-field induction motors, the authors design and demonstrate a macro-scale electric-field induction motor whose rotor circuitry emulates the deep-bar effect, thereby improving torque over a wide range of operating speeds by exploiting frequency-dependent rotor dynamics.Link

Whole-Body Humanoid Control via Stochastic Motion TokenizationGerman Research Center for AI (DFKI); Robotics Institute Germany (RIG); Technical University of Darmstadt; hessian.AIThe paper proposes a whole-body humanoid control framework that uses a stochastic motion tokenizer to produce concise motion representations, conditioned on behavior labels or text commands, which are fed as detailed motion references into a tracking policy. In motion reconstruction for the Unitree G1, the adopted stochastic motion tokenizer achieves higher reconstruction quality than finite scalar quantization (FSQ) and vector-quantized autoencoder (VQ-VAE) at matched capacity. Using the discrete motion-token interface, the system conditions whole-body tracking policies that execute generated dance, fight, and walk motions of 16.8 s each and a 35 s fight-to-dance sequence on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot.Link
GRAFT: Geometry-Aware Residual Adaptation for Foundation TrackersUnknown affiliationThe paper introduces GRAFT, a geometry-aware residual adaptation framework that leaves a humanoid behavior foundation model (BFM) frozen while a lightweight adapter adds terrain-conditioned corrections to lower-body execution targets based on task and foot-geometry rewards. GRAFT is trained by distilling privileged terrain observations into onboard depth sensing and is regularized to suppress unnecessary corrections on flat ground, treating terrain adaptation as an execution-level residual problem rather than retraining the full tracker. Instantiated on four frozen humanoid trackers, GRAFT enables locomotion over stairs, gaps, and platforms in simulation and allows a Unitree G1 with the adapted SONIC tracker to traverse real-world terrain using onboard depth and unmodified motion references.Link
REACH: An RL-based Hierarchical Humanoid Crawling Framework for Adaptable Confined Space TraversalChinese University of Hong Kong; Southern University of Science and TechnologyThe paper presents REACH, an RL-based hierarchical framework that combines variable-height bipedal walking and crawling policies for a Unitree G1 humanoid to traverse height-constrained environments. The learned locomotion policies regulate body height over 1.27–0.50 m in bipedal mode and 0.50–0.20 m in crawling mode, extending traversal beyond the clearance limits of crouched walking, with a history-conditioned latent representation to handle contact-rich crawling dynamics. Integrated with a LiDAR-based planner that generates collision-free 3D paths and proactive height targets, REACH achieves accurate height and velocity tracking, robust terrain traversal, and reliable mode transitions for confined-space traversal in real-world experiments.Link
Mimetic Embodiment Transfer of Human-Humanoid Interaction from In-the-Wild VideosUnknown affiliationThe paper proposes HumiTransfer, a mimetic embodiment transfer framework that converts in-the-wild human–human interaction videos into physically plausible human–humanoid interactions by reconstructing 3D human motion with consistent contacts and retargeting one participant to a target humanoid using interaction-focused modeling and post-optimization for balance, contact, and collision avoidance. Applying HumiTransfer to 1,200 in-the-wild videos yields the HumiMimic dataset with about 500,000 frames of diverse interactions for the 26-DoF Unitree R1 Edu and 23-DoF HighTorque Mini-hi humanoid robots, where HumiTransfer outperforms baselines in motion fidelity, preservation of inter-body relationships, and contact accuracy. The paper demonstrates that HumiMimic supports motion generation methods for interactive humanoid behaviors that follow a human partner and that the transferred motions are physically executable on real humanoid robots.Link
On Shaky Ground: Characterising Non-Rigid Base Effects in Mobile ManipulationAustralian Cobotics Centre; Faculty of Engineering; Queensland University of TechnologyThe paper investigates non-rigid base effects in lightweight mobile robot manipulators via a sanding case study combining simulation and experiments on two unactuated wheeled testbeds, jointly measuring contact force, tool motion, and base vibration during sustained contact. Under low base–ground resistance, the experiments show that only 54–70% of a commanded 100 N contact force is realized and tool-center-point errors can reach 100 mm, while increasing base–ground resistance improves force realization and halts further tool displacement. The study finds that base vibration does not vary consistently with force-tracking error in the tested conditions and argues that characterizing lightweight mobile manipulation under contact should jointly consider force, motion, and vibration to understand non-rigid base behavior.Link
A fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand with human-level joint structure enabled by linkage-based actuationRobotics Institute of Zhejiang University; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper presents Torch-Hand, a fully articulated humanoid dexterous hand with 21 DoF that replicates the human hand’s joint structure and motion hierarchy, including a five-DoF thumb with multi-axis CMC motion and four DoF in each remaining finger. Torch-Hand employs a fully linkage-based transmission with screw-linkage actuation and all actuators integrated in the palm in a serial-robot-like configuration, yielding a compact anthropomorphic form, high grasping force and payload capacity, and a direct mapping between motor motion and joint kinematics with mechanically decoupled functional DoFs. Experimental evaluations of Torch-Hand’s workspace, fingertip coordination, thumb opposition, 33-grasp taxonomy coverage, grasping force, payload capacity, and task-level manipulation indicate that the design provides a robust hardware platform for dexterous embodied manipulation.Link
Keeping Contact: Task-Adaptive Friction Margins for Bimanual Non-Prehensile ManipulationNanyang Technological University (NTU); R3 Institute (R3I) SingaporeThe paper introduces a contact-planning method for bimanual non-prehensile manipulation that uses signed friction distance as a quantitative measure of friction margin in force space and derives task-adaptive margins based on the force correction needed to balance pivot moment deviations during tilting. These task-adaptive friction margins are incorporated into a sequence of second-order cone programs that compute globally optimal contact forces at each tilt angle, balancing the object about an environmental pivot while keeping robot and pivot contacts away from nominal friction boundaries. Simulation and real-world experiments on bimanual tilting show that the proposed planner successfully executes tasks under variations in object properties and model mismatch, and that fixed-margin or no-margin strategies perform worse, supporting signed friction distance margins as an effective mechanism to maintain contact.Link
Omni-IGN: Onboard Image-Goal Navigation for Drones with Omnidirectional VisionNational University of SingaporeThe paper presents Omni-IGN, a fully onboard image-goal navigation system for drones that combines quad-fisheye RGB perception, an attention-based RL policy for continuous control, and a collision-aware safety layer, eliminating the need for dedicated depth sensors. Omni-IGN’s policy adaptively fuses goal-relevant features from multiple omnidirectional views to guide exploration, goal-directed navigation, and obstacle avoidance, and achieves the best performance among continuous-action baselines on Habitat ImageNav benchmarks. In IsaacSim flight tests, integrating learning-based depth estimation with the safety layer improves collision avoidance, and real-world drone experiments demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer without fine-tuning or external localization.Link
RAVEN: A Unified Active SLAM System for Rapid Autonomous Visual Exploration with Aerial Robots under Localization UncertaintyHong Kong University of Science and TechnologyThe paper introduces RAVEN, a unified active SLAM framework for quadrotors that jointly plans exploration and loop-closure actions by generating frontier- and loop-closure-based global tour candidates and scoring them with a utility vector that separately quantifies exploration efficiency and localization accuracy, tunable via a prioritization parameter in [0,1]. RAVEN includes a perception-aware navigation planner that produces smooth execution trajectories by minimizing visual state estimation error based on an online Fisher information field capturing real-time visual localization information. Benchmark evaluations show that RAVEN outperforms state-of-the-art active SLAM methods and can realize a spectrum of exploration–localization trade-offs, while fully onboard field experiments with a stereo-inertial quadrotor validate its applicability in challenging environments.Link
VLA-Stem: A Lightweight Safety Mechanism for Vision-Language-Action Models via Reinforcement LearningPeking University (PKU); University of Macau (UM); University of Science and Technology of China (USTC)The paper proposes VLA-Stem, a lightweight safety mechanism for vision-language-action (VLA) systems that leaves the base VLA policy frozen and instead converts its action chunks into motion references, allowing a learned Stem policy to adjust trajectories and timing at execution while preserving high-level task intent. Conditioned on motion references, robot proprioception, and local obstacle information, the Stem policy is trained via reinforcement learning with teacher–student distillation and curriculum learning to provide trajectory-level guidance that adapts to spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles without degrading base-policy performance in unconstrained settings. Across six constrained simulation scenarios, VLA-Stem attains an average 52.8% collision-free task success rate versus 23.7% for the strongest safety baseline, and real-world experiments similarly show reduced collisions under static and dynamic obstacles while preserving performance in obstacle-free tasks.Link
