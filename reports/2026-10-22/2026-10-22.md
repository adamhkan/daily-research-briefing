## Executive Summary

- SuperMimic jointly trains a differentiable world model and tracking policy via short imagined rollouts, reportedly surpassing a state-of-the-art humanoid motion tracker while reducing GPU-hours by more than 1000× and eventually achieving zero simulation tracking failures, with policies transferring to a physical Unitree G1 and model prediction error remaining stable as motion data scales. ([arXiv:2610.28480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28480))
- REACT learns deployable humanoid–human interaction policies from human–human demonstrations by VLM-guided semantic contact modeling and generative partner-state prediction, and is reported to perform all five tested contact-rich interactions on a Unitree G1 using only onboard egocentric depth sensing, with improved contact preservation and recall under heavy occlusion in simulation and hardware. ([arXiv:2610.28523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28523))
- AdaDex converts seconds-long human hand–object demonstrations into goal-conditioned dexterous skills by training a shared policy across tracking and generalization environments, reportedly yielding diverse behaviors such as in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting and dynamic throw-and-catch, outperforming baselines in generalization and functionality and transferring across simulators and to real robots. ([arXiv:2610.28628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28628))
- EmbodyHOI integrates a differentiable inverse-kinematics solver for a target humanoid into the sampling loop of a frozen text-conditioned HOI trajectory generator, and on ARCTIC trajectories reportedly cuts retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while improving task success rates, enabling cross-embodiment transfer at inference without retraining the trajectory model. ([arXiv:2610.27242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27242))
- EchoNav predicts evidential bird’s-eye-view occupancy from short RGB sequences using temporal aggregation and adaptation of frozen VGGT-Omega features, achieving 89.6% success in simulated sequential two-goal navigation (a 32.5 percentage point gain over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% success on a real robot across 60 trials versus 33.3% for the strongest baseline. ([arXiv:2610.27616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27616))
- A verification-guided training framework for neural Hamilton-Jacobi safety certificates uses probabilistic constraint enumeration and counterexample-driven fine-tuning to expand certified safe regions and reduce certification time; in Isaac Sim and real-world autonomous racing, a controller shielded by the refined certificates is reported to incur zero safety constraint violations compared to a nominal controller and an LQR-based backup policy. ([arXiv:2610.27870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27870))
