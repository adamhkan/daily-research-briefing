| SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model | UC Berkeley | The authors propose SuperMimic, which amortizes humanoid motion-tracking learning across many motions using a single differentiable world model of shared dynamics trained from logged simulator transitions, providing analytic gradients via backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts. The authors report that in 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs, reducing GPU-hours by more than $1000\times$ and failing on only 25 of 129,514 motions versus 179 failures for the baseline, and achieving zero failures after 9.8 hours on two GPUs. | VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human Interaction | Carnegie Mellon University | The authors introduce REACT, a framework that learns deployable humanoid-human interaction policies from human-human demonstrations by using VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting to identify semantically meaningful contacts and transfer them to humanoid-human geometry while preserving interaction structure. The authors report that privileged interaction policies trained with interaction-aware contact rewards are distilled into egocentric depth policies using photorealistic human depth rendering, and that generative partner-state modeling objectives predict partner motion from occluded egocentric views to address severe partial observability. The authors evaluate REACT in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid across five contact-rich interactions, reporting that semantic contact modeling substantially improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on four of five interactions under heavy occlusion, and that on real hardware REACT performs all five interactions using onboard sensing alone. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28523 |
| Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking | Carnegie Mellon University | The authors propose AdaDex, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework that converts a single hand-object trajectory from mocap or in-the-wild HOI reconstruction into an adaptable dexterous skill by training a shared policy on both motion tracking environments and generalization environments. On ARCTIC trajectories for Unitree G1 and H1-2 humanoids, the authors report that their guidance-based method reduces retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while preserving text-to-HOI quality and increases task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. The authors demonstrate that by injecting a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5 without retraining, they cut footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5%, eliminate collisions in a braking-relevant closed-loop battery when sufficient stopping distance exists, and shift plan dependence from ego speed to obstacle range.
