  • REACT learns deployable humanoid–human interaction policies from human–human demonstrations by VLM-guided semantic contact modeling and generative partner-state prediction, and is reported to perform all five tested contact-rich interactions on a Unitree G1 using only onboard egocentric depth sensing, with improved contact preservation and recall under heavy occlusion in simulation and hardware. (arXiv:2610.28523)

(arXiv:2610.28628)

(arXiv:2610.27242)

(arXiv:2610.27616)

SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World ModelUC BerkeleyThe authors report that SuperMimic uses a single differentiable world model trained jointly with a tracking policy via backpropagation through short imagined rollouts to provide analytic gradients and efficient policy learning for humanoid whole-body motion tracking. The authors report that, in simulation benchmarks, SuperMimic surpasses a state-of-the-art motion tracker trained with much larger compute, reducing GPU-hours by more than 1000× and dramatically lowering motion-tracking failures, eventually achieving zero failures. The authors report that policies trained with SuperMimic transfer to a physical Unitree G1 and that the learned world model maintains stable prediction error as motion data scales, with compute growing slowly with dataset size.Link
VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human InteractionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors report REACT, a framework that learns deployable humanoid–human interaction policies from human–human demonstrations by using a VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting pipeline to identify semantically meaningful contacts and transfer them to humanoid–human geometry while preserving interaction structure. The authors report that REACT uses privileged interaction policies with interaction-aware contact rewards, then distills them into deployable egocentric depth policies trained entirely from onboard sensing with photorealistic depth rendering, and introduces generative partner-state modeling to handle severe partial observability by predicting partner motion from occluded egocentric views. The authors report that in simulation and on a real Unitree G1, semantic contact modeling improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on most interactions under heavy occlusion, and REACT performs all five contact-rich interactions on hardware using only onboard sensing.Link
Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous MimickingCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors report AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that converts a single short hand–object trajectory into an adaptable dexterous skill by training a shared policy on both motion-tracking environments with dense tracking objectives and generalization environments with noised initial states and sparse object-success rewards. The authors report that from seconds of human motion per skill, AdaDex learns diverse and adaptable dexterous skills such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic throw-and-catch behaviors. The authors report that AdaDex outperforms baselines in generalization and functionality and that the learned policies transfer across simulators and to the real world.Link
EmbodyHOI: Embodiment-Aware Generation of Hand-Object Interactions for Humanoid Dexterous ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors report EmbodyHOI, which incorporates a differentiable inverse-kinematics solver for a specific humanoid embodiment into the sampling loop of a frozen text-conditioned HOI trajectory generator, feeding back IK gradients to jointly optimize the humanoid’s position relative to the object during generation. The authors report that because guidance acts only at inference, a single HOI trajectory generator trained on human hand poses can transfer across humanoid embodiments without retraining. The authors report that on ARCTIC trajectories for two humanoids, their guidance-based method cuts retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm and improves task success rates, while cheaper post-hoc alternatives achieve lower IK energy but train worse policies.Link
Planning under Noise with Neural OperatorsNew York UniversityThe authors report that at inference time PUN performs test-time scaling with bounded local search guided by the predicted global field and that across dimensions and noise levels it matches or outperforms other planners while maintaining low runtime. The authors report that in 4D at the highest tested noise level, PUN achieves substantial relative gains in success rate and speed over other methods.Link
What Would Removing This Corruption Change? Failure Attribution for Vision-Language-Action ModelsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe authors report a factorized risk model for failure attribution in VLA policies that learns separate scene and corruption representations to estimate the benefit of removing specific corruptions via latent intervention, without requiring paired clean images or counterfactual rollouts. The authors report that their method, evaluated on OpenVLA and $\pi_{0.5}$ in simulation and $\pi_{0.5}$-DROID on a Franka robot, outperforms monolithic and VLM baselines and achieves approximately 3× lower attribution error than frontier VLMs. The authors report that their approach adapts to execution context and corruption intensity and can be used for online failure monitoring on hardware.Link
Reasoning as a Distribution: Generative Plan Latents for Vision-Language-Action ModelsStanford UniversityThe authors report the Generative Plan Latent (GPL), which represents reasoning in VLA policies as a low-dimensional conditional distribution over plans, enabling plan sampling, user-steered plan selection, and outcome-based reweighting without retraining the underlying VLA. The authors report that on SimplerEnv with $\pi_0$ and additional domains including RoboCasa, LIBERO, and a maze environment, steering the plan latent increases the fraction of executions that follow a request from 0.48 to 0.94, whereas similar guidance applied directly to the native action distribution does not yield reliable gains. The authors report that explicit plan latents are most beneficial when they encode decision information not already determined by the observation.Link
Perceives but Doesn't Brake: A Perception-Action Binding Gap in Reasoning Driving VLAsStanford UniversityThe authors report that reasoning-augmented driving VLAs exhibit a perception–action binding gap, in which hazard information is encoded in visual representations and reasoning traces but has limited influence on generated trajectories, which are dominated by ego-speed history. The authors report that the trajectory generator in Alpamayo-1.5 is capable of braking but sometimes fails due to the binding gap, and that injecting a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler at generation time restores coupling between hazard perception and control without retraining. The authors report that with exact obstacle geometry, their guidance cuts footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5%, eliminates collisions in a braking-relevant closed-loop battery, and reverses plan dependence from ego speed to obstacle range.Link
Embodied Playground: A Long-Horizon Agentic Robotics Benchmark for Physical, Strategic, and Spatial ReasoningMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe authors report Embodied Playground, a benchmark of 410 task environments that evaluate how foundation-model agents coordinate physical, strategic, and spatial reasoning across long-horizon tasks requiring progress tracking, constraint satisfaction, and plan revision from execution feedback. The authors report curated subsets EP-100, EP-Easy, EP-Hard, and EP-20 to provide representative and difficulty-structured task selections for evaluation. The authors report a shared protocol specifying observation and tool access, resource budgets, repeated trials, and independent checks of goal completion, rule compliance, and physical validity to provide a unified framework for studying long-horizon embodied agents.Link
RoboJudge: Multimodal Language Models as Judges for Embodied Video GenerationUC San DiegoThe authors report RoboJudge-Bench, a benchmark of 800 human-annotated embodied videos from multiple corpora and generators, designed to evaluate MLLMs as judges of video Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment, revealing uneven agreement between existing judges and human ratings. The authors report constructing RoboJudge-Train, a 12K-video dataset with 25K human-verified scores and model-assisted rationales, and fine-tuning a 9B-parameter MLLM with supervised learning and reinforcement learning to obtain RoboJudge-9B. The authors report that RoboJudge-9B achieves the highest agreement with humans on RoboJudge-Bench, with a Pearson correlation of 0.719 and a statistically significant 0.089 gain over Gemini-3.7-Flash, and that training on RoboJudge-Train also improves performance on external physical-reasoning video benchmarks.Link

Towards Autonomous Bricklaying Using Boston Dynamics SpotCentre for Tactile Internet with Human-in-the-Loop (CeTI), Dresden, Germany; Department of Architecture, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, United States; Deutsche Telekom Chair of Communication Networks, TU Dresden, GermanyThe authors present a proof-of-concept system for autonomous brick stacking using a Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped with a manipulator arm, leveraging AprilTag fiducials, autowalk missions, and CAD-specified brick poses for navigation and execution between supply and assembly areas. The authors report that, over ten repeated assemblies of a curved four-layer wall with 14 wooden bricks, the system achieves mean translation errors below 10 mm, mean yaw errors below 1.5 degrees, and an overall RMSE of 27.72 mm translation and 2.06 degrees rotation, with additional outdoor tests including stairs.Link
EchoNav: Evidential BEV Completion from Historical Observations for Visual NavigationThe Chinese University of Hong Kong; The University of Hong KongThe authors introduce EchoNav, a visual navigation framework that predicts an evidential bird’s-eye-view occupancy map from short RGB sequences using temporal aggregation of historical residual proposals and a decoder that adapts frozen VGGT-Omega features without requiring depth, camera pose, or explicit geometric warping. The authors report that in simulated sequential two-goal navigation their system achieves 89.6% success, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline by 32.5 percentage points, and on a wheeled robot across 60 trials in 20 scenes it achieves 73.3% success versus 33.3% for the strongest baseline.Link
CovNav: Conformal Prediction-Guided Adaptive Inference for Object Goal NavigationChinese Academy of Sciences; Peking University; School of Advanced Manufacturing and Robotics, Peking University, Beijing 100871, China; School of Engineering, Newcastle University, UK; State Key Laboratory of Multimodal Artificial Intelligence Systems, Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, ChinaThe authors propose CovNav, a training-free framework that uses Conformal Prediction with a dual-process LLM to adaptively decide when to invoke slow chain-of-thought spatial reasoning for object-goal navigation by casting each spatial query as a structured binary decision over object pairs. The authors report that on Gibson and HM3D benchmarks CovNav surpasses the strongest zero-shot baseline by 0.8 and 1.7 percentage points in success rate on HM3D v1 and v2, while using slow reasoning for only 12.4% of queries and retaining most of the benefit of always-on thinking at lower latency, with additional validation on a TurtleBot 4 robot.Link
Enumeration-Guided Training of Neural Hamilton-Jacobi Safety CertificatesAIT Austrian Institute of Technology, Vienna, Austria; TU Wien, Vienna, Austria; Università Politecnica delle Marche, ItalyThe authors propose a verification-guided training framework for neural Hamilton-Jacobi safety certificates that uses probabilistic enumeration of safety constraints to detect forward-invariance violations and generate compact counterexamples, which are then used to fine-tune a pretrained HJ value function to yield larger certified regions and reduced certification time. The authors report that a shielding mechanism based on the refined certificates, evaluated in Isaac Sim and real-world autonomous racing experiments, achieves zero safety constraint violations compared to a nominal controller and an LQR-based backup policy.Link
LocoPilot: Single-Operator Bilateral Teleoperation for Loco-Manipulation Policy LearningDynamic Legged Systems Lab, Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT); Istituto Italiano di TecnologiaThe authors present LocoPilot, a single-operator bilateral teleoperation and data-collection framework on a Unitree Go2 quadruped with a Piper L manipulator and dual RealSense cameras, in which a second Piper L arm plus handheld controllers provide simultaneous control of base, posture, manipulator, and gripper, while interaction forces are estimated without a dedicated force/torque sensor and rendered through a passivity-constrained haptic channel.Link
Large Multimodal Models for Direct Robot Control: GPT-6 as a BeginningIndependentResearcher; InstituteofAutomationoftheChineseAcademyofSciences; NanjingUniversity; NanyangTechnologicalUniversity; PekingUniversityThe authors systematically evaluate GPT-6 Astra for closed-loop robotic manipulation using a lightweight agent execution framework, probing depth perception, embodied in-context learning, and tactile understanding, and demonstrate that the model can exploit depth information, acquire simple manipulation strategies from contextual demonstrations, and use tactile feedback to correct actions. The authors report that GPT-6 Astra achieves 26.92% success on RoboDojo and 76.0% on RoboTwin 2.0 but only 11.1% zero-shot success on the LIBERO-GeoMotion benchmark, highlighting a gap between semantic reasoning and precise, consequence-aware physical execution that is corroborated by real-world experiments showing persistent deficits in motion feasibility, control precision, and safety alongside substantial inference latency.Link
SPAR: Search-trained Push Action Ranker for Efficient Navigation Among Movable ObstaclesUnknown affiliationThe authors introduce SPAR, a Search-trained Push Action Ranker that learns to prioritize push primitives for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles using supervision from simulated searches over up to two pushes per object, with a transformer scoring primitives via contact tokens conditioned on scene geometry and reachability, and a single model guiding both SPAR Search in simulation and SPAR Policy for direct execution.Link
Physical AI for Intralogistics: Benchmarking the NVIDIA Sim-to-Real Ecosystem for Cosmos-Enhanced COMPASS Navigation on Industrial Mobile RobotsIdealworks; NVIDIAThe authors benchmark NVIDIA’s X-Mobility and COMPASS workflow for learned navigation on Idealworks industrial robots by extending the COMPASS simulation benchmark with two robot embodiments and a digital twin of the Idealworks R&D center, then fine-tuning policies in the digital twin—with and without NVIDIA Cosmos sim-to-real augmentation—and deploying them onboard the iw.hub to compare against COMPASS trained only on NVIDIA environments and the existing navigation stack. The authors report that training in the digital twin with Cosmos sim-to-real augmentation raises the real-world success rate of learned navigation from 65.3% to 91.7% across four scenarios and nearly halves weighted travel time versus simulation-only training, bringing performance within 8.3 points of the production navigation stack and, with an integrated global planner, achieving successful navigation to far, occluded goals in every trial.Link
