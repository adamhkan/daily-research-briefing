Towards Autonomous Bricklaying Using Boston Dynamics SpotMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe authors present a proof-of-concept system in which a Boston Dynamics Spot quadrupedal robot with a manipulator autonomously stacks bricks between a supply and assembly area using AprilTag-based localization, autowalk missions, and CAD-specified target poses. The authors report that over ten repeated assemblies of a curved four-layer wall of 14 wooden bricks, the system achieves mean translation errors below 10 mm, mean yaw errors below 1.5 degrees, and overall RMSE of 27.72 mm (translation) and 2.06 degrees (rotation). The authors demonstrate that the workflow is modular, extendable to different wall geometries, and can operate outdoors with stairs between the supply and assembly areas.Link
SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World ModelUC BerkeleyThe authors propose SuperMimic, which amortizes humanoid motion-tracking learning across many motions using a single differentiable world model of shared dynamics trained from logged simulator transitions, providing analytic gradients via backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts. The authors report that in 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs, reducing GPU-hours by more than 1000× and failing on only 25 of 129,514 motions versus 179 failures for the baseline, and achieving zero failures after 9.8 hours on two GPUs. The authors report that their learned policy transfers to a physical Unitree G1 across diverse and acrobatic motions, and that SuperMimic removes compute as the bottleneck to scaling humanoid whole-body control.Link
VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human InteractionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors introduce REACT, a framework that learns deployable humanoid-human interaction policies from human-human demonstrations by using VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting to identify semantically meaningful contacts and transfer them to humanoid-human geometry while preserving interaction structure. The authors report that privileged interaction policies trained with interaction-aware contact rewards are distilled into egocentric depth policies using photorealistic human depth rendering, and that generative partner-state modeling objectives predict partner motion from occluded egocentric views to address severe partial observability. The authors evaluate REACT in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid across five contact-rich interactions, reporting that semantic contact modeling substantially improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on four of five interactions under heavy occlusion, and that on real hardware REACT performs all five interactions using onboard sensing alone.Link
Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous MimickingCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors propose AdaDex, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning framework that converts a single hand-object trajectory from mocap or in-the-wild HOI reconstruction into an adaptable dexterous skill by training a shared policy on both motion tracking environments and generalization environments. The authors report that from seconds of human motion per skill, their method learns diverse adaptable dexterous skills, including functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch, and that it outperforms baselines in generalization and functionality with transfer across simulators and to the real world.Link
EmbodyHOI: Embodiment-Aware Generation of Hand-Object Interactions for Humanoid Dexterous ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe authors present EmbodyHOI, which incorporates a differentiable inverse-kinematics solver for a specific humanoid inside the sampling loop of a frozen flow-matching HOI trajectory generator, feeding back gradients to jointly optimize the humanoid’s position relative to the object so that reachability constrains generation-time trajectories. On ARCTIC trajectories for Unitree G1 and H1-2 humanoids, the authors report that their guidance-based method reduces retargeted wrist error from 8.9 cm to 0.6 cm while preserving text-to-HOI quality and increases task success from 68% to 79% on 50 random G1 tasks and from 62% to 76% on a four-task H1-2 suite. The authors report that alternative embodiment-gap-bridging approaches such as post-hoc wrist refinement and compute-matched best-of-N selection achieve lower IK energy but nonetheless train worse policies than EmbodyHOI.Link
Planning under Noise with Neural OperatorsNew York UniversityThe authors introduce Planning Under Noise (PUN), a neural operator that learns geometry-to-field mappings of noise-aware value fields for planning in unseen geometries, imposing reflecting Neumann conditions on obstacles and a Dirichlet condition at the goal and extending the Walk on Stars Monte Carlo solver to higher dimensions. The authors report that PUN amortizes computation by learning from noisy, partially converged Monte Carlo labels and, across four noise levels, matches or outperforms compared planners, achieving 95-100% success on a 4D replanning benchmark with the lowest runtime and up to 10.7-60.6% relative gains in success rate while being 3.29-32.15 times faster at the highest tested noise level.Link
Embodied Playground: A Long-Horizon Agentic Robotics Benchmark for Physical, Strategic, and Spatial ReasoningMassachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford UniversityThe authors introduce Embodied Playground, a benchmark of 410 task environments designed to evaluate how foundation-model agents coordinate physical, strategic, and spatial reasoning over long-horizon tasks with interdependent stages, intermediate constraints, and feedback-based plan revision. The authors curate EP-100, EP-Easy, EP-Hard, and EP-20 subsets along with a shared evaluation protocol specifying observation and tool access, resource budgets, repeated trials, and independent checks of goal completion, rule compliance, and physical validity to provide a unified framework for studying long-horizon embodied agents.Link
Perceives but Doesn't Brake: A Perception-Action Binding Gap in Reasoning Driving VLAsStanford UniversityThe authors identify a perception-action binding gap in reasoning-augmented VLA driving models, showing via matched simulated counterfactuals and replay that in-path hazards are encoded in visual representations but longitudinal plans are dominated by ego-speed history and only weakly respond to hazard proximity or the model’s own reasoning trace. The authors demonstrate that by injecting a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5 without retraining, they cut footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5%, eliminate collisions in a braking-relevant closed-loop battery when sufficient stopping distance exists, and shift plan dependence from ego speed to obstacle range. The authors argue that their analysis separates hazard perception from hazard control in driving VLAs and that unsafe behavior can arise even when the model has both evidence and the ability to act, while their guidance method provides a simple fix for this binding gap.Link
What Would Removing This Corruption Change? Failure Attribution for Vision-Language-Action ModelsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe authors formulate failure attribution for VLA models under visual corruptions and introduce a factorized risk model that learns separate scene and corruption representations, enabling estimation of the benefit of removing specific corruptions via latent intervention without clean image pairs or additional counterfactual rollouts. The authors report that their method, evaluated on OpenVLA and π₀.₅ in simulation and π₀.₅-DROID on a Franka robot, outperforms monolithic and VLM baselines, achieving approximately 3x lower attribution error than frontier VLMs and adapting to execution context and corruption intensity.Link
RoboJudge: Multimodal Language Models as Judges for Embodied Video GenerationUC San DiegoThe authors introduce RoboJudge-Bench, a benchmark of 800 human-annotated embodied videos from 8 corpora and 10 video generators, measuring Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment to systematically evaluate MLLMs as judges of embodied video generation. Motivated by uneven alignment of existing judges with human ratings, the authors construct RoboJudge-Train with 12K videos and 25K human-verified scores and fine-tune a 9B MLLM with supervised learning and reinforcement learning to obtain RoboJudge-9B, which achieves the highest agreement on RoboJudge-Bench with a Pearson correlation of 0.719 and a 0.089 gain over Gemini-3.7-Flash. The authors report that training on RoboJudge-Train also improves performance on external physical reasoning benchmarks such as VideoPhy2 and Physion-Eval, and they use RoboJudge-9B as a downstream evaluator to benchmark video generation models on Physical Adherence and Instruction Alignment.Link
Reasoning as a Distribution: Generative Plan Latents for Vision-Language-Action ModelsStanford UniversityThe authors introduce Generative Plan Latents (GPL) that represent reasoning as a low-dimensional conditional distribution over plans, enabling sampling, steering, and outcome-based reweighting without retraining the underlying VLA policy. In simulation, the authors report that steering the plan latent substantially increases adherence to user requests compared to steering native actions, which shows no reliable improvement. The authors find that explicit plan representations are most beneficial when they encode decision information not already fixed by current observations.Link
UniWALM: Unified World Action Language Model for Embodied Robot ControlUniversity of WashingtonThe authors present UniWALM, a unified world-action-language model that jointly learns video, action, and language within a single mixture-of-transformers backbone, decoding subtask, textual memory, and action in one pass without a separate planning stage. They introduce Flow Matching with Correction (FMC) as a training objective to maintain reliable actions under the richer joint language-action objective by teaching recovery from perturbed trajectories without extra inference cost. In simulated and real-robot evaluations, the authors report that UniWALM improves over same-backbone baselines on compositional manipulation and memory-dependent tasks, and that generating textual memory notably boosts performance on a real-world Find-the-Item task compared to both its non-language variant and state-of-the-art VLAs.Link

DexGraspFlow: Topology-Aware SE(3) Flow Matching for Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Grasp GenerationNanyang Technological University; ROKAE RoboticsThe authors propose DexGraspFlow, a conditional link-level SE(3) flow-matching framework that integrates geometric relations and kinematic topology via a dual-stream DiT with topology-aware attention biases to generate dexterous grasp poses across different robotic hands. In simulation across three robotic hands and multiple object–hand pairs, the authors report that DexGraspFlow attains high grasp success with substantially reduced generation latency compared to a state-of-the-art baseline. In real-robot experiments, a deployment-specific DexGraspFlow model reportedly achieves a high grasp success rate, supporting its applicability to physical execution.Link
XoNav: An Exoskeleton as a Personalized Haptic Interface for Assisted NavigationCSIR-Central Mechanical Engineering Research Institute; Human Centered Robotics and Cybernetics GroupThe authors introduce XoNav, a four-actuator hip exoskeleton that provides navigation guidance via personalized multimodal haptic cues at the hip joints, coordinated with gait using a bilateral LSTM-based gait-state classifier. The authors report that their gait-state classifier generalizes to unseen users with high recall and low detection delay for gait transitions, enabling timely cue delivery. In blindfolded Wizard-of-Oz navigation trials, the authors find that participants maintained path following within narrow corridors and improved progression speed over trials, while cues remained within safety limits and were reliably distinguished.Link
Design and Control of a Multimodal Legged-Aerial Robot Through Separate Control Contraction MetricsCalifornia Institute of Technology; Center for Autonomus Systems and Technologies; Northeastern University; Silicon Synapse LabsThe authors design a multimodal quadrupedal aerial-legged robot and propose a unified control framework using Control Contraction Metrics (CCM), where separate domain-specific metrics for walking and flight are found offline and unified online via an energy-based CLF-QP for cross-domain control allocation. Through simulations, the authors report that the unified CLF-QP-based controller enables optimal control allocation across locomotion and flight domains. Experimental results reportedly validate both the mechanical viability of the hardware and the feasibility of thruster-assisted trotting, motivating deployment of the framework for full multimodal operation.Link
Embodied Task Agents Can Perform Fine Manipulation through Tactile In-Context LearningFudan University; Jilin University; Nanjing University; Shanghai Innovation Institute; Tencent; Zhejiang UniversityThe authors propose Tactile-ETA, a system that augments embodied task agents with tactile experience via in-context learning, pairing robot motion with synchronized visual, tactile, and proprioceptive observations without updating model parameters. In simulation across four manipulation tasks, the authors report that adding tactile observations to the same two expert demonstrations increases average task success while keeping sensing and execution interfaces fixed. On real-robot fine manipulation tasks, the authors find that demonstrations with aligned visual and tactile observations substantially improve success rates, supporting the feasibility of tactile in-context learning.Link
EchoNav: Evidential BEV Completion from Historical Observations for Visual NavigationThe Chinese University of Hong Kong; The University of Hong KongThe authors introduce EchoNav, a visual navigation framework that predicts an evidential bird’s-eye-view occupancy map from short RGB sequences by adapting frozen VGGT-Omega features and aggregating historical residual proposals without requiring depth, camera pose, or explicit geometric warping. Their evidential model explicitly treats observed free space separately and predicts occupancy with associated confidence over the remaining region, trained on simulated RGB sequences with complete and visibility-masked occupancy labels. In simulated and real-robot navigation experiments, including demonstrations on multiple robot types, the authors report that EchoNav substantially outperforms the strongest evaluated baseline in success rate.Link
CovNav: Conformal Prediction-Guided Adaptive Inference for Object Goal NavigationChinese Academy of Sciences; Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; Newcastle University; Peking UniversityThe authors propose CovNav, a training-free framework for ObjectNav that uses conformal prediction on a fast local LLM’s token probabilities to decide when to escalate spatial queries to a slow chain-of-thought reasoning mode, turning dual-process inference into an uncertainty-aware decision. By casting spatial reasoning as structured binary decisions over object pairs and calibrating uncertainty, CovNav aims to retain most of the benefits of slow reasoning while limiting its use to ambiguous queries. In simulation on Gibson and HM3D and on a real TurtleBot 4, the authors report that CovNav improves success over the strongest zero-shot baselines while invoking slow reasoning on a small fraction of queries and approaching the performance of always-on thinking at lower latency.Link
LocoPilot: Single-Operator Bilateral Teleoperation for Loco-Manipulation Policy LearningDynamic Legged Systems Lab; Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia; Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT)The authors present LocoPilot, a single-operator bilateral teleoperation framework for a quadruped with a manipulator that enables simultaneous control of base, posture, arm, and gripper, while estimating interaction forces without a dedicated force/torque sensor and rendering them via a passivity-constrained haptic channel. Their ROS 2-based pipeline records synchronized multimodal observations with a unified 11D action interface shared between teleoperation and policy deployment, aiming to streamline loco-manipulation policy learning on the same embodiment. The authors report that LocoPilot supports multiple loco-manipulation tasks and that policies fine-tuned on collected pick-and-place demonstrations can be deployed on the physical robot without action retargeting or additional inverse kinematics.Link
SPAR: Search-trained Push Action Ranker for Efficient Navigation Among Movable ObstaclesUnknown affiliationThe authors introduce SPAR, a Search-trained Push Action Ranker that uses simulated search outcomes over short push sequences to train a transformer to prioritize push primitives for Navigation Among Movable Obstacles, distinguishing immediate openings from setup pushes. They deploy the learned ranker both to guide simulator-based search (SPAR Search) and to select pushes directly (SPAR Policy) within a NAMO planner that solves successive keyhole problems to access neighboring free-space regions. In simulated keyhole and NAMO planning benchmarks and in real-robot trials, the authors report that SPAR Search substantially reduces simulator calls and planning time while improving success over Random Search, and that SPAR Policy achieves high success without a physics-based digital twin.Link
