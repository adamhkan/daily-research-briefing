## Executive Summary

- MIT leads with two strong contributions on safe, time-optimal navigation and motion planning around obstacles, including exact signed-distance CBFs for polytopic robots and a biconvex minimum-time planner.
- Multiple works advance trajectory optimization and MPC for complex, uncertain dynamics, from stochastic multiple shooting to GPU-native ADMM-MPC that scales to long horizons and multi-agent scenarios.
- Humanoid and whole-body control see notable progress via perceptive locomotion and contact-implicit motion retargeting, pushing toward dynamic, contact-rich behaviors that transfer to real hardware.
- Dexterous and bimanual manipulation benefit from new generative and flow-based models for grasping, video-based skill distillation, and human-/world-in-the-loop learning under tight compute budgets.
- Navigation and exploration under uncertainty are strengthened by POMDP-based science planning, UAV path planning with social constraints, and robust real-time monitoring of safety specifications.
- A growing body of work analyzes and improves Vision-Language(-Action) and world-model-based control stacks, targeting robustness, sample efficiency, and better use of temporal and state information.

| Control Barrier Functions via Minkowski Operations for Safe Navigation among Polytopes | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | Introduces a nonsmooth control barrier function framework that computes exact signed distance between polytopic robots and obstacles using Minkowski operations, avoiding conservative spherical/ellipsoidal approximations. The method derives analytical gradients, reveals new local minima from geometry–kinematics coupling, and enables non-conservative, safety-recovering navigation maneuvers in unicycle and translation-only scenarios. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02886 |
| Biconvex Optimization for Smooth Minimum-Time Trajectories around Convex Obstacles | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | Proposes a biconvex minimum-time trajectory optimization scheme that jointly convexifies time and derivative constraints and enforces collision avoidance via time-varying separating planes. Alternating between plane computation and trajectory optimization yields an anytime planner that escapes local minima and converges from simple collision-free initializations, demonstrated on drone navigation and dual-arm bin unloading. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02834 |
