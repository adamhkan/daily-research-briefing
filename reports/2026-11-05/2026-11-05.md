## Executive Summary

- WayTrace converts web-map directions into atomic language commands with a vision-language orchestrator, predicts per-command image-space paths from over one million automatically labeled street-level images, and tracks them via a reinforcement learning policy; in long-range simulated missions it remains robust under GNSS noise with ~10× fewer crosswalk/sidewalk violations than point-goal baselines, and on a wheeled-legged robot it completes urban, rural-trail, and campus routes up to 1.37 km at ~1.1 m/s, negotiating crosswalks, stairs, and construction sites. ([arXiv:2611.05129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05129))
- Multiple works propose new world and safety models for navigation and loco-manipulation: a continual spatial world model in dynamic mazes separates episodic spatial changes from continuous action effects via Cross-episode Spatial Change Memory and state–action–outcome experience, reducing future route corrections and enabling transfer to unseen spatial transitions when past changes are informative; a CUDA-based WENO5/TVD-RK3/Lax-Friedrichs Hamilton–Jacobi reachability solver achieves real-time 2D–4D backward-reachable-tube computation at 61–147 Hz on desktop GPUs and tens of Hz on Jetson while matching double-precision baselines; a causal-inference framework transfers high-level causal structure from simulation to real human environments by updating only parameters, improving safe navigation over non-causal baselines; and a legged Vision-Language Navigation safety architecture adds a low-level, SDF-based, Bézier-parameterized MPPI controller with Discrete Exponential Barrier constraints that provably cage high-level VLA planners, yielding efficient, collision-avoiding language-driven navigation in real humanoid and quadruped experiments. ([arXiv:2611.04016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04016); [arXiv:2611.04096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04096); [arXiv:2611.04027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04027); [arXiv:2611.04972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04972))
- Several systems target long-horizon multimodal world and action modeling: 3D World Simulator learns a hybrid particle–voxel latent over point clouds with an action-conditioned dynamics model that jointly predicts future geometry, appearance, and tactile feedback at 50 Hz, supporting multi-view, tactile-aware policy training and planning on unseen objects; HiLo-WAM decouples sparse long-horizon and dense short-horizon visual streams within a dual-DiT world action model, achieving 93.70% average success on RoboTwin 2.0 and a 17.43 progress score on RoboDojo, with progressive inference providing a 2.93× speedup and improved performance; and action-conditioned latent foresight evaluates humanoid loco-manipulation command sequences via a visual-feature endpoint predictor plus joint temporal prior and action-consistency regularization, reaching 89.7% success on SIMPLE simulation and 85.7% (standard) / 65.7% (unseen objects) success on real tasks, outperforming prior sequence and GR00T-based baselines. ([arXiv:2611.04492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04492); [arXiv:2611.05023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05023); [arXiv:2611.04839](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04839))
- New frameworks address high-precision and physically grounded control: PolicyIK is a goal-conditioned RL inverse-kinematics policy with joint-offset tracking, two-scale pose errors, and progress-and-dwell rewards that in simulation holds mean end-effector error within 1 mm and 0.2° over the final second on 93.5% of held-out tasks (98.9% with deployment protocol), and on real robots surpasses four classical IK controllers and executes π₀.₅ VLA pick-and-place commands with 95.7% success; Self-Consistent Neural Control for systems with implicit equilibria controls sensitivity variations via a finite-difference approximation, doubling stable optimization rate over adjoint learning on thin-walled tubes (83.3% vs. 41.7%) and reducing loss by >1.5 orders of magnitude; and a physics-aware diffusion policy couples 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable MPM to infer material properties from videos and uses a memory-bank-conditioned, material-aware diffusion controller that improves simulated and real manipulation success on heterogeneous objects over prior diffusion policies. ([arXiv:2611.05372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05372); [arXiv:2611.03885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03885); [arXiv:2611.04493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04493))
- Several works focus on hierarchical, orchestrated, or ambiently supervised robot behavior: FEIJOA is an LLM-based mobile manipulation agent that iteratively queries a grounding tool over RGB-D scans and uses an embodiment-aware harness, resolving 94.2% of 103 open-ended grounding queries and completing 35/50 real Spot tasks, with most failures due to low-level skills; ActOR fuses wall and head cameras into an object-level digital twin for LLM planning in simulated and physical operating rooms, cutting exploration when targets start off-camera (1.05→0.12 calls), raising success from 46.7% to 83.3% under nominal conditions and from 8.3% to 51.7% under perturbations, and enabling 14/20 successful disposal trials on a humanoid; and HLO-AMP trains a low-level, object-aware motion-prior on retargeted MoCap of core primitives and constrains high-level policy commands to its empirical envelope, yielding a single Unitree G1 policy for bimanual box pick-and-place that generalizes across object masses, dimensions, and poses and recovers from deliberate drops. ([arXiv:2611.04080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04080); [arXiv:2611.04255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04255); [arXiv:2611.05113](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05113))
- Other contributions improve data usage, registration, safety, and dexterity: a mixture-of-transformer VLA study finds that coupling the flow-matching expert’s gradients into the VLM substantially boosts closed-loop driving and manipulation (≈17% route-progress gain with, and ≈40% without, action-token supervision) when training from scratch, while gradient insulation is competitive in open-loop with action tokens; Geometric Support Behavior Regularization penalizes distance to a base policy’s support rather than its distribution, raising average success from 33% to 95% and delivering ≈23% higher normalized throughput than the next-best RL method across 19 robot tasks; AIRoA-0 trains a bimanual manipulation system purely from 16,151 hours of handheld YUBI-gripper demonstrations to reach 78% average success across ten long-horizon real tasks versus 27% for the best post-trained VLA baseline; ARC integrates <10 ms whole-body tactile reflexes with learned control, halving contact impulse (0.2632→0.1225 N·s) and cutting first peak force by 34.1% on a Unitree G1 while preserving complex behaviors; a sparse-anchor multi-session visual registration method combines robust absolute pose “anchors” and Gaussian-feature-field alignment to reach 94.4% / 100% recall at 1 m / 5 m on HILTI26, surpassing image-localization and SfM registration; and DexHOT learns a relation-conditioned hand–object configuration transfer primitive from synthetic configuration pairs, attaining 80.5% transfer on seen and 69.4% on unseen DexHOTBench objects, 61.5% success over 200 real transfers, and 90% success on six real dexterous tasks. ([arXiv:2611.05249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05249); [arXiv:2611.05295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05295); [arXiv:2611.03815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03815); [arXiv:2611.04424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04424); [arXiv:2611.05084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05084); [arXiv:2611.05119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05119))

| WayTrace: A Map-to-Language-to-Trace Hierarchy for Embodied Urban Navigation | ETH Zurich; UC Berkeley | The paper proposes WayTrace, a hierarchical navigation system that converts web-map turn-by-turn directions into atomic language commands using an orchestrator vision-language model, predicts per-command image-space paths with a trace predictor trained on over one million automatically labeled street-level images, and tracks these paths using a reinforcement learning policy. In long-range simulated missions, WayTrace maintains robustness to GNSS noise and incurs roughly ten times fewer crosswalk and sidewalk violations than point-goal navigation baselines, which degrade sharply under the same conditions. On a wheeled-legged robot in real-world tests, WayTrace completes urban, rural-trail, and campus navigation missions up to 1.37 km at about 1.1 m/s, successfully handling crosswalks, staircases, and construction sites. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05129 |
| Physical Experience Builds Continual Spatial World Models | University of Washington | The paper introduces a continual spatial world modeling framework in a dynamic maze that separates episodic spatial changes from continuous action consequences, combining Cross-episode Spatial Change Memory for learning relations among transformations with state-action-outcome experience for refining motion predictions. Experiments show that structured repeated physical experience progressively reduces the need for future route corrections, enables transfer to spatial transitions not seen during learning, and that this advantage disappears when past environmental changes carry no information about future ones. Continuous navigation and physical tests demonstrate that predictive memory updates behavior when prior decisions become invalid, that correct topological knowledge alone does not guarantee accurate action prediction, and that accumulating physical experience reduces this mismatch within the modeled dynamics. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04016 |
| PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning | New York University | The paper presents PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision inverse kinematics that updates joint-position references from pose feedback, tracks accumulated reference offsets relative to measured joints, and uses two-scale pose errors with error-dependent action gains plus progress-and-dwell rewards to both reach and maintain a desired tolerance. In simulation, a PPO-trained PolicyIK policy keeps mean end-effector errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks with a single attempt, and a deployment protocol increases this success rate to 98.9%. Trained directly on a physical robot arm with off-policy RL, PolicyIK attains higher reaching and holding success than four classical IK controllers under equal time budgets, and in simulated pick-and-place it executes the end-effector commands of the π₀.₅ VLA policy with 95.7% success. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05372 |
| Online Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability Onboard the Robot Control Loop: A Real-Time GPU Solver for Safety-Critical Autonomy | UC Berkeley | The paper introduces a CUDA-based implementation of the canonical WENO5 + TVD-RK3 + Lax-Friedrichs Hamilton-Jacobi-Isaacs level-set solver for 2D–4D dynamics, enabling Hamilton-Jacobi reachability to run inside a robot’s control loop as a real-time safety layer. On a single NVIDIA RTX 4000 Ada GPU in FP32, the solver computes the 3D Air3D BRT at 112 Hz, a 4D pursuit–evasion BRT at 61 Hz, and a 2D wind-affected drifter BRT at 74–147 Hz, achieving roughly 5.9–17.5× speedup over the JAX hj_reachability implementation and 1.7–16× over a matched reimplementation, while in FP64 it agrees with hj_reachability on eight benchmarks and reproduces Mitchell’s Air3D BRT cell count within 0.04% at 2.0–4.6× speedup. On an embedded NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor, the solver computes Air3D at 66 Hz (N=41) and the 2D drifter at 54 Hz, 3.8–8.9× faster than hj_reachability, and an in-kernel label-propagation extension recovers K-agent capture-basin Voronoi tessellations from a single combined solve, running at 4.0 ms per tick in a closed-loop wildfire-response simulation of six quadcopters under time-varying, non-uniform wind. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04096 |
| Sparse Anchors for Visual Multi-Session Registration in Changing Environments | ETH Zurich | The paper proposes a multi-session visual registration method that uses a sparse set of reliable absolute pose constraints (“anchors”) obtained from visual place recognition, feature matching, geometric verification, and sequence consensus, combined with within-session relative-pose constraints in a robust pose-graph optimization, to register a query trajectory to a reference map under substantial scene changes. Absolute pose hypotheses are refined via featuremetric alignment against a Gaussian feature field, and inconsistent hypotheses are rejected through sequence consensus to retain only anchors that support a common query-to-reference alignment. On 15 cross-session experiments from the HILTI26 construction-site dataset, the method achieves 94.4% recall within 1 m and 100.0% within 5 m, outperforming independent image localization (65.3% and 70.7%) and direct registration of independently reconstructed SfM geometry, indicating that sparse reliable anchors can yield accurate and complete trajectory registration even when cross-session correspondences exist only along parts of the query path. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05084 |
| Self-Consistent Neural Control: Learning Trajectories for Implicit Equilibrium | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper introduces Self-Consistent Neural Control (SCNC), a trajectory optimization framework for systems with implicitly defined equilibria that explicitly controls sensitivity variations along trajectories, derives a bound on the resulting gradient error, and implements a practical finite-difference approximation to improve learning beyond the frozen-tangent adjoint approximation. Validated on deformable-object systems ranging from quadratic energy models (elastic rods) to complex thin-walled tubes, measuring tapes, and Slinky-like structures, SCNC achieves faster and more stable optimization than adjoint learning and the gradient-free iCEM baseline by reducing switching to undesirable equilibrium branches. On thin-walled tubes, where sensitivity variations are pronounced, SCNC doubles the stable optimization rate compared to adjoint learning (83.3% vs. 41.7%) and reduces average loss by more than 1.5 orders of magnitude. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03885 |
| Hybrid Particle-Voxel Dynamics for Multimodal 3D World Simulation | UC Berkeley | The paper presents 3D World Simulator, an action-conditioned multimodal world model that uses a hybrid particle-voxel autoencoder to compress point clouds into compact 3D voxel latents and an action-conditioned latent dynamics model to jointly predict future geometry, appearance, and tactile observations from a unified 3D representation. The shared 3D latent representation supports geometrically consistent rendering from arbitrary viewpoints and integration of spatially aligned tactile signals, enabling interactive long-horizon multimodal prediction at 50 Hz in experiments. Using large datasets of diverse objects, the model supports scalable data generation for policy training and evaluation with multi-view and tactile observations, and demonstrates model-based planning on unseen objects and robot configurations, highlighting hybrid particle-voxel dynamics as a basis for multimodal 3D world simulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04492 |
| HiLo-WAM: Decoupling Long-Horizon Task Evolution and Local Dynamics in World Action Models | UC San Diego | The paper proposes HiLo-WAM, a world action model architecture that decouples long-horizon task evolution from local visual dynamics via two visual streams—a sparse long-horizon stream for historical context and future evolution, and a dense short-horizon stream for local robot–object interactions—implemented within a dual-DiT model where actions are guided by structured cross-stream attention. HiLo-WAM introduces progressive inference with stream-wise partial denoising, joint denoising, and dynamic skipping to reduce computation while leveraging the complementary temporal scales of the two streams. Experiments show that HiLo-WAM attains a 93.70% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 and a 17.43 progress score on RoboDojo long-horizon manipulation tasks, with robust in-domain and out-of-distribution performance on the Fourier GR-3 robot, and that progressive inference yields a 2.93× speedup while further improving performance; ablations confirm the importance of both long-horizon and local dynamics streams and appropriate temporal-scale balancing. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05023 |
| Should the Action Expert Train the VLM? Action-Token Supervision and Gradient Routing in Mixture-of-Transformer VLAs | Stanford University | The paper studies training recipes for mixture-of-transformer vision-language-action models that pair a pre-trained VLM with a flow-matching action expert, independently varying whether expert losses backpropagate into the VLM (coupling vs. insulation) and whether the VLM is supervised to predict discrete action tokens encoding target actions. In open-loop driving, when action-token supervision is present, gradient insulation performs competitively with coupling, but without action tokens, coupling reduces trajectory-prediction error by roughly 25% compared to insulation. When both components are trained jointly from scratch, coupling the expert’s gradients into the VLM improves closed-loop driving route progress by approximately 17% with action-token supervision and about 40% without it, and similarly boosts robot manipulation performance under both conditions, indicating that the benefit of insulating the VLM depends strongly on action supervision and evaluation setting. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05249 |
| GSBR: Geometric Support Behavior Regularization for Online RL | UC Berkeley | The paper proposes Geometric Support Behavior Regularization (GSBR), an online reinforcement learning regularizer that penalizes actions based on their distance to the support of a pretrained base policy, imposing no penalty within the estimated support while allowing smooth, value-driven exploration outside it; the distance is estimated from finite samples of the base policy, requiring only sample access. By regularizing toward the geometric support rather than the full base-policy distribution, GSBR encourages the RL actor to focus on high-value modes and avoids forbidding beneficial actions that lie just outside the base policy’s support, addressing the over-restrictiveness of behavioral cloning regularizers and hard support constraints. Across 19 tasks on four robotic benchmarks, GSBR improves average success rate from 33% for the base policy to 95%, achieving the highest average success and about 23% higher normalized throughput than the next-best RL method. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05295 |

| ReflexArc: Bottom-Up Integration of Instantaneous Tactile Reflexes into Whole-Body Humanoid Control | Galbot.; Peking University; Tsinghua University | The paper presents ARC (Ascending Reflex Control), a bottom-up framework that integrates an instantaneous whole-body tactile reflex pathway (delivering withdrawal poses to motors within 10 ms) with learned whole-body control via reflex-in-the-loop learning, reflex-transparent alignment, and reflex-informed adaptation to support sustained withdrawal, balance, and recovery. | HLO-AMP: Hierarchical Loco-Manipulation from Object-Aware Motion Priors | Robotics and AI Institute | The paper proposes HLO-AMP, a hierarchical loco-manipulation framework in which the low-level controller is trained as an object-aware motion prior via motion imitation on retargeted MoCap of core task primitives with the manipulated object present, thereby capturing natural human-like coordination and realistic distributed contact wrenches while exposing a compact task-space command interface. A reference-free high-level policy is then trained atop the low-level prior, with its action space constrained to the empirical envelope of low-level training data so that it can extend behaviors to long-horizon, multi-stage tasks without issuing off-distribution commands that could destabilize the low-level controller.
