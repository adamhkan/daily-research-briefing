# Daily Robotics Briefing

Submission date covered: **2026-11-16**
Papers in this briefing: **15**

## Filters Used

- Institution filters: Carnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford University; EPFL; ETH Zurich; UC Berkeley; New York University; UC San Diego; University of Washington
- Topic filters: mobile manipulation; navigation; motion planning; dextrous manipulation; whole-body control

## Executive Summary

- Several works advance navigation and exploration: budget-conditioned object search with Uni-NaVid uses room-level Bellman experts to supervise VLN, improving reachable-target discovery on HM3D to 72.7% at 2000 steps over greedy, object-level, and pretrained VLN baselines; ERA enables training-free object navigation with RGB-predicted depth and error-correcting mapping, achieving state-of-the-art among no-depth-sensor methods and boosting VLFM success from 10.8% to 61.7% under predicted depth; and X-Nav combines a learned vision navigation model (MELIOR) with an agentic workflow to execute a ~100 m indoor–outdoor mission on a bipedal humanoid, transferring from a digital twin to successful real-world deployment and competitive R2R-CE/RxR-CE performance. ([arXiv:2611.17442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17442); [arXiv:2611.17730](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17730); [arXiv:2611.17598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17598))
- New sim-to-real pipelines and control approaches extend robot capabilities: TouchTwin replays human tactile data in MuJoCo to train manipulation policies that match 0.99× human grip force and generalize across real and perturbed demonstrations while substantially improving contact prediction over vision-based replays; reinforcement learning in simulation enables a 5-tonne skid-steer loader to autonomously perform two-wheel balancing, rough-terrain driving, spinning, and bucket swinging maneuvers previously reserved for elite operators; and a body-aligned virtual joint space standardizes humanoid locomotion interfaces, yielding stable training across three robots and nearly 6× reduction in heading drift on Unitree G1 versus native coordinates. ([arXiv:2611.17619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17619); [arXiv:2611.18365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18365); [arXiv:2611.18003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18003))
- Policy and planning frameworks integrate dynamics, control, and objectives: PoliMop is a kinodynamic RRT that expands a tree via policy rollouts guided by a simple mass-point preplan, reducing motion-planning time by up to 63% in simulations with quadrupeds, humanoids, and wheeled robots and exploiting environmental modifications like added planks; Objectives as Policies (OaP) has a VLM generate editable objective programs driving a shared MPC for contact-rich manipulation, with real and simulated tabletop experiments showing performance gains over strong baselines; and a two-stage informative planner for persistent monitoring learns unknown disturbance fields 25.0–52.3% faster than environment-wide estimation while identifying disturbance-aware monitoring paths. ([arXiv:2611.18110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18110); [arXiv:2611.18298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18298); [arXiv:2611.18505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18505))
- Learning in representation and policy space improves planning and control: a small residual adapter trained on the temporal order of successful vs. failed trajectories over frozen I-JEPA embeddings refines latent goal costs, raising goal-reaching success from 54%→98% on DotWall and 82%→96% on PointMaze under matched planners, and improving alignment with shortest-path costs on unseen DotWall layouts; PROOF uses a teacher–student perceptive locomotion framework to explicitly model perception reliability on a quadruped, enabling high-dynamic parkour under normal vision and robust fallback to proprioception under severe visual degradation. ([arXiv:2611.17529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17529); [arXiv:2611.18305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18305))
- New control formulations target collectives and spatial priors: a broadcast control framework for swarmalator collectives uses a single spatially affine field and quadratic-program-based control barrier functions to jointly govern motion, deformation, and safety, adapting group size for corridor traversal and allowing two independently controlled, phase-labeled collectives to traverse a shared junction without coordination in simulation; an evaluation of Qwen3.5-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it as spatial priors for object-goal navigation finds that their generated layouts capture room-type frequencies and relative object placements but misrepresent proximity to walls and room boundaries, yielding much smaller search-area reductions than priors learned from real layouts and suggesting LLMs are better suited for object–room associations and geometry-checked coordinate proposals than primary placement priors. ([arXiv:2611.17859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17859); [arXiv:2611.18123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18123))
- At the model and system level, PEAR and a survey of VLAs chart directions for generalist robot reasoning and control: PEAR applies robot-use reinforcement learning entirely in simulation to post-train an open-weight 27B reasoning model that outputs chain-of-thought and end-effector poses, raising success on LIBERO from 1.6% to 74.4% and on a real dual-arm robot from 0% to 60%, outperforming a demonstration-tuned vision-language-action model on LIBERO-Pro task generalization without measurable degradation on nine general reasoning, coding, and vision benchmarks; a life-cycle survey of 88 VLA works catalogs architectures, data, and deployment, noting growth from ~10,000 hours of teleoperation to datasets like 23,000 community episodes and 20,000 hours of egocentric video, a bifurcation into 3–7B and sub-1B models, emerging generalist manipulation-plus-navigation VLAs, and robustness gaps where near-perfect benchmark scores can mask failures under small scene perturbations. ([arXiv:2611.18562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562); [arXiv:2611.18372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18372))

| Racing the Clock: Teaching VLNs Budget-Conditioned Object Search | Stanford University; UC Berkeley | The paper introduces budget-conditioned object search in vision-language navigation, defining discovery geometrically and casting expert supervision as a Bellman recursion over different scene abstractions (object-level vs. room-level). Distilling room-level Bellman experts into Uni-NaVid yields higher mean object collection than object-level and greedy-frontier experts at all tested budgets, finding 72.7% of reachable targets at 2000 steps versus 68.5% (greedy-frontier), 65.5% (object-level), and 44.8% (pretrained VLN) on held-out HM3D scenes. The results show that higher expert return does not necessarily imply better supervision quality for VLN exploration, motivating expert designs whose information matches that of the student rather than optimizing expert return alone. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17442 |
| TouchTwin: Scaling Human Touch Data via Real-to-Sim-to-Real | Stanford University | The paper proposes TouchTwin, a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that reconstructs human manipulation demonstrations in MuJoCo by replaying measured tactile data from an 880-taxel glove, aligning contact locations and forces per finger rather than relying on vision-only hand pose. In simulation, policies trained on touch-grounded replays achieve a grip force of 0.99× the recorded human force and succeed on 20/20 held-out recordings and 35/36 unseen perturbations, while a contact predictor trained on these replays misses 13% of real contact frames compared with 32% for vision-based replays. Each touch-grounded replay is further used to synthesize perturbed demonstrations with simulated contact and force labels, rendered as realistic videos from new viewpoints and scenes, effectively scaling human touch data. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17619 |
| PoliMop: Policy-in-the-Loop Motion Planning in 3D Spaces for Mobile Robots | ETH Zurich | The paper presents PoliMop, a kinodynamic RRT motion planner that integrates physics simulation and robot control policies, sampling in a reduced 3D state space and using policy rollouts to expand the tree according to the robot’s true physical and control capabilities. PoliMop guides sampling with a preplanned path from a simple mass-point model, which focuses exploration on promising regions and reduces planning time by up to 63% in simulation experiments. Simulation studies with quadrupeds, humanoids, and wheeled robots show that PoliMop can be integrated with diverse robots and policies, automatically exploiting predefined environmental modifications (e.g., adding a plank as a bridge) as the robot’s reachable space grows. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18110 |
| Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots | Massachusetts Institute of Technology; UC Berkeley; UC San Diego | The paper introduces PEAR, a post-training engine that uses robot-use reinforcement learning (RURL) entirely in simulation to train an open-weight 27B reasoning model to control real robots natively via chain-of-thought reasoning and end-effector pose generation, without demonstrations or task-specific harnesses. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model’s success rate rises from 1.6% to 74.4% on LIBERO and from 0% to 60% on a real dual-arm robot, approaching proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared to π₀.₅, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, the RURL-trained model is substantially more robust to task changes on LIBERO-Pro (57.4% vs. 0.75% success on new tasks in familiar scenes) while showing no measurable degradation on nine general reasoning, coding, and vision benchmarks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562 |
| Heavy Metal Acrobatics: Learning Dynamic Control for Skid-Steer Loaders | ETH Zurich | The paper demonstrates that reinforcement learning in simulation followed by sim-to-real transfer can control a 5-tonne John Deere 332G skid-steer loader to perform dynamic acrobatic maneuvers that push its hydraulic actuation to the limits. Learned policies autonomously swing the machine onto two wheels and, while balancing, drive over rough terrain, trace specified trajectories, spin, and swing the bucket before safely landing back on four wheels, matching maneuvers previously achievable only by elite human operators. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18365 |

| X-Nav Technical Report: Real-World Long-Horizon Indoor-to-Outdoor Navigation on a Humanoid Robot | AgiBot X-Lab (Boson Lab); MBZUAI; Nanyang Technological University; The University of Melbourne; University of Electronic Science and Technology of China; University of Macau; Wuhan University | The paper presents X-Nav, a full data-to-deployment pipeline for language-instructed long-horizon indoor-to-outdoor navigation on a bipedal humanoid, combining a vision-based navigation model (MELIOR) with an agentic workflow (X-Navarch) for sub-task decomposition and obstacle-aware execution. MELIOR, using only RGB from the robot’s onboard camera and supported by LiDAR and odometry for low-level control, successfully guides a Lingxi X2 humanoid on an approximately 100 m mission from an office lobby to a store in a neighboring building, including destination changes, door traversal, and obstacle avoidance. Adapting MELIOR with data collected in a digital twin of the deployment site improves closed-loop success in simulation, and the adapted model transfers to successful real-world deployment while also achieving competitive results on R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17598 |
| ERA: Error-Resistant Autonomous Object Navigation with Predicted Depth | Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Tsinghua University | The paper proposes ERA, a training-free object navigation system that replaces depth sensors with depth predicted from RGB and poses, and implements a "Move with Error" paradigm where navigation continuously corrects depth-induced map errors via revisable occupancy and exploration from reachable viewpoints. ERA augments predicted-depth navigation by continuing exploration even when conventional frontiers vanish and by verifying targets across multiple views before stopping, achieving state-of-the-art performance among training-free, no-depth-sensor methods on three benchmarks. Integrating ERA’s mapping and exploration components into VLFM raises VLFM’s success rate under predicted depth from 10.8% to 61.7%, and ERA is further deployed on a real robot, demonstrating feasibility beyond simulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17730 |
| Reusing Locomotion Training Recipes Across Humanoids via Body-Aligned Virtual Joints | 1X Technologies; Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST); LG Electronics; Yonsei University | The paper introduces a body-aligned virtual joint space that standardizes policy action and observation coordinates across humanoid robots, with a deterministic kinematic layer mapping virtual joint targets to each robot’s native joint space while leaving the existing transmission and low-level control stack unchanged. Using a single unmodified locomotion training recipe across three humanoids with different leg-axis layouts, the virtual joint interface is the only tested option that yields accurate tracking, maintains foot alignment with the direction of travel, and trains stably on all three robots. On Unitree G1 hardware, locomotion policies using the body-aligned virtual joint space reduce heading drift by nearly a factor of six compared to native-coordinate baselines. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18003 |
| Objectives as Policies: Vision-Language Model Cost Programs for Contact-Rich Manipulation | University of Illinois Chicago | The paper presents Objectives as Policies (OaP), a framework where a pretrained vision-language model generates editable objective programs that specify multi-stage cost structures for a shared sampling-based MPC, enabling VLM-guided planning for contact-rich manipulation in a simulation-based digital twin. Given natural-language instructions and scene observations, OaP uses real-to-sim reconstruction, VLM-generated objective programs, and iterative refinement based on MPC rollouts before deploying the final program on a real robot. Experiments on four tabletop manipulation tasks in simulation and the real world show that OaP outperforms strong baselines for contact-rich manipulation when combined with MPC. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18298 |
| PROOF: Perceptive Robustness via Out-of-Distribution Fallback for Legged Locomotion | Pengcheng Lab; Southern University of Science and Technology; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou); Tsinghua University | The paper introduces PROOF, a perceptive locomotion framework for legged robots that uses a two-stage teacher–student paradigm to explicitly model perception reliability, learning both perception-driven and proprioceptive fallback behaviors and suppressing exteroceptive features under out-of-distribution visual inputs. Deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped with a RealSense D435 depth camera, PROOF enables successful high-dynamic parkour maneuvers under normal vision, including traversing large gaps, elevated platforms, and narrow beams. Under severe visual degradation such as heavy occlusion and strong illumination changes, PROOF maintains stable and robust locomotion performance by falling back to proprioception when visual inputs are unreliable. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18305 |
| Learning Latent Goal Costs for Visual World-Model Planning with an Expanding Curriculum | Technical University of Crete | The paper proposes learning a small residual adapter on top of frozen I-JEPA visual embeddings, trained from the order of observations along successful and failed planning trajectories with an expanding-start-state curriculum, to refine the latent goal cost for image-goal planning without retraining the encoder or world model or using task-specific progress labels. In simulation on DotWall and PointMaze with identical starts, goals, world models, and planner settings, using the learned adapter increases paired 50-case goal-reaching success rates from 54% to 98% on DotWall and from 82% to 96% on PointMaze. On 16 unseen DotWall layout-goal cases, an adapter trained on a single fixed layout improves agreement between the latent-cost ordering and the shortest legal-path ordering from 71.9% to 85.8%, indicating that planning experience can improve a frozen visual representation’s goal-cost ordering across layouts. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17529 |
| Per-Agent-Safe Navigation of Globally Driven Robot Collectives via Affine Field Design | Nanyang Technological University; University of Michigan | The paper introduces a broadcast control framework for swarmalator robot collectives in which a single spatially affine field jointly governs collective motion and deformation, with the scalar component regulating collective size and the spatial gradient inducing translation via non-reciprocal interactions. Using control barrier functions within a single quadratic program that directly incorporates both affine-field components, the method enforces per-robot wall, actuation, and cohesion constraints while continuously adapting collective size to the local environment, enabling safe traversal of corridors narrower than the unconstrained collective without pre-planned resizing. The authors derive how the available actuation budget bounds reachable speed and minimum passable corridor width and demonstrate in simulation that two independently driven collectives with distinct fixed phase labels can simultaneously pass through a shared junction without inter-collective coordination. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17859 |
| Evaluating Language Models as Spatial Priors for Object-Goal Navigation | Toyohashi University of Technology | The paper evaluates two open-weight language models, Qwen3.5-27B and Gemma-4-31B-it, as spatial priors for object-goal navigation by prompting them for distributions over object positions in rooms and analyzing their likelihoods and generated layouts using real room geometries from CubiCasa5K and ScanNet. The study finds that while generated layouts reflect correct object frequencies and room sizes by room type and can place related objects relative to a given object or wall, the models do not reproduce real patterns of object proximity to walls, often prefer configurations with movable furniture piled near the room center, and sometimes place objects outside specified room rectangles despite wall constraints. As priors for object search, LLM-generated placements reduce searched area relative to a uniform prior but achieve less than one-third of the reduction obtained from priors learned from real layouts (and less than two-thirds under spatial smoothing), supporting LLM use for object–room associations and constrained coordinate generation checked geometrically, but not as primary placement priors when real layout data are available. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18123 |
| Disturbance-Aware Informative Planning in Spatiotemporal Environments | Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University; University of Michigan | The paper proposes a two-stage informative planning framework for persistent monitoring in spatiotemporal environments with unknown, spatially varying but time-invariant disturbances, combining informed disturbance sampling—prioritizing disturbance measurements along geometry-derived candidate monitoring paths—with disturbance-aware trajectory optimization that incorporates a learned disturbance belief. In four simulation scenarios, the proposed method reduces the time needed to learn the disturbance field by 25.0–52.3% compared with environment-wide disturbance estimation, while identifying monitoring paths that may appear costly under nominal dynamics but become preferable once disturbances are taken into account. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18505 |
| Vision-Language-Action Models: A Life-Cycle Survey of Design, Training and Deployment | University of Huddersfield; Witsense AI | The paper presents a life-cycle survey of 88 vision-language-action (VLA) works from December 2022 to October 2026, organizing them along seven axes spanning inputs and memory, architecture, action outputs, intermediate predictions, pretraining data, post-training adaptation, and real-time deployment, with a detailed analysis of 22 representative models including their data, size, inference speed, and hardware. From the surveyed literature, the authors report three trends: training data has expanded from roughly 10,000 hours of teleoperated robot data to resources such as 23,000 community-collected episodes (SmolVLA) and 20,000 hours of egocentric human video (GR00T N1.7); model sizes have bifurcated into 3–7B-parameter models and sub-1B models that run on consumer GPUs; and generalist VLAs capable of both manipulation and navigation, such as UniVLA and Qwen-VLA, are emerging. The survey contrasts VLAs with world-action models that also predict future observations and highlights evidence that some VLAs with near-perfect benchmark scores can still fail under minor changes in object or camera pose, motivating open research questions on robustness and evaluation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18372 |
