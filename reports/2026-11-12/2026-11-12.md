## Executive Summary

- Several works advance geometry- and navigation-oriented representations: DeRep pretrains a depth-based encoder using combined bird’s-eye-view reachability supervision and depth reconstruction, then freezes it for downstream policies, yielding lower linear policy prediction error, higher navigation success across linear probing, imitation learning, and PPO (beating purely reconstruction-based objectives in all six PPO conditions), and enabling zero-shot sim-to-sim and sim-to-real quadrotor navigation in cluttered environments; UGG introduces a unified generative geometry model that jointly models RGB video, depth, and camera pose as conditional completions under modality- and time-dependent noise, learns from heterogeneous partially annotated datasets, supports search over multiple plausible 4D scene hypotheses, and outperforms non-unified baselines on camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and pose prediction, especially with sparse observations. ([arXiv:2611.13499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13499); [arXiv:2611.14440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14440))
- New planning and safety frameworks target robustness under uncertainty: L2P-DR-Forest is a sampling-based motion planner for nonlinear stochastic systems that learns optimal-transport-based ambiguity sets over unknown initial and process noise distributions, propagates them through dynamics, and checks safety, with proofs that returned plans satisfy specifications with prescribed confidence and are probabilistically complete under added assumptions, and empirical outperformance of existing stochastic planners; DOBB defines Dynamic Oriented Bounding Boxes that translate each agent’s state, uncertainty, and response into direction-dependent safety margins used for decentralized learning-based collision avoidance, achieving a 2.6% mean total failure rate across four multi-agent environments with collision failures ≤2.3% even under out-of-distribution actuation and estimation shifts; LBS proposes Latent Barrier Steering for generative planning, first steering behavior latents toward higher safety margins and then applying a path-space control-barrier-function QP, which increases safety margins, offers finite-time recovery guarantees, and reduces correction effort while maintaining safety and improving goal-reaching over path-only filters; SIGMO introduces a sigmoid-gated, interaction-aware driving planner that computes physics-grounded yield scores via polynomial sigmoids for surrounding agents and embeds them in a receding-horizon planner, remaining computationally lightweight while balancing proactive and defensive actions to avoid freezing and over-conservatism in simulation and hardware driving scenarios. ([arXiv:2611.13863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13863); [arXiv:2611.13142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13142); [arXiv:2611.13227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13227); [arXiv:2611.14414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14414))
- Several systems enhance physically grounded control and locomotion: GRF-MPC incorporates live ground reaction forces and center-of-pressure into a human centroidal-dynamics model and solves a real-time QP under an underactuated 4-cable configuration, where, versus a fully actuated 8-cable Cartesian impedance controller in ~300 trials with six participants, it reduces peak planar CoM error by 19.6%, trajectory tracking error by 24.1%, and net mechanical work by 28.7% while increasing normalized voluntary excursion area by 45.9% at 100 Hz replanning; a nonlinear 6-DoF MPCC for holonomic AUVs augments nominal dynamics with Gaussian-Process-learned residuals via a zero-order robust optimization that preserves 25 Hz real-time control, with simulation ablations highlighting residual selection and combined simulations/in-water experiments on the MOLA AUV showing better constrained trajectory tracking than nominal MPCC and a field-proven PID; ReACT is a humanoid locomotion framework that uses reflection-consistent disturbance modulation and cross-load tracking consistency training to preserve action correspondence under mirrored, asymmetrically loaded conditions, improving velocity tracking and reducing directional bias under varying asymmetric loads in simulation and achieving collision-free goal reaching in all 40 real-world navigation trials with unilateral hand loads. ([arXiv:2611.13204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13204); [arXiv:2611.14373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14373); [arXiv:2611.13929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13929))
- Task-level autonomy is extended to complex manipulation and process planning: Curie’s Jarvis enables a Franka Panda to execute chemistry protocols from photos of printed instructions on unstructured benches without fiducials, locating and label-reading visually similar containers from four camera views, autonomously completing 5/5 runs of one children’s kit and 4/5 of another and correctly identifying all containers in 160 printed/handwritten-label trials; DexSeed converts human hand–object interaction data into low-cost dexterous robot demonstrations by clustering by grasp mode with shared language, retargeting via InteractionMesh, augmenting arm trajectories, and using an RL finger controller in simulation to generate labeled rollouts for fine-tuning a vision-language-action model that, in simulation and real world, executes language-specified grasps, with experiments reporting improved contact fidelity and more reliable whole-arm execution than baselines; an offline optimization-based path planner for industrial manipulators unifies joint-path regularity, self-collision penalties, and process-specific costs/constraints using full-path and boundary-coupled chunked optimization with Iterative Weight Tuning from KPI–target gaps, and on UR10e carbon-fiber winding and 3D printing tasks produces hardware-executable paths that satisfy geometric tolerances and meet all KPI targets (with printing evaluated without extrusion); a deployment adaptation method for next-best-view selection learns a local sensor-return adapter atop a fixed pretrained world model to estimate measurement dropout, leading in real-robot structured-light experiments to higher surface coverage with 60% fewer captures and successful recovery of a surface region that a nominal strategy repeatedly targets but fails to measure. ([arXiv:2611.13251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13251); [arXiv:2611.14092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14092); [arXiv:2611.13983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13983); [arXiv:2611.13229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13229))
- Language and cognitive-style models are applied to planning and explanation: an uncertainty-aware lunar rover assistant couples a large language model with a sampling-based planner via a ReAct-style tool interface to explain trajectories in domain terms and re-run planning under user-specified conditions; in a simulator inspired by NASA’s VIPER mission and evaluated on plan comparison, feasibility, and lookup queries, this tool-augmented setup achieves 90.9% response faithfulness to planner outputs, nearly a 65% improvement over zero-shot prompting of the same LLM without tools; GenEA defines a generative agent with a multilevel state space linking articulated body biomechanics and object interactions, extending task-and-motion planning with simulation-based intuitive physics to obtain a joint distribution over goals and embodied plans whose transformations, across manipulation and navigation tasks, are reported to reproduce human reasoning about plans and to consistently outperform heuristic models, standard task-and-motion planning, and a state-of-the-art vision-language model. ([arXiv:2611.13292](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13292); [arXiv:2611.13669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13669))
- New generative and streaming frameworks target expressive robot behaviors: AHEADance is a real-time dance generation system for humanoids that uses ListenAhead to condition motion chunks on past audio and a frozen music model’s future-audio forecasts, weighted by reliability and time-aligned to motion; forecasted features preserving beat timing improve synchronization and focus motion energy at the beat frequency, while Commit Forcing (training continuations from the model’s own committed chunks) reduces low-activity motion from 83.5% to 10.3%, aligning with real dance data, and a Unitree G1 deployment demonstrates online, beat-synchronized dancing that starts and stops with the music without falls. ([arXiv:2611.13370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13370))

| DeRep: Learning Depth Representations for End-to-End Navigation in Cluttered Environments | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper proposes DeRep, a depth-based navigation-oriented representation-learning method that combines local bird’s-eye-view reachability supervision with depth reconstruction, with encoders pretrained and then frozen for downstream policy learning. Across linear policy probing, flow-matching imitation learning, and PPO-based reinforcement learning, DeRep yields lower linear policy prediction error and higher navigation success than depth reconstruction-only objectives in all six tested PPO conditions, while reachability-based objectives in imitation learning achieve higher mean success rates than reconstruction alone. A PPO policy using a frozen DeRep encoder is deployed without fine-tuning in Gazebo and on a physical quadrotor, demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-sim and sim-to-real transfer in cluttered navigation tasks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13499 |
| Large Language Model Explainability for Planning Lunar Rover Trajectories under Uncertainty | Carnegie Mellon University; Stanford University | The paper proposes an interactive assistant based on a large language model that explains uncertainty-aware lunar rover trajectories in domain vocabulary and can re-run a sampling-based planner under user-specified conditions via a ReAct-style tool-augmented interface. Paired with a simulator inspired by NASA’s VIPER mission and evaluated on plan comparison, feasibility, and lookup queries, the assistant achieves 90.9% response faithfulness to planner outputs, representing nearly a 65% improvement over zero-shot prompting of the same LLM without tool access. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13292 |
| Learn-To-Propagate: Distributionally Robust Motion Planning for Nonlinear Uncertain Systems | ETH Zurich | The paper introduces Learn-To-Propagate Distributionally-Robust Forest (L2P-DR-Forest), a sampling-based motion planning algorithm for nonlinear stochastic systems with unknown initial state and process noise distributions, using optimal-transport-based learned ambiguity sets that are propagated through the dynamics and checked for safety. The authors prove that L2P-DR-Forest is correct in the sense that every returned plan satisfies the specifications with prescribed confidence and, under additional feasibility and distributional assumptions, is probabilistically complete. Evaluations on several robotic benchmarks indicate that L2P-DR-Forest significantly outperforms existing motion planning methods for stochastic systems while providing high-confidence formal guarantees. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13863 |
| Curie's Jarvis: Autonomous Robotic Chemistry Experiments from Printed Protocols | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper presents Curie’s Jarvis, a system enabling a robot arm to execute chemistry experiments from a photo of a printed protocol on an unstructured bench, locating containers without fixed positions or fiducial markers and disambiguating visually identical containers by reading their labels from four camera views. On a Franka Panda arm, Curie’s Jarvis autonomously completes 5/5 runs of one children’s chemistry kit protocol and 4/5 runs of another without human intervention, and correctly identifies every container in 160 trials involving printed and handwritten labels. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13251 |
| A computational framework for understanding embodied plans | Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford University | The paper introduces GenEA, a generative agent representation with a multilevel state-space that couples articulated body biomechanics with object interactions by extending task-and-motion planning using simulation-based intuitive physics to yield a joint probability distribution over goals and embodied plans. Across experiments in both object manipulation and navigation domains with diverse tasks and behavioral measures, GenEA’s transformations of its joint distribution reproduce human reasoning about embodied plans and consistently outperform heuristic models, standard task-and-motion planning, and a state-of-the-art vision-language model. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13669 |
| UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model | Massachusetts Institute of Technology; UC San Diego | The paper proposes UGG, a unified generative geometry model that jointly models RGB video, depth, and camera pose by treating visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion under differing noise levels across modalities and time. UGG’s unified generative formulation allows learning from heterogeneous datasets with partial geometric or visual annotations and supports inference-time search over multiple plausible 4D scene hypotheses using geometric cues extracted from observed RGB frames. Experiments show that UGG outperforms non-unified baselines on camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, particularly in sparse observation scenarios. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14440 |

| Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control for Underactuated Upright Physical Assistance | Columbia University; SUNY Korea | The paper presents Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control (GRF-MPC), which incorporates live ground reaction force and center-of-pressure measurements into a human centroidal dynamics model and solves a real-time quadratic program to optimize assistive forces and moments for upright physical assistance with a cable-driven robot. Implemented on the RobUST trainer in an underactuated 4-cable configuration and compared to a fully actuated 8-cable Cartesian impedance controller over nearly 300 trials with six healthy participants, GRF-MPC reduces peak planar CoM error during perturbations by 19.6%, lowers trajectory tracking error by 24.1%, requires 28.7% less net mechanical work, and increases normalized voluntary excursion area by 45.9%. The underlying quadratic program solves in under 1 ms, enabling 100 Hz replanning and demonstrating that incorporating live human–ground interaction into predictive control can provide stronger, more transparent assistance with fewer actuators. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13204 |
| Incorporating World Models in Decision Making for Next Best View Selection in Active Sensing | GrayMatter Robotics Inc. | The paper proposes a deployment adaptation methodology for next-best-view selection in robotic 3D reconstruction that learns a sensor-return adapter from limited local calibration data while keeping a pretrained world model fixed, estimating measurement dropout over predicted surfaces to guide view selection. In real-robot experiments with a structured-light sensor, the adapted next-best-view method achieves higher measured surface coverage with 60% fewer captures than the nominal selection strategy and successfully recovers a surface region that the nominal method repeatedly targets but fails to measure. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13229 |
| AHEADance: Anticipatory Humanoid Dance from Streaming Audio | University College London | The paper introduces AHEADance, a streaming dance generation system for humanoid robots that uses ListenAhead to condition each motion chunk on past audio and a frozen music model’s forecasts of future audio, weighted by reliability and time-aligned to target motion segments. Using forecasted music features that preserve beat timing, ListenAhead improves synchronization and concentrates motion energy at the song’s beat frequency, while Commit Forcing—training continuation from the model’s own committed chunks—reduces low-activity motion from 83.5% to 10.3%, matching the low-activity level of real dance data. Deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, AHEADance produces online, beat-synchronized dancing that starts and stops with the music without causing the robot to fall. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13370 |
| ReACT: Reflection-Consistent Modulation and Cross-Load Consistency for Humanoid Velocity Tracking Under Asymmetric Loads | Eastern Institute of Technology; National University of Singapore; The Hong Kong Polytechnic University; Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University | The paper presents ReACT, a humanoid locomotion framework for accurate velocity tracking under asymmetric upper-body loads, combining reflection-consistent disturbance modulation—using estimated wrenches to modulate actor features while preserving action correspondence under mirrored observations—with cross-load tracking consistency training that aligns tracking performance between loaded and unloaded conditions. Simulation experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid demonstrate improved velocity tracking and reduced directional bias across varying asymmetric loads, and real-world tests show collision-free goal reaching in all 40 navigation trials under unilateral hand loads using a fixed mapless navigation policy. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13929 |
| Optimization-Based Path Planning for Industrial Manipulators: Experimental Validation in Additive Manufacturing | Zürich University of Applied Sciences | The paper proposes an offline optimization-based path planner for industrial manipulators that unifies joint-path regularity, self-collision penalties, and process-specific costs and constraints, using full-path and boundary-coupled chunked optimization plus Iterative Weight Tuning (IWT) to set cost weights from KPI–target gaps. In experiments on carbon-fiber winding and robotic 3D printing with a UR10e, the planned joint paths satisfy the specified geometric tolerances and meet all KPI targets, and are executable on hardware (with joint-level tracking recorded and printing performed without extrusion). | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13983 |
| DexSeed: Expanding Human Hand-Object Interactions into Robot Demonstrations for Dexterous Policy Learning | Robbyant; The University of Hong Kong; Zhejiang University | The paper introduces DexSeed, a framework that generates low-cost dexterous robot demonstrations from human hand–object interaction data by grouping sequences by grasp mode with shared language descriptions, retargeting them via InteractionMesh, augmenting arm trajectories, and using an RL finger-control policy with arm-tracking dynamics in physics simulation. Rendered visual observations and recorded robot actions from successful simulated rollouts are paired with grasp-language descriptions to fine-tune a vision-language-action (VLA) model, which in simulation and real-world deployment can execute language-specified grasps using only these generated demonstrations. Experiments indicate that DexSeed improves contact fidelity in retargeting and yields more reliable whole-arm execution compared to evaluated baselines in simulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14092 |
| Gaussian Process-Augmented Model Predictive Contouring Control for 6-DoF Trajectory Tracking with a Holonomic AUV | KTH Royal Institute of Technology; The Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute (MBARI) | The paper presents a nonlinear 6-DoF Model Predictive Contouring Control (MPCC) scheme for a holonomic AUV that augments a nominal dynamics model with residual dynamics learned via Gaussian Process regression, incorporated through a zero-order robust optimization formulation to maintain 25 Hz real-time control. Simulation ablations show that appropriate residual selection is critical to controller design, and combined simulation and in-water experiments on the MOLA AUV demonstrate that the GP-augmented MPCC outperforms a nominal MPCC and a field-proven PID controller for constrained trajectory tracking. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14373 |
| SIGMO: Sigmoid Gated Interaction-Aware Motion Planning | Honda Research Institute, USA, Inc.; University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign | The paper proposes SIGMO, a sigmoid-gated interaction-aware motion planner that analytically computes yield scores for surrounding agents using physics-grounded interaction features via polynomial sigmoid functions, and embeds these scores in a receding-horizon planner that can use arbitrary trajectory predictions. Simulation and hardware experiments in interaction-intensive driving scenarios indicate that SIGMO remains computationally lightweight while balancing proactive and defensive behaviors, avoiding both freezing and overly conservative navigation, and providing a strong baseline for autonomous driving mobility stacks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14414 |
| DOBB: Dynamic Oriented Bounding Boxes for Multi-Agent Collision Avoidance under State Uncertainty and Heterogeneous Dynamics | University of Notre Dame | The paper introduces Dynamic Oriented Bounding Boxes (DOBB), an online geometric safety representation that converts each agent’s current state estimate, uncertainty, and response characteristics into a direction-dependent safety margin, and derives interaction features and a reward function that preserve the physical meaning of these margins for decentralized learning-based collision avoidance. Across four multi-agent evaluation environments, DOBB-based policies achieve a lowest reported mean total failure rate of 2.6%, with collision-based failure remaining at or below 2.3% under tested out-of-distribution shifts in actuation dynamics and state-estimation uncertainty. The authors attribute the robustness of DOBB-based collision avoidance under uncertainty and heterogeneous dynamics to its geometric abstraction of safety margins, which reduces coupling between learned behavior and the specific uncertainty and actuation regimes seen during training. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13142 |
| Latent Barrier Steering: Hierarchical Safety for Generative Planning | University of Massachusetts | The paper presents Latent Barrier Steering (LBS), a hierarchical framework for flow-matching-based generative planning that first steers a behavior latent toward trajectories with larger safety margins, then applies a path-space control-barrier-function (CBF) quadratic program to correct any remaining safety violations. By separating behavior selection from path certification, latent steering in LBS increases the available safety margin for the path-space CBF corrector, providing finite-time recovery guarantees and reducing the correction burden compared with approaches that only filter trajectories at the path level. Experiments on navigation and robot end-effector planning tasks show that LBS maintains safety while improving goal-reaching performance and lowering correction effort relative to pure path-level safety filters applied to generative planners. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13227 |
