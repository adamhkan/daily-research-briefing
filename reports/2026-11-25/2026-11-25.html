Executive Summary

  • Several works advance planning and control for complex robot motion, including MoMa-DemoSmith’s two-stage global/local pipeline that auto-generates millions of whole-body mobile-manipulation demonstrations and trains MoMa-NMP to 87.0% success in simulation and 77.5% over 80 real trials, TAKT’s kinodynamic lattice framework that yields up to 2,000× faster multi-robot planning for ~1,000 robots with comparable or better makespan and cost, a hybrid TSP–Potential-Field–RRT/A* planner for powered wheelchairs that explicitly models elliptical robot geometry to shape collision behavior, and SBTenv, a MuJoCo split-belt treadmill environment that reproduces steady-state walking velocities (R² = 0.643, RMSE = 0.0162 m/s) without prior empirical corrections. (arXiv:2611.29913; arXiv:2611.29548; arXiv:2611.28394; arXiv:2611.28547)
  • New shared-representation and coordination frameworks target multi-agent and cross-embodiment collaboration: SEER aggregates multi-robot egocentric observations into a shared latent world model that improves navigation for both specialized and general vision-language agents while preserving single-agent capabilities, and MorphHOI provides a canonical body interface plus embodiment-conditioned transformer policies that reach cooperative transport success rates up to 96.7% on seen embodiments, 95.6% on unseen ones, and 91.4% on novel payloads across diverse human–object interaction skills. (arXiv:2611.30310; arXiv:2611.30312)
  • Several papers enhance world/action modeling, foresight, and spatial lookahead for embodied policies: MOVER adds an event-routing mechanism that prioritizes history-dependent residual updates, reducing a ResWorld UniAD-style planner’s nuScenes L2 error from 0.65 m to 0.55–0.50 m and collision rate from 0.23% to 0.12–0.09% with ~4.1 ms/frame overhead; SANA-Robot introduces a 4.5B-parameter world action model with hybrid linear/sliding-window attention achieving state-of-the-art memory on RoboDojo and top success on LIBERO-Plus/Pro/Max at 30 Hz control; ζ₀ converts VLA policies into WAMs by jointly predicting future vision and joint trajectories, improving success on HumanoidArena by 9.0–18.8 points and on six real-world tasks by 10.0–16.7 points over strong bases; SG-LL adds spatially grounded future latents via a frozen spatial teacher and zero-init residual fusion, improving LIBERO-Plus, CALVIN, and real-robot manipulation performance; JEPA-ICL learns JEPA-space predictive plans that boost Imitator Game success to 80.0% (12.0 points over Zero-WAM) and raise LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus success by up to 10.3 points over a VLA-JEPA variant; and NAT introduces a causal neural action tokenizer trained on 49,000 hours of heterogeneous data that outperforms FAST-based tokenizers on multiple simulation and real-robot benchmarks while enabling low-latency autoregressive control, including a version trained only on human hand motion that matches robot-trained baselines. (arXiv:2611.30022; arXiv:2611.28943; arXiv:2611.29277; arXiv:2611.28839; arXiv:2611.30516; arXiv:2611.28873)
  • A suite of works tackles sim-to-real, test-time adaptation, and higher-level orchestration: NFC (two complementary expositions) uses conditional flow matching, anomaly-triggered recalibration, and exploration policies to infer joint posteriors over simulator parameters and residual fidelity terms, outperforming existing simulators and increasing a wheeled robot’s rough-terrain success from 26% to 70% while cutting orientation jerk by 88%; RoboAda keeps high-level plans fixed and adapts low-level commands via inverse-dynamics-based hypothesis reweighting, raising average LIBERO success from 27.8% to 62.9% under 11 physical shifts and from 24.9% to 78.8% on RoboTwin 2.0 with joint-target offsets; RoboThink trains a real-time policy to leverage a reasoning agent’s high-level instructions and low-level feedback, boosting success under unknown drift on LIBERO from 0.09 to 0.87 and enabling 0.91–1.00 success on corrected requests with fewer tokens and latency than a strong agent acting alone; JiL adds a learned judgment token to frozen WAMs to estimate calibrated success probabilities and schedule System 2 takeovers and distillation for improved reliability at near-WAM speed; ReWeave jointly optimizes an agent harness and task topology without changing policy weights, consolidating 72 units to 44 groups and lifting held-out success from 89.00% to 98.75% on LIBERO-PRO and from 25.50% to 58.25% on RoboDojo; and Real2Worlds2Real builds and refines digital twins and “cousin” worlds from sparse observations to drive iterative policy learning and skill reuse, with experiments indicating improved simulator fidelity, transfer performance, and world-building efficiency as diversity and interaction data grow. (arXiv:2611.28260; arXiv:2611.28274; arXiv:2611.30209; arXiv:2611.30111; arXiv:2611.30188; arXiv:2611.31053; arXiv:2611.31098)
  • Tactile, visuotactile, and dexterous manipulation methods expand robustness and cross-embodiment generalization: TacitTouch distills a visuotactile teacher into a vision-only student by supervising denoising trajectories and intermediate actions, improving mean success by 16.0–22.5 points over vision-only baselines and staying within 2.0–3.75 points of the teacher on simulated and real contact-rich tasks; VTLA-RL shares encoders between visual and tactile streams to output joint motion and force targets, using demonstration-initialized two-stage RL to achieve higher real-robot grasping success under load variation and perturbations than supervised-only baselines; DUO Policy gates between a human-video–pretrained hand policy and a sparse robot in-hand policy, surpassing the strongest baseline by 12.9 points across simulated fine-grained tasks and 51.7 points on three real-world tasks while matching robot-only training with 2.75× less robot data; and FunGrasp-Zero uses object-centric functional contact maps and field-conditioned graph attention to achieve an 86% average zero-shot success across five unseen simulated hands and 80% average success on four unseen physical hands for novel functional grasps. (arXiv:2611.28697; arXiv:2611.28752; arXiv:2611.29101; arXiv:2611.29195)
  • Additional contributions span state estimation, hardware design, sensing, and evaluation: a probabilistic state estimator for sensorless continuum robots fusing actuation and camera images with a signed distance field reduces tip error from 2.5% to 1.6% of length in simulation and from 20.0% to 11.3% in hardware while tracking into target branches; a cable-driven 4-DoF salamander-inspired limb with proximal actuators and decoupling mechanisms cuts mass and projected area by ~20% and yaw-axis inertia by 31.9% while maintaining large joint ranges and ≥5.4× bodyweight load capacity; a belief-driven control rule for Ramsey quantum magnetometers adapts phase and interrogation time from navigation beliefs, yielding 20–44% navigation error reductions over fixed settings on USAF flight data and 15–40% mission error reduction when paired with information-aware routing; MobileNeTTT offers a 1.95M-parameter network that estimates time-to-transit from monocular images as a depth proxy for bio-inspired navigation, validated in simulation and lab robots; and RoboLiveMem benchmarks robot memory under live vs paused environments, showing that eight Claude/GPT models can drop from 97% paused success to 50% live success on realistic BEHAVIOR-1K tasks, that latency alone does not fix stale-frame actions, and that simple “final look” mitigations close much of the gap except under repeated identical-object swaps. (arXiv:2611.28521; arXiv:2611.30026; arXiv:2611.28416; arXiv:2611.29454; arXiv:2611.30047)

MoMa-NMP: Scene-Scale Neural Motion Planning for Mobile ManipulatorsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes MoMa-DemoSmith, a demonstration-generation pipeline that decomposes scene-scale mobile manipulation planning into global path discovery in large complex scenes and local motion refinement for collision avoidance, enabling the automatic generation of millions of whole-body trajectories in procedurally generated environments. Using demonstrations from MoMa-DemoSmith, the authors train MoMa-NMP via behavior cloning followed by privileged on-policy distillation to perform closed-loop motion planning for mobile manipulators from partial observations. On their benchmark, MoMa-NMP attains an 87.0% success rate in simulation and a 77.5% success rate across 80 trials in unseen real-world scenes.Link
Real-Time Kinodynamic Multi-Agent Motion PlanningMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces Tickwise Actions to Kinodynamic Trajectories (TAKT), a framework that pre-accounts for robot dynamics and reduces kinodynamic multi-agent motion planning to a lattice search for double-integrator robots, with analytically bounded clearance loss during smoothing and a closed-form convolution that converts any valid discrete plan into dynamically feasible, continuous-time collision-free trajectories. For robots spanning multiple lattice vertices, the authors propose a diamond lattice formulation that allows one-tick sideways yielding and adapt MAPF solvers PIBT and LaCAM to this setting. In 2D hall and 3D scenarios with 50 robots, TAKT-based planners run over 2,000× faster in 2D and over 100× faster in 3D than the strongest baseline while achieving comparable or lower makespan and cost, and they can plan for about 1,000 robots in seconds in 2D and tens of seconds in 3D, enabling real-time replanning for up to 1,000 robots.Link
MOVER: Planning Beyond MotionETH ZurichThe paper introduces MOVER, an event-routing mechanism that complements residual world models by prioritizing spatial event queries that weakly activate the residual pathway yet depend on temporal history, using a training-time teacher that measures sensitivity to removing past frames and a student that predicts event priorities at inference without additional cost. Under fixed routing mass, MOVER adjusts both a low-rank future bird’s-eye-view update and trajectory-conditioned event reading, and on nuScenes it reduces a ResWorld UniAD-style planner’s average L2 error from 0.65 m to 0.55 m and collision rate from 0.23% to 0.12% without direct ego status, and to 0.50 m and 0.09% respectively with ego status, at an added 4.1 ms per frame. On the BridgeDrive-LEAD / Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, MOVER achieves a 96.52 ± 0.65 driving score over three seeds and consistently outperforms matched control variants within each seed, with ablations indicating that the complementary teacher signal and its allocation primarily benefit training.Link
Seeing Together, Acting Apart: Shared Environmental Understanding for Multi-Robot NavigationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes Shared Environmental Understanding, a policy-agnostic multi-robot representation that aggregates egocentric observations from multiple robots into a shared latent world model to provide common environmental context while allowing decentralized decision-making. To realize this concept, the authors introduce SEER (Shared Environment Encoding and Retrieval), which stores multi-robot observations in a shared memory, retrieves relevant spatio-temporal context, encodes it via a predictive latent world model, and injects the resulting tokens into downstream navigation policies. Experiments show that SEER learns action-relevant environmental structure and consistently improves navigation performance for both specialized vision-and-language navigation agents and general vision-language model agents, while preserving the capabilities of single-agent systems.Link
Environment-Aware Probabilistic State Estimation of Continuum Robots from Planar ImagingStanford University; UC San DiegoThe paper presents a probabilistic state estimator for continuum robots that incorporates a known environment represented as a signed distance field, yielding a continuous (in arclength and time) estimate of the full robot state and its uncertainty from asynchronous measurements along the body. The estimator fuses planar images of body-fixed markers from a moving camera with commanded actuation, allowing state estimation for sensorless continuum robots without embedded sensors. In both a simulated aortic arch and a hardware phantom, using the known environment reduced the tip position error from 2.5% to 1.6% of robot length in simulation and from 20.0% to 11.3% in hardware, while successfully tracking the robot into the target branch.Link
NAT: Scaling Causal Neural Action Tokenizers Across EmbodimentsStanford UniversityThe paper introduces Neural Action Tokenizer (NAT), a causal action tokenizer whose decoder produces executable actions as tokens are generated, enabling autoregressive token-based control across diverse embodiments and data sources. Trained on 49,000 hours of action data from real robots, handheld grippers, simulation, and humans, NAT outperforms FAST and prior neural action tokenizers on four simulation benchmarks for unseen robot embodiments, and as an action space in vision-language-action models it surpasses FAST+ on four benchmarks and a real-robot task while reducing the latency for generating initial actions and, on some benchmarks, narrowing the gap to a flow-matching action head. Experiments indicate that robot data may not be strictly necessary for learning an effective action space, as a NAT trained solely on human hand motion performs comparably to tokenizers trained on robot datasets.Link
SANA-Robot: Efficient World Action Model with Hybrid MemoryMassachusetts Institute of Technology; UC San DiegoThe paper presents SANA-Robot, a 4.5B-parameter world action model with a hybrid memory mechanism that combines linear attention for fixed-size global memory with sliding-window softmax attention for propagating past information, achieving minute-scale episode memory at constant inference cost. By applying different noise levels to action and video tokens, SANA-Robot serves as both a control policy and a world simulator, supported by a full-stack infrastructure for efficient memory training and inference, including data packing, shuffling, and memory-phase-based asynchronous execution. SANA-Robot achieves the strongest memory performance on RoboDojo among medium-sized (~5B) models and the highest success rates on LIBERO-Plus, LIBERO-Pro, and LIBERO-Max, while enabling real-time closed-loop robot control at 30 Hz on edge devices; on an RTX 5090 it runs at 135 ms per action chunk with FP8 GEMMs and 115 ms with NVFP4 GEMMs.Link
Design of a Compact Cable-driven 4-DoF Salamander Robotic Limb with Large Range of Motion and High Load CapacityEPFLThe paper designs a compact cable-driven 4-DoF salamander-inspired robotic limb that uses a spatial cable-routing architecture to relocate actuators proximally, reducing limb size and inertia while preserving large ranges of motion and high load capacity needed for amphibious locomotion. Transmission-induced coupling among joints is mitigated via mechanically compensated multi-segment cable paths, model-based feedforward decoupling, and joint-angle feedback at the output side, enabling independent and decoupled control. Compared with a direct-drive limb, the proposed design reduces mass by 20.4%, projected envelope area by 20.5%, and estimated effective yaw-axis inertia by 31.9%, while experimentally achieving independent control over joint ranges of 45° (hip pitch), 118° (hip yaw), 118° (hip roll), and 135° (knee flexion/extension) and supporting a vertical load of at least 5.4 times its own weight.Link
SBTenv: A Literature-Validated Split-Belt Treadmill Simulation EnvironmentMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces SBTenv, an open-source split-belt treadmill simulation environment implemented in MuJoCo to support high-throughput physics-based studies of locomotor adaptation. By modeling compliant contacts at the spoke tips of a rimless wheel, SBTenv reproduces published experimental steady-state walking velocities on a split-belt treadmill with R² = 0.643 and RMSE = 0.0162 m/s, without requiring the empirical geometric corrections used in prior simulations.Link
RoboThink: Let the Agent Think, Let the Policy ActMassachusetts Institute of Technology; New York University; Stanford UniversityThe paper proposes RoboThink, a framework in which a separate reasoning agent provides high-level instructions and low-level motion feedback to a real-time robot policy via dedicated channels, and the policy is trained (using agent-labeled DAgger, role tokens, and counterfactual feedback) to interpret and act on this guidance without handing over moment-to-moment control. RoboThink enables mid-episode instruction revision (e.g., correcting targets) and compensates for actuation drift through feedback-driven motion adjustment, allowing the robot to undo or alter its own actions without additional training at inference time. On LIBERO tasks with Claude Opus 5.5 as the agent, RoboThink increases success rates from 0.09 to 0.87 under unknown constant drift and from 0.42 to 0.84 under random drift, achieves 0.91–1.00 success on misheard or changed requests, and on two-arm RoboTwin 2.0 tasks raises success from 0.12 to 0.74 (constant drift) and from 0.31 to 0.56 (random drift), while using far fewer tokens and less time per episode than a strong agent acting alone (e.g., GPT-6 Astra).Link
Calibrating World Action Models: From Adaptive System 2 Takeover to Self-EvolutionUC San DiegoThe paper proposes JiL, a framework that equips a frozen world action model (WAM) with a lightweight judgment token trained via cross-fitted temporal-difference learning on binary rollout success/failure, to produce calibrated success probabilities without affecting action predictions. JiL uses the calibrated success probability to adaptively switch control between fast System 1 (WAM) and a slower, more reliable System 2 agent on a chunked schedule, aiming to retain WAM-like speed while approaching System 2 reliability. JiL distills System 2 takeover trajectories—triggered only when WAM confidence is low—back into the WAM, using mastered-skill anchoring and dynamic confidence gating to repair capability gaps while preventing degradation of existing skills so that System 1 can operate standalone at test time.Link
RoboAda: Adapting Frozen Robot Policies at Test Time to Physics They Cannot SeeMassachusetts Institute of Technology; New York UniversityThe paper proposes RoboAda, a test-time adaptation method that keeps a pretrained robot policy and its high-level plan fixed while correcting low-level commands by inferring how the deployment-time robot maps commands to motion via an inverse-dynamics model of the original robot and competing response hypotheses. RoboAda adaptively reweights hypotheses of changed robot dynamics, using inverse-dynamics-derived ‘free labels’ from observed motion after each action chunk, and selects the commands that best realize the plan under the most predictive hypothesis, without rewards, gradients, or data from the changed robot. In experiments on five frozen policies (two WAMs and three vision-language-action models) on LIBERO under eleven physical shifts, RoboAda increases average success from 27.8% to 62.9%, outperforms the best of seven test-time adaptation baselines on Cosmos Policy by 17.3 percentage points, largely maintains performance when nothing has changed, and on bimanual RoboTwin 2.0 raises success under joint-target offsets from 24.9% to 78.8%.Link
ReWeave: Emergent Physical Task Topologies for Embodied Harness Optimization and Cross-Task Meta-DistillationStanford UniversityThe paper introduces ReWeave, a framework that jointly optimizes an embodied agent harness and an emergent physical task topology from interaction data while keeping execution policies and perception frozen, by co-optimizing context, workflows, and control loops without policy weight updates. ReWeave continuously revises its task topology based on new evidence, reprojects past trials onto the updated structure to expose retained progress, regressions, and bottlenecks, and uses these diagnostics to guide parent selection, search width, and cross-task transfer via reusable mechanism units. Across tasks, ReWeave consolidates 72 specialist units into 44 reusable groups (0.34× full concatenation) in a compact shared harness, improves held-out success from 89.00% to 98.75% on LIBERO-PRO and from 25.50% to 58.25% on RoboDojo, recovers retained progress in 89% of 159 failed RoboDojo trials, and on eight unseen LIBERO-PRO tasks reaches 51.3% success versus 41.3% for direct distillation, rising to 75.0% after continual learning.Link
Real2Worlds2Real: Scaling Self-Improving Agentic Real2Sim2Real for Robot ManipulationCarnegie Mellon University; UC BerkeleyThe paper proposes Real2Worlds2Real, a self-improving agentic Real2Sim2Real framework that constructs scalable simulation worlds (digital twins and diverse digital cousins) from limited visual observations and task descriptions, using a tool-orchestration harness that adaptively revisits and refines geometry and physical properties based on visual and execution feedback. Real2Worlds2Real orchestrates policy development over its constructed worlds by coordinating data generation, policy learning, and refinement based on task requirements and rollout feedback, while distilling validated procedures into reusable skills that can accelerate subsequent tasks. Experiments indicate that Real2Worlds2Real improves simulator fidelity and enables successful real-world policy transfer, with gains from increased world diversity and interaction-data volume, and that skill reuse further improves world-building efficiency and long-horizon task performance.Link
Feedback Completion Certificates for Repeated Dwell Tasks with Time WindowsETH ZurichThe paper analyzes repeated dwell tasks with time windows under bounded disturbances and shows that, for a common spatial predicate and dwell length with redundant windows removed, the remaining obligations can be ordered so that on any valid history the served windows form a suffix, implying that one boundary and one capped run length suffice as a feedback completion certificate for future satisfaction. Using this reduced state, the authors construct feedback policies over a verified library of branching motion primitives, proving that for one task type a scalar threshold per run length suffices and can be computed with work linear in the number of branches per time layer for fixed dwell, while multiple task types require a joint recursion. In simulations on a quadrotor inspection library and a two-station MuJoCo patrol, threshold-based completion filters reduce the cost of constructing reusable full-domain policies and, compared to a sparse Boolean baseline and safety-only filters, more reliably retain task completion certificates—preserving them in all 8 of 8 initially certified runs versus 3 of 8 for safety-only filtering—while still meeting all time windows in the tested runs.Link
RoboLiveMem: Is Robot Memory Solved When the World Does Not Wait?Carnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; UC San DiegoThe paper introduces RoboLiveMem, a benchmark that evaluates robot memory when the world continues to change during decision-making by running identical tasks twice—once with the simulator paused while the model thinks and once with the world running—across 29 task families spanning realistic BEHAVIOR-1K home/workshop/restaurant scenes and controlled ManiSkill tasks. On 34,176 episodes with 16 models, RoboLiveMem reveals that paused evaluations mask severe degradation under live changes: in realistic scenes where a remembered object changes, eight Claude and GPT models average 97% success with the world paused but only 50% with it running, with some models (e.g., Claude Opus 5.5) dropping from never failing when paused to 44% success when live. Analysis shows that lower inference latency does not solve the problem—models with only 2 s per decision still lose at least 36 percentage points relative to paused performance—and that models often perceive changes but act on stale frames; adding a final look before acting closes much of this gap in realistic settings, yet under repeated identical-object swaps all nine tested models still lose performance due to memory limitations.Link
One Policy, Many Bodies: Cooperative Human-Object Interaction with Heterogeneous TeamsETH ZurichThe paper presents MorphHOI, a decentralized physics-based human-object interaction framework that enables cooperative manipulation across heterogeneous embodiments by introducing a canonical body interface to unify observation and action spaces and an embodiment-conditioned transformer that tailors cooperative decisions to each agent’s physical capabilities and team composition. MorphHOI extends to new interaction skills by reusing the learned cooperative policy with task-specific tokens and lightweight adaptation, aiming to add new capabilities while preserving previously learned cooperation behaviors. In simulation, MorphHOI achieves cooperative transport success rates up to 96.7% on seen embodiments, 95.6% on unseen embodiments, and 91.4% on novel payloads, and maintains high success across diverse skills such as box carrying, stair transport, and waypoint following while preserving prior cooperative capabilities.Link
DUO Policy: Bridging Human Video Coverage and Robot Precision for Fine-Grained ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes DUO Policy, a dual-actor manipulation framework that splits control into a hand policy pretrained on human videos for global free-space motion and a robot policy trained on sparse in-hand data for local precise corrections, with a learned gate (trained from human interventions) switching between them based on state-dependent precision requirements. By leveraging human data primarily where precision demands are low and robot data near precision-critical states, DUO Policy is designed to tolerate moderate embodiment mismatch in human videos while generalizing to scene layouts beyond those covered by robot data alone. On fine-grained manipulation tasks such as K-cup coffee making, desk cleanup, and cluttered candy sorting, DUO Policy outperforms the strongest baseline by an average of 12.9 percentage points across four simulated tasks and 51.7 points across three real-world tasks, and matches the performance of robot-only training with 2.75× less robot data collection time.Link
JEPA-ICL: Progress-Aligned Latent Plans for In-Context Robotic ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper introduces JEPA-ICL, a vision-language-action framework that learns predictive plans in JEPA feature space by forecasting ordered future states up to subtask completion, thereby encoding what remains to be done and using these latent plans both to guide action generation and to align demonstrations with the robot’s current progress. JEPA-ICL leverages JEPA-supervised content features to locate progress-matched segments in demonstrations and uses this contextual guidance during training and inference to update a shared policy that jointly produces plans and actions, coupling self-prediction with demonstration reuse. On the Imitator Game benchmark, JEPA-ICL reaches 80.0% success on seen tasks under C15 pretraining, outperforming Zero-WAM by 12.0 percentage points; without contextual demonstrations it improves success from 96.1% to 97.6% on LIBERO and from 62.9% to 73.2% on LIBERO-Plus compared to a VLA-JEPA variant without human-video pretraining, and with demonstration-based training plus contextual guidance further boosts LIBERO-Plus success from 73.2% to 75.2%.Link

Neural Fidelity Calibration: Turning Field Anomalies into Simulation Fine-TuningIndiana UniversityThe paper proposes Neural Fidelity Calibration (NFC), a conditional flow matching–based framework that infers, online and from robot execution data, posterior distributions over both simulator physical parameters and residual fidelity terms capturing unmodeled dynamics and discrepancies between perceived and true environment geometry. NFC uses a simulation-trained anomaly detector on onboard sensory signals to decide when to trigger recalibration or fine-tuning and employs an exploration policy that modulates posterior uncertainty via optimism in the face of uncertainty to improve inference quality. Across five robot platforms with up to 39 calibrated parameters and a 128×128 residual terrain map, NFC yields more accurate posterior estimates than established simulation-based inference baselines and, in physical outdoor tests with a wheeled robot with an axle failure on snow, mud, and wet grass, increases rough-terrain navigation success from 26% to 70% while reducing orientation jerk by 88%.Link
Neural Fidelity Calibration for Informative Sim-to-Real AdaptationLuddy School of Informatics, Computing, and Engineering Indiana UniversityThe paper proposes Neural Fidelity Calibration (NFC), which uses conditional flow matching to jointly calibrate simulator physical parameters and a high-dimensional residual fidelity domain online during deployment, capturing both sim-to-real dynamics discrepancies and perception-induced environment uncertainty in a latent space for posterior sampling and policy fine-tuning. NFC includes an informative adaptation scheme—fine-tuning is triggered only on anomaly detection, with sequential NFC seeding from a simulation-pretrained estimator and a hallucination policy modulating posterior uncertainty using optimism in the face of uncertainty. Across five robots with up to 39 physical parameters and a 128×128 residual terrain, NFC attains more accurate posteriors than established simulation-based inference methods, and on a wheeled robot with a broken axle on snow, mud, and wet grass, it increases rough-terrain success from 26% to 70% and reduces orientation jerk by 88% in real-world deployment.Link
A Hybrid Multi-Goal Path Planning Framework for Powered Wheelchairs in Dynamic Environments: From Simulation to Real-World ValidationNortheastern UniversityThe study presents a hybrid multi-goal path planning framework for autonomous powered wheelchairs that combines TSP-based global task ordering with Potential-Field guidance and local RRT or A* planners, where PF guidance transfers global path information to help the robot stay aligned while avoiding static and dynamic obstacles. Local planning is enhanced using direction-aware, extended-neighborhood A* or RRT with environment-aware sampling and two-step extension, and an elliptical robot model is introduced to explicitly account for wheelchair dimensions during collision avoidance. Simulation evaluations in normal, crowded, and extra-crowded environments, along with hardware tests using motion capture and onboard LiDAR/RGB-D sensing, show that incorporating wheelchair geometry via the elliptical model changes observed collision behavior and yields comparable collision avoidance performance between the TSP-PF-RRT and TSP-PF-A* hybrid planners.Link
Belief-Driven Quantum Magnetometer Control for GPS-Denied NavigationEmbry-Riddle Aeronautical UniversityThe paper introduces a belief-driven control rule for Ramsey-type quantum magnetometers in magnetic anomaly navigation, where the navigation filter’s position belief, propagated through the anomaly map, determines the sensor’s phase and interrogation time at each epoch to balance ambiguity and decoherence limits. Evaluated on real U.S. Air Force flight data with only the Ramsey sensor modeled, belief-driven sensing is favored in 63 of 72 independent flight windows over fixed-setting baselines (p = 2×10⁻¹¹), reducing navigation error by 20–27% for low-SNR sensors and 30–44% for mid-contrast sensors, with smaller gains for high-quality sensors with accurate maps. Ablation studies show phase alignment primarily improves steady-flight performance and can match the best hindsight-tuned fixed setting, while interrogation time adaptation is crucial after a lost start, cutting median error by 37–42% even versus hindsight-tuned phase-aligned baselines; on an IBM superconducting qubit, the rule’s interrogation times stay within 1.3× of the empirical optimum, and when combined with information-aware routing, belief-driven sensing reduces mission error by 15–40%.Link
Distilling Touch into Vision-Only Policies for Contact-Rich ManipulationAmap, Alibaba Group; Tsinghua UniversityThe paper proposes TacitTouch, a distillation framework that trains a vision-only student policy to imitate a frozen visuotactile teacher’s tactile-guided behavior by supervising the student’s denoising predictions and intermediate action representations at matched noisy actions and flow times, supplemented with demonstrated-action supervision, without reconstructing tactile observations. During deployment, TacitTouch uses only visual input and no tactile encoder, expert, or generator, effectively shifting from learning predicted tactile signals to directly learning tactile-guided action patterns. Across four simulated UniVTAC tasks and four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, TacitTouch improves mean success rates by 16.0 and 22.5 percentage points over vision-only baselines, while remaining within 2.0 and 3.75 percentage points of the visuotactile teacher, and in robustness tests it outperforms TacImag by up to 20.0 percentage points in mean success rate.Link
VTLA-RL: Learning Visuotactile Policies for Load-Adaptive GraspingNVIDIA; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)The paper introduces VTLA-RL, a visuotactile grasping framework that shares a visual encoder between tactile-image histories and RGB inputs to jointly generate and execute motion and bilateral force targets in a unified action space, aiming to handle load-adaptive grasping under varying object masses and slip conditions. VTLA-RL is initialized with mixed real and simulated demonstrations to establish motion and contact priors, and then optimized using two-stage multi-task reinforcement learning solely in simulation, with the resulting policy deployed directly to real robots without further real-data supervision. Comparative evaluations of tactile representations and RL post-training variants in simulation, and hardware tests under load variation and placement perturbations, show that the full VTLA-RL method achieves higher grasping success than its supervised-only initialization and that RL post-training benefits depend on the chosen representation and training route.Link
Spatially Grounded Latent Lookahead for Vision-Language-Action ModelsPrismaX Robotics; Shanghai Jiao Tong UniversityThe paper presents Spatially Grounded Latent Lookahead (SG-LL), which augments vision-language-action models by predicting an actionable near-future representation that is spatially grounded in the current scene, using a frozen spatial teacher to distill geometric priors into spatial tokens and a current-conditioned bottleneck to learn a compact future latent without access to future observations. SG-LL predicts the future latent from the current policy context and initial Action Expert features, and fuses this latent into the Action Expert via zero-initialized residual connections to refine action generation while preserving stability at initialization. Experiments on the LIBERO-Plus and CALVIN benchmarks and on three real-world manipulation tasks show that SG-LL improves performance over compared baselines in both simulation and real-robot settings, indicating benefits from spatially grounded lookahead for manipulation.Link
FunGrasp-Zero: Field-Conditioned Graph Attention Policy for Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Dexterous Functional GraspingShenzhen Loop Area Institute; Sun Yat-sen University; X SQUARE ROBOTThe paper proposes FunGrasp-Zero, a reinforcement learning framework for zero-shot cross-embodiment dexterous functional grasping that represents functional intent through an object-centric, embodiment-consistent contact map, which is mapped to diverse hand morphologies and converted into a hand-object potential field providing attractive and repulsive guidance vectors. ζ₀: A Framework for World Action Models with Joint Scene and Body Foresight in Humanoid ControlFudan University; Lenovo Group Limited; Shanghai Innovation Institute; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Shanghai University; Tongji University; University of Science and Technology of China; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper introduces ζ₀, a framework that converts vision-language-action policies into World Action Models by jointly predicting future visual features and measured joint trajectories in a shared latent space, using a teacher conditioned on recorded futures and dual-response transfer to train a causal predictor to match both teacher responses and executing-policy actions. ζ₀ trains all components jointly but uses only causally predicted futures at deployment, providing joint scene and body foresight rather than purely visual foresight or unsupervised latent-distance objectives. On seven HumanoidArena tasks, ζ₀ built on Ψ₀ and GR00T N1.7 achieves mean success rates of 63.6% and 79.5%, improving over the respective bases by 18.8 and 9.0 percentage points; on six real-world tasks, it reaches 78.3% and 85.0% success, with gains of 16.7 and 10.0 percentage points, and on GR00T it outperforms all compared seven-task joint policies.Link
MobileNeTTT: a Lightweight DNN-based Time-To-Transit Estimator for Visual NavigationBoston UniversityThe paper proposes MobileNeTTT, a lightweight 1.95M-parameter neural network that estimates Time-to-Transit (TTT) for many visual features across the full field of view from a single monocular camera frame, aiming to provide a coarse depth proxy for bio-inspired robot navigation. MobileNeTTT is designed to be relatively insensitive to camera rotation and to automatically adapt TTT estimates to path geometry and obstacles, enabling simpler navigation control compared to large visual depth foundation models while remaining small enough for real-time execution on edge hardware. Simulation and laboratory robot experiments in dynamic and spatially complex environments validate that MobileNeTTT’s TTT-based navigation model can effectively guide mobile robots using monocular vision, with code, data, and video made available.Link
