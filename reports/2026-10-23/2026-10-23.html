Submission date covered: 2026-10-22

Executive Summary

  • T-WLA improves tactile-conditioned hand-motion prediction and real-robot dexterous manipulation, cutting finger-joint MPJPE by 30% versus the strongest reported baseline and achieving ~50.0% average success across real-robot tasks, according to the authors’ experiments. (arXiv:2610.28814)
  • Compact learned perception encoders and preprocessing pipelines can materially accelerate policy learning and improve navigation or locomotion robustness: RangeNav’s 183k-parameter LiDAR encoder with semantic and BEV pretraining raises simulation success by 32.03 and 23.73 percentage points over reconstruction-only baselines and shows robust zero-shot real-world navigation, while anticipated foothold-based elevation-map sampling lets a humanoid locomotion policy reach a target terrain difficulty in roughly one-third of the training iterations versus full-map inputs and transfer zero-shot to diverse real terrains. (arXiv:2610.29845; arXiv:2610.29612)
  • Several works demonstrate robust sim-to-real transfer in challenging dynamics regimes: a multistage RL policy achieves accurate lunar-gravity point-to-point jumping in simulation and transfers to hardware with torque/power profiles matching simulation within limits; HyperDrone’s HyperLux renderer enables training a vision-based yaw controller in simulation that runs successfully onboard a real Crazyflie; an EDF-integrated observation space supports zero-shot collision avoidance transfer to a 7-DoF arm in unseen real environments; and PPO+β(s) achieves 98% and 95% simulated success on dense-obstacle maps with bounded attitude and zero-shot transfer to a two-wheeled inverted-pendulum robot. (arXiv:2610.29586; arXiv:2610.28950; arXiv:2610.29452; arXiv:2610.29142)
  • New frameworks for humanoid control—covering locomotion, teleoperation, fall recovery, and sparse-pose loco-manipulation—report strong simulation and hardware performance: gravity- and terrain-randomized training predicts high survival (up to 0.99 on rough terrain) across Earth–Moon conditions under stated contact and soil assumptions; HuMiT attains ~20 ms communication-to-inference latency and validates split-second loco-manipulation on a Unitree G1; RISE enables task-steerable fall recovery with diverse behaviors on varied terrains; and SP-HLM leverages five sparse poses and a latent motion prior to improve motion tracking and loco-manipulation success over prior methods on real humanoids. (arXiv:2610.30080; arXiv:2610.29903; arXiv:2610.30061; arXiv:2610.30044)
  • Memory, representation editing, and world-action modeling architectures aim to extend horizon and flexibility in robot behavior: PAL’s hierarchical memory improves RoboMME and real manipulation success over other memory-augmented baselines; FLEX’s language-driven feature editing (add/generalize/mask) yields higher normalized rewards and compact, transferable representations across navigation and manipulation; and agentic/world action models AW-0 and CW-WAM jointly predict code–video–actions or video–motion–wrench, with CW-WAM substantially increasing tight-clearance insertion success and reducing force-reference RMSE and force fluctuations under an admittance controller. (arXiv:2610.29329; arXiv:2610.28893; arXiv:2610.30136; arXiv:2610.30117)
  • Tooling for learning from human data, active perception, and large-scale training shows reported gains in task performance and safety: SpatialDex, pre-trained on 1M human videos and few robot demos, outperforms prior methods on bimanual dexterous tasks; Real2Sem2Real enables policies trained from one human video per task to match or exceed teleoperation-based policies on two robots and four tasks; GGraph’s GPU-accelerated graph library cuts graph-based exploration training wall-clock by one order of magnitude via hundreds of parallel robots; an active next-best-view framework reduces task-dependent map entropy and navigation risk versus passive baselines; a twisting sidewinding gait for snake robots expands traversable terrains and reaches up to 49% higher speed than conventional sidewinding; and RoPT is reported to achieve higher tracking survivability under rapid motion in challenging benchmarks and real-robot demos. (arXiv:2610.29716; arXiv:2610.28880; arXiv:2610.28953; arXiv:2610.29964; arXiv:2610.29461; arXiv:2610.28962)

T-WLA: Bridging Slow Vision and Fast TouchMassachusetts Institute of Technology; UC BerkeleyThe authors report that T-WLA improves tactile-conditioned hand-motion prediction and real-robot dexterous manipulation, reducing finger-joint MPJPE by 30% relative to the strongest reported baseline and achieving an average success rate of approximately 50.0% across real-robot tasks.Link

RangeNav: Task-Aligned LiDAR Range-Image Pretraining for Robot NavigationETH ZurichThe authors propose RangeNav, a compact 183k-parameter LiDAR range-image encoder with anisotropic convolutions and circular padding, pretrained on synthetic data with a navigation-aligned objective combining range reconstruction, semantic segmentation, and BEV prediction before being frozen for policy learning. The authors report that adding semantic and BEV supervision during RangeNav pretraining increases simulation success rates over a reconstruction-only baseline by an average of 32.03 and 23.73 percentage points in procedurally generated training and out-of-distribution realistic test environments, respectively, and that zero-shot real-world deployments show robust navigation where limited-FOV depth policies struggle.Link
End-to-End Reinforcement Learning for Quadrupedal Jumping in Lunar GravityETH ZurichThe authors present a multistage reinforcement-learning approach that yields a single end-to-end policy for commanded point-to-point jumping in lunar gravity using only mission-compatible onboard sensing. The authors report that in simulation the learned policy achieves accurate point-to-point jumps with landing errors smaller than the robot's body size and maintains performance under control-update freezes of up to 1 s, and that hardware transfer demonstrates successful vertical jumping under lunar-gravity offloading with torque and power profiles consistent with simulation and within hardware limits.Link
Watch Your Step: Anticipated Perception for Humanoid LocomotionETH ZurichThe authors propose an anticipated perception scheme for humanoid locomotion in which a lightweight foothold prediction model uses proprioception and velocity commands to predict future footholds, and the elevation map is sampled only in small patches around these predicted footholds before being passed to the policy. The authors report that with this lightweight preprocessing, the locomotion policy reaches a given terrain difficulty in roughly a third of the training iterations compared to using the complete map, outperforms complete-map policies where footholds are sparse and matches an attention-based encoder at several times lower cost, while transferring zero-shot to a Unitree G1 humanoid on diverse real-world terrains.Link
Agentic World Action ModelsUC BerkeleyThe authors introduce AW-0, an agentic world action model that jointly models code, future video, and robot actions as three coupled generative streams in a mixture-of-transformers architecture, where a code expert generates parameterized skill routines and control logic while video and action experts generate future visual states and low-level motor commands.Link
GGraph: A GPU-Accelerated Library for Graph-Based Robot Deployment and LearningStanford UniversityThe authors present GGraph, an open-source GPU-accelerated graph library that supports both ROS 2-based real-robot deployment and vectorized GPU-based policy training, with built-in support for tasks such as 3-D navigation, exploration, and loop-closure integration. The authors report that GGraph enables graph updates in milliseconds for single-robot operation and allows graph-based exploration policies to train across hundreds of parallel robots, reducing wall-clock training time by one order of magnitude in simulation and real-world experiments.Link
Do Earth-Trained Humanoid Controllers Walk on the Moon? Gravity, Terrain, and Soil TransferStanford UniversityThe authors study gravity, terrain, and soil transfer for humanoid locomotion by training 12 policies on a simulated Unitree G1 under varying gravity, terrain geometry, and ground contact models, including Earth-to-Moon fine-tuning and gravity randomization, and additionally testing a frozen generalist controller (SONIC). The authors report asymmetric gravity transfer in which Earth-trained policies retain 0.64 to 0.75 survival at the Moon on flat rigid ground while Moon-trained and fine-tuned policies cannot stand at Earth and SONIC survives at most 0.10 at the Moon, and find that rough training can raise combined-tile survival from 0.53 to 0.98 at Earth and 0.80 to 0.99 at the Moon. The authors report that a gravity-randomized policy trained on rough 3D RFT terrain achieves seed-mean survival of at least 0.84 at Earth and 0.96 at the Moon on every key test cell up to 20 degrees, and that Moon-trained policies in sampled rollouts of held-out soil models keep most of their rigid-ground progress on lunar regolith with per-seed progress ratios of 0.84 to 1.39, while noting that lunar results are simulation predictions conditional on the contact model and soil stiffness.Link
Flexible Language-Guided Representation Editing for Adaptive Robot BehaviorNew York UniversityThe authors propose FLEX, a Flexible Feature Editing Pipeline that uses language models to iteratively refine a robot’s internal task-feature representation from natural language corrections by adding missing features, generalizing existing specific features, and masking irrelevant features. The authors report that across 2D navigation, 7DoF Franka tabletop manipulation, and a real-world study with naturalistic language corrections, FLEX requires all three operations in interactive scenarios and achieves consistently higher normalized reward than other feature editing approaches while keeping representations compact and transferable.Link
HyperDrone: Simulation-to-Reality Transfer at Millions of Frames per SecondUC BerkeleyThe authors introduce HyperDrone, a quadrotor simulator that combines highly optimized multirotor dynamics with HyperLux, a new GPU ray-tracing engine supporting Nvidia OptiX, Vulkan, Apple Metal, and WebGPU backends, designed for extremely fast high-performance batch rendering. The authors report that HyperLux can render realistic 3D scenes at millions of frames per second on consumer-grade GPUs, and demonstrate HyperDrone’s utility by training a vision-based yaw-control policy purely in simulation and successfully transferring it to an onboard-running real Crazyflie quadrotor with a camera.Link
Plan First, Act Later: Streaming Reasoning for Executable Memory in Robot ControlUC BerkeleyThe authors propose PAL, a hierarchical memory framework for vision-language robot manipulation that maintains a short-term visual buffer, a semantic-event memory, and an executable subtask list generated from streaming visual observations by a high-level VLM and executed by a low-level VLA policy. The authors report that PAL outperforms prior memory-augmented methods on the RoboMME benchmark and on real-world manipulation tasks, achieving higher success rates than FrameSamp-Modul and MemER. The authors report that proactively writing future-relevant subtasks into hierarchical memory improves long-horizon visual reasoning and robot control.Link
RoPT: Robust 6DoF Object Pose Tracking in VideoNew York UniversityThe authors report that RoPT achieves significantly higher tracking survivability under rapid motion on challenging benchmarks and in real-world robotic demonstrations.Link
Real2Sem2Real: 3D Semantic Augmentations for Robot Learning from a Single Human RGB DemonstrationETH Zurich; Stanford UniversityThe authors report that policies trained on data generated by Real2Sem2Real from one human video per task match or exceed policies trained on teleoperation across diverse object instances on two real robots and four tasks.Link

Navigation Explainability for Object Avoidance with LLMs for Assistive RobotsUnknown affiliationThe authors present the Context-Aware Navigation Explanation framework, which integrates perceptual context with planned future trajectories to generate natural-language explanations of guide dog–style robot movements for obstacle avoidance and navigation using a large language model. The authors report that indoor evaluations on a Unitree Go2 platform show a strong positive relationship between explanation satisfaction and trust, and that combining obstacle information with future trajectory yields the most effective explanations.Link
State-Dependent Objective Coordination for End-to-End Balance and NavigationUnknown affiliationThe authors propose a lightweight state-dependent objective coordination method for end-to-end reinforcement learning on two-wheeled inverted-pendulum robots, using a pitch-conditioned scalarization coefficient β(s) to balance navigation and attitude stabilization without auxiliary controllers. The authors report that PPO+β(s) attains 98% and 95% success rates with small attitude excursions on fixed and randomly generated high-obstacle-density maps in simulation, and that zero-shot hardware experiments show bounded attitude and consistent obstacle avoidance using only onboard observations.Link
Distance Field Driven Reinforcement Learning for Reactive and Robust Collision AvoidanceNational Institute of Informatics; Tokyo University of Science; Universidad Carlos III de Madrid; University of Technology SydneyThe authors propose a reinforcement learning framework that integrates the Euclidean Distance Field directly into the observation space to provide continuous geometric cues for stable, reactive collision avoidance with streamlined reward design. The authors report zero-shot transfer of the EDF-based policy from simulation to a 7-DoF real manipulator, with generalization to unseen real environments and robust online avoidance of dynamic obstacles despite training only with randomized static objects.Link
Linear Twisting Sidewinding Gaits for Twistable Snake Robot Motion Planning on Complex TerrainsShanghai Jiao Tong UniversityThe authors develop a new family of twisting sidewinding gaits for snake robots based on a linear twisting function that distributes workload across the body and reduces peak output power per twist joint under matched turning speed and total output power. The authors report that the new twisting sidewinding gaits enable locomotion on various terrains where the existing rolling vector approach fails and achieve up to 49% higher speed than conventional sidewinding without twisting in real-robot experiments.Link
Reconstruction-Augmented Visuomotor Pre-Training of 3D-Grounded Bimanual Dexterity from RGB-Only Human VideosCornell UniversityThe authors report that SpatialDex, pre-trained on 1M human-video clips and fine-tuned with few robot demonstrations, outperforms state-of-the-art methods on simulated and real-world bimanual dexterous tasks, and that including reconstructed geometry in pre-training improves success over hand-motion-only pre-training.Link
HuMiT: Low-Latency Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation via Compact Reference TrackingRenmin University of China; The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Tsinghua College AI; Tsinghua IIIS; Tsinghua Weiyang CollegeThe authors introduce HuMiT, a whole-body humanoid teleoperation system based on a unified tracking policy that uses only a compact current-frame reference (root height, root velocity, and SE(3) poses of four sparse keypoints) while achieving tracking fidelity competitive with methods requiring dense reference targets and future frames. The authors report that HuMiT attains a communication-to-inference latency of about 20 ms and a median video-based optical flow latency of 65 ms, and validate on a Unitree G1 humanoid with split-second reactive responses and loco-manipulation capabilities.Link
Object- and Task-Aware Active Perception for Safe Navigation of Mobile Manipulators in Semi-Static EnvironmentsEpson Canada Ltd.; Munich Institute for Robotics and Machine Intelligence (MIRMI); Simon Fraser University; Technical University of Munich; University of Toronto; Vector Institute for Artificial IntelligenceThe authors propose an active perception framework for high-DoF mobile manipulators that uses an object- and task-aware probabilistic next-best-view formulation integrated into hierarchical-task model predictive control to inspect uncertain or safety-critical regions with the hand camera during navigation. The authors report that real-world tests and additional simulation and real-world sequences show improved map consistency, reduced task-dependent map entropy, lower navigation risk, and responsive behavior to semi-static scene changes compared to passive or non-task-aware baselines.Link
SP-HLM: Sparse-Pose-Driven Motion Generation and Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Loco-ManipulationMoore Threads; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; The University of Hong KongThe authors present SP-HLM, a sparse-pose-driven humanoid loco-manipulation framework that uses five task-critical body poses, a learned latent motion prior, and a pipeline of SP-VLA, SP-MGM, a pretrained motion encoder, and SP-GMT to bridge high-level task policies and whole-body control. The authors report that experiments in both simulation and on a real humanoid robot show improved motion tracking, whole-body coordination, and loco-manipulation success over prior methods.Link
Test-Time Motion Steering for Perceptive Humanoid Fall RecoveryUniversity of MichiganThe authors present RISE, a perceptive humanoid fall recovery approach that learns a recovery vocabulary and uses a visual diffusion planner to predict motion intent as future body states, enabling task objectives to steer how the robot returns to standing at inference time. The authors report that simulation experiments, complemented by real-world tests on a Unitree G1, demonstrate diverse recoveries, terrain adaptation, and test-time motion steering without retraining, guided by task costs such as heading, rise speed, or path.Link
CW-WAM: A Structured Contact-Wrench World Action Model for Force-Sensitive ManipulationAnt GroupThe authors introduce CW-WAM, a structured world action model that jointly predicts video, motion, and contact wrench using a unified interaction module, pair-causal attention over motion-wrench dependencies, and a long fixed-length wrench memory, with the predicted wrench chunk serving as a force reference in a slow-fast admittance-controlled system. The authors report that CW-WAM improves contact prediction, substantially increases insertion success rates for square pegs with 1-mm clearance and power adapters compared with WAM without wrench input, and improves lifting-height accuracy across loads. The authors further report that their framework with an admittance controller reduces force-reference RMSE for peg insertion and wiping and achieves smaller force fluctuations during successful execution.Link
