## Executive Summary

- Planning and computation: FLASH-CBF-RRT reportedly matched RRT-Connect's 100% success across 689 MotionBenchMaker problems while reducing median planning time by 3.82x. CusADi2 reportedly lowered GPU evaluation latency versus all tested baselines at batch sizes of 64 to 4096, but only matched them at larger batch sizes. ([arXiv:2610.24149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24149); [arXiv:2610.25476](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25476))
- Long-horizon manipulation: DreamCan reports 91.2% success on LIBERO-Long using sparse subgoals from action-free videos and a 0.1B-parameter low-level policy, plus real-world bimanual demonstrations. FIBER reports 42.0% mean simulation success across 128 variations per task, versus 11.6% for projected latent replay and 3.5% for kinematic planning; its hardware evidence consists of demonstrations. ([arXiv:2610.24981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24981); [arXiv:2610.24675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24675))
- Hardware demonstrations show distinct capabilities, with different evaluation scopes: a quadruped completed three autonomous Rubik's Cube solves using only its feet, each taking about 30 steps over 8-9 minutes. CLARO reports 88.8% robot-arm retrieval success in clutter, while DexOAK reports 46/56 successful open-loop hardware replays across five tasks. ([arXiv:2610.24610](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24610); [arXiv:2610.24016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24016); [arXiv:2610.26762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26762))
- Adaptation and deployment: PIA reports reliable few-shot autonomous-racing adaptation with less than one hour of on-vehicle interaction. ChairNav reports increasing mean distance between user interventions from 29.2 m to 57.4 m after post-training and autonomous operation for 93.4% of total driving time on kilometer-scale urban sidewalk routes. ([arXiv:2610.26757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26757); [arXiv:2610.24012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24012))
- Evaluation exposes important limitations: on six ContactBench tasks, evaluated vision-only policies struggled with contact events, and visual-tactile policies did not meaningfully improve performance. ForagerSim's learned policies fell short of humans in larger or topologically complex environments under simplified 2D sensing. ACTS showed contact-consistent tactile predictions in inspected robot rollouts, but mixed visual transfer, sometimes worse than frame copying. ([arXiv:2610.24512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24512); [arXiv:2610.26395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26395); [arXiv:2610.24285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24285))
- Workforce implications remain conditional: the robotics-exposure study reports higher exposure in physically intensive occupations and those with lower wages and education requirements. Its authors explicitly characterize these measures as an upper bound on potential exposure—not evidence of deployment readiness, cost competitiveness, or adoption. ([arXiv:2610.24267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24267))
