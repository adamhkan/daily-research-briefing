Submission date covered: 2026-11-05

Executive Summary

  • Several works advance humanoid autonomy and loco-manipulation: Ctrl-F introduces a hierarchical motion–force framework that augments humanoid motion tracking with explicit 3D force commands, enabling large, sustained interaction forces and online load adaptation; in simulation and on real hardware it achieves hand- and foot-based dragging, pushing, lifting, and kicking of boxes up to 20 kg where a force-blind tracker fails. HALO generates large-scale paired vision–language–motion data in simulation across ~1,000 procedurally generated homes to train perceptive humanoid policies that compose skills like navigation, crouching, and box pushing, and transfers zero-shot to physical humanoids with 91% success over 360 trials in six unmapped homes. Soccerbot uses a hierarchical RL framework with a latent motion prior distilled from human soccer motion capture to learn a single skill-conditioned policy for moving, dribbling, trapping, and kicking; policies transfer zero-shot to a Unitree G1, exhibiting agile, human-like soccer skills in the real world. (arXiv:2611.05507; arXiv:2611.06908; arXiv:2611.06564)
  • Data-driven and reinforcement learning methods are used to enhance control of complex robots and policies: an aSSM-based MPC framework learns an adiabatic spectral submanifold model for a soft trunk robot and achieves full-body dynamic shape regulation on hardware with ~10× accuracy over linear baselines, enabling section-wise shaping and 25+ s human-in-the-loop tennis-ball balancing where linear models and a 12 h-trained model-free RL policy fail. Another study shows that generalist robot foundation models can be improved online over diverse tasks via a carefully chosen reinforcement learning recipe, with value-based RL and training-task distribution identified as key to cross-task generalization, yielding gains on both trained and unseen tasks. A method that learns object-centric motion priors from human–object interaction data uses predicted future object states as a task-agnostic RL reward for dexterous manipulation, outperforming prior methods in success rate and generalization to novel objects across three simulated and real tasks and transferring skills across different robotic hands. A separate investigation of self-repair in code-as-policy agents for simulated manipulation finds that template-based execution feedback for Qwen2.5-Coder-7B recovers 85.3% of 252 failures within five retries versus 68.3% for resampling, with similar trends under fixed token budgets, and shows that mismatched templates can harm performance, motivating richer baselines for robot self-repair evaluations. (arXiv:2611.06625; arXiv:2611.06905; arXiv:2611.05979; arXiv:2611.06863)
  • New benchmarks and evaluation frameworks target agentic and multimodal robotic systems: Variational Automation is proposed as a setting where robots must handle known variations in object shape and pose, and VABAR is introduced as a simulation-based benchmark with six standardized manipulation classes, difficulty tiers, metrics, and 33 skill primitives with human-authored reference solutions; baseline experiments using a Graph-as-Policy manipulation harness and Isaac Lab Arena illustrate systematic evaluation of agentic systems. For the RoboMME benchmark, a three-tier architecture combines GPT-6 Astra planning over grounded subtasks, a π₀.₅ vision-language-action policy for execution, and a Qwen3-VL-4B monitor for subtask completion detection; this design reuses high-level instructions until completion signals and achieves 79.13% episode success with 3.63 Astra calls and 80.84 s per episode, surpassing the best non-oracle baseline by 34.62 percentage points and approaching a GroundSG+Oracle upper bound within 4.95 points. A controlled LeRobot-based real-robot study with visuo-tactile sensing factorizes tactile input into RGB, normal/depth, and marker-flow cues and shows that adding tactile perception raises overall success from 57.8% to 78.9%, with marker flow alone reaching 74.4% and cue-factorized encoders consistently outperforming non-factorized ones; cross-attention emerges as a lightweight yet competitive visuo-tactile fusion method. (arXiv:2611.06881; arXiv:2611.06184; arXiv:2611.06673)
  • Several motion planning and trajectory-generation advances improve performance for rigid, humanoid, and continuum systems: a contact-implicit whole-body trajectory optimization framework plans dynamic humanoid motions over smooth, uneven terrains (e.g., steps, ramps) without predefined contact sequences by modeling full dynamics and novel rigid contact constraints, with feasibility shown in simulation and on the ErgoCub humanoid. GRASP introduces gradient-informed motion retargeting without differentiable simulators by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts; GRASP-TO augments tempered SMC with these gradients, GRASP-MPC scales to long demonstrations, and experiments report 92.8% success over 1,352 bimanual OakInk2 demos and improved PPO tracking for dexterous and humanoid tasks, including successful real-hardware dexterous and whole-body motions. A trajectory generator is proposed that yields C^∞-smooth trajectories respecting kinematic limits and ensuring continuity of all derivatives (velocity through higher orders), covering null boundary conditions and addressing the higher smoothness needs of soft continuum robots; simulations and hardware experiments confirm its suitability for soft-robot motion planning. A unified planning and control framework for mobile continuum robots couples a global A* plus minimum-jerk path planner with a local geometry-consistent IK and Riemannian optimization layer, improving motion consistency, feasibility, and adaptability over multiple baselines in simulated pipe traversal and real-world navigation/manipulation in clutter. cuRoboV2-Fast performs GPU-accelerated B-spline trajectory optimization with differentiable inverse dynamics and a TimeOpt procedure using banded Newton solves, achieving 4.4 ms mean planning time on an RTX 5090 (14.7× faster than cuRoboV2), surpassing prior GPU–CPU combinations even on a Jetson AGX Orin, and reducing a YAM pick-and-drop cycle time by 34.6% to 5.93 s. (arXiv:2611.05585; arXiv:2611.05754; arXiv:2611.05615; arXiv:2611.06214; arXiv:2611.06913)
  • New sensing and assistance systems target physical interaction and navigation under uncertainty: OUCH, a wrist-worn pneumatic device with stacked pouch actuators and a rigid indenter isolated by a buckling spacer, delivers controlled noxious mechanical stimuli for neurological assessment; characterization shows ~18,500 kPa estimated contact pressure at 200 kPa inflation (over 25× a commercial pressure algometer tip) while requiring less total force and localizing deformation near the surface, and preliminary tests indicate similar discomfort at ~89% lower applied force than the algometer. A planner-conditioned air–ground assistance framework for off-road navigation triggers UAV sensing only when a UGV’s MPPI rollouts degrade due to map uncertainty; by scoring rollouts against hypothesized resolved maps and requesting only the most beneficial aerial measurements, it achieves 98.9% goal completion over 180 simulations while cutting mean UAV flight time by 87.7% versus continuous greedy mapping, with field trials over ~2 km showing recovery of successful navigation in real terrain. (arXiv:2611.05741; arXiv:2611.06702)

Ctrl-F: Force as an Interface for Humanoid Loco-ManipulationUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes Ctrl-F, a hierarchical motion-force framework that augments humanoid motion tracking with explicit 3D force commands at designated contact bodies, combining a unified motion-force controller with a high-level online force planner based on object state and proprioception. In both simulation and hardware experiments, Ctrl-F enables loco-manipulation behaviors requiring large, sustained interaction forces and online adjustment to changing loads that are not achievable by a force-blind motion tracker. On real humanoid hardware, Ctrl-F successfully executes hand-based dragging, pushing, and lifting, and foot-based kicking, including manipulating boxes up to 20 kg in pushing and kicking tasks where a force-blind baseline fails, while adapting online to additional mid-task loads.Link
Dynamic Shape Control of Soft Trunk RobotsETH ZurichThe paper extends adiabatic spectral submanifold (aSSM) methods by learning an aSSM model of the full body of a trimmed-helicoid soft trunk robot from data and using it within a model predictive control scheme for dynamic full-body shape control. On hardware, the aSSM-based MPC achieves closed-loop dynamic shape regulation with accuracy outperforming available linear baselines by a factor of 10 and successfully completes section-by-section shaping tasks. In human-in-the-loop demonstrations, the proposed controller balances a tennis ball on the soft trunk for over 25 seconds, while linear baselines and a model-free reinforcement learning policy trained for 12 hours on hardware fail on the same balancing task.Link
VABAR: A Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic RoboticsUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces Variational Automation (VA) as a setting where robots must reliably perform specified tasks under known variations in object shape and pose, and proposes benchmark design criteria tailored to agentic robotics, including accessibility, repeatability, diversity, relevance, physical realizability, resistance to saturation, and computational efficiency. It presents VABAR, an open, simulation-based Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic Robotics, comprising six manipulation task classes (tool sorting, tool hanging, gear meshing, syringe packing, cable insertion, and cable routing) with standardized variations, difficulty tiers, success metrics, and 33 skill primitives with human-authored reference solutions. The authors provide initial baseline evaluations on VABAR using an open agentic manipulation harness (Graph-as-Policy, GaP) and Isaac Lab Arena, demonstrating how the benchmark can be used to systematically evaluate agentic robotics systems.Link
Rethinking Self-Repair in Code-as-Policy Agents: What Drives Retry Success?Stanford UniversityThe paper studies self-repair in code-as-policy agents for simulated robot manipulation by comparing failure-driven template feedback against simple resampling and static task guidance using a frozen code model and a fixed feedback-template lookup keyed by failure labels and API variant. On 252 identical initial failures, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B with template-based execution feedback recovers 85.3% of failures within five retries, versus 68.3% for resampling under the same retry cap, and this advantage persists under fixed total-token budgets. Additional experiments show that feedback from other episodes and fixed guided prompts can yield recovery similar to template-based feedback, that coarse matching of failure labels provides no detectable benefit, and that mismatched template instructions can reduce performance (e.g., on a rim-grasp nut-insertion task), motivating inclusion of resampling, mismatched feedback, and concise static guidance as baselines in robot self-repair evaluations.Link
Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy ImprovementCarnegie Mellon University; ETH Zurich; UC BerkeleyThe paper investigates whether generalist robot foundation models can be improved online across many diverse tasks using reinforcement learning, and develops structured evaluations to study design choices in value estimation, policy extraction, and resource allocation for multi-task deployment adaptation. Empirical results show that generalist policies can be improved online across diverse tasks via an appropriate reinforcement learning recipe, leading to increased performance not only on training tasks but also on tasks not included in online training. The study finds that both the choice of training task distribution and the use of value-based reinforcement learning are critical for achieving cross-task generalization during online improvement of generalist robot policies.Link
Can Astra Solve RoboMME without Breaking the Bank?Stanford UniversityThe paper presents a three-tier system for the RoboMME benchmark in which GPT-6 Astra (System 2) selects grounded subtasks from stored visual history, a π₀.₅ vision-language-action policy (System 1) executes actions, and a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B monitor (System 1.5) detects subtask completion after each action chunk. By reusing the same high-level instruction until the monitor signals completion or a task-specific event occurs, the system reduces calls to the large planner while allowing the low-level policy to act on new observations. On RoboMME, the proposed system achieves a 79.13% episode success rate with an average of 3.63 Astra calls and 80.84 seconds per episode, outperforming the best published non-oracle baseline by 34.62 percentage points and trailing the GroundSG+Oracle upper bound by 4.95 percentage points.Link
OUCH: Objective, Uniform, Controlled Haptic Stimulation for Automated Neurological AssessmentStanford UniversityThe paper introduces OUCH, a wrist-worn pneumatic device combining stacked pouch actuators, a rigid indenter, and a compliant spacer that buckles at a set load to deliver controlled, repeatable noxious mechanical stimuli for neurological assessment. Characterization via blocked force tests, clinician evaluation, finite element analysis, and a deformable pressure testbed shows that at 200 kPa inflation, the OUCH indenter achieves an estimated contact pressure of 18,500 kPa—over 25 times that of a commercial pressure algometer tip—while requiring less total force and localizing deformation near the surface with comparable deformation at depth. Preliminary perceptual testing indicates that OUCH elicits discomfort at approximately 89% lower applied force than the algometer, demonstrating the feasibility of evoking noxious sensations with a compact, wearable soft actuator for objective neurological assessment.Link

Contact-Implicit Whole-Body Trajectory Optimization for Dynamic Humanoid Locomotion on Parameterizable Uneven TerrainIstituto Italiano di Tecnologia; University of Manchester; University of PisaThe paper proposes a contact-implicit whole-body trajectory optimization framework for humanoid robots that plans dynamic motions on complex, parameterizable uneven terrains without predefined contact sequences, modeling full robot dynamics and kinematics. It introduces a mathematical model that approximates ground geometry as a smooth surface and formulates novel rigid contact constraints, extending contact-implicit planning from flat, horizontal terrain to arbitrary smooth surfaces such as steps and ramps. Using nonlinear programming, the approach generates dynamic bipedal locomotion trajectories on various non-coplanar terrains, with preliminary feasibility demonstrated in both simulation and experiments on the ErgoCub humanoid.Link
$\mathcal{C}^\infty$ Smooth Trajectory Planning with Application to Soft RobotsMohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial IntelligenceThe paper introduces a trajectory generator that produces trajectories that are $ mathcal{C}^{ ackslash infty}$-smooth while respecting specified kinematic limits, ensuring continuity of velocity, acceleration, jerk, snap, and all higher derivatives between sequential states. The proposed generator is designed to satisfy potentially higher smoothness requirements of soft continuum robots than those typically used for rigid systems (e.g., $ mathcal{C}^4$ for collaborative robots and $ mathcal{C}^5$ for quadcopters), including the special case of null boundary conditions where all derivatives vanish at trajectory start and end. The approach is verified and evaluated in both simulations and hardware experiments, demonstrating its applicability to motion planning for soft robots.Link
GRASP: Gradient-Guided Sampling for Contact-Rich Physics-Based Motion RetargetingHarvard UniversityThe paper proposes GRASP (Gradient Recovery from Archived Samples for Proposals), a physics-based motion retargeting method that combines sampling-based exploration with gradient-guided refinement by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts, enabling gradient-informed Markov chain Monte Carlo rejuvenation without requiring a differentiable simulator. GRASP-TO extends tempered sequential Monte Carlo with these locally fitted gradients, while GRASP-MPC scales the optimization to long demonstrations via warm-started receding-horizon planning, with Metropolis-Hastings correction preserving the target distribution for fixed proposal kernels. Experiments on dexterous hand and humanoid systems show accurate retargeting under a shared tracking objective without task-specific contact rewards, including 92.8% success on 1,352 bimanual OakInk2 demonstrations, improved PPO tracking for pen spinning and humanoid-object interaction, and successful dexterous manipulation and whole-body humanoid motion on real hardware.Link
Learning Object-Centric Motion Priors from Human for Robotic Dexterous ManipulationZhejiang UniversityThe paper proposes a method that learns object-centric motion priors from Human-Object Interaction datasets by holistically predicting future hand-object states, and uses the predicted future object states as a general-purpose reward term for reinforcement learning to reduce task-specific reward engineering and improve task generalization in dexterous manipulation. Across three manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world, the proposed approach outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in success rate and generalizability to novel objects. The method is shown to be cross-embodiment compatible by successfully deploying the learned skills on different robotic hands.Link
Riemannian Optimization-based Whole-body Obstacle Avoidance for Mobile Continuum Robots in Cluttered EnvironmentsBeijing Institute of Technology; Delft University of Technology; Hong Kong University of Science and Technology; Imperial College London; Meijo University; Nagoya University; University of Hong KongThe paper proposes a unified planning and control framework for mobile continuum robots in cluttered and confined environments that combines a global A*-plus-minimum-jerk trajectory planner with a local layer using a geometry-consistent inverse kinematics solver and Riemannian optimization to coordinate mobile base motion and continuum deformation under kinematic and environmental constraints. In simulated pipe-traversal tasks and real-world experiments involving navigation among dense obstacles and manipulation in confined spaces, the framework yields improved motion consistency, feasibility, and adaptability compared with multiple baseline planners, supporting coordinated whole-body motion for mobile continuum robots.Link
Playing Soccer Like Humans Do: Learning Versatile and Agile Humanoid Soccer via Latent Motion PriorsPeking University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; State Key Laboratory of General Artificial Intelligence, BIGAI; University of Science and Technology of ChinaThe paper introduces soccerbot, a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that uses a structured latent motion prior distilled from large-scale human soccer motion-capture data to constrain policy learning within a dynamically plausible motion manifold, enabling a single skill-conditioned policy for moving, dribbling, trapping, and kicking with human-like motion and smooth transitions. Through robustness-oriented simulation design, policies trained with soccerbot transfer zero-shot to the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, where the system demonstrates agile, natural, and robust execution of the full soccer skill repertoire under real-world conditions.Link
Which Physical Tactile Cues Matter for Contact-Rich Imitation Learning? A Controlled Real-Robot StudyUnknown affiliationThe paper conducts a controlled real-robot study using the LeRobot platform and a visuo-tactile perception system to factorize a single vision-based tactile stream into RGB images, normal/depth maps, and marker-flow fields, and compares policies built from these individual and combined tactile cues, tactile encoders, and visuo-tactile fusion strategies under a shared policy backbone and demonstrations. Across 720 real-robot trials, adding tactile perception improves overall task success rate from 57.8% to 78.9%, with marker flow identified as the most effective single cue (74.4% success) and particularly important for regulating established contact. The study finds that cue-factorized encoding consistently improves performance over non-factorized approaches and that, while the benefit of tactile fusion is task-dependent, cross-attention provides a lightweight fusion mechanism with performance comparable to pretrained tactile encoders.Link
When to Ask for Help: Uncertainty-Triggered Aerial Refinement for Off-Road NavigationUnknown affiliationThe paper presents a planner-conditioned air-ground assistance framework for off-road navigation in which a UAV is requested only when the UGV’s MPPI rollouts are degraded by map uncertainty; the method scores rollouts under hypothesized resolved map layers, selects the map information that most improves predicted navigation, requests the corresponding aerial measurement, fuses it, and replans. In 180 simulation trials over three off-road routes, the method attains 98.9% goal completion while reducing mean UAV flight time by 87.7% relative to continuous greedy aerial mapping, with each second of estimated UAV flight time saving 2.94 seconds of UGV navigation time when aggregated across routes. Field experiments over approximately 2 km of UGV operation show that the approach can resolve environmental uncertainty and recover successful navigation in real-world off-road settings.Link
Perceptive Humanoid Motion from SimulationAppleThe paper introduces HALO, a humanoid autonomy system that synthesizes paired vision-language-motion data at scale in simulation across about a thousand procedurally generated homes, enabling training of perceptive humanoid control policies without human-operated data collection. Using this synthetic corpus, HALO learns a repertoire of physical skills (e.g., sitting on chairs, navigating to named objects, sidestepping through half-open doors, crouching and crawling under tables, and pushing boxes) and can orchestrate them to solve composite, long-horizon tasks. Without training on real homes, HALO transfers zero-shot to physical humanoid hardware and achieves a 91% success rate over 360 trials in six unmapped real-world environments.Link
cuRoboV2-Fast: Fast Torque-Limited Collision-Free Motion PlanningUnknown affiliationThe paper presents cuRoboV2-Fast, a GPU-accelerated motion planner that performs B-spline trajectory optimization together with accelerated robot computations and differentiable inverse dynamics, and then applies a TimeOpt procedure to optimize traversal speed along a fixed spline path under sampled velocity, acceleration, jerk, and torque constraints using efficient banded Newton solves on the GPU. On an RTX 5090 with a 3 kg payload, cuRoboV2-Fast achieves a mean planning time of 4.4 ms, which is 14.7 times faster than cuRoboV2, and on a Jetson AGX Orin it plans faster than the combination of cuRoboV1 and TOPPRA running on an RTX 5090. In a stereo-camera-based deployment on the YAM system, cuRoboV2-Fast yields a mean completed pick-and-drop cycle time of 5.93 s, representing a 34.6% reduction compared with cuRoboV2.Link
