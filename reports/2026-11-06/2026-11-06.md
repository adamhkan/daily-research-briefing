## Executive Summary

- Several works advance humanoid autonomy and loco-manipulation: Ctrl-F introduces a hierarchical motion–force framework that augments humanoid motion tracking with explicit 3D force commands, enabling large, sustained interaction forces and online load adaptation; in simulation and on real hardware it achieves hand- and foot-based dragging, pushing, lifting, and kicking of boxes up to 20 kg where a force-blind tracker fails. HALO generates large-scale paired vision–language–motion data in simulation across ~1,000 procedurally generated homes to train perceptive humanoid policies that compose skills like navigation, crouching, and box pushing, and transfers zero-shot to physical humanoids with 91% success over 360 trials in six unmapped homes. Soccerbot uses a hierarchical RL framework with a latent motion prior distilled from human soccer motion capture to learn a single skill-conditioned policy for moving, dribbling, trapping, and kicking; policies transfer zero-shot to a Unitree G1, exhibiting agile, human-like soccer skills in the real world.  ([arXiv:2611.05507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05507); [arXiv:2611.06908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06908); [arXiv:2611.06564](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06564))
- Data-driven and reinforcement learning methods are used to enhance control of complex robots and policies: an aSSM-based MPC framework learns an adiabatic spectral submanifold model for a soft trunk robot and achieves full-body dynamic shape regulation on hardware with ~10× accuracy over linear baselines, enabling section-wise shaping and 25+ s human-in-the-loop tennis-ball balancing where linear models and a 12 h-trained model-free RL policy fail. Another study shows that generalist robot foundation models can be improved online over diverse tasks via a carefully chosen reinforcement learning recipe, with value-based RL and training-task distribution identified as key to cross-task generalization, yielding gains on both trained and unseen tasks. A method that learns object-centric motion priors from human–object interaction data uses predicted future object states as a task-agnostic RL reward for dexterous manipulation, outperforming prior methods in success rate and generalization to novel objects across three simulated and real tasks and transferring skills across different robotic hands. A separate investigation of self-repair in code-as-policy agents for simulated manipulation finds that template-based execution feedback for Qwen2.5-Coder-7B recovers 85.3% of 252 failures within five retries versus 68.3% for resampling, with similar trends under fixed token budgets, and shows that mismatched templates can harm performance, motivating richer baselines for robot self-repair evaluations.  ([arXiv:2611.06625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06625); [arXiv:2611.06905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06905); [arXiv:2611.05979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05979); [arXiv:2611.06863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06863))
- New benchmarks and evaluation frameworks target agentic and multimodal robotic systems: Variational Automation is proposed as a setting where robots must handle known variations in object shape and pose, and VABAR is introduced as a simulation-based benchmark with six standardized manipulation classes, difficulty tiers, metrics, and 33 skill primitives with human-authored reference solutions; baseline experiments using a Graph-as-Policy manipulation harness and Isaac Lab Arena illustrate systematic evaluation of agentic systems. For the RoboMME benchmark, a three-tier architecture combines GPT-6 Astra planning over grounded subtasks, a π₀.₅ vision-language-action policy for execution, and a Qwen3-VL-4B monitor for subtask completion detection; this design reuses high-level instructions until completion signals and achieves 79.13% episode success with 3.63 Astra calls and 80.84 s per episode, surpassing the best non-oracle baseline by 34.62 percentage points and approaching a GroundSG+Oracle upper bound within 4.95 points. A controlled LeRobot-based real-robot study with visuo-tactile sensing factorizes tactile input into RGB, normal/depth, and marker-flow cues and shows that adding tactile perception raises overall success from 57.8% to 78.9%, with marker flow alone reaching 74.4% and cue-factorized encoders consistently outperforming non-factorized ones; cross-attention emerges as a lightweight yet competitive visuo-tactile fusion method.  ([arXiv:2611.06881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06881); [arXiv:2611.06184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06184); [arXiv:2611.06673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06673))
- Several motion planning and trajectory-generation advances improve performance for rigid, humanoid, and continuum systems: a contact-implicit whole-body trajectory optimization framework plans dynamic humanoid motions over smooth, uneven terrains (e.g., steps, ramps) without predefined contact sequences by modeling full dynamics and novel rigid contact constraints, with feasibility shown in simulation and on the ErgoCub humanoid. GRASP introduces gradient-informed motion retargeting without differentiable simulators by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts; GRASP-TO augments tempered SMC with these gradients, GRASP-MPC scales to long demonstrations, and experiments report 92.8% success over 1,352 bimanual OakInk2 demos and improved PPO tracking for dexterous and humanoid tasks, including successful real-hardware dexterous and whole-body motions. A trajectory generator is proposed that yields C^∞-smooth trajectories respecting kinematic limits and ensuring continuity of all derivatives (velocity through higher orders), covering null boundary conditions and addressing the higher smoothness needs of soft continuum robots; simulations and hardware experiments confirm its suitability for soft-robot motion planning. A unified planning and control framework for mobile continuum robots couples a global A* plus minimum-jerk path planner with a local geometry-consistent IK and Riemannian optimization layer, improving motion consistency, feasibility, and adaptability over multiple baselines in simulated pipe traversal and real-world navigation/manipulation in clutter. cuRoboV2-Fast performs GPU-accelerated B-spline trajectory optimization with differentiable inverse dynamics and a TimeOpt procedure using banded Newton solves, achieving 4.4 ms mean planning time on an RTX 5090 (14.7× faster than cuRoboV2), surpassing prior GPU–CPU combinations even on a Jetson AGX Orin, and reducing a YAM pick-and-drop cycle time by 34.6% to 5.93 s.  ([arXiv:2611.05585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05585); [arXiv:2611.05754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05754); [arXiv:2611.05615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05615); [arXiv:2611.06214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06214); [arXiv:2611.06913](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06913))
- New sensing and assistance systems target physical interaction and navigation under uncertainty: OUCH, a wrist-worn pneumatic device with stacked pouch actuators and a rigid indenter isolated by a buckling spacer, delivers controlled noxious mechanical stimuli for neurological assessment; characterization shows ~18,500 kPa estimated contact pressure at 200 kPa inflation (over 25× a commercial pressure algometer tip) while requiring less total force and localizing deformation near the surface, and preliminary tests indicate similar discomfort at ~89% lower applied force than the algometer. A planner-conditioned air–ground assistance framework for off-road navigation triggers UAV sensing only when a UGV’s MPPI rollouts degrade due to map uncertainty; by scoring rollouts against hypothesized resolved maps and requesting only the most beneficial aerial measurements, it achieves 98.9% goal completion over 180 simulations while cutting mean UAV flight time by 87.7% versus continuous greedy mapping, with field trials over ~2 km showing recovery of successful navigation in real terrain.  ([arXiv:2611.05741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05741); [arXiv:2611.06702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06702))

| Ctrl-F: Force as an Interface for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation | UC Berkeley | The paper proposes Ctrl-F, a hierarchical motion-force framework that augments humanoid motion tracking with explicit 3D force commands at designated contact bodies, combining a unified motion-force controller with a high-level online force planner based on object state and proprioception. In both simulation and hardware experiments, Ctrl-F enables loco-manipulation behaviors requiring large, sustained interaction forces and online adjustment to changing loads that are not achievable by a force-blind motion tracker. On real humanoid hardware, Ctrl-F successfully executes hand-based dragging, pushing, and lifting, and foot-based kicking, including manipulating boxes up to 20 kg in pushing and kicking tasks where a force-blind baseline fails, while adapting online to additional mid-task loads. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05507 |
| Dynamic Shape Control of Soft Trunk Robots | ETH Zurich | The paper extends adiabatic spectral submanifold (aSSM) methods by learning an aSSM model of the full body of a trimmed-helicoid soft trunk robot from data and using it within a model predictive control scheme for dynamic full-body shape control. On hardware, the aSSM-based MPC achieves closed-loop dynamic shape regulation with accuracy outperforming available linear baselines by a factor of 10 and successfully completes section-by-section shaping tasks. In human-in-the-loop demonstrations, the proposed controller balances a tennis ball on the soft trunk for over 25 seconds, while linear baselines and a model-free reinforcement learning policy trained for 12 hours on hardware fail on the same balancing task. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06625 |
| VABAR: A Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic Robotics | UC Berkeley | The paper introduces Variational Automation (VA) as a setting where robots must reliably perform specified tasks under known variations in object shape and pose, and proposes benchmark design criteria tailored to agentic robotics, including accessibility, repeatability, diversity, relevance, physical realizability, resistance to saturation, and computational efficiency. It presents VABAR, an open, simulation-based Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic Robotics, comprising six manipulation task classes (tool sorting, tool hanging, gear meshing, syringe packing, cable insertion, and cable routing) with standardized variations, difficulty tiers, success metrics, and 33 skill primitives with human-authored reference solutions. The authors provide initial baseline evaluations on VABAR using an open agentic manipulation harness (Graph-as-Policy, GaP) and Isaac Lab Arena, demonstrating how the benchmark can be used to systematically evaluate agentic robotics systems. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06881 |
| Rethinking Self-Repair in Code-as-Policy Agents: What Drives Retry Success? | Stanford University | The paper studies self-repair in code-as-policy agents for simulated robot manipulation by comparing failure-driven template feedback against simple resampling and static task guidance using a frozen code model and a fixed feedback-template lookup keyed by failure labels and API variant. On 252 identical initial failures, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B with template-based execution feedback recovers 85.3% of failures within five retries, versus 68.3% for resampling under the same retry cap, and this advantage persists under fixed total-token budgets. Additional experiments show that feedback from other episodes and fixed guided prompts can yield recovery similar to template-based feedback, that coarse matching of failure labels provides no detectable benefit, and that mismatched template instructions can reduce performance (e.g., on a rim-grasp nut-insertion task), motivating inclusion of resampling, mismatched feedback, and concise static guidance as baselines in robot self-repair evaluations. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06863 |
| Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement | Carnegie Mellon University; ETH Zurich; UC Berkeley | The paper investigates whether generalist robot foundation models can be improved online across many diverse tasks using reinforcement learning, and develops structured evaluations to study design choices in value estimation, policy extraction, and resource allocation for multi-task deployment adaptation. Empirical results show that generalist policies can be improved online across diverse tasks via an appropriate reinforcement learning recipe, leading to increased performance not only on training tasks but also on tasks not included in online training. The study finds that both the choice of training task distribution and the use of value-based reinforcement learning are critical for achieving cross-task generalization during online improvement of generalist robot policies. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06905 |
| Can Astra Solve RoboMME without Breaking the Bank? | Stanford University | The paper presents a three-tier system for the RoboMME benchmark in which GPT-6 Astra (System 2) selects grounded subtasks from stored visual history, a π₀.₅ vision-language-action policy (System 1) executes actions, and a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B monitor (System 1.5) detects subtask completion after each action chunk. By reusing the same high-level instruction until the monitor signals completion or a task-specific event occurs, the system reduces calls to the large planner while allowing the low-level policy to act on new observations. On RoboMME, the proposed system achieves a 79.13% episode success rate with an average of 3.63 Astra calls and 80.84 seconds per episode, outperforming the best published non-oracle baseline by 34.62 percentage points and trailing the GroundSG+Oracle upper bound by 4.95 percentage points. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06184 |
| OUCH: Objective, Uniform, Controlled Haptic Stimulation for Automated Neurological Assessment | Stanford University | The paper introduces OUCH, a wrist-worn pneumatic device combining stacked pouch actuators, a rigid indenter, and a compliant spacer that buckles at a set load to deliver controlled, repeatable noxious mechanical stimuli for neurological assessment. Characterization via blocked force tests, clinician evaluation, finite element analysis, and a deformable pressure testbed shows that at 200 kPa inflation, the OUCH indenter achieves an estimated contact pressure of 18,500 kPa—over 25 times that of a commercial pressure algometer tip—while requiring less total force and localizing deformation near the surface with comparable deformation at depth. Preliminary perceptual testing indicates that OUCH elicits discomfort at approximately 89% lower applied force than the algometer, demonstrating the feasibility of evoking noxious sensations with a compact, wearable soft actuator for objective neurological assessment. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05741 |
