Submission date covered: 2026-11-18

Executive Summary

  • Several works advance bimanual and dexterous manipulation: ALOHA Lightning uses kinesthetic teaching plus a test-time pipeline with action smoothing and visual masking to execute near-human-speed bimanual behaviors, achieving over 80% success on diverse tasks such as ball tossing/catching, folding shorts, and bussing tables from only 50 demos per task; PriDex converts human-video-derived object and wrist motion priors into robot-specific bimanual dexterous demonstrations in simulation via reinforcement learning that adaptively balances object- and wrist-level guidance, then uses the refined policies to generate data that train a vision-based diffusion policy for tasks like pouring, sweeping, and unscrewing caps; HandUMI provides a modular, robot-agnostic, hand-worn interface for capturing bimanual demonstrations with VR tracking and aperture encoders, retargeting them with ≤1.2 mm median error to seven simulated bimanual arms and enabling real PiPER-arm towel-folding policies where ACT trained on 562 HandUMI demos succeeds in 12/20 trials versus 0/20 when trained on only the first 101 demos. (arXiv:2611.21919; arXiv:2611.21908; arXiv:2611.22146)
  • New policy and world-model formulations target better control and sample efficiency: a task-centric inference-time refinement framework for Euclidean and pose-valued Riemannian Flow Matching policies uses parallel physics rollouts, task-performance funnels, Lipschitz bounds, and CMA-ES constrained to radial quantile bands to select action chunks that guarantee net task progress, boosting success on PushT from 31.3% to 88.5% and on three-cube stacking from 77.0% to 86.0% while cutting out-of-distribution motion windows from 34.1% to 11.3%; Progress Predicts Policy (PPP) instead learns a language-conditioned scalar progress field composed with a shared world model, reading out actions as gradients at a fixed command and training via imitation plus double backpropagation to shape local control, outperforming behavioral cloning baselines with the same architecture in simulated and real manipulation; Anchored Path Matching (APM) distills World Action Models in a few steps by supervising endpoints and short transitions from student-visited states using teacher rollouts and anchor-priority updates, yielding a single checkpoint that supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step video–action generation, reduces real-robot control latency by 21.3× versus an eight-step teacher while improving task success over a one-step teacher, and increases RoboTwin 2.0 PSNR from 30.169 to 32.317 dB with modest success gains in both clean and domain-randomized settings. (arXiv:2611.22609; arXiv:2611.22491; arXiv:2611.22592)
  • CoBoVLA, a 250M-parameter vision-language-action model with discrete, interpretable step and action codes that decouple language understanding, planning, and control, achieves 91.4% success on LIBERO-Goal (vs. 82.8% for SmolVLA under a matched protocol) with 17 ms control latency and 81.1% average success on LIBERO-Para across 4,092 unseen paraphrases, far surpassing a SmolVLA LoRA baseline (78.5% vs. 36.5% on matched episodes), while ablations indicate language encoding dominates residual failures (91% success when instructions are correctly encoded versus 27.2% otherwise) and the model can complete 48.8% of 160 free-form two-step tasks without two-step demos. (arXiv:2611.22395)
  • Weight-space programming is explored for low-level control: large language models (GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1/5.2) directly read and edit the weights of a 253-parameter neural controller for tractor path following based on natural-language specifications and limited simulated evaluations, repairing a controller trained on a different tractor and synthesizing a functioning controller from zero weights in simulation where an evolution strategy from zero fails, and on a real tractor these LLM-programmed controllers follow line-and-arc paths autonomously with tracking errors on the same order as a pure-pursuit baseline, while gradient descent attains higher accuracy but requires substantially more simulation. (arXiv:2611.21958)
  • Thermally and physically aware locomotion and humanoid motion control are advanced in several systems: a 13-node lumped RC thermal model for the Unitree Go2, fitted from two runs and calibrated against power, predicts all 12 motor temperatures with mean RMSE <1 °C across gaits, payloads, and robots, enabling a reinforcement-learning reward based on hottest predicted asymptotic temperatures that shifts real-hardware performance from temperature-limited to battery-limited (e.g., 5 kg payload at 1 m/s increases runtime from 19 to 174 minutes and distance from 1.1 to 10.0 km with a 14 °C safety margin); building on related ideas, an autonomous Unitree G1 running system combines vision-based lane perception, path tracking, and an RL running policy with a human-motion prior, plus head-camera stabilization, IMU-assisted leg odometry, and torque- plus temperature-based thermal management, and demonstrates fully autonomous completion of 100 m, 400 m, and 1500 m track events at the World Humanoid Robot Games 2026, placing 13th/74 (100 m), 10th/44 in the 400 m first round, and 7th/35 (1500 m). (arXiv:2611.22231; arXiv:2611.22445)
  • Navigation and whole-body motion in complex environments are improved via specialized memory and perception–control augmentation: EvoNav’s Experience-Evolving Memory organizes semantic observations, spatial relations, and feedback into sparse “experience atoms” that are consolidated and linked into environment-specific structural knowledge, guiding search in new navigation tasks and improving path efficiency over prior memory-enhanced methods across single-goal, multi-floor, multimodal, and real-robot settings; DGV-DLIVO uses LiDAR-degenerate directions to steer visual feature selection and subspace-selective fusion so visual constraints only correct motion where LiDAR is weak, maintaining continuous, directionally consistent estimates in challenging hydropower tunnels and achieving a 91.4% progress ratio on a 40 m tunnel where LIO/LIVO/VIO baselines suffer stagnation and drift; ALERT augments pretrained humanoid motion-tracking policies with localized collision-avoidance adaptations that preserve reference-motion quality and enable collision-aware whole-body tracking in shared human–robot settings; Heave-Ho combines multi-agent post-training of a humanoid foundation tracking model with a high-level vision-language planner and speech-based leader–follower coordination (no inter-robot networking) to produce decentralized, physically coupled loco-manipulation by 2–10 humanoids, consistently outperforming baselines on a new HH-Bench in simulation and real-world evaluations. (arXiv:2611.22321; arXiv:2611.22385; arXiv:2611.22258; arXiv:2611.22275)

ALOHA Lightning: Learning Fast and Precise ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes ALOHA Lightning, a bimanual manipulation system that uses kinesthetic teaching to collect near-human-speed demonstrations and a learning pipeline with test-time action smoothing and visual masking to enable smooth, high-speed policy execution. Using 50 demonstrations per task, ALOHA Lightning autonomously completes diverse bimanual manipulation tasks (e.g., ball tossing and catching, folding shorts, battery insertion, bussing tables) with over 80% success rates at speeds close to human execution.Link
From Human Videos to Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation Data: Confidence-Guided RL Refinement of Video PriorsUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces PriDex, a framework that converts human-video-derived priors into executable bimanual dexterous robot demonstrations in simulation by using reinforcement learning to refine object and wrist trajectory priors under robot-specific kinematic and physical constraints. PriDex adaptively balances object-level and wrist-level motion guidance based on confidence in object-motion estimates, preserving task-relevant interaction structure while regenerating embodiment-specific robot behaviors for tasks such as pouring, sweeping into a dustpan, bottle-cap unscrewing, and plate cleaning. The refined PriDex policies are used to generate simulated robot demonstrations that subsequently train a vision-based diffusion policy, which is evaluated on four bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in terms of both simulation-based data generation and downstream visuomotor policy learning.Link
Task Convergent Flow Matching for Generative RoboticsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes a task-centric inference-time refinement framework for flow-matching policies that uses parallel physics-based rollouts and task performance funnels, together with interval-wise Lipschitz bounds, to select action chunks that guarantee net progress toward task completion while allowing bounded temporary regressions. To handle contact-rich hybrid dynamics, the method refines source seeds of Euclidean and pose-valued Riemannian Flow Matching policies using CMA-ES constrained to radial quantile bands of the source distribution, aiming to retain the learned motion prior while improving task convergence. On PushT and three-cube stacking, the proposed refinement raises success from 31.3% to 88.5% and from 77.0% to 86.0%, respectively; on PushT it reduces the average maximum loss of achieved progress by 19% compared with endpoint-only constraints at similar success, and with the radial band reduces out-of-distribution motion windows to 11.3% versus 34.1% for unconstrained search, comparable to held-out demonstrations.Link
CoBoVLA: Decoupling Language from Action with Discrete Codes for Robust and Interpretable Robot ManipulationCarnegie Mellon University; UC San DiegoThe paper introduces CoBoVLA, a 250M-parameter vision-language-action model that decouples language understanding, planning, and control using learned discrete, interpretable step and action codes, enabling language-independent low-level control and explicit error localization to individual stages. CoBoVLA achieves 91.4% success on LIBERO-Goal (vs. 82.8% for SmolVLA under a matched protocol) with 17 ms control latency, and 81.1% average success on LIBERO-Para across 4,092 unseen paraphrases and 78.5% on matched episodes (vs. 36.5% for SmolVLA LoRA fine-tuned on paraphrases). In free-form two-step tasks without two-step demonstrations, CoBoVLA completes 48.8% of 160 tasks, and an ablation shows success of 91% when instructions are correctly encoded versus 27.2% otherwise, indicating that language understanding is the dominant source of residual failures.Link

Weight-space programming for tractor guidance controller: large language models natively write neural network weights that steer a real tractorAalto University; Munich Institute of Robotics and Machine Intelligence (MIRMI); Technical University of Munich (TUM)The paper studies weight-space programming ("Weight-Space Engineering"), where large language models read and directly edit the weights of a 253-parameter neural network controller for tractor path following from natural-language specifications and limited simulated evaluations, without gradient-based optimization. GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1/5.2 can repair a neural controller trained on a different tractor and synthesize a functioning controller from zero weights in simulation, while an evolution strategy starting from zero finds no working controller; with simulated feedback, both models succeed in single sessions, producing sparse, cascaded-structure controllers. On a real tractor, all language-model-programmed controllers steer along line-and-arc paths without human intervention and achieve tracking errors of the same order as a pure pursuit baseline, while gradient descent attains higher accuracy given substantially more simulation.Link
HandUMI: A Modular Hand-Worn Parallel-Jaw Interface for Robot-Free Bimanual Data CaptureMcGill University; Mila - Quebec AI Institute; Mu Robotics; Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú (PUCP); Technical University of Munich (TUM); Universidad Peruana de Ciencias Aplicadas (UPC)The paper presents HandUMI, a modular hand-worn parallel-jaw interface that captures robot-agnostic bimanual manipulation demonstrations using tracked VR controllers and aperture encoders, with swappable fingertips matching various robot grippers. Robot-free HandUMI recordings are retargeted via inverse kinematics to seven simulated bimanual arms, achieving median position error ≤1.2 mm and requiring rejection of at most 4.7% of episodes for retargeting failures. Using 562 HandUMI demonstrations of bimanual towel folding to train policies for real PiPER arms, ACT achieves 12/20 successes and fine-tuned π₀.₅ achieves 7/20, whereas ACT trained only on the first 101 demonstrations and the base π₀.₅ each achieve 0/20 successes.Link
From Temperature-Limited to Battery-Limited Legged LocomotionWoven by Toyota, Inc.The paper develops a 13-node lumped RC thermal model for a Unitree Go2 quadruped, fitted from two runs and calibrated against electrical power, which predicts all twelve motor temperatures with mean RMSE below 1 °C across different robot instances, gaits, payloads, and speeds; under sustained gaits, steady-state temperatures are determined by gait-averaged squared torques and joint speeds. Building on this model, the authors design a reinforcement learning reward based on the hottest predicted asymptotic motor temperatures, which respond within a gait cycle to torque and speed, enabling training of thermally aware locomotion policies without direct temperature observations despite long physical thermal time constants. On hardware, the thermally aware policy transforms locomotion from temperature-limited to battery-limited: with a 5 kg payload at 1 m/s, a baseline policy shuts down thermally after 19 minutes (1.1 km), whereas the proposed policy runs 174 minutes (10.0 km) with the hottest motor 14 °C below shutdown at 26 °C ambient; on an unloaded factory circuit with stairs and ramps, the baseline overheats after 25 laps (6.2 km) while the proposed policy completes 47 laps (11.2 km) with 22 °C temperature margin.Link
ALERT: Extending Humanoid Motion Trackers with a General and Lightweight Human Avoidance PipelineBeijing Institute of Technology; Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; University of Chinese Academy of SciencesThe paper proposes ALERT, a lightweight pipeline that augments pretrained humanoid motion-tracking policies with human perception and proactive avoidance, enabling collision-aware whole-body tracking in shared human-robot environments while preserving reference motion quality. ALERT incorporates human avoidance through localized trajectory adaptations that balance collision avoidance with tracking of the original reference motion, aiming to maintain whole-body coordination and allow rapid recovery of nominal tracking once potential collisions are resolved. Simulation and real-world experiments across multiple tracking backbones indicate that ALERT achieves reliable collision-free execution with preserved motion capabilities and tracking continuity, though detailed quantitative metrics are not specified in the abstract.Link
Heave-Ho: Decentralized Long-Horizon Multi-Humanoid CollaborationUniversity of Southern California; VinMotionThe paper introduces Heave-Ho, a decentralized framework for long-horizon loco-manipulation by multiple humanoids, combining a multi-agent post-training procedure that adapts a humanoid foundation tracking model to physically coupled interactions with a high-level vision-language planner and speech-based leader-follower coordination without inter-robot network communication. Heave-Ho’s low-level controllers operate from local observations to produce coordinated whole-body behaviors, while the speech-based leader-follower mechanism synchronizes decentralized execution, enabling collaboration among teams trained with two identical humanoids to scale to up to ten robots and generalize to heterogeneous teams without retraining. Using the proposed HH-Bench of 12 multi-humanoid tasks involving 2–10 robots, systematic evaluations in simulation and real-world settings show that Heave-Ho consistently outperforms baseline methods and achieves robust decentralized collaboration, although specific numerical performance gains are not detailed in the abstract.Link
Beyond Episodic Recall: Experience-to-Knowledge Evolution for Lifelong Embodied NavigationHohai University; Xidian UniversityThe paper proposes EvoNav, an Experience-Evolving Memory framework for lifelong embodied navigation that organizes semantic observations, spatial relations, and task feedback into sparse "experience atoms" which are incrementally consolidated and linked based on observed transitions during online operation. At a slower timescale, EvoNav distills accumulated co-occurrences of semantic categories and feedback signals into environment-specific structural knowledge, without relying on predefined object–room relations, and combines this distilled knowledge with direct goal evidence from experience atoms to guide search over the current traversability map for new goals. Experiments on single-goal, sequential multi-floor, and multimodal navigation tasks, including transfer to a real robot, show that EvoNav enables effective experience reuse with compact persistent memory, improving path efficiency relative to prior memory-enhanced navigation methods, though the abstract does not state quantitative margins.Link
DGV-DLIVO: Degeneracy-Guided Visual Fusion for Direct LiDAR-Inertial-Visual Odometry in Degenerate Tunnel EnvironmentsTongji UniversityThe paper proposes DGV-DLIVO, a direct LiDAR-inertial-visual odometry method that uses LiDAR-degenerate directions to actively guide visual feature selection, form LiDAR-anchored visual associations, and apply subspace-selective fusion so that visual constraints are injected only into motion directions where LiDAR is deficient while unreliable LiDAR information in those directions is adaptively suppressed. In real-world hydropower tunnel experiments with both cart-mounted and handheld setups, DGV-DLIVO maintains continuous, directionally consistent tunnel-axis motion estimation, whereas representative state-of-the-art LIO, LIVO, and VIO baselines exhibit stagnation, oscillation, severe under-travel, curved drift, or divergence. On a 40 m tunnel sequence, DGV-DLIVO achieves a 91.4% progress ratio and significantly improves longitudinal motion recovery and trajectory stability under severely degraded underground sensing conditions.Link
Autonomous Track Racing with a Commercially Available Humanoid: System Design and Real-World DeploymentGMO Internet Group, Inc.; GMO Various Robotics Inc.The paper presents an autonomous humanoid running system for the commercially available Unitree G1 that integrates vision-based lane perception, path tracking, and a reinforcement-learning-based running policy with a human motion prior, augmented by head-camera stabilization and IMU-assisted leg odometry to handle flight phases. To manage hardware limits, the system incorporates motor thermal management by penalizing hip-pitch torque during training, adding heat-dissipation plates, and applying temperature-based speed limiting with event-specific pacing, such that thermal margin effectively determines the achievable running pace. In real-world deployment at the World Humanoid Robot Games 2026, the system completed 100 m, 400 m, and 1500 m track events without intervention in some runs, placing 13th/74 in the 100 m, 10th/44 in the 400 m first round before a fall in the semifinal due to lane-detection error, and 7th/35 in the 1500 m.Link
Progress Predicts PolicyNational University of SingaporeThe paper introduces Progress Predicts Policy (PPP), which learns a language-conditioned scalar progress field over states and composes it with a shared world model so that the policy is obtained by reading out action gradients of this field at a fixed reference command, yielding a hybrid continuous–discrete action policy without a separate action head or search. PPP trains the induced commands via imitation learning and uses double backpropagation on the continuous gradient readout to shape local control geometry, with demonstration timing used to calibrate progress values as supervision for progress monitoring during execution, and can still learn the continuous readout without explicit progress labels. In simulated and real robot manipulation experiments, PPP outperforms behavioral cloning baselines under a shared architecture, indicating improved policy performance from the progress-field-based control formulation.Link
Distilling World Action Models for Efficient Bimanual Manipulation via Anchored Path MatchingAstribot; Chongqing University; Harbin Institute of Technology; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)The paper proposes Anchored Path Matching (APM) for few-step distillation of World Action Models, supervising both endpoint and short-transition predictions from states visited by the distilled student by rolling out the teacher to zero noise from each such state and using its endpoint and first intermediate state as dual targets, with modality-routed adapters and anchor-priority updates that favor endpoint consistency when gradients conflict. APM produces a single distilled checkpoint that supports 1-, 2-, and 4-step joint video-and-action generation, allowing the deployment-time choice of sampling budget without retraining or additional teacher queries, and reduces control-cycle latency by 21.3× relative to an eight-step teacher on two real-robot tasks while also improving task success over the one-step teacher. On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, training only on Clean demonstrations and evaluating zero-shot on the domain-randomized Random setting, one-step APM improves closed-loop success over continued base distillation by 2.0 percentage points on Clean and 1.4 percentage points on Random, and increases video quality with PSNR from 30.169 to 32.317 dB alongside gains in SSIM and LPIPS.Link
