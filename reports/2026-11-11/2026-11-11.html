Executive Summary

  • Several works advance robust and risk-aware control and navigation: BRACE uses hierarchical RL with disturbance beliefs for reach-avoid under unknown spatially varying disturbances, improving performance over MPC and RL baselines in simulated quadcopter wind fields and on Crazyflie hardware; a capturability-aware sequential MPC for Linear Inverted Pendulum humanoids plans footsteps via safe-corridor rectangles with recursive feasibility and handles unexpected clutter (e.g., closed doors) in high-fidelity Digit simulations; SmoothedTurboMPC combines SQP with a log-barrier-smoothed ADMM inner loop to yield GPU-parallel differentiable MPC whose coupling with SHAC boosts training throughput up to 4.4× versus differentiable MPC baselines and achieves higher-return, 46× faster vehicle-racing training and successful full-scale race-car transfer; an SMC-based view of sampling MPC unifies many planners (MPPI, CEM, etc.) and shows that resampling cuts collision rates in long-horizon navigation and, with programmable proposals, raises push-T success from 1% to 100% in simulation and 10% to 90% on a Franka FR3; and a transformer policy that imitates LQG delivers median closed-loop cost within 0.4% of optimal on seen LTI systems and within 1.0% after light fine-tuning on unseen ones without explicit plant models or state estimators at run time. (arXiv:2611.10938; arXiv:2611.10169; arXiv:2611.09937; arXiv:2611.10877; arXiv:2611.11815)
  • New frameworks improve dexterous, bimanual, and humanoid manipulation: GUARD Q-steers generative robot policies using anomaly-informed value functions to boost Adroit dexterous manipulation success to 96.0% nominal and 80.0% perturbed in simulation and add 40 and 25 percentage points in sim-to-real screwdriver tasks; object-centric bimanual RL with shared rewards yields emergent skills such as pre-grasping, regrasping, and handover, and a DAgger/RL-distilled policy achieves 76.3% success over four real tasks and 85.0% simulated barcode-scanning success versus 60.4% for two single-arm policies; BetaGrasp adds a grasp-taxonomy label to a 3D diffusion DP3 point-cloud policy, raising correct grasp-type rate from 25% to 94% in simulation and, on hardware climbing-hold trials, increasing mean slip force from 9.6 N to 14.3 N while cutting fully slipping grasps from 44% to 10%; and DexForesight injects representations from an action-conditioned spatial world model into dexterous policies, improving DexJoCo success from 49.4% to 60.5% and raising real-robot dexterous task success from 50.0% to 58.3%. (arXiv:2611.10343; arXiv:2611.10410; arXiv:2611.12017; arXiv:2611.11166)
  • Several systems target cross-embodiment learning, motion understanding, and coding-based robot control: UniLatent aligns source and target visual encoders via simulated paired renderings to enable cross-embodiment policy transfer, reaching up to 93% zero-shot success and beating pixel-level baselines by >30 percentage points, and in xArm-to-Franka transfer attains 27/30 successful fine-tuned rollouts using only 10 target demos per task; a unified inertial encoder with kinematic IMU synthesis jointly trains on human and retargeted humanoid motion to produce a shared inertial interface, achieving the lowest velocity error (including 0.099 m/s on a real Unitree G1) and supporting motion–description retrieval for motions only seen in human data; BATCH learns behavior-aligned temporal representations and a video-action diffusion policy with cycle-consistent human–robot alignment and style transfer, improving real-world manipulation generalization over unaligned and prior cross-embodiment baselines; a self-learning coding agent pipeline progressively acquires bimanual object-flipping skills in simulation, boosting success from 24.7% to 95.0% and encoding strategies into graph-based control code that transfers to physical robots; and the Embodied First Exam provides 1,156 coding-agent control tasks on 47 robots with three evaluation settings, where the strongest evaluated agent reaches 34% success on 456 core tasks, indicating large headroom and enabling detailed failure-stage analysis. (arXiv:2611.10061; arXiv:2611.10137; arXiv:2611.10148; arXiv:2611.11941; arXiv:2611.12177)
  • Vision-language-action and multi-domain decision-making are advanced by new architectures and distillation schemes: DASH decouples a slow vision-language backbone from a fast flow-matching action head into asynchronous System 2/System 1 processes, maintaining 20 Hz control for 0.8B–8B backbones where synchronous pipelines fall to 4.1 Hz, and achieving higher RoboTwin benchmark success and better real-humanoid pick-and-place performance; DreamVLA converts generated human motion trajectories into RL-trained task trajectories and uses reference-conditioned DAgger restarts to mitigate distribution shift, training VLAs on humanoid tasks like platform picking, drawer opening, and button pressing; DeltaForge consolidates N per-domain autonomous-driving VLA experts into a post-hoc mixture-of-experts that SVD-decomposes weight deltas into shared and tiny domain-specific components with lightweight routers, matching per-domain experts and outperforming merging, routing, centralized, and federated baselines across six driving domains while enabling cheap addition of new domains and generalization to unseen domain combinations; and Kinematic Action Models treat whole-body motion generation (via the Kimodo backbone plus ControlNet conditioning and root–body–hand hierarchy) as the core loco-manipulation action, with Kimodo-Policy reaching 72.14% success on HumanoidArena, competitive SIMPLE results, and 60% mean success on eight real tasks using a 678M-parameter model with 92M trainable parameters. (arXiv:2611.11854; arXiv:2611.10872; arXiv:2611.11856; arXiv:2611.11554)
  • Multiple works improve modeling, simulation, and representation learning for complex dynamics and deformable or soft systems: an extended aSSM framework learns a 5D reduced ODE directly on a soft-helicoid trunk robot and embeds it in MPC to achieve higher-accuracy real-time tracking across 2D/3D tasks than linear and model-free RL baselines; a complementary study uses a 6D aSSM-reduced model as a sample-efficient RL training environment for a pneumatic soft arm, allowing 3 hours of simulator training (vs. an estimated 3 years on hardware) and yielding 40% lower hardware tracking error than policies trained on much larger RNN and transformer surrogates whose identification requires 13 and 100 hours; DeepSoMo enforces self-supervised latent prediction for proprioceptive deformable motion estimation and, against FEM ground truth, consistently improves global trajectory, orientation, shape, and contact inference over recurrent baselines; SoftPAT3D turns text prompts into physics-consistent 3D scenes with deformable objects by generating rest shapes, material models, watertight supports, and using grid search plus adjoint-based differentiable simulation to fix prompt failures and jointly optimize placements and materials, producing 65 prompted scenes without unwanted interpenetrations; and a differentiable-simulation taxonomy clarifies gradient referents and adjoint realizations, introduces an orthogonal contact-handling taxonomy and four quantified gradient-degradation mechanisms, and provides a diagnostic tool, checklist, and research agenda outlining when exact derivatives, adjoints, or even zeroth-order methods are preferable. (arXiv:2611.11709; arXiv:2611.11718; arXiv:2611.11242; arXiv:2611.11087; arXiv:2611.12162)
  • New analyses, data-collection schemes, and benchmarks refine learning, perception, and navigation strategies: a study of VLA adaptation on LIBERO shows π₀.₅ needs roughly 2–5× fewer demonstrations than π₀ and is most sample-efficient at all data budgets, GR00T N1.5 adapts more efficiently than N1.6/N1.7 on most suites despite weaker zero-shot performance, and freezing vision-language modules affects π and GR00T families differently; vine sampling for behavior cloning augments selected states with k demonstrations from the same observation, enabling diffusion policies to better capture multi-modal action distributions and improving proximity to demonstrators and best-of-N and expert-iteration performance on a 2D truck-unloading task; a controlled peeling benchmark shows that coordinate frames and information content critically shape learned predictive models—surface-relative coordinates aid consequence prediction and counterfactual selection but remain sensitive to coordinate uncertainty and contact/execution errors; a preference-augmented distributional RL navigation method encodes risk preferences as shifted quantile weights and, by optimizing for worst-case preference distributions, reduces collisions while improving task success over scalar CVaR-based approaches across simulated and real/simulated vehicles; CPNav calibrates VLM action scores via conformal prediction for map-free object-goal navigation, yielding SR/SPL gains of 6.2/11.4% on HM3D and 12.4/8.8% on MP3D, and 24.9/15.2% on a real robot over uncalibrated baselines; monocular model-based imitation learning with a learned collision-risk model and demonstration-tuned optimization parameters reduces collision rates and improves reliability across different robots versus end-to-end imitation and classic model-based baselines; DeepSoMo-based work (separate from the taxonomy) and BAT-WAM demonstrate improved tactile and deformable sensing—BAT-WAM’s RGB-conditioned tactile/action codebooks cut tactile RMSE by up to ~39% versus HandTouch and slash future tactile-prediction RMSE by 85.56% relative to OpenWAM; MOSU2 adds contextual reasoning plus conditional retrieval of geometric/dynamic evidence for outdoor navigation, raising real-world mean success from 61.8% to 94.2% and condition-detection accuracy from 72.2% to 91.4%; FLAVOR’s hierarchical spatio-temporal mapping for UAV exploration compresses global information into keyframes and topological graphs, cutting exploration time by ~32–42% and map memory by 94–99% in simulation and by 62–93% in real deployments; stochastic challenges are proposed as a benchmark paradigm using reproducible yet varied real scenes, with a mobile-manipulation benchmark and cross-lab robot comparison illustrating platform-agnostic evaluation; a study of teleoperator demonstration quality defines a ‘data multiple’ and finds that on robomimic’s Square task, Worse operators need 1.55× more demos at a 25 s time limit (shrinking to 1.11× at 100 s), mixed-quality datasets underperform a linear-value model, and Worse operators are only cost-effective if their wage is under one-third of Better operators given longer demo times; a particle-filter wrapper around a single diffusion model that jointly represents states, actions, and observations maintains multiple state histories and reweights them by observational likelihood, improving state estimation and task performance versus direct model action generation in simulated partially observed navigation and manipulation; and a hybrid tendon–linkage robotic finger with four mechanical DOFs and three actuators demonstrates contact-dependent adaptive grasping without an actively driven DIP joint and achieves a mean peak fingertip force of 4.56 N across diverse object shapes. (arXiv:2611.09964; arXiv:2611.10949; arXiv:2611.11869; arXiv:2611.09932; arXiv:2611.10366; arXiv:2611.10635; arXiv:2611.10967; arXiv:2611.11148; arXiv:2611.11489; arXiv:2611.12804; arXiv:2611.11947; arXiv:2611.11732; arXiv:2611.11885)

GUARD: Anomaly-Informed Q-Steering for Generative Robot Policies in Dexterous ManipulationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes GUARD, an anomaly-informed Q-steering framework that combines sparse task rewards with dense anomaly scores learned from the policy’s training data, using a learned Q-function to steer the denoising process of generative robot policies without modifying the pretrained policy. In simulation on four Adroit dexterous manipulation tasks, GUARD achieves average success rates of 96.0% under nominal dynamics and 80.0% under perturbed dynamics, outperforming the strongest baseline by 21.0 and 20.0 percentage points respectively while maintaining the lowest anomaly scores. In sim-to-real experiments on in-hand screwdriver rotation and reorientation tasks, GUARD transfers directly to hardware, improving success rates by 40 and 25 percentage points compared to baselines.Link
SimDuoReal: Object-Centric Bimanual Policy for Emergent DexterityCarnegie Mellon University; Stanford University; UC San DiegoThe paper trains bimanual reinforcement learning policies with a shared object-centric reward across varied environments, leading to emergent behaviors such as pre-grasp manipulation, grasping, regrasping, in-hand adjustment, and handover without hand-designed motion sequences. Expert policies are refined on transition states and distilled via DAgger and reinforcement learning into a single bimanual policy that composes the learned behaviors for diverse tasks. On real hardware, the distilled policy achieves 76.3% success (61/80 trials) across four tasks with unseen objects and no task-specific fine-tuning, and in simulated barcode scanning it attains 85.0% success versus 60.4% for two single-arm policies without handover.Link
BRACE: Belief-guided Residual Adaptive Control for Safe Navigation under Unknown DisturbancesETH Zurich; Massachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces BRACE, a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework for reach-avoid navigation under unknown, bounded, spatially varying disturbances that infers a disturbance belief from model prediction errors and conditions a high-level policy combining nominal and belief-guided residual terms, trained with a likelihood-ratio advantage reweighting heuristic. In three simulated quadcopter environments with abruptly switching wind fields and on Crazyflie hardware with fan-generated disturbances, BRACE improves reach-avoid performance compared to model predictive control and reinforcement learning baselines.Link
Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids (DASH): Latency-Agnostic Scaling of Vision-Language-Action Models for Humanoid ManipulationUniversity of WashingtonThe paper presents DASH, a Decoupled Asynchronous System for Humanoids that splits a vision-language-action policy into a slow vision-language backbone (System 2) and a fast flow-matching action head (System 1) running as concurrent processes, so that control rate is determined by the action head and becomes independent of backbone size. DASH’s action head fuses stale semantics from the frozen backbone with fresh visual inputs from its own ResNet encoder, is trained under desynchronized conditions to handle observation staleness, and uses an adaptive temporal aggregator to align overlapping action chunks under variable inference latency. On the asynchronous RoboTwin benchmark, DASH maintains a 20 Hz control rate for backbone sizes from 0.8B to 8B parameters while a synchronous pipeline drops from 9.7 to 4.1 Hz, achieving higher success at every size; on a real humanoid with dexterous hands, DASH yields clearly higher pick-and-place success than the synchronous pipeline.Link
Grasp-Taxonomy Conditioning as a Mode Switch for Dexterous Grasping of Climbing HoldsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces BetaGrasp, a 3D diffusion-based DP3-style point-cloud grasping policy for a Franka arm with a 16-DoF LEAP hand, conditioned on a four-class grasp taxonomy label (crimp, sloper, pinch, jug) to act as a discrete mode switch for dexterous grasping of climbing holds. Analysis shows the point-cloud encoder alone does not separate the four hold types, and a label-free DP3-style baseline collapses to jug grasps from the reset pose, achieving 25% correct grasp type versus 94% for BetaGrasp in simulated rollouts. On 80 paired hardware trials on a climbing-hold benchmark with a slip-force ratchet testbed, BetaGrasp increases average slip force from 9.6 N to 14.3 N and reduces fully slipping grasps from 44% of trials to 10%.Link
Benchmarking Real-World Robotic Manipulation through Stochastic ChallengesETH ZurichThe paper proposes stochastic challenges as a new paradigm for real-world robot manipulation benchmarking, using reproducible but stochastically varied real-world scenes to mimic the rigor and surprise of hidden test sets while enabling system-level innovation and cross-platform comparability via success rates over shared task distributions. A concrete stochastic benchmark for mobile manipulation is implemented, and two robots tested in two different labs are compared on this benchmark to demonstrate how performance across different platforms and locations can be evaluated under the proposed paradigm.Link
Capturability-Aware Footstep Planning in Cluttered Environments: A Recursively Feasible Sequential MPC on Axis-Aligned Safe CorridorsUC BerkeleyThe paper presents a capturability-aware sequential Model Predictive Control framework for Linear Inverted Pendulum humanoid footstep planning in cluttered environments, combining obstacle avoidance with capturability constraints using axis-aligned safe corridors formed by sequences of intersecting rectangles, and providing recursive feasibility guarantees for the planning layer. The planner can autonomously adapt to constrained scenarios, including switching to in-place walking when forward motion is blocked by unexpected obstructions such as doors assumed open during planning but closed during execution, and its performance remains largely independent of prediction horizon, enabling high success rates at low computational cost. The approach is validated in high-fidelity simulations with the humanoid robot Digit, demonstrating practical capturable footstep planning in cluttered environments.Link
DreamVLA: From Generated Human Motions to Humanoid Vision-Language-Action PoliciesUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes DreamVLA, a method that uses generated human motion trajectories to guide reinforcement learning in simulation, converting them into reliable task execution trajectories that can train vision-language-action models without additional task-specific teleoperation data. A central component is a reference-conditioned DAgger distillation procedure that, after failed learner rollouts, restarts the RL expert from sampled learner states with the same reference suffix and aggregates successful corrective trajectories, mitigating distribution shift left by offline imitation. DreamVLA is evaluated on humanoid tasks including picking from a platform, opening a drawer, pressing a button, and walking and picking from a platform, with ablations over DAgger iterations, residual reinforcement learning, and the scale of the generated dataset.Link
DeltaForge: A Post-hoc Mixture-of-Experts VLA for Multi-Domain Autonomous DrivingUniversity of WashingtonThe paper introduces DeltaForge, a post-hoc mixture-of-experts vision-language-action model for multi-domain autonomous driving that consolidates N independently fine-tuned per-domain experts into a single model using only their weights and a small proxy dataset. DeltaForge jointly decomposes all per-domain weight updates via singular value decomposition into a shared component and tiny domain-specific components, and trains two clip-level blind routers (for VLM reasoning and flow-matching dynamics) that route only the small domain-specific pieces based on soft labels from the proxy dataset, keeping routing efficient. Across six diverse driving domains, DeltaForge approaches the per-domain experts’ accuracy and surpasses merging, routing, centralized, and federated baselines at near single-model parameter and compute cost, while allowing new domains to be added by projecting their weight updates onto the shared basis without end-to-end retraining and enabling generalization to unseen domain combinations.Link

The benchmark defines three evaluation settings: a Standard Setting with only sensor observations and no default environment resets, a Realtime variant where the environment continues running while the agent reasons, and a Privileged Setting with full simulator state and unlimited resets for searching oracle solutions and establishing approximate upper bounds. On 456 core tasks in the Embodied First Exam, the strongest of eight evaluated agents, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, achieves a 34% success rate in the Standard Setting, indicating substantial remaining headroom for improvement; failure analysis categorizes the stages at which attempts break down across agents, settings, and stage categories.Link

Action Representation for Spatial Generalization in Robot World ModelsUniversity of WashingtonThe study systematically compares matched action representations defined in world, object, and surface coordinate frames on a controlled robotic peeling benchmark to analyze how spatial descriptions influence learned predictive models and spatial generalization. Experiments with global pose and contact relocation show that limited training data incurs a cost when equivalent interactions receive different spatial descriptions, and removing peel thickness information yields an irreducible ambiguity where identical inputs correspond to different material removal outcomes. Action coordinates defined relative to the surface enable better consequence prediction and counterfactual action selection under accurate local observations, but learned predictors remain vulnerable to uncertainty in the coordinate frame; joint centering of surface and tool removes sensitivity to origin translation while incorrect contact geometry and execution deviations introduce distinct failure modes, illustrating that preserving action–state physical relationships is key for spatial generalization.Link

The Relative Value of Demonstration Data: Execution Time Limits and Operator Mixtures in Behavior CloningStanford UniversityThe paper proposes measuring the relative value of robot teleoperator demonstration data via a 'data multiple': the ratio of demonstration counts two operator groups require for behavior cloning policies to reach the same success rate under a given execution time limit. On robomimic’s simulated Square task, within the 25-second time limit, policies trained on all-Worse demonstrations require 1.55× as many demonstrations as those trained on all-Better demonstrations, but this gap shrinks to a 1.11× multiple with a 100-second limit, indicating that much of the difference is due to later task completion rather than outright failure. Generalizable Output Feedback Optimal Control via Transformers Without Explicit State EstimationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes a transformer-based policy that learns generalizable output-feedback optimal control for a heterogeneous family of partially observed, noisy MIMO LTI systems by imitating LQG controllers, using standardized, padded, and masked representations augmented with encodings of cost and noise covariances, without explicit access to plant matrices or a state estimator at run time. The authors characterize how an optimal LQG controller, expressed as a dynamic state-space model, relates to a static transformer that acts on a finite window of noisy measurements, and they express the excess closed-loop cost of any causal policy as the energy of its deviation from the optimal LQG action. On 28 simulated systems, the trained transformer policy attains a median cost within 0.4% of optimal LQG for seen systems, and for unseen systems a light fine-tuning step yields a median cost gap of 1.0% relative to optimal.Link
Same Physics, Different Gradients: A State-of-the-Art Report on Differentiable Physics SimulationCarnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford UniversityThe paper presents a taxonomy for differentiable physics simulation that separates two axes: the referent of the returned derivative (finite computation, converged equation, or continuous dynamics) and the realization of the backward pass (automatic differentiation, equation-level adjoint solve, or custom adjoint), clarifying that the perceived trade-off between gradient exactness and memory stems from implementation choices rather than fundamental constraints. An orthogonal taxonomy is developed for handling contact in differentiable simulation, and four mechanisms by which useful gradients can be degraded are identified and empirically measured on a common testbed, showing that the benefit of exact derivatives over equation-level adjoints depends on both the fitting target and solver budget. The survey reviews model classes, systems techniques, and applications of differentiable simulation through this lens, and introduces a reporting checklist, an accompanying diagnostic tool that computes it, and a research agenda aimed at improving the use and evaluation of simulator gradients, including recognizing regimes where zeroth-order methods can outperform gradient-based approaches.Link

Preference-Augmented Distributional Reinforcement Learning for Risk-Sensitive NavigationJilin University; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology; The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)The paper introduces a preference-augmented distributional reinforcement learning approach for navigation that represents risk preferences as shifts in quantile-weight distributions within an Implicit Quantile Network framework, rather than relying on a single scalar Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) level.
