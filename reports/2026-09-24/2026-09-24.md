## Executive Summary

- CMU introduces JAMB, a joint action–motion diffusion policy that substantially boosts success and robustness in bimanual manipulation.
- New motion-planning and control methods advance constrained planning, dynamic deformable-object manipulation, and humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation.
- Multiple works push dexterous and forceful manipulation forward, from all-linear Cartesian fingers and visually grounded fingertip forces to tool-use via hand–object mechanisms.
- Navigation and SLAM see progress in prediction-aware legged obstacle avoidance, monocular and drone navigation, semantic orchard mapping, and radar–lidar SE(3) localization.
- Vision-language(-action) systems continue to mature, with geometry-aware and safety-wrapped VLA manipulation, sparse semantic navigation, and unified embodied models.
- UC San Diego, UC Berkeley, and Stanford contribute institution-aligned work spanning continuum catheter localization, free-flyer space manipulation, driving-safety benchmarking, and humanoid-RL education.

| JAMB: Joint Action-Motion Diffusion for Bimanual Manipulation | Carnegie Mellon University | JAMB jointly denoises bimanual actions and future 3D point tracks in a shared transformer, explicitly coupling control and predicted scene geometry. On 16 simulated tasks and several real-robot evaluations, it strongly outperforms action-only and auxiliary-geometry baselines, with better generalization in cluttered and out-of-distribution scenes. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25322 |
| Shaft-Configuration-Adaptive Catheter Tip Position Estimation via Motor-History Conditioned Residual Learning | UC San Diego | This work augments a calibrated geometric catheter model with a motor-history-conditioned GRU residual corrector to estimate 3D tip pose without explicit shaft sensing. Across multiple catheters and bent configurations, it cuts tip-position error by ~59% versus a constant-curvature baseline, improving navigation accuracy for tendon-driven continuum instruments. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26304 |
| A Deployable Four-Finger Payload for Teleoperated Free-Flying Manipulation with Astrobee | UC Berkeley | The authors design a 17-DoF, four-finger payload on a linear rail for NASA’s Astrobee, controlled via VR teleoperation to map human hand motions to bimanual free-flyer manipulation. Zero-gravity MuJoCo simulations on cargo transport tasks show feasible performance and quantify success, contact maintenance, and task times. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25614 |
| NavSafe-$\infty$: Benchmarking Closed-Loop Driving Safety in Photorealistic Environments | UC San Diego | NavSafe-$\infty$ is a photorealistic closed-loop benchmark of 280 driving scenarios with safety-centric taxonomies for crashes, violations, and incidents. Evaluating 20 end-to-end policies reveals weak correlation between open-loop metrics and closed-loop safety, and shows how common training remedies can trade safety margin for progress. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26618 |
| Teaching Reinforcement Learning and Humanoid Robotics to High-School Students: An Expert-Validated Curriculum Design on a Low-Cost Open Platform | Stanford University | This paper proposes a research-inspired curriculum where high-school students assemble an open-source humanoid, train a walking policy in simulation, and deploy it on hardware. Expert review surfaces key design tensions—authenticity vs. cognitive load, integration vs. visible progress, and teamwork vs. accountability—informing a reusable framework for precollege robotics courses. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25674 |

| Brace Yourself: Task-Conditioned Environmental Bracing for Forceful Humanoid Manipulation | Queensland University of Technology | This work introduces Supporting Hand Strategy (SHS), which learns to use one arm to brace against the environment while the other performs forceful tasks. | VisForce: Visual Grounding of Current and Desired Forces for Goal-Conditioned Dexterous Manipulation |  | VisForce encodes current and desired fingertip forces as rendered visual cues over wrist and goal images, then uses cross-attention to produce force-aware actions. On a UR10 with a dexterous hand, it achieves consistent force-conditioned grasps and 40–70% success on multi-stage force-sensitive tasks such as slip-modulated peg-in-hole. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25785 |
| The Cartesian Hand: In-Hand Manipulation with All-Linear Fingers |  | The Cartesian Hand is a 7-DoF end-effector with two independent parallel grippers and four translating fingertips that generate relative object motion using only linear DOFs. Its configuration-independent fingertip kinematics enable complex in-hand tasks—threaded closures, pipetting, triggers, screwdriving—on 35 objects, including bimanual use on a humanoid. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25696 |
| Relative Contact Velocity-Controlled Hand-Object Mechanism for Dexterous Tool Manipulation |  | This paper models a hand and tool as a unified hand–object mechanism controlled via Cartesian relative contact velocities and forces between generalized contact frames. A lightweight least-squares motion planner and complementary filter enable five different multi-finger hands to execute full tool-use sequences—grasp, reorient, and wield—from the same simple references. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25619 |
| CableVLA: Simulation-Privileged Global-Local Representation Learning for Cable Routing |  | CableVLA couples a topology-aware visual head (TopoHead) with a tactile encoder (TacSense) to learn global cable configuration and local contact dynamics from simulation-privileged signals. Nonlinear feedback laws provide explicit steady-state convergence conditions, and simulations plus real-time experiments with online shape estimation demonstrate stable manipulation beyond quasi-static regimes. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26004 |
