Executive Summary

  • Several works advance navigation and motion planning: TravSUITE combines an efficient voxel mapper using visual foundation models with a unified self-supervised BEV predictor for geometry, semantics, speed, cost, and uncertainty, and shows that jointly learning costs and map inpainting is needed for state-of-the-art off-road planning, cutting real-world navigation errors by 80% and raising traversal speed by 5%; Graphs of Trajectory Bundles (GTB) iteratively builds a graph-of-convex-sets over trajectory bundles to solve shortest-path problems whose iterates become maximally dynamically feasible while enforcing dynamics, collision, and safety constraints; RepresentNav fuses multiple vision foundation models via a compact transformer and a latent “dreaming” predictor to achieve new SOTA on VLNVerse, VLN-CE, EVT-Bench, and point-goal tasks while reducing collisions by ~25%; OWLS uses an open-vocabulary VLM to generate language-conditioned speed fields from a single monocular image for zero-shot rover navigation without training or mapping and demonstrates applicability on physical and simulated rovers in indoor, off-road, and planetary-like terrains; and a natural-language policy interface for ROS 2 Nav2 converts English instructions to JSON policies injected via adapters into the existing costmap stack, yielding 18.4–24.5% faster simulated and 23.1–35.0% faster real TurtleBot3 navigation than a baseline without such policies on evaluated tasks. (arXiv:2610.32130; arXiv:2610.32365; arXiv:2610.31762; arXiv:2610.32596; arXiv:2610.32633)
  • New datasets, architectures, and analyses target multi-skill and robust control: DexPort introduces a portable handheld interface that logs commanded joint targets and wrist motions for dexterous hands, where behavior cloning on commands boosts success from 11% to 64% and adding in-the-wild data raises success from 36% to 78%; EgoForesight jointly learns from high-quality demos and noisy egocentric video by reliability-conditioned corruption, weighted regression, and future representation distillation, improving real-world open-loop action MSE by 20.5% and RoboCasa few-shot success by 8.1 points at 30k pretraining hours; MAST-VLA augments a VLA policy with skill-conditioned LoRA Mixture-of-Experts and trajectory-affordance priors to reach SOTA 4.65 average sequence length on CALVIN ABC→D and demonstrate real-robot multi-skill sequences; RobustRLlib unifies 17 robust RL algorithms and evaluates them across 273 perturbation settings to map where current methods remain effective and where robustness gaps persist; a study of pretrained LeWorldModel (LeWM) shows scale-dependent discrepancies between its local action geometry and simulator responses, with median direction mismatches of ~36°/42° on small perturbations and improved Gram agreement at larger scales, leaving implications for control performance open; and deploying a tabletop VLA model on a legged manipulator highlights that broader demonstrations and added secondary skills reduce end-effector error by 30% and expand behavioral modes without harming baseline grasping. (arXiv:2610.32969; arXiv:2610.31845; arXiv:2610.32312; arXiv:2610.32971; arXiv:2610.31716; arXiv:2610.32271)
  • Several works improve manipulation, contact-rich control, and task understanding: KiRoSe learns Sparse Motion Tokens from kinematic trajectories and uses a language model over SMT sequences to segment robot demonstrations into temporal and semantic subtasks, substantially outperforming prior segmentation baselines and transferring zero-shot across robots and to egocentric human motion while enabling progress tracking and reversal detection without video; FORCA is an agent that writes and refines Python programs for contact-rich probing and force-responsive motion using language, vision, and force/torque feedback, outperforming a vision-only coding agent on grasp accuracy, success rates, and contact stability across five real tasks; BM2PC formulates multimodal MPC for deformable linear objects as Bayesian set optimization over a control-as-inference posterior, achieving accurate long-horizon rope tracking with 44.0% RMSE reduction on the longest real trajectory and 29–66% lower planning time than existing multimodal MPC for obstacle avoidance; and a geometry-aware RL framework for robot-assisted dressing combines a staged curriculum with a finger-wise insertion gate, reaching 84.7 ± 1.2% success on deformable bracelet insertion in simulation and 15/20 successful real mannequin trials, with ablations showing the insertion gate gives the largest single gain. (arXiv:2610.31801; arXiv:2610.31841; arXiv:2610.31852; arXiv:2610.32143)
  • Generative frameworks create training environments and structures with strong physical properties: SceneBloom uses a hierarchical diffusion-based layout sampler and a contract-guided 3D compiler to produce simulation-ready indoor scenes that, across five room types, have higher visual quality and stability than baselines and are uniquely collision-free and in-bounds, with navigation agents trained solely on SceneBloom outperforming those trained on equal amounts of real data on held-out real scans; StackGen3D trains a physics-informed GNN to filter stable block assemblies and then uses a conditional flow-matching model to generate 3D block structures matching target silhouettes, where Best-of-10 sampling improves stability from 45.3% to 72.4% while maintaining high silhouette IoU of 0.970 without physics at inference. (arXiv:2610.32978; arXiv:2610.31803)
  • Work on humanoid safety, control, and coordinated motion highlights both vulnerabilities and new capabilities: an analysis of humanoid safety filters introduces goal insertion attacks and SIREN, a tool that automatically discovers misconfigured-filter vulnerabilities from normal user interactions, finding attacks in all vulnerable SPARK scenarios, tripling attack discoveries over a baseline, and inducing real robot collisions; CoCarry releases a dual-human cooperative carrying dataset and a Contact-Support-Structured Retargeting method that attains ~0.9995 contact preservation and 67–71% penetration reduction versus GMR and OmniRetarget on A3 humanoids, and 0.9906 contact preservation with zero sliding on Unitree G1, with motions validated in simulation and real dual-humanoid experiments; and ViT-H uses a diffusion model to map monocular human videos directly to humanoid reference motions, with motion-tracking–guided preference optimization and an autoregressive variant for <400 ms latency teleoperation, outperforming reconstruction–retargeting–tracking pipelines on Unitree G1 in both motion quality and executability, and showing that scaling model and data further improves executability. (arXiv:2610.31923; arXiv:2610.32460; arXiv:2610.32966)
  • Additional studies explore failure recovery, emergent behavior, and control evaluation: Backtrack adds a lightweight failure-prediction head to frozen VLA policies and automatically triggers backtracking and retries when failure is likely, reducing failure by 19–48% and boosting success by up to 7.4 points on LIBERO-Long while consistently improving all 12 SmolVLA-based controllers tested on MetaWorld; a reinforcement learning study of 20 simulated lunar robots trained to maximize the mean temperature of the five coldest robots over a 354 h night finds emergent penguin-like aggregation and circulation that allows all robots to survive with 37% less stored energy than a motionless huddle and within 13% of a physics-based energy lower bound, transferring qualitatively from 20 to 40 robots without retraining; and evaluation of LeWorldModel (LeWM) via Gram-matrix-based action sensitivity on Reacher and OGBench Cube reveals that alignment with environment action geometry improves at larger perturbation scales, but model errors remain higher than an environment-based predictor, emphasizing the need for scale-aware assessment and leaving downstream control impacts unresolved. (arXiv:2610.32941; arXiv:2610.31868; arXiv:2610.31716)

TravSUITE: Traversability via Self-Supervised, Uncertainty-Aware IRL and Terrain EstimationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes TravSUITE, a traversability system for high-speed off-road navigation that combines an efficient voxel mapper using visual foundation models to build a geometric-semantic local map with a unified neural network predicting BEV geometry, semantics, speed, cost, and uncertainty, trained entirely via self-supervised tasks such as map inpainting and inverse reinforcement learning. Ablation studies and comparisons to state-of-the-art off-road path planning methods indicate that both cost learning and auxiliary map inpainting each significantly improve planning quality, and their combination is necessary to achieve state-of-the-art performance. In real-world hardware experiments, using TravSUITE’s inpainting and risk adaptation based on its uncertainty estimates reduces navigation errors by 80% and increases autonomous traversal speeds by 5% compared to a baseline without these components.Link
DexPort: A Hand-Held Skill Interface for Dexterous Hand Data CollectionStanford University; UC San DiegoThe paper introduces DexPort, a portable hand-held data collection interface that uses the same high-DoF robotic hand hardware for both demonstration and deployment, recording commanded joint targets and measured joint states while allowing operators to specify wrist motion and choose or adjust pre-defined finger coordination skills instead of teleoperating all joints individually. Using over 2,500 real-world demonstrations collected with DexPort across four dexterous manipulation tasks, behavior cloning on recorded wrist motions and hand commands (without explicit skill labels) yields better policies than using only joint states, with task success improving from 11% to 64% when using commands instead of states. Including in-the-wild data collected with DexPort substantially improves generalization, increasing task success from 36% to 78% compared to training without such data on the evaluated tasks.Link
Graphs of Trajectory Bundles for Nonlinear Motion PlanningMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes Graphs of Trajectory Bundles (GTB), a motion planning method for systems with nonlinear or black-box dynamics that combines sampling-based motion planning, trajectory optimization, and graph search on a graph-of-convex-sets decomposition of the workspace to iteratively build a graph of trajectory bundles capturing local dynamics at each vertex. By solving a shortest-path problem over the trajectory-bundle graph, GTB produces iterates that become maximally dynamically feasible in the limit and satisfy system dynamics, collision avoidance, and additional safety or specification constraints by construction of the underlying graph, enabling planning for highly nonlinear, unstable, and black-box systems with complex constraints.Link
EgoForesight: Effectively Scaling Policy Learning with Noisy Egocentric ActionsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces EgoForesight, a vision-language-action framework that jointly learns from high-quality robot demonstrations and noisy egocentric video-derived actions via a joint vision–action flow, combining reliability-conditioned corruption, reliability-weighted regression, and privileged-future representation distillation to reduce the impact of noisy action labels and exploit demonstrated outcomes. Across the tested pretraining regimes, EgoForesight scales more effectively than baseline methods, reducing real-world open-loop action mean-squared error by 20.5% and improving RoboCasa few-shot success by 8.1 percentage points at 30,000 hours of pretraining data. After post-training, EgoForesight achieves strong absolute performance on both simulated and real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, indicating that its outcome-informed and reliability-aware training mechanisms transfer to practical control settings.Link
SceneBloom: Agentic Indoor Environment Generation with Realistic and Diverse Layouts for Embodied LearningNew York University; UC San DiegoThe paper presents SceneBloom, an agentic framework for generating simulation-ready indoor environments by combining a hierarchical diffusion-based visual layout sampler that arranges furniture within functional zones with a contract-guided 3D compiler that uses extracted spatial relations for geometric fitting and local pose correction. Across five room types, SceneBloom produces 3D scenes with higher visual quality and physical stability than compared methods, and is the only approach reported to have no detected mesh collisions or out-of-bounds objects while generating layouts closer to the distribution of real rooms than all baselines. Object navigation policies trained solely in SceneBloom-generated scenes achieve higher success rates on held-out real scans than policies trained on an equal number of real training scans, suggesting that the generated environments can be more effective for training embodied agents than limited real data of the same quantity.Link
Not an Accident: Exploiting Safety Filter Inadequacy in Humanoid RobotsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper analyzes safety filters for humanoid robots and shows that their formal collision-avoidance guarantees depend critically on task- and environment-specific parameter configurations, which are difficult to set correctly in practice and can lead to failures after deployment. The authors propose goal insertion attacks and develop SIREN, an automated tool that discovers vulnerable inserted goals for a given task and environment, enabling adversaries with only normal user interaction capabilities to exploit misconfigured safety filters. Experiments on the SPARK dataset and a real humanoid robot show that SIREN identifies attacks in all vulnerable scenarios and finds three times as many attacks as a baseline, and can induce actual robot collisions, demonstrating that current safety filters can themselves introduce exploitable safety risks.Link
Emergent Cooperative Thermoregulation in Lunar Swarm RoboticsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe study uses reinforcement learning with an evolution-strategies estimator to train a single shared policy for a swarm of twenty lunar robots that maximizes the mean temperature of the five coldest robots at the end of a simulated 354 h lunar night, without explicitly encoding positional or motion objectives. Despite the minimal reward specification, the learned policy exhibits emergent cooperative thermoregulation behaviors analogous to emperor penguins, with the swarm aggregating and robots circulating between a sheltered core and exposed rim, improving thermal survival. Across ten runs, the emergent strategy yields a survival threshold of 155.6 ± 1.3 pcm-h (with 1 pcm-h = 7.2 kJ), which is 37% lower stored energy per robot than a motionless huddle and within 13% of a physics-based lower bound, allowing all robots to survive at a budget where a motionless huddle loses six robots, and the behavior transfers from 20 to 40 robots without retraining and without requiring shared spatial reference or physical contact.Link
RobustRLlib: A Unified Library and Benchmark for Robust Reinforcement Learning AlgorithmsUC Berkeley; UC San DiegoThe paper introduces RobustRLlib, a unified algorithm-centric library and benchmark that integrates 17 representative robust reinforcement learning algorithms (including online, offline, and safe RL methods) under a common interface and provides an enhanced evaluation platform spanning diverse real-world shifts and advanced humanoid and vision-language-action tasks. A systematic evaluation across 273 perturbation configurations using RobustRLlib yields an up-to-date capability map of current robust RL methods, identifying regimes where they remain effective and highlighting substantial remaining robustness gaps. As an open-source and extensible codebase, RobustRLlib is designed to facilitate reproducible application of existing robust RL algorithms to new problems and to support the development and comparison of new robust RL methods under a standardized evaluation protocol.Link
Does LeWorldModel Preserve Local Action Geometry?Massachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper evaluates pretrained LeWorldModel (LeWM) on how well it preserves local action geometry by comparing action sensitivity via Gram matrices between simulator and model responses across three perturbation scales on nearly a thousand states from Reacher and OGBench Cube. At the smallest perturbation scale, the most sensitive action directions in LeWM differ from the environment’s by median angles of about 36° on Reacher and 42° on Cube, and increasing the perturbation magnitude tenfold reduces median normalized Gram discrepancy by roughly 70% on Reacher and 47% on Cube, indicating scale-dependent agreement. On held-out action directions, environment-based Gram predictions achieve median embedding response errors of 17% and 28% on Reacher and Cube, respectively, versus 26% and 38% for an isotropic reference; LeWM’s errors are higher at this scale, suggesting that scale-specific evaluation is important and leaving the impact of these discrepancies on control performance as an open question.Link
KiRoSe: Using Kinematic Data for Efficient Segmentation of Robot DemonstrationsUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes KiRoSe, a framework for segmenting robot demonstrations that focuses on robot kinematic data by introducing Sparse Motion Tokens (SMTs), a discrete vocabulary of recurring, temporally localized kinematic events learned via encoding kinematic trajectories with temporal segment supervision and quantizing the resulting features. Using subtask annotations to supervise an open language model on SMT sequences, KiRoSe automatically segments demonstrations into temporal and semantic subsegments, substantially outperforming baseline segmentation methods in F1 quality on human-labeled data and transferring zero-shot to unseen robot embodiments and egocentric human motion while preserving open-vocabulary language understanding. Beyond segmentation, KiRoSe can track task progress on an unseen robot and detect when an action is being reversed using only the SMT-based kinematic representations and learned language model, without relying on raw video streams.Link
StackGen3D: Stable 3D Block Stack Generation from Multiview SilhouettesStanford UniversityThe paper proposes StackGen3D, a two-stage framework that first uses a physics-informed graph neural network (PI-GNN) as a fast stability predictor to curate a dataset of stable block assemblies from given block inventories and three orthographic silhouettes, and then trains a conditional flow-matching model to generate diverse 3D block configurations matching the target silhouettes. Preliminary experiments indicate that PI-GNN generalizes from simple stacks to complex structures substantially better than a parameter-matched multilayer perceptron for stability prediction. For structure generation, using Best-of-10 selection improves the physical stability rate of sampled structures from 45.3% to 72.4% while maintaining high silhouette fidelity with an IoU of 0.970, without relying on physics simulation at inference time.Link
If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAsETH Zurich; Stanford UniversityThe paper introduces Backtrack, a lightweight failure detection and recovery mechanism for frozen vision-language-action (VLA) policies that trains a small failure prediction head on outcome-labeled rollouts to monitor internal representations and trigger automatic backtracking and retry when failure probability exceeds a threshold, without policy retraining or additional models for control. Across LIBERO benchmarks, including LIBERO-Long and three shorter suites, Backtrack reduces failure rates of three different VLA policies (SmolVLA, π0.5, π0-FAST) by 19–48% on LIBERO-Long, increasing success by up to 7.4 percentage points, while having smaller effects where baseline success is already 85–99%. On MetaWorld, all twelve tested controller configurations using SmolVLA with Backtrack outperform the corresponding unassisted policies, indicating that the approach generalizes across tasks and control configurations.Link

See What I Need, Dream What I Want: A Navigation Foundation Model on a Self-Constructed Representation SpaceDataCanvas; Peking University; University College London, University of LondonThe paper proposes RepresentNav, a navigation foundation model that constructs its own visual representation space from multiple vision foundation models via a compact three-layer transformer over multi-layer features and uses a latent chain-of-thought "dream"—a joint-embedding predictive architecture—to predict next-scene representations before each action for navigation. RepresentNav achieves new state-of-the-art performance on VLNVerse, VLN-CE R2R, EVT-Bench, and two point-goal benchmarks; for example, on VLNVerse fine-grained it attains 83.80% success, 20.05 percentage points higher than the prior state of the art, and on EVT-Bench distracted tracking it reaches 85.5% success with a single camera (+10.1 percentage points). Using the self-constructed representation space and the dreaming mechanism reduces collisions by about 25%, outperforms simple feature concatenation with the same encoders, and yields a single latent that can be decoded into depth, semantics, and 3D occupancy for the near-future scene more accurately than applying pretrained encoders to the real future frame, despite occupancy being unsupervised.Link
FORCA: A Force-Aware Coding Agent for Contact-Rich Robot ManipulationTsinghua University; University of Illinois Urbana-ChampaignThe paper presents FORCA, a coding agent that, given language instructions, visual observations, and wrist force/torque measurements, autonomously writes and iteratively refines Python programs for contact probing and force-responsive motion in contact-rich manipulation, by inspecting force logs and visual outcomes between executions to diagnose failures and adjust both motion and force-feedback control logic, without demonstrations, task-specific training, or simulation. Across five real-robot tasks, FORCA improves performance over a vision-only variant of the same coding agent, achieving higher grasp accuracy, higher overall task success, and reduced contact loss in tasks such as wiping and peeling.Link
Multimodal MPC via Bayesian Set Optimization for Dynamic Manipulation of Deformable Linear ObjectsIstituto Italiano di TecnologiaThe paper proposes BM2PC, a Bayesian Multimodal Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for dynamic manipulation of deformable linear objects that formulates multimodal planning as a set optimization problem over a control-as-inference posterior, maintaining multiple promising control modes via efficient incremental updates and incorporating an execution dynamics model to compensate for execution mismatch in fast manipulation. In simulation and hardware experiments on rope tracking, BM2PC generalizes better to unseen trajectories than a learning-based baseline without requiring task-specific policy training or fine-tuning, achieving accurate long-horizon tracking and reducing RMSE by 44.0% on the longest real-world tracking task. For obstacle avoidance with deformable linear objects, BM2PC attains performance comparable to existing multimodal MPC methods while reducing planning time by 29% to 66%, demonstrating improved computational efficiency within a unified multimodal MPC framework.Link
Geometry-Aware Reinforcement Learning for Close-Fitting Wearable InsertionMicrosoft Research Asia; University of Edinburgh; Waseda UniversityThe paper formulates robot-assisted dressing as a geometry-aware insertion problem that focuses on task-relevant spatial relationships between the body part and garment opening, and proposes a reinforcement learning framework combining a staged curriculum (from coarse alignment to insertion and placement) with a finger-wise insertion soft gate that provides continuous feedback on each finger’s insertion progress and suppresses premature insertion. In rigid and deformable bracelet dressing simulations, the full framework (curriculum plus insertion gate) achieves 84.7 ± 1.2% success on post-training deformable evaluations, with ablations showing that the insertion gate yields the largest individual performance gain and that adding the curriculum on top achieves the best results. Deployed on a real robotic system dressing a mannequin hand, the same learned policy succeeds in 15 out of 20 trials, indicating transfer from simulation to hardware for close-fitting wearable insertion tasks.Link
From Tabletop to Walking Bot: Deploying a VLA Model on a Legged Manipulator RobotIstituto Italiano di Tecnologia (IIT)The work studies deploying a vision-language-action (VLA) model, originally developed for tabletop manipulation, on a legged manipulator platform (Unitree Go2 with an AgileX Piper arm), analyzing how embodiment changes require adaptations in data collection, model adaptation, and inference design to achieve closed-loop loco-manipulation that jointly coordinates base navigation, body posture, and end-effector motion. Empirical analysis on a pick-and-place task shows that expanding demonstration coverage reduces held-out end-effector error by 30%, and that adding secondary skills such as pushing introduces new behavioral modes without degrading baseline grasping performance. Closed-loop real-world experiments validate that dataset composition strongly shapes success and failure modes of the deployed VLA policy, supporting the proposed training and deployment pipeline for more complex loco-manipulation tasks.Link
MAST-VLA: Skill-Conditioned Adaptation with Trajectory-Affordance Priors for Multi-Skill Robot ManipulationEast China Normal University; Shanghai Innovation Institute; Tianjin University; Tongji UniversityThe paper introduces MAST-VLA, a dual-conditioning framework for multi-skill robot manipulation that augments a vision-language-action policy with skill-conditioned LoRA Mixture-of-Experts adapters in the language backbone—guided by skill-query tokens and routing biases—and with a trajectory-affordance prior that converts predicted affordance heatmaps into patch-level spatial priors for the action head. On the CALVIN ABC→D long-horizon multi-skill cross-scene generalization benchmark, MAST-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance, reaching an average sequence length of 4.65 over five-subtask evaluation sequences, indicating improved multi-skill execution compared with prior methods. Real-robot experiments on multi-skill task sequences further show that MAST-VLA can be deployed under real-world perception and control noise, supporting its practical applicability for generalist manipulation.Link
CoCarry: Contact-Support-Structured Retargeting for Dual-Humanoid Cooperative CarryingAGIBOT; RobotScale.AI; Shanghai Innovation Institute; Technical University of Munich; Tongji UniversityThe paper presents CoCarry, a cooperative carrying dataset comprising 220 synchronized two-person motion sequences across 21 task schemes, five shared-object geometries, four terrain types, and about 61 minutes of motion, and proposes Contact-Support-Structured Retargeting, which jointly refines two humanoids’ nominal motions around a prescribed object trajectory using cooperation-aware object-local hand anchors and complementary object-interaction and support structures to preserve grasps and coordinated support. Across all 220 CoCarry sequences retargeted to two A3 humanoids, the proposed retargeting method achieves 0.9995 contact preservation, reduces penetration duration by 67.0% and 71.0% relative to GMR and OmniRetarget, respectively, and maintains a 100% retargeting success rate. On CORE4D with Unitree G1 humanoids, the method obtains 0.9906 contact preservation with zero measured sliding, and generated reference motions are further validated via physics simulation and real-world dual-humanoid cooperative carrying experiments, demonstrating suitability for downstream control.Link
OWLS: Open-world Language-conditioned Speed Fields for Zero-Shot Rover NavigationJet Propulsion Laboratory, California Institute of Technology; University of MichiganThe paper proposes OWLS (Open-world Language-conditioned Speed Fields), a zero-shot rover navigation method that uses an open-vocabulary vision-language model to score pixels in a single camera image given a natural-language description of traversable terrain, projects these scores onto the ground, applies platform-specific morphological operations, and uses the result as a speed field for minimum-time planning so that terrain appearance and obstacle clearance jointly shape the trajectory. OWLS operates without any task-specific training, terrain annotation, persistent mapping, or exteroceptive sensors beyond a monocular camera, relying solely on semantic information from the vision-language model as a proxy for physical traversability. The approach is evaluated on a physical rover both indoors and off-road, on simulated Mars and Moon rovers across 200 procedurally generated terrains, and on real planetary imagery, demonstrating its applicability to diverse navigation environments, including planetary-like settings.Link
Natural-Language to Nav2: Operational Policies for Costmap-Based NavigationDaegu Gyeongbuk Institute of Science and Technology (DGIST)The paper proposes a natural-language policy interface for ROS 2 Nav2 in which a local large language model converts English instructions into JSON policies specifying destinations, spatial constraints, and temporal or event conditions, which are then projected into the existing costmap-based navigation stack via three adapters without modifying the planner or controller. On 150 researcher-authored English commands, four local language models generate 107–142 correct accepted policies compared to 44 for a rule-based parser, with 3–20 incorrect acceptances per model, and fault-injection tests show that the system rejects eight endpoint/JSON faults and 29 validator-stage faults. In navigation experiments using correct task policies, the system achieves 18.4–24.5% lower mean travel times in simulation and 23.1–35.0% lower mean travel times on a TurtleBot3 testbed for Tasks 1–3 compared to a baseline without such policies, and demonstrations with a native KeepoutFilter and two-robot setups evaluate keepouts and shared policy projection behavior.Link
ViT-H: Generative Video-To-Humanoid Control with Motion Tracking FeedbackBeijing Institute for General Artificial Intelligence; Beijing Key Laboratory of Brain-Computer Interface and Mental Health Modulation; PKU-Wuhan Institute for AI; Peking University; State Key Lab of General AI; XS VisionThe paper introduces ViT-H, a video-to-humanoid control framework that uses a diffusion model to directly generate humanoid reference motions from monocular human videos, bypassing explicit human motion reconstruction and retargeting, and then leverages motion-tracking outcomes with direct preference optimization to post-train the generator for improved executability. An autoregressive variant of ViT-H is proposed to enable real-time humanoid video teleoperation, achieving sub-400 ms latency. Experiments on the Unitree G1 robot report that ViT-H outperforms state-of-the-art reconstruction–retargeting–tracking pipelines in both motion quality and executability, and that scaling model size and both pre-training and post-training data, together with motion-tracking feedback, consistently improves executability across different motion trackers.Link
