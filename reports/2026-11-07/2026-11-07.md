## Executive Summary

- Several works advance robot manipulation and navigation by coupling high-level reasoning with low-level control: MessyNav uses VLM-based affordance reasoning to plan zero-shot navigation in cluttered real-world scenes, boosting mobile-manipulator success rates by 27 percentage points over the strongest baseline; Semantics-to-Contact leverages VLM-based semantic focusing plus residual RL with force feedback to improve robustness on six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks; GPWM introduces a 3D generative particle world model trained on 25.5M-frame ParticleWorlds that cuts prediction error by 31% vs. prior learned-dynamics baselines and enables effective latent-space planning for pushing and picking in simulation; Latent RL Prior distills privileged RL policies into a frozen flow-matching action decoder and learns a visual interface that matches or surpasses full-data baselines on dexterous manipulation using 5–10× fewer demos; and Spatial-UMI-Dream pretrains a 5B-parameter RGB-D world action model that improves in-domain and transfer success on a 20-task RoboTwin-UMI benchmark and outperforms baselines by up to 24.2 percentage points on six real-world UMI tasks. ([arXiv:2611.07417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07417); [arXiv:2611.07532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07532); [arXiv:2611.08338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08338); [arXiv:2611.08458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08458); [arXiv:2611.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07229))
- New benchmarks and evaluation frameworks target grounded robot reasoning and realistic testing of world models: E2W-Bench links dynamic hybrid scene graphs with real product manuals and, with the Embodied RAG++ retrieval framework, achieves pair accuracy up to 1.00 on capability-grounded robot–object assignment under physical constraints while reducing preprocessing time and token usage vs. baselines; RoboARCade defines long-horizon MuJoCo manipulation tasks across three embodiments and shows current multimodal agents achieve only 43.6–49.8% success with frequent misreporting, though in-context demonstrations and self-authored prompts can raise scores to ~72–73%; an empirical audit of test-time scaling for world-action models finds limited exploitable decision signal, modest realized speedups (≤1.25× decode-excluded), and sensitivity to a previously undetected rendering bug, arguing for multi-premise evaluation beyond task success; VisionSIM introduces a physically grounded multi-modal sensor simulator and large indoor dataset for heterogeneous sensor suites, enabling controlled cross-modality comparisons and reproduction of modality-specific failure modes; and RoboTwin-UMI provides a 20-task fisheye-based simulation benchmark to test embodiment and environment robustness of UMI policies. ([arXiv:2611.07517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07517); [arXiv:2611.08209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08209); [arXiv:2610.41970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41970); [arXiv:2611.08368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08368); [arXiv:2611.07229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07229))
- Humanoid and whole-body robot control systems are extended for social interaction, compliant coordination, and accurate embodiment: Social Humanoid combines a hierarchical sequence model with a whole-body tracking controller and an adaptively retargeted 260-hour Human-Humanoid Interaction benchmark to generate coordinated social motions in simulation and on hardware, including through occlusions; CRISP decomposes a humanoid into dynamically coupled subsystems and uses NMPC over interaction wrenches and GRFs to achieve stable dual-arm trajectory tracking and whole-body coordination on a G1 platform; HITCH introduces a whole-body harness where a VLM selects 3D goal points and a learned tracker executes coherent pelvis, torso, hand, walking, and gaze motions, reaching up to 95% task success on SIMPLE with only five trajectories per task and demonstrating box carrying and goal walking on a real G1 without real training data; AffectiveVLA adds intent-gated social-touch recognition, impedance adjustment, and safe-stop mechanisms to a VLA pipeline on a Unitree G1, raising macro-F1 for a GR00T-based policy from 0.75 to 0.90 under real-time latency; and a whole-body elasto-geometrical calibration method jointly identifies waist and dual-arm kinematics and compliances, reducing mean held-out pose errors to 0.50–1.11 mm and outperforming adapted baselines by 17–63% across datasets, largely via improved waist compliance modeling. ([arXiv:2611.08407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08407); [arXiv:2611.07268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07268); [arXiv:2611.08039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08039); [arXiv:2611.07712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07712); [arXiv:2611.07560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07560))
- New methods and analyses address safety, validation, and formation control in autonomous systems: a tutorial-style overview of safety certificate synthesis unifies Hamilton–Jacobi reachability and control barrier function methods, comparing them on two vehicle-system benchmarks to give practical guidance for safety-critical control; a validation study of the G-Maneuver stunt-driving controller advocates training-independent tests via varying physical parameters and alternative acceptance metrics, showing that a looser terminal-heading score can mis-rank unsafe settings and identifying which kinematic vs. force/roll indicators transfer reliably from simulation to real tests; a distributed two-layer estimation-control scheme for differential-drive robot formations uses a single absolute anchor, graph-proximal estimators with finite-step consensus, and a Lyapunov-analyzed controller to achieve locally exponentially convergent rigid formations, validated in Gazebo and with five physical robots; and an active environment-modification framework for UAV VIO triggers feature creation by dropping markers or wall markings based on information metrics, cutting VIO position RMSE by about 35% in corridor experiments relative to baseline VIO. ([arXiv:2611.07537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07537); [arXiv:2611.07516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07516); [arXiv:2611.07163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07163); [arXiv:2611.07687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07687))
- Advances in perception, sensing, and tactile interaction support richer robot feedback: XM4D formulates camera motion and non-rigid deformation as rotation-only QCQPs with SDP relaxations solved via a Burer–Monteiro Riemannian optimizer, yielding globally certifiable solutions that scale to thousands of frames and provide state-of-the-art geometry for dynamic Gaussian splatting; VisionSIM models raw sensor physics for modalities such as event, SPAD, iToF/dToF, thermal, and LiDAR, exposing parameters to reproduce real failure modes and releasing a large synchronized indoor dataset; TactileLab proposes a rigid-body-based simulated tactile flow plus depth representation and a policy architecture that, in simulation, learns efficient contact-rich behaviors and transfers via a real-to-sim translation pipeline to diverse real-world tactile-manipulation tasks; a multimodal haptic display prototype combines particle jamming and water-based thermal transfer, achieving a 300% stiffness modulation and 10–50°C uniform surface temperatures controlled within 0.2°C; and an event-based framework tracks PWM-encoded LED markers with shared frequency but distinct duty cycles, using dual-rate asynchronous tracking to maintain LED identities under fast motion and enabling onboard 6-DoF pose estimation for closed-loop quadrotor hovering and target-following under HDR illumination. ([arXiv:2611.07902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07902); [arXiv:2611.08368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08368); [arXiv:2611.07881](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07881); [arXiv:2611.08398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08398); [arXiv:2611.07630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07630))
- Systems-level contributions target efficient resource use and capability-grounded scheduling on robots: an energy-aware scheduling policy for YOLO-World-S open-vocabulary detection switches between low-, mid-, and high-power modes based on odometry, map changes, and detector confidence, improving bottle/cup search mission success to 8/10 vs. 7/10 fixed baselines while cutting Jetson input power by 5.2% and OVD call rate by 34% in navigation trials and reducing power from 8.045 W to 7.301 W in a stationary microbenchmark; and Embodied RAG++ on E2W-Bench restricts document retrieval to candidate-object manuals, caches per-model capabilities, and applies deterministic spatial reasoning over robot and object states, achieving pair accuracy up to 0.90–1.00 with LLM backends while matching or beating baselines in preprocessing time and token efficiency for robot–object assignment under physical constraints. ([arXiv:2611.07663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07663); [arXiv:2611.07517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07517))

| GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle Motion | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper introduces GPWM, a generative particle world model that operates directly in 3D physical space using an object-aware multi-resolution transformer with flow matching and diffusion forcing to predict particle and rigid-body motion for rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials under passive physics and robotic interaction. Trained on the 25.5-million-frame ParticleWorlds corpus, GPWM reduces prediction error by 31% on average compared to the strongest prior learned-dynamics baseline across six physical domains, and a single Unified GPWM model outperforms per-domain baselines and even specialist GPWM models in two domains. | Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation | UC Berkeley | The paper proposes Latent RL Prior, a two-stage framework that first distills large-scale privileged-information RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context, then freezes this decoder and learns a visual interface that maps images to latent context using only action supervision for data-efficient visual policy learning, including shared decoders for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated dexterous manipulation tasks, Latent RL Prior outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of π₀.₅ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime, and on door lifting and bimanual assembly it matches or surpasses full-data baselines using 10× and 5× fewer demonstrations, respectively. | Training-Independent Validation of a Learning-Based Controller for Stunt Maneuvers | New York University | The paper argues for training-independent validation of learned vehicle controllers by varying physical parameters held fixed during training and using acceptance criteria not present in the training objective, and applies this to G-Maneuver, a learning-based stunt-driving policy with 90.11% success under its original conditions. Automotive safety analysis and coverage tests reveal that kinematic stunt-driving indicators (heading change, sideslip, speed-normalized yaw rate) match well between simulation and real-world tests, while force and roll indicators do not, identifying which metrics can reliably support safe deployment. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07516 |

| When Does Test-Time Search Help World Action Models? An Empirical Audit of Candidate Selection and Simulator Integrity | Carnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford University; UC Berkeley | The paper empirically audits test-time scaling for world-action models on Cosmos Policy and RoboCasa, finding that on clean data a learned value-based candidate selector performs 1.66 percentage points worse than random selection and that even a hindsight oracle yields only a 1.40-percentage-point gain with an episode-level confidence interval including zero, indicating limited exploitable decision signal because 82% of states share identical candidate outcomes.
