Submission date covered: 2026-11-06

Executive Summary

  • Several works advance robot manipulation and navigation by coupling high-level reasoning with low-level control: MessyNav uses VLM-based affordance reasoning to plan zero-shot navigation in cluttered real-world scenes, boosting mobile-manipulator success rates by 27 percentage points over the strongest baseline; Semantics-to-Contact leverages VLM-based semantic focusing plus residual RL with force feedback to improve robustness on six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks; GPWM introduces a 3D generative particle world model trained on 25.5M-frame ParticleWorlds that cuts prediction error by 31% vs. prior learned-dynamics baselines and enables effective latent-space planning for pushing and picking in simulation; Latent RL Prior distills privileged RL policies into a frozen flow-matching action decoder and learns a visual interface that matches or surpasses full-data baselines on dexterous manipulation using 5–10× fewer demos; and Spatial-UMI-Dream pretrains a 5B-parameter RGB-D world action model that improves in-domain and transfer success on a 20-task RoboTwin-UMI benchmark and outperforms baselines by up to 24.2 percentage points on six real-world UMI tasks. (arXiv:2611.07417; arXiv:2611.07532; arXiv:2611.08338; arXiv:2611.08458; arXiv:2611.07229)
  • New benchmarks and evaluation frameworks target grounded robot reasoning and realistic testing of world models: E2W-Bench links dynamic hybrid scene graphs with real product manuals and, with the Embodied RAG++ retrieval framework, achieves pair accuracy up to 1.00 on capability-grounded robot–object assignment under physical constraints while reducing preprocessing time and token usage vs. baselines; RoboARCade defines long-horizon MuJoCo manipulation tasks across three embodiments and shows current multimodal agents achieve only 43.6–49.8% success with frequent misreporting, though in-context demonstrations and self-authored prompts can raise scores to ~72–73%; an empirical audit of test-time scaling for world-action models finds limited exploitable decision signal, modest realized speedups (≤1.25× decode-excluded), and sensitivity to a previously undetected rendering bug, arguing for multi-premise evaluation beyond task success; VisionSIM introduces a physically grounded multi-modal sensor simulator and large indoor dataset for heterogeneous sensor suites, enabling controlled cross-modality comparisons and reproduction of modality-specific failure modes; and RoboTwin-UMI provides a 20-task fisheye-based simulation benchmark to test embodiment and environment robustness of UMI policies. (arXiv:2611.07517; arXiv:2611.08209; arXiv:2610.41970; arXiv:2611.08368; arXiv:2611.07229)
  • Humanoid and whole-body robot control systems are extended for social interaction, compliant coordination, and accurate embodiment: Social Humanoid combines a hierarchical sequence model with a whole-body tracking controller and an adaptively retargeted 260-hour Human-Humanoid Interaction benchmark to generate coordinated social motions in simulation and on hardware, including through occlusions; CRISP decomposes a humanoid into dynamically coupled subsystems and uses NMPC over interaction wrenches and GRFs to achieve stable dual-arm trajectory tracking and whole-body coordination on a G1 platform; HITCH introduces a whole-body harness where a VLM selects 3D goal points and a learned tracker executes coherent pelvis, torso, hand, walking, and gaze motions, reaching up to 95% task success on SIMPLE with only five trajectories per task and demonstrating box carrying and goal walking on a real G1 without real training data; AffectiveVLA adds intent-gated social-touch recognition, impedance adjustment, and safe-stop mechanisms to a VLA pipeline on a Unitree G1, raising macro-F1 for a GR00T-based policy from 0.75 to 0.90 under real-time latency; and a whole-body elasto-geometrical calibration method jointly identifies waist and dual-arm kinematics and compliances, reducing mean held-out pose errors to 0.50–1.11 mm and outperforming adapted baselines by 17–63% across datasets, largely via improved waist compliance modeling. (arXiv:2611.08407; arXiv:2611.07268; arXiv:2611.08039; arXiv:2611.07712; arXiv:2611.07560)
  • New methods and analyses address safety, validation, and formation control in autonomous systems: a tutorial-style overview of safety certificate synthesis unifies Hamilton–Jacobi reachability and control barrier function methods, comparing them on two vehicle-system benchmarks to give practical guidance for safety-critical control; a validation study of the G-Maneuver stunt-driving controller advocates training-independent tests via varying physical parameters and alternative acceptance metrics, showing that a looser terminal-heading score can mis-rank unsafe settings and identifying which kinematic vs. force/roll indicators transfer reliably from simulation to real tests; a distributed two-layer estimation-control scheme for differential-drive robot formations uses a single absolute anchor, graph-proximal estimators with finite-step consensus, and a Lyapunov-analyzed controller to achieve locally exponentially convergent rigid formations, validated in Gazebo and with five physical robots; and an active environment-modification framework for UAV VIO triggers feature creation by dropping markers or wall markings based on information metrics, cutting VIO position RMSE by about 35% in corridor experiments relative to baseline VIO. (arXiv:2611.07537; arXiv:2611.07516; arXiv:2611.07163; arXiv:2611.07687)
  • Advances in perception, sensing, and tactile interaction support richer robot feedback: XM4D formulates camera motion and non-rigid deformation as rotation-only QCQPs with SDP relaxations solved via a Burer–Monteiro Riemannian optimizer, yielding globally certifiable solutions that scale to thousands of frames and provide state-of-the-art geometry for dynamic Gaussian splatting; VisionSIM models raw sensor physics for modalities such as event, SPAD, iToF/dToF, thermal, and LiDAR, exposing parameters to reproduce real failure modes and releasing a large synchronized indoor dataset; TactileLab proposes a rigid-body-based simulated tactile flow plus depth representation and a policy architecture that, in simulation, learns efficient contact-rich behaviors and transfers via a real-to-sim translation pipeline to diverse real-world tactile-manipulation tasks; a multimodal haptic display prototype combines particle jamming and water-based thermal transfer, achieving a 300% stiffness modulation and 10–50°C uniform surface temperatures controlled within 0.2°C; and an event-based framework tracks PWM-encoded LED markers with shared frequency but distinct duty cycles, using dual-rate asynchronous tracking to maintain LED identities under fast motion and enabling onboard 6-DoF pose estimation for closed-loop quadrotor hovering and target-following under HDR illumination. (arXiv:2611.07902; arXiv:2611.08368; arXiv:2611.07881; arXiv:2611.08398; arXiv:2611.07630)
  • Systems-level contributions target efficient resource use and capability-grounded scheduling on robots: an energy-aware scheduling policy for YOLO-World-S open-vocabulary detection switches between low-, mid-, and high-power modes based on odometry, map changes, and detector confidence, improving bottle/cup search mission success to 8/10 vs. 7/10 fixed baselines while cutting Jetson input power by 5.2% and OVD call rate by 34% in navigation trials and reducing power from 8.045 W to 7.301 W in a stationary microbenchmark; and Embodied RAG++ on E2W-Bench restricts document retrieval to candidate-object manuals, caches per-model capabilities, and applies deterministic spatial reasoning over robot and object states, achieving pair accuracy up to 0.90–1.00 with LLM backends while matching or beating baselines in preprocessing time and token efficiency for robot–object assignment under physical constraints. (arXiv:2611.07663; arXiv:2611.07517)

MessyNav: Zero-Shot Navigation via Manipulation in Messy EnvironmentsStanford University; UC BerkeleyThe paper proposes MessyNav, a zero-shot open-world interactive navigation framework that uses vision-language-model-based affordance reasoning (swipe, pick up, avoid) to generate high-level plans for navigating through cluttered real-world environments without task-specific training. On a mobile manipulator in real-world indoor and outdoor environments, MessyNav improves success rate by 27 percentage points over the strongest baseline and achieves statistically significant gains in a larger-scale offline evaluation.Link
Social Humanoid: Socially Responsive Human-Humanoid InteractionUC BerkeleyThe paper presents Social Humanoid, a hierarchical system for socially responsive whole-body motion on a 29-DoF humanoid robot, combining a high-level sequence model over shared human–humanoid tokens with a low-level whole-body tracking controller to generate motion conditioned on a human partner, including when the partner is occluded. The authors introduce a two-phase adaptive retargeting procedure to construct a 260-hour Human-Humanoid Interaction benchmark from five motion-capture datasets by retargeting one partner to the robot and morphing the other to preserve contact dynamics, reachability, and body scale. Experiments in simulation and on real hardware show that Social Humanoid produces coordinated, human-aligned behavior across diverse interaction types, demonstrating effective physical coordination with humans.Link
GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle MotionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces GPWM, a generative particle world model that operates directly in 3D physical space using an object-aware multi-resolution transformer with flow matching and diffusion forcing to predict particle and rigid-body motion for rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials under passive physics and robotic interaction. Trained on the 25.5-million-frame ParticleWorlds corpus, GPWM reduces prediction error by 31% on average compared to the strongest prior learned-dynamics baseline across six physical domains, and a single Unified GPWM model outperforms per-domain baselines and even specialist GPWM models in two domains. GPWM supports contact-rich manipulation planning by searching in its latent noise space instead of action space, which in simulation substantially outperforms action-space search on pushing and picking tasks, enabling open-loop goal-directed manipulation without reference trajectories or goal-specific policies.Link
Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous ManipulationUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes Latent RL Prior, a two-stage framework that first distills large-scale privileged-information RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context, then freezes this decoder and learns a visual interface that maps images to latent context using only action supervision for data-efficient visual policy learning, including shared decoders for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated dexterous manipulation tasks, Latent RL Prior outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of π₀.₅ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime, and on door lifting and bimanual assembly it matches or surpasses full-data baselines using 10× and 5× fewer demonstrations, respectively. Ablations show that directly conditioning the decoder on visual-to-latent predictions improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing through a pretrained state encoder, and that the learned decoder retains RL-acquired control knowledge as evidenced by robustness to unseen mass variations and linearly accessible contact-force information.Link
Training-Independent Validation of a Learning-Based Controller for Stunt ManeuversNew York UniversityThe paper argues for training-independent validation of learned vehicle controllers by varying physical parameters held fixed during training and using acceptance criteria not present in the training objective, and applies this to G-Maneuver, a learning-based stunt-driving policy with 90.11% success under its original conditions. Using a simulation-to-reality-to-simulation loop, the authors select safe operating zones for real-vehicle stunt trials with a 16.9% acceptance rate and show that a looser terminal-heading score can incorrectly rank the worst setting as best, illustrating the risk of misaligned validation metrics. Automotive safety analysis and coverage tests reveal that kinematic stunt-driving indicators (heading change, sideslip, speed-normalized yaw rate) match well between simulation and real-world tests, while force and roll indicators do not, identifying which metrics can reliably support safe deployment.Link
Embodied RAG++: Retrieval-Augmented Generation From What Exists to What Works in Embodied ScenesCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces E2W-Bench, a benchmark that links dynamic hybrid scene graphs with real product manuals to evaluate capability-grounded robot–object assignment under availability, mobility, vertical-reach, and connector constraints in human environments. The authors propose Embodied RAG++, a spatially grounded retrieval framework that restricts retrieval to manuals of candidate objects, caches capabilities per product model, and performs deterministic spatial reasoning over robot and object states to assess feasibility of assignments. On E2W-Bench, Embodied RAG++ achieves pair accuracy of 0.90 with Qwen2.5-32B and 1.00 with Llama3.3-70B, compared to at most 0.30 and 0.55 for baselines, while also attaining the shortest preprocessing time among retrieval-based methods and tying for the lowest total token usage.Link
Spatial-UMI-Dream: Scaling World Action Models with Metric Geometry for UMI ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents Spatial-UMI-Dream, a 5B-parameter spatial World Action Model pretrained in two stages on 605 hours of RGB-D UMI data to jointly predict future RGB, fisheye-aligned metric depth, and actions, using stereo-derived depth to make scene scale and spatial relationships explicit in a dual-gripper RGB-D representation. To evaluate UMI policies under embodiment and environment changes, the authors introduce RoboTwin-UMI, a 20-task simulation benchmark with wrist-mounted fisheye observations built on RoboTwin, enabling controlled tests of robustness and generalization without additional fine-tuning. On RoboTwin-UMI, Spatial-UMI-Dream attains 79.10% in-domain success and 72.15% success under combined embodiment and environment shifts, outperforming the strongest baseline by 4.65 and 10.30 percentage points respectively and showing the smallest embodiment-transfer drop; RGB-only pretraining reduces in-domain success by 5.75 percentage points compared with RGB-D, and in six real-world tasks the model achieves 85.0% average success, 24.2 percentage points above the strongest baseline.Link
When Does Test-Time Search Help World Action Models? An Empirical Audit of Candidate Selection and Simulator IntegrityCarnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford University; UC BerkeleyThe paper empirically audits test-time scaling for world-action models on Cosmos Policy and RoboCasa, finding that on clean data a learned value-based candidate selector performs 1.66 percentage points worse than random selection and that even a hindsight oracle yields only a 1.40-percentage-point gain with an episode-level confidence interval including zero, indicating limited exploitable decision signal because 82% of states share identical candidate outcomes. Token pruning reduces per-forward FLOPs by up to 47%, but measured end-to-end speedup reaches at most 1.15× on the deployed path (1.25× when excluding decode), falling short of a 1.5× target, and an adaptive-allocation experiment remains inconclusive under a power gate, highlighting a gap between nominal and realized efficiency gains. The audit uncovers a silent rendering corruption bug whose incidence increases along compute-heavy planning paths while leaving the official direct-policy results unaffected; the authors provide a detector, fix, and scripts and argue that test-time scaling should be evaluated as a chain of premises (decision signal, realized efficiency, evaluation integrity) rather than by success rate alone, with conclusions limited to the tested configuration.Link
RoboARCade: Benchmarking Agentic Robot ControlStanford UniversityThe paper introduces RoboARCade, a MuJoCo-based benchmark of long-horizon manipulation tasks across three robot embodiments that stresses in-context adaptation, memory, and reasoning for precise agentic robot control, with tasks packaged as self-contained data packs and agents interfaced via interchangeable APIs. Benchmarking shows current multimodal agents are far from solving the tasks: GPT-6 Astra reaches 49.8% success and Claude Fable 5.1 reaches 43.6%, with richer visual interfaces providing only 1.5 to 6.8 percentage points improvement at 1.7 to 2.3 times higher cost, and common failure modes involve suboptimal or imprecisely executed manipulation strategies rather than scene misunderstanding; agents also misreport success on 24%–39% of attempted subtasks. RoboARCade experiments indicate that agents can self-improve without human labels: for a five-task subset, a single successful in-context demonstration raises Astra's score from 53.5% to 73.0%, and a prompt written and iteratively revised by the agent itself achieves 72.0%.Link
Unified Overview of Safety Certificate Functions: Techniques from Hamilton--Jacobi Reachability and Control Barrier FunctionsMassachusetts Institute of Technology; Stanford UniversityThe paper provides a tutorial-style unified overview of safety certificate synthesis centered on Hamilton–Jacobi reachability and control barrier functions, covering their general formulations and both classical and learning-based methods for constructing certificate functions that guarantee safety specifications. The authors introduce two vehicle-system benchmark problems of differing complexity to compare certificate quality and safety-critical control effectiveness across synthesis methods, and use these comparisons to offer practical guidance on method selection for autonomous systems safety.Link
Learning Robotic World Models with Semantic and Visual FeedbackCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper proposes RoboImagine, a post-training framework for autoregressive robotic world models that compares diverse candidate future trajectories using a frozen multimodal semantic evaluator plus pixel- and perceptual-level visual feedback, optimized via group-relative advantages with fixed initial context and rolling branch histories for long-horizon prediction. On the RT-1 manipulation dataset, RoboImagine improves visual prediction quality over continued supervised fine-tuning under matched training conditions, increasing PSNR by 1.26 dB and reducing LPIPS by 11.93%. Ablation studies on RT-1, BridgeV2, and RoboCasa show that diverse search improves prediction quality, combining semantic and visual feedback yields higher external multimodal evaluation scores than visual-only feedback at similar visual fidelity, and rolling histories further enhance long-horizon prediction.Link
XM4D: 4D Reconstruction through Certifiable Geometric OptimizationUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces XM4D, a 4D reconstruction framework that separates camera motion estimation and non-rigid deformation into two structured QCQPs over rotations, each admitting an SDP relaxation solved via a GPU-accelerated Burer-Monteiro Riemannian optimizer with certifiable global optimality. For camera motion, XM4D lifts static point tracks to 3D using depth predictions and formulates a scaled bundle adjustment that jointly estimates camera poses, depth scales, and landmarks while analytically eliminating translations and landmarks, reducing the problem to a rotation-only QCQP. Experiments on seven benchmarks show that XM4D achieves accurate camera motion estimation on static and dynamic scenes, scales to thousands of frames efficiently, and attains state-of-the-art novel-view rendering quality after using the recovered geometry to initialize dynamic Gaussian splatting.Link
VisionSIM: Beyond Idealized Sensors - A Multi-Modal Simulator for Robot PerceptionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents VisionSIM, a physically grounded multi-modal simulation platform that models raw measurement processes for diverse sensor modalities (including event, SPAD, iToF/dToF, thermal, and LiDAR), exposing fine-grained physical parameters to reproduce modality-specific failure modes such as pileup, glare, phase wrapping, saturation, motion artifacts, refractory drops, and history-dependent emission. VisionSIM enables principled cross-modality comparison by simulating heterogeneous sensor suites on identical scenes and trajectories, going beyond idealized sensor models used in general-purpose simulators. The authors release VisionSIM together with a large indoor dataset containing synchronized raw measurements and rich ground-truth annotations to support research on realistic robot perception with heterogeneous sensors.Link
A Multimodal Haptic Interface for Simultaneous Softness and Thermal ModulationMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces a multimodal haptic display that can simultaneously modulate perceived softness and temperature, combining particle jamming for stiffness control with water-based thermal transfer for heating and cooling. Mechanical evaluation of the prototype demonstrates a controllable stiffness increase of 300%, while thermal characterization shows uniform surface temperature across a 10°C–50°C range with closed-loop control maintaining temperature within 0.2°C of the setpoint.Link

Distributed Geometric Optimization and Control of Rigid Differential-Drive Formations from Relative-Position MeasurementsEge University; University of WaterlooThe paper proposes a distributed, two-layer estimation-control scheme for rigid formations of differential-drive robots using one absolute position anchor, absolute headings, body-frame relative position measurements, and neighbor communication to regulate formation shape, position, and orientation. Graph-proximal estimators are derived to reconstruct the formation centroid, the anchor vector, and centered coordinates; exact proximal updates reach consensus in finitely many steps, and a finite number of primal-dual iterations yields bounded reconstruction error. A controller that combines proximal centroid/orientation steps, distance-gradient shape regulation, and a finite corrective rotation is shown via discrete Lyapunov analysis to achieve local exponential convergence once estimators converge, and is validated in ROS 2 Gazebo and on five physical robots.Link
CRISP: Constraint-Resolved Interactions between Subsystems for Predictive Whole-Body CooperationUniversity of Texas at El PasoThe paper presents CRISP, a layered control framework that decomposes a humanoid into dynamically interconnected subsystems—a single rigid-body torso and floating-base arm and lower-body models—linked by holonomic constraints whose Lagrange multipliers encode interaction wrenches at shoulders and between dual-arm end-effectors. A high-level nonlinear model predictive controller jointly optimizes subsystem state trajectories, arm torques, ground reaction forces, and interaction wrenches, enabling online design of interaction forces while preserving dynamic coupling and mapping optimized GRFs into lower-body joint torques for whole-body coordination. Numerical simulations and hardware experiments on a G1 humanoid demonstrate that the framework produces interaction wrenches consistent with simulated contact measurements, achieves cooperative dual-arm trajectory tracking, and maintains stable whole-body coordination under online NMPC execution.Link
Semantics-to-Contact: A Stagewise Framework for Robust Contact-Rich ManipulationDexForce Co., Ltd; Shenzhen Loop Area Institute; The Chinese University of Hong Kong, ShenzhenThe paper proposes Semantics-to-Contact (S2C), a stagewise framework for contact-rich manipulation that first uses a vision-language model to localize task-relevant regions across visually diverse scenes (semantic focusing) and then applies human-in-the-loop residual reinforcement learning with force feedback to refine fine-grained contact behaviors within the localized region. An object-centric visual augmentation strategy randomizes background appearance while preserving the robot and task-relevant objects to improve visual robustness of the learned policies. On six real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks, S2C achieves consistently higher success rates than strong baselines and maintains reliable performance under substantial visual and positional variations.Link
Whole-Body Elasto-Geometrical Calibration of Wheeled Humanoid Robots Using a Shared-Waist Dual-Chain ModelAgiBotThe paper introduces a whole-body elasto-geometrical calibration method for wheeled humanoid robots with a shared actuated waist, jointly identifying waist and dual-arm kinematics and compliances in a single dual-chain optimization using optical tracker measurements of target positions on both flanges. The kinematic model includes waist-base, parallel-axis, and branch parameters; arm compliances are driven by measured torques and waist compliances by gravity torques from a robot mass model, with Gaussian priors and evidence-maximizing scales providing regularization, while independent and sequential single-chain calibration arise as restricted cases whose waist information equals the sum of their Schur complements. On two robots with different waists and payload conditions, the method reduces mean held-out pose errors from 11.3–31.1 mm to 0.50–1.11 mm and achieves 17–63% lower mean errors than adaptations of two existing whole-body calibration methods across training-set sizes from 20 poses to full data, mainly due to improved waist compliance modeling; using an independently calibrated waist for both arms can increase the other arm’s error up to 3.21 mm.Link
Real-Time Event-Based LED Tracking and Pose Estimation with PWM-Encoded MarkersHunan University; Nanjing UniversityThe paper presents a real-time event-based framework for tracking PWM-encoded LED markers and estimating 6-DoF relative pose, where multiple LEDs share a common blinking frequency and encode identities via distinct duty cycles to reduce frequency usage and computation. A dual-rate asynchronous tracking scheme combines low-rate LED detection with high-rate raw-event updates to maintain robust identity association and accurate pixel-level LED tracking under rapid motion, enabling reliable 2D–3D correspondences for metric 6-DoF pose estimation. Experiments on a self-collected dataset show robustness to large motion flow and transient LED overlap, and deployment on a coordinated quadrotor–Mecanum robot platform demonstrates closed-loop drone hovering and target-following under time-varying HDR illumination on an onboard Jetson Orin NX.Link
Energy-Aware Open-Vocabulary Detection Scheduling for Low-Power Robotic AutonomyNew York University Abu DhabiThe paper proposes an energy-aware scheduling policy for open-vocabulary detection (OVD) on low-power mobile robots that switches among low-, mid-, and high-power YOLO-World-S modes based on odometry, map change, and detector confidence, instead of fixed-rate inference. On an AgileX LIMO robot over 40 real navigation trials searching for bottle and cup targets, the scheduler attains 8/10 mission success versus 7/10 for each fixed-rate baseline, while reducing mean Jetson input power by 5.2% and lowering OVD call rate by 34.0% compared to an 8 Hz high-rate baseline, although mission-window Jetson input energy is not always minimized due to longer mission durations. In a 60 s stationary microbenchmark isolating perception workload, the scheduler reduces mean Jetson input power from 8.045 W to 7.301 W and energy from 484.7 J to 438.1 J relative to the 8 Hz baseline, indicating that robot-state-aware OVD scheduling can save power while maintaining competitive task reliability.Link
Not Losing Your Way: Mitigating Visual-Inertial Odometry Drift through On-Demand Visual Feature CreationKRUSH Labs B.V.; University of Naples Federico IIThe paper proposes an active environment-modification framework for UAV visual-inertial odometry that creates visual features on demand by releasing ground markers or marking walls, using an information-based indicator with temporal confirmation and hysteresis to trigger interventions, without prior maps or extra localization sensors. In simulation across three environments with a 900-run factorial campaign, lower cruise speed and active marking are associated with reduced VIO divergence, with the accuracy of non-divergent runs depending on speed–strategy interactions. In corridor hardware experiments using OptiTrack for flight control while VIO-based marking decisions run online, the combined framework reduces reported position root mean square error by approximately 35% relative to baseline VIO, indicating that active feature creation can mitigate VIO drift.Link
AffectiveVLA: Safe and Intent-Aware VLA Control for Human-Humanoid Social InteractionSkolkovo Institute of Science and TechnologyThe paper presents AffectiveVLA, an intent-gated Vision-Language-Action pipeline for a Unitree G1 humanoid that uses a lightweight RGB-based model to infer human social-touch intent, convert it into language prompts for VLA policies, suppress motions under low-confidence predictions, adjust arm impedance via interaction-specific compliance profiles, and provide a voice-triggered latched safe-stop mechanism. Using about 1,300 VR-teleoperated multimodal interaction episodes to fine-tune four VLA models (GR00T, RLDX-1, Being-H0.5, UnifoLM-VLA-0), on-robot evaluation identifies GR00T as the best standalone model and shows that integrating intent recognition increases its macro-F1 score from 0.75 to 0.90 without exceeding a p95 intent-model inference latency of 6.27 ms. The results indicate that explicit intent gating in the control pipeline improves the reliability of VLA-based humanoid social touch interactions while maintaining real-time operation constraints.Link
TactileLab: Sim-to-Real Dexterous Robot Manipulation with Efficient Shear-Sensitive Tactile SimulationUnknown affiliationThe paper introduces TactileLab, a scalable multimodal tactile simulation framework built on IsaacLab that replaces soft-body shear modeling with a rigid-body-based ‘simulated tactile flow’ representation, estimating dense tangential motion at the contact interface and combining it with a contact depth field. It proposes a novel policy architecture that jointly processes long-horizon proprioceptive signals and rich tactile observations, showing in simulation that policies trained with simulated tactile flow learn efficiently across multiple contact-rich tasks and display emergent contact-aware behaviors. The work further presents a real-to-sim tactile translation method that maps real tactile image streams to simulated tactile flow and depth fields, along with a new tactile data collection procedure, and uses this pipeline to demonstrate sim-to-real deployment on diverse real-world tasks including dual-arm and dexterous in-hand manipulation with heterogeneous tactile sensors.Link
HITCH: Teaching a Vision-Language Model to Operate a Humanoid through a Whole-Body HarnessUnknown affiliationThe paper proposes HITCH, a whole-body harness interface for humanoids where a vision-language model selects image points whose 3D positions are recovered by perception, and a learned tracker executes commands for pelvis, torso, hands, walking, and gaze as coherent whole-body motions. Through analysis, the authors find that both accurate scene measurement and accurate command execution via HITCH contribute to increased task success when a VLM controls the humanoid. By fine-tuning a VLM on trajectories from teacher policies solving five base tasks through HITCH, the system achieves 51% success on SIMPLE and 42.5% on HumanoidArena without task-specific trajectories, and with only five trajectories per SIMPLE task reaches 95% SIMPLE success; on a real G1, it carries a box and walks to a goal despite using no real training data.Link
