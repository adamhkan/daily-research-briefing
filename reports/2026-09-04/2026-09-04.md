## Executive Summary

- UC Berkeley and ETH Zurich feature prominently today, with new methods for humanoid safe stopping, surgical dexterity, and multi-view surgical perception benchmarks.
- Mobile and autonomous driving motion planning advances include unified diffusion+search planning (DiffuSearch), risk-aware diffusion RL for driving (DiDrive), and better trajectory scoring datasets.
- Manipulation and whole-body control see progress in uncertainty-aware active mapping (MS-MEM), torque-sampling MPPI for compliant manipulation, and local-geometry-based dexterous transfer (DemoMimic).
- Humanoid and legged locomotion are pushed by world-model-augmented visual locomotion on constrained footholds (WM-LOCO) and reliability-weighted proprioceptive odometry (FOCUS).
- World models and VLA-centric control for manipulation are strengthened with sparse residual world models for object-centric pushing, 3D-aware world action models (SA-WAM), and cross-embodiment VLA transfer (ZETA).
- Navigation and multi-robot coordination contributions span efficient vision-language navigation (LookStep), terrain-adaptive greenhouse navigation, UAV task coordination with reactive avoidance (TriSAR), and evaluation frameworks for future-aware planners (PDT).

| Humanoid Safe Stop via Learned Stoppability Value | UC Berkeley | Safe-Stop casts emergency stopping for humanoids as a reach-avoid problem, pairing a learned stop policy with two complementary "stoppability" estimators: one learned from stop outcomes and one from Hamilton–Jacobi reachability. At runtime, the system commits to stopping only when both estimators indicate feasibility, otherwise handing off to a fall policy, yielding task-agnostic, transferable safety behavior across upstream controllers. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02358 |
| MACAW: Reliable And Efficient Surgical Debridement Using Monocular Adaptive Compact Attention Windows | UC Berkeley | MACAW combines image-plane visual servoing with a new monocular depth-control method using adaptive compact attention windows to automate surgical debridement on the da Vinci platform. The system reduces pixel offset from 37 to under 5 pixels in under 0.4 s and achieves over 90% success in both single-arm and bimanual debridement, significantly improving throughput versus procedural and learned baselines. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01961 |
| MV-dVRK: A Multi-Viewpoint Benchmark for Spatial Surgical Perception | ETH Zurich | MV-dVRK is the first ex-vivo surgical dataset with synchronized multi-stereo viewpoints, accurate surface geometry, and camera poses, enabling rigorous evaluation of 3D reconstruction in endoscopic conditions. Benchmarking shows multi-stereo and optimization-based multi-view methods substantially outperform current feed-forward foundation models in coverage and accuracy, and dynamic sequences support future work on deformable-tissue perception. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02717 |
