## Executive Summary

- Strong motion-planning and control advances span kinodynamic tree search (DFT*), real-time navigation (RTNav), certified safe driving trajectories, and soft/continuum actuator control.
- Manipulation-focused work ranges from thin-object and soft-gripper grasping to compact and history-aware VLA policies, rapid on-robot learning for dynamic skills, and robust failure recovery.
- Foundation-model-style world and action models (Riemann-1.0, CLAP, WALL-SS, CLIPPER, CLAP world models, TemporalFlow-VLA) push long-horizon prediction, multi-embodiment simulation, and planning.
- Humanoid and legged locomotion, plus safe goal-conditioned RL, move toward long-horizon, safety-aware whole-body and navigation behaviors with minimal supervision.
- Multi-agent navigation and MAPD in constrained warehouses receive new guarantees and efficiency via haven-based reservation and adaptive retreat-planning schemes.
- Selected works from MIT, UC Berkeley, Stanford, and UC San Diego highlight institution-aligned progress in kinodynamic planning, instruction-conditioned control, continual robot learning, and low-latency VLA inference.

| Dispersive Forward Tree Search for Optimal Control: Coverage, Complexity, and Computation | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | Introduces DFT*, a kinodynamic motion planner for differentially flat nonlinear systems that builds a forward tree of dispersive control commands with deterministic finite-sample near-optimality guarantees. The method uses cost-conditioned dominance pruning to keep tree size polynomial in the horizon, maps naturally to parallel hardware, and outperforms state-of-the-art planners on unicycle, trailer car, and quadrotor problems, including receding-horizon deployment in dynamic environments. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26314 |
| Decoupling Planning and Control for Instructable Agents | UC Berkeley | Proposes Instruct-to-Act, which couples a VLM-based high-level planner with a fast world-model controller conditioned on sparse language instructions. Through instruction relabeling and joint behavior cloning with reward and world-model objectives, the system achieves better performance than controller-only and direct VLM-action baselines across seven embodied tasks, including multi-agent coordination, while enabling planner modularity and low-latency control. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26788 |
| FlashVLA: Streaming Action Decoding for Fast and Asynchronous VLA Inference | UC San Diego | Introduces FlashVLA, a streaming action-decoding framework for flow-matching VLAs that maintains a multi-chunk action buffer at different noise levels and uses chunk-wise causal attention. This design yields one executable action chunk per inference step, preserving temporal continuity while achieving ≥30 Hz control on a single GPU for smooth, asynchronous real-world manipulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27384 |
| Memory Anchors for Continual Robot Learning | Stanford University | Identifies "Memory Anchors"—high-conflict regions where new-task observations overlap old-task representations but require different actions—as critical samples for replay-based continual robot learning. Emphasizing these anchors in the buffer sharply reduces catastrophic forgetting on LIBERO and enables successful multi-task continual learning on real robots. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26545 |
