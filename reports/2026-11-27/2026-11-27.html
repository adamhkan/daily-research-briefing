Executive Summary

  • A comparative study of small hexapod robots DASH and RoACH quantifies how limb morphology and stride frequency interact with ground physics, showing that on rigid substrates, managing kinetic energy is key to avoiding yaw/pitch/roll instabilities, while on sand, fluidization effects raise cost of transport and reduce speed, leading to distinct terrain-specific locomotion capabilities for the two designs. (arXiv:2611.33702)
  • New motion-generation and mission-planning frameworks advance dexterous and embodied control: FineDex learns human body–hand–object motions conditioned on fine-grained finger-level instructions and uses them as priors for a physics-based agent, reporting state-of-the-art controllable HOI generation and improved dexterous manipulation with real-robot transfer; DexEngine converts human hand-object data into physics-feasible arm–hand trajectories via residual RL refinement, generating large-scale corrected datasets that yield better sim and real-robot policies than uncorrected or post-hoc–attached-arm data; a mission-planning framework based on a bisimilar finite-state abstraction and block-conservative specifications guarantees safety and feasibility transfer from the abstract model to the real manipulator, demonstrated on diverse tasks with a Unitree humanoid. (arXiv:2611.34884; arXiv:2611.33579; arXiv:2611.33721)
  • Several works target large-scale, generalist manipulation and mobile-manipulation benchmarks: RoboARC offers 90 graded physics puzzles across regimes, revealing strong drops from privileged to sensor-only settings and highlighting perception and single-attempt constraints as bottlenecks; HeadLight adapts world-action models by selectively tuning motion- and next-interaction–related attention heads, achieving higher success on unseen RoboTwin tasks while updating only 1.1% of parameters; Nav4Manip jointly co-evolves navigation and dexterous manipulation affordances through multi-stage training (co-tuning, RFT, RLAIF), attaining state-of-the-art navigation and successful real-world mobile-manipulation execution; HoloBrain-0.5 standardizes action spaces across 15 robot embodiments and, after post-training, achieves strong zero-shot and few-shot transfer on multi-robot manipulation benchmarks, including humanoid control with limited demonstrations. (arXiv:2611.33390; arXiv:2611.33963; arXiv:2611.34655; arXiv:2611.34449)
  • New planning, safety, and localization methods exploit geometric structure and Gaussian-splat representations: FOCI models both robots and environments as Gaussians and defines collisions via an overlap integral, enabling orientation-aware trajectory optimization for manipulators, legged robots, and UAVs in large Gaussian-splat scenes within seconds; MERLoc pre-encodes dense pose-indexed reference views from a single 3D Gaussian Splat scan, with pose-based retrieval matching or surpassing appearance-based methods and reducing localization RMSE by 15–60% on real data while running at 5 Hz onboard; GAO characterizes collision-inducing accelerations via tight half-plane constraints in acceleration space and embeds them in a QP, achieving millisecond-level safe navigation with low control effort and scalability to 1,000 simulated agents; an exact branch-price-and-cut for the Vehicle Routing Problem with Floating Targets uses a dominance criterion with closed-form trajectory bounds to outperform prior exact methods and yield a faster heuristic that matched all certified optima in tests. (arXiv:2611.33380; arXiv:2611.34932; arXiv:2611.34586; arXiv:2611.34490)
  • Multiple systems demonstrate sim-to-real advances for locomotion, navigation, and perception: RIPPLE trains humanoid policies on an elastic-terrain model (ElasticMembrane) with staged transfer, maintaining ≥98.7% standing success under perturbations across varied deformable terrains in simulation and enabling zero-shot balancing and jumping on a real humanoid; an image-goal policy for agile quadrotors learns to output thrust and angular rates directly from monocular visual input and history, achieving 82.3% success in unseen simulated environments and 84% zero-shot success on a real quadrotor without SLAM/VO; a 3D SPAD simulation pipeline with realistic photon-noise modeling generates synthetic low-light data that transfer to real SPAD cameras for pose estimation and semantic segmentation, with rotation errors below 1° and strong accuracy under <2 lux; a CBF approach that models obstacles as optimized half-planes reduces conservatism, cutting arrival-time delays by over 33% in simulation and demonstrating safe navigation on an autonomous hydrofoil boat. (arXiv:2611.34569; arXiv:2611.34046; arXiv:2611.33498; arXiv:2611.34463)
  • VLA and odometry systems are extended to new domains and tasks: a vision–language–action model pretrained on manipulator data is adapted to autonomous forklift unloading using 9.38 h of simulation and 0.8 h of real demonstrations, successfully executing multi-step unloading in 72 real-world trials; xFORM compresses a LiDAR odometry optimizer’s feature matches into 34 scalar residuals per scan pair via exact factor compression, with CPU and GPU implementations delivering 1.1–1.4× and 2.0–4.0× speedups over FORM across 15 sequences while retaining numerically indistinguishable accuracy; BOP-Refer augments nine BOP datasets with human-verified referring expressions and precise 2D/3D boxes, showing that current VLMs (best: 72.6% AP_IoU2D vs. 12.4% AP_IoU3D) struggle with depth and metric-scale estimation for 3D object grounding; a single RL policy enables humanoid pivot walking of heavy boxes by coordinating stepping, balance, and manipulation, and after fine-tuning to be object-blind achieves consistent >1 m box displacements on hardware before safety stops. (arXiv:2611.33880; arXiv:2611.34053; arXiv:2611.34862; arXiv:2611.33828)

Systematic study of the performance of small robots on controlled laboratory substratesUC BerkeleyThe paper presents a systematic comparative study of two small six-legged robots, DASH and RoACH, examining their average speed, mean mechanical power, cost of transport, and stability across rigid and flowing (sand) substrates while varying ground properties and limb stride frequency. Experiments show that robot performance and stability are strongly dependent on ground-interaction physics: on hard ground, managing kinetic energy is critical to avoiding yaw, pitch, and roll instabilities, whereas on sand, fluidizing interactions cause higher cost of transport and lower running speeds. The study finds that differences in limb morphology and kinematics between DASH and RoACH lead to distinct capabilities in traversing different terrains, highlighting the role of morphology in terrain-specific locomotion performance.Link
FineDex: Bridging Fine-Grained Human-Object Interaction Generation to Dexterous Humanoid ManipulationUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes FineDex, a framework that learns coordinated human body, hand, and object motions that obey fine-grained instructions about hand choice, individual fingers, and contacts with articulated objects, making finger-level intent explicitly controllable in HOI generation. FineDex uses generated fine-grained interactions as actionable priors for an agent that, in a physics-based digital twin, discovers physically feasible contact strategies at test time without per-instance retraining, enabling generalization across fine-grained instructions and object configurations. Experiments report state-of-the-art quality and fine-grained controllability in human–object interaction generation, substantial gains in dexterous manipulation success in simulation, and successful transfer of the learned strategies to a real dexterous humanoid robot.Link
MERLoc: Robust Robot Localization in Dynamic Environments with Pose-Based Retrieval over 3D Gaussian Splatting ReferencesETH ZurichThe paper introduces MERLoc, a localization system that converts a single metric 3D Gaussian Splatting scan into a dense functional reference memory by sampling and pre-encoding reference views over the robot’s navigable space, then retrieves references at inference time using pose-based (spatial and viewing-direction) criteria instead of appearance. Across simulated and real dynamic environments and four learned plus one classical localizers, pose-based retrieval in MERLoc matches or outperforms appearance-based retrieval in most cases and, on real data, reduces position RMSE by 15–60% in 9 of 10 backend/split combinations. MERLoc’s rendered reference memory surpasses real-image caches derived from Structure-from-Motion, and the system runs onboard a Jetson Orin NX at 5 Hz, enabling successful hallway navigation amidst moving people and rearranged objects.Link
FOCI: Trajectory Optimization on Gaussian Splats for Gaussian RobotsETH ZurichThe paper proposes FOCI (Field Overlap Collision Integral), a trajectory optimization algorithm that operates directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting representations by modeling both the environment and the robot as Gaussians and defining collisions via a novel overlap-integral formulation. FOCI’s Gaussian representation supports different robot morphologies (manipulators, legged robots, UAVs) and both static and dynamic Gaussian-splat environments, enabling orientation-aware motion planning that can exploit tight and narrow free-space passages beyond conservative bounding-box models. Experiments on synthetic and real 3D Gaussian Splat environments demonstrate that FOCI can compute collision-free trajectories for legged, aerial, and manipulator robots within seconds even when the environment contains hundreds of thousands of Gaussians.Link
xFORM: Accelerating Fixed-Lag LiDAR Odometry with Exact Factor CompressionCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents xFORM, an accelerated LiDAR odometry system that compresses the FORM optimizer’s large set of feature matches into 34 scalar residuals per scan pair via exact factor compression, preserving the original optimization objective and Gauss–Newton system in exact arithmetic. Two implementations, xFORM-CPU (with parallelized feature extraction) and xFORM-GPU (with GPU-accelerated feature processing, correspondence search, and factor compression), are integrated into the LO pipeline to reduce latency in feature extraction, matching, and optimization. Across 15 sequences from three datasets, xFORM-CPU and xFORM-GPU achieve overall speedups of 1.1–1.4× and 2.0–4.0× over FORM, respectively, while maintaining numerically indistinguishable estimation accuracy from the original system.Link
RoboARC: Physical Puzzles for Robotic Manipulation under Spatial ConstraintsCarnegie Mellon University; UC BerkeleyThe paper introduces RoboARC, a benchmark of 90 physics-based manipulation puzzles spanning six spatial-constraint types, rigid and soft materials, and three robots in MuJoCo, each with a frozen scene, an automatic grader, and a bit-identical reference solution, under both a Privileged (full-state, resettable) and a Standard (sensor-only, single-attempt) regime. Evaluation of seven coding agents and four zero-shot VLA policies shows that the strongest coding agent (GPT-6 Astra in Codex) solves 86% of tasks in the Privileged regime but only 43% in the Standard regime, while all four VLA policies solve none of their 18 one-arm puzzles. Analysis on 15 puzzles mis-solved in the Standard but solved in the Privileged regime indicates that providing object poses adds 4 percentage points and part geometry 13, whereas providing both raises success from 24% to 60%, and most failed runs are blocked by a single step, suggesting that perception and single-attempt constraints are key current bottlenecks.Link
An Exact Branch-Price-and-Cut Algorithm for the Vehicle Routing Problem with Floating TargetsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper presents an exact branch-price-and-cut algorithm for the Vehicle Routing Problem with Floating Targets (VRPFT), where each target can move from its home location at a bounded speed and solutions must jointly choose target-visit sequences and spatiotemporal meeting points for each vehicle. A key methodological contribution is a dominance criterion for the pricing labeling algorithm that uses closed-form lower and upper bounds on a vehicle’s trajectory cost for a fixed sequence of targets, and the authors prove that this criterion preserves optimality. Computational experiments on benchmarks show that the proposed exact algorithm is faster than the prior exact dynamic-programming approach on identical hardware, and a relaxed version of the dominance criterion yields a heuristic that is even faster while matching every certified optimum observed in tests.Link
A 3D Simulator for Passive SPAD ImagingETH ZurichThe paper introduces a 3D simulation pipeline for passive SPAD cameras that couples controllable scene geometry and motion with a sensor-informed observation model accounting for photon shot noise, dark counts, and spatial crosstalk to generate realistic photon detections along with camera-motion and semantic labels. Calibration experiments show close agreement between simulated and real photon counts, and classifiers trained only on synthetic SPAD data achieve high accuracy on real low-light recordings at illumination levels below 2 lux without any fine-tuning on real data. Models trained exclusively on simulated data attain mean absolute rotation errors below 1° on real 20° and 30° camera sweeps at 0.5 and 5.0 lux, and synthetic dense labels further enable semantic segmentation models that transfer effectively to real low-light SPAD recordings, demonstrating practical sim-to-real utility of the simulator.Link
Targeted Adaptation of World-Action Models via Interaction and Motion HeadsMassachusetts Institute of Technology; UC BerkeleyThe paper analyzes World-Action Models (WAMs) for manipulation and identifies two functionally specialized families of attention heads: Next-Interaction Heads, which localize upcoming grasp-and-release targets, and Motion Heads, which track motion across frames. It introduces HeadLight, a parameter-efficient adaptation method that selectively refines Next-Interaction and Motion Heads instead of performing full fine-tuning or uniform LoRA adaptation, thereby targeting manipulation-relevant internal mechanisms. On unseen RoboTwin tasks, HeadLight achieves 62.28% success while updating only 1.1% of parameters, outperforming full fine-tuning (57.89%) and LoRA (58.66%), and ablations show that removing Motion Heads nearly eliminates success (57.89% to 0.94%) and removing Next-Interaction Heads reduces it to 40.61%, confirming their importance across WAM architectures and real-robot settings.Link
BOP-Refer: Benchmark for Monocular 3D and 2D Object GroundingNew York UniversityThe paper introduces BOP-Refer, a benchmark for monocular 2D and 3D object grounding that augments images from 9 diverse BOP datasets with VLM-generated, human-verified referring expressions and derives precise 3D and 2D bounding boxes from available 3D models and 6DoF poses. Systematic evaluation of 17 state-of-the-art VLMs (including Gemini, GPT, Claude, Qwen, Gemma families) shows that the best-performing model, Gemini 4 Carbon, attains 72.6% AP_IoU2D but only 12.4% AP_IoU3D, even under more lenient 3D IoU thresholds. Error analysis reveals that predicted 3D boxes tend to be well-centered in the image plane but often have depth errors exceeding the object size, indicating that metric-scale estimation and reliable object size priors are currently the dominant failure modes for 3D grounding.Link
Embodied Lower-limb Exoskeleton Assistance: Survey, Challenges, and PerspectivesETH ZurichThe paper defines the concept of embodied lower-limb exoskeletons and surveys the literature along four dimensions—structural self-adaptation, task adaptation, user-specific adaptation, and control self-regulation—highlighting associated challenges such as lightweight compliant design, data acquisition/fusion, complex policy optimization, and robust compliant control. From an embodied perspective, the review proposes a closed-loop framework that couples mechanical structure, sensing, and control to analyze lower-limb exoskeleton performance and human-robot interaction, and uses this framework to identify principal limitations of current research and outline directions toward lightweight, comfortable, practical assistance.Link

DexEngine: Scaling Human Demonstrations into Physically Executable Whole-Robot DataPOSTECH; RLWRLDThe paper presents DexEngine, a simulation pipeline that converts human hand-object manipulation data into kinematically feasible, physically executable arm-hand trajectories by placing hand-object motions within reachable space, generating arm motions, and refining them via residual reinforcement learning in physics simulation. Using nine human hand-object datasets, DexEngine generates approximately 5,200 clips per dexterous hand (65,000 trajectories, 116 h; 325,000 episodes, 578 h total) and achieves more accurate object tracking than prior floating-hand methods while running over 10× faster than RL-based approaches. Policies trained on DexEngine-generated data significantly outperform those trained on the same clips without physics correction or with arms attached after floating-hand correction, and mid-training on DexEngine data further improves policy performance both in simulation and on a real robot, indicating that physics-corrected data are important for fine-tuning.Link
A general-purpose safe manipulator planning framework for diverse environments without retrainingHuazhong University of Science and Technology; Nanyang Technological UniversityThe paper proposes a general-purpose manipulator mission-planning framework that uses a finite-state abstracted model, bisimilar to the original robot dynamics, to represent accessibility of the space in front of the robot under a given arm and gripper, and a block-conservative specification generation method that converts manipulation tasks in specific environments into state-accessibility specifications on this abstracted model. The framework guarantees that any task proven feasible and safe on the abstracted model is achievable and safe for the actual robot, enabling reuse of a single abstracted model across different operating environments without retraining controllers; different tasks are handled by generating task-specific block-conservative specifications for a generic mission planner with vision-based feedback. Real-world case studies on a Unitree G1 U1 humanoid show that everyday manipulation tasks such as grasping objects, pressing elevator buttons, and picking berries can be executed within this framework using a single pre-built abstracted model, avoiding repeated model reconstruction.Link
Learning a Whole-Body Pivot-Walking Gait for Heavy-Box Transport with a HumanoidUniversity of TokyoThe paper introduces a single reinforcement-learning policy for humanoid robots that jointly coordinates stepping, balance control, and object manipulation to achieve pivot walking for transporting heavy boxes, using rewards that encourage alternating pivot motions and a phase reference tied to object displacement during simulation training. For real-world deployment, the learned policy is fine-tuned into an object-blind version driven by a periodic clock instead of object-state feedback, enabling execution without direct object-state sensing. On a real humanoid robot, the deployed policy successfully pivot-walks a box forward by more than 1 m in all five trials, each ending at a base-tilt safety stop, demonstrating transfer of the learned pivot-walking controller from simulation to hardware.Link
Integration of Vision-Language-Action Control for Autonomous Forklift Unloading with Limited Real-World DataToyota Central R&D Labs., Inc.The paper demonstrates the application of a vision–language–action (VLA) control framework to autonomous forklift unloading, adapting a VLA model pretrained mainly on robotic manipulator data to forklift control using 9.38 hours of simulation data and 0.8 hours of real-world demonstrations (about 8% of total forklift data). Because no public forklift datasets exist, the authors construct a scalable simulation-based training dataset and integrate it with the pretrained VLA model to handle multi-step control actions directly from visual observations and language prompts. Across 72 real-world trials evaluating four model variants, the integrated VLA-based system is shown to successfully accomplish a forklift unloading task despite relying on limited real-world data.Link
End-to-End Image-Goal Navigation for Agile Quadrotors via Recurrent State DistillationUniversity of ZurichThe paper presents an image-goal navigation policy for agile quadrotors that directly outputs thrust and angular rates from onboard visual input in 3D environments, without using state estimation methods such as SLAM, VO, or SfM, or a separate velocity controller. The policy is trained via a four-stage pipeline that combines geometric and motion supervision with privileged-teacher distillation and end-to-end reinforcement learning fine-tuning, enabling it to infer 3D displacement and appropriate continuous control from monocular image differences and history. On 1,000 held-out flights in unseen environments, the learned policy attains an 82.3% success rate with a mean terminal position error of 0.10 m on successful flights, and in zero-shot deployment on a real quadrotor achieves an 84% success rate over 25 trials in an unseen indoor environment.Link
HoloBrain-0.5 Technical ReportHorizon Robotics HoloBrain TeamThe paper introduces HoloBrain-0.5, a family of vision–language–action policies for robot manipulation across multiple embodiments that share an action space by standardizing joint axes, coordinate frames, and tool-center points in URDFs and encoding each robot’s kinematic graph into a shared 3D representation; HoloBrain-0.5-Continuous uses diffusion to generate action chunks, while HoloBrain-0.5-Discrete uses residual finite scalar quantization and delayed autoregression. Both policies are pretrained on over 10,000 hours of mixed real-robot, simulation, and human-manipulation data spanning 15 embodiment categories; in six closed-loop simulation settings, the discrete policy converges faster and reaches a higher mean success rate than the continuous policy (56.77% vs. 49.71%), and on seven real-robot tasks achieves a mean progress score of 87.76% without task-specific post-training. After post-training, the continuous policy outperforms a baseline π₀.₅ on a seven-task benchmark (94.52% vs. 86.25%) and an 11-task dual-arm benchmark (79.07% vs. 69.41%); it transfers five tasks zero-shot from a Piper to a Piper-X robot with an average score of 63.28%, and with target-robot demonstrations reaches 89.2% on Franka Panda and 92.94% on a mobile dual-arm platform, while limited Unitree G1 demonstrations yield 70.24% mean progress across three humanoid tasks versus 11.00% for π₀.₅.Link
Least Restrictive Halfplane Control Barrier Functions for an Autonomous Hydrofoil Boat with Moving ObstaclesRoyal Institute of Technology (KTH)The paper proposes a control barrier function (CBF) formulation for Dubins-car agents that represents obstacles as supporting half-planes, and introduces methods to reduce the conservatism of half-plane CBFs by optimizing the orientation of each supporting half-plane to keep the safe control input close to the desired input. To handle complex obstacle geometries, the method employs multiple half-planes per obstacle and derives rules for managing their intersections, yielding less conservative safe sets compared to existing CBF techniques. Simulation results indicate that the proposed least-restrictive half-plane CBFs enable tighter obstacle clearance and faster goal arrival, with delays reduced by more than 33% relative to baselines, and experiments on an autonomous hydrofoil boat demonstrate successful real-world implementation.Link
Riding the Wave: Sim-to-Real Humanoid Control on Elastic TerrainDreame Technology; East China University of Science and Technology; Magiclab Robotics Technology (Wuxi) Co., Ltd.; Nanjing University; National University of Singapore; Tsinghua University; University of OxfordThe paper introduces RIPPLE, a sim-to-real learning framework for humanoid control on deformable elastic terrains, built on ElasticMembrane—a pretensioned mass-spring mesh integrated with deformable contact into MJLab—to model spatially coupled surface deflection, energy storage, and rebound for GPU-parallel policy learning using only proprioceptive inputs. Through staged training that transfers rigid-ground skills to elastic-terrain balance and jumping, RIPPLE yields policies that, in simulation, maintain at least 98.7% standing success (mean over three seeds) on ElasticMembrane, IndependentSprings, and BouncyPlate under 0.3 m/s perturbations, whereas policies trained on rigid ground or simple contact compliance transfer poorly to ElasticMembrane and IndependentSprings. Deployed zero-shot on a MagicBot Z1 humanoid, the learned policies enable balancing, consecutive jumping, and entering a trampoline from a rigid platform, and a fixed policy generalizes in simulation to terrain stiffnesses from 0.35 to 2.8 times the training value, beds up to 7 m wide, and seven additional support scenarios without stiffness calibration.Link
Geometric Acceleration Obstacles: Physical Boundary Structure and Affine Safety CertificatesUnknown affiliationThe paper presents Geometric Acceleration Obstacles (GAO), which analytically characterizes collision-inducing accelerations via configuration-dependent half-planes that globally support the collision set in acceleration space, proves the tightness of these half-planes, and uses them to separate certified collision-free inputs from collision-inducing ones. From these geometric results, GAO derives unified affine safety constraints that, when combined with actuator limits, define a quadratic program capable of real-time safe navigation for acceleration-limited robots in dynamic environments. Experiments show that GAO achieves competitive task success rates with the lowest control effort among evaluated methods and millisecond-level computation times, scales in simulation to 1,000 agents in circle-interchange scenarios, and is validated in physical experiments involving pedestrian encounters, reciprocal multi-agent navigation, and high-speed maneuvering.Link
Nav4Manip: From Navigation to Dexterous Mobile Manipulation through Co-EvolutionGalbot; Peking University; QwenThe paper proposes Nav4Manip, a bidirectionally coupled co-evolution system that jointly trains long-horizon navigation (Nav4Manip-N) and a world-action model for dexterous manipulation (Nav4Manip-M), allowing navigation policies to be refined using manipulation feedback and affordance prediction. Nav4Manip-N is trained in three stages—multi-task co-tuning for navigation and manipulation-aware affordance prediction, rejection sampling fine-tuning (RFT) to discard low-quality policy-generated paths, and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) using counterfactual preferences—to improve precise base positioning and orientation for downstream manipulation. Experiments on public vision-language navigation benchmarks and the newly introduced Nav-N-Manip benchmark show that Nav4Manip achieves state-of-the-art performance, and real-world evaluations indicate that it can successfully complete diverse dexterous mobile-manipulation tasks.Link
