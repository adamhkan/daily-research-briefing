Executive Summary

  • Several works advance scalable optimal control and optimization for robot motion: Sequential Contact Insertion (SCI) parameterizes multi-robot trajectories via contact events and analytically reconstructs minimum-energy, collision-free paths for double-integrator robots, planning for up to one million robots in minutes with median speedups of several orders of magnitude over a fixed-grid sequential convex programming baseline while matching its solution accuracy; a companion analysis for two double-integrator robots with squared-acceleration cost proves all optimal collision-free trajectories fall into eight contact families and provides a geometric characterization and numerical solver; and cuPIQP introduces a batched, differentiable GPU quadratic-program solver based on a proximal interior-point method that yields up to 18.8× and 4.26× solve-time speedups over Moreau and an 8-core PIQP implementation, respectively, and accelerates a differentiable-robot-learning pipeline by 4.6×–7.8× over existing QP solvers. (arXiv:2610.41548; arXiv:2610.41558; arXiv:2610.41021)
  • New safety and certification frameworks target heterogeneous and formally guaranteed control: the Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter (CA-BRF) constructs capability-dependent backup-recoverable sets and filters navigation commands so multi-robot teams remain recursively feasible and safe even when nominal safety optimization fails, validated in simulations with up to 30 heterogeneous robots and hardware tests on five kinematic classes; the Capability-Conditioned Heterogeneous Graph Control Barrier Function (CC-HGCBF) uses Radial Capability Fields to encode robot-specific reachable velocities, enabling decentralized, zero-shot safe navigation and coordination for mixed teams of seen and unseen platforms in simulation and physical trials; AC-SMPC embeds an explicit-constraint MPC layer in an actor–critic framework, with the actor tuning MPC objectives and an elastic QP relaxation handling local infeasibilities, improving multi-robot navigation efficiency in dynamic, narrow environments while maintaining safety compared to existing MPC-based baselines; and Proof-Carrying Evolution (PCE) evolves controllers together with formal certificates, showing that enforcing certification during search consistently produces certified docking controllers and that language-model-guided synthesis can outperform classical baselines on linear problems, while CMA-ES and hand-tuned designs remain competitive on SO(3). (arXiv:2610.41188; arXiv:2610.41214; arXiv:2610.41477; arXiv:2610.42043)
  • Multiple systems leverage large models and harness design to improve embodied control: REA (Routed Embodied Agent) coordinates deliberative, routed, and reactive policies under budgeted escalation and judge-gating, achieving 95.9% success on 3,000 LIBERO-Pro episodes (vs. 94.7% for the best single policy) with 25% fewer control steps and 17% less control time, and 84–86% success on RoboTwin 2.0 versus 80% for the strongest VLA baseline while also transferring to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real humanoid; RoboArchitect uses a coding model to iteratively edit the agent harness around a frozen policy, boosting average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks by discovering improved control logic and memory without changing policy weights; LEXP wraps a multimodal reasoner (ChatGPT-6-Astra) with an experience module that extracts and reuses lessons from prior executions, raising success over 12 real-world manipulation tasks to 63.9% versus 52.8% with memory alone and 5.6% with vanilla Astra, with ablations showing both planning and experience retention are critical; and Proof-Carrying Evolution demonstrates that language models can participate in certified controller design by proposing candidate laws with accompanying proofs checked during search. (arXiv:2610.42053; arXiv:2610.42141; arXiv:2610.41320; arXiv:2610.42043)
  • New benchmarks and datasets expose limitations of current policies and provide richer supervision: MolmoSpaces-CommonSense defines a manipulation benchmark grounded in developmental cognitive science to test occlusion, containment, support, grasp semantics, and hidden-object reasoning, finding that zero-shot generalist policies suffer >50% success drops with added distractors, select wrong objects ~30% of the time in clutter, succeed only ~50% on affordance-sensitive grasps even with targeted language prompts, and that VLM-based rollout scoring is unreliable in these scenes; MotorSense releases 100 hours of egocentric video paired with bimanual EMG, IMU, and audio, showing that cross-modal video–EMG pretraining yields sensorimotor-aligned visual representations that outperform video-only self-supervision across four action-centric tasks and eight datasets and enable EMG-conditioned diffusion to generate fine-grained manipulation videos; and a large-scale real-robot study of 281 imitation-learning policies for RJ45 grasping/insertion shows that observation/action representations (e.g., point-cloud cropping, robot-frame coordinates, encoder normalization, and absolute vs. relative action choices) dominate optimizer/sampling effects, with some choices improving success by ~70–100 percentage points, while only five smoothness/directness-related offline metrics correlate (r = -0.70 to -0.80) with real insertion success and only within a single policy family. (arXiv:2610.41377; arXiv:2610.41127; arXiv:2610.40828)
  • Several methods propose new policy and world-model formulations for generalist and dexterous control: CWAM augments world action models with a sparse routing path so that, under a sparsity constraint, action-generating latents provably become an invertible function of action-relevant variables alone, with synthetic and real-robot experiments indicating more efficient closed-loop task success; Hourglass factorizes planning into a body-agnostic world model and robot-specific differentiable ego-dynamics, allowing a single parameter-conditioned policy to match per-robot specialists and trail a parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher by only 1.4–2.8 percentage points across seven simulated robots, while transferring zero-shot from synthetic training to a new real skid-steer platform after ~3 minutes of dynamics identification; DiffWBC uses a one-step diffusion controller over humanoid trajectories to achieve the lowest local tracking error among six trackers (20.1 mm on BONES), support 50 Hz control on a Unitree G1, enable strong zero-shot test-time steering (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.10 clean passes under a low ceiling), and identify likely physical failures with 91% precision in the top 2% high-dispersion rollouts, half of which are fixed by targeted fine-tuning; DMAT models bimanual joint-space action chunks directly on an action manifold with stochastic interpolants and STR refinement, outperforming Improved 3D Diffusion Policy and DiTFlow3D by 129.1% and 95.7% relative success, respectively, while generating actions 1.34–2.06× faster and remaining robust under repeated 50 N disturbances; and HandMimicX trains dexterous object-centric reinforcement learning policies from egocentric human RGB-D videos by optimizing object motion with hand–object interaction constraints, with simulations and real-robot trials indicating efficient skill learning and generalization to new spatial configurations. (arXiv:2610.42096; arXiv:2610.41330; arXiv:2610.41354; arXiv:2610.41643; arXiv:2610.41336)
  • New hardware and fielded systems demonstrate sim-to-real transfer and autonomous inspection: an open-source knee exoskeleton and biological torque controller trained purely in musculoskeletal simulation, without device-specific data, reduce peak biological knee extension moment by 21.5% and vastus medialis activation by 26.7% during squats in seven participants compared to unassisted squatting (p<0.05), showing that simulation-trained torque estimation can transfer directly to hardware; and SAFARI integrates semantic detections as probabilistic channels into a unified map to guide frontier-based exploration and risk-aware planning, yielding 98.7% average coverage of accessible area and 97.6% autonomy across 11 quadruped missions in three complex field sites while detecting hazardous objects as small as 15 cm, with ablations underscoring the importance of object-size awareness and safety-aware path planning. (arXiv:2610.41981; arXiv:2610.41456)

Contact-based optimization for collision-free motion planning for a million robotsETH ZurichThe paper proposes sequential contact insertion (SCI), an optimization algorithm that parameterizes multi-robot trajectories via contact events at minimum separation, solving a coupled algebraic system in contact variables and reconstructing trajectories analytically between contacts for double-integrator robots with minimum-energy objectives and collision-avoidance constraints. Numerical experiments report that SCI can plan collision-free motions for one million robots in minutes and thousands of robots in fractions of a second, achieving median speedups of several orders of magnitude over a fixed-grid sequential convex programming baseline while matching its solution accuracy on problems feasible for the baseline.Link
A complete analytical characterization of optimal collision-free motion for two robotsETH ZurichThe paper analytically characterizes optimal collision-free trajectories for two robots under double-integrator dynamics with a squared-acceleration objective, assuming both robots start and end at rest. It proves that all such optimal trajectories fall into eight distinct contact families and specifies which family applies for each configuration of initial and final positions, providing associated geometrical analysis and a numerical algorithm to compute these trajectories. The authors argue that insights from this analytical structure can be used to improve the performance of existing numerical trajectory optimizers for multi-agent motion planning.Link
REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum ControlUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces REA (Routed Embodied Agent), a unified runtime where a deliberative System 2, an embodied router System 1, and reactive System 0 policies are coordinated under the rule that no running policy waits on the foundation-model "brain", using route memory, a boundary-triggered judge gate, and a shared-budget escalation ladder to manage control and failure recovery. On 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA achieves 95.9% success, outperforming the best single-agent baseline in its policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In 100 RoboTwin 2.0 episodes, REA attains 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex, and also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid with the highest reported success among compared methods.Link
MolmoSpaces-CommonSense: Evaluating Commonsense in Robotic Manipulation PoliciesUniversity of WashingtonThe paper introduces MolmoSpaces-CommonSense, a robotic manipulation benchmark and taxonomy grounded in developmental cognitive science that targets commonsense reasoning skills such as occlusion, containment, support, semantically appropriate grasp points, spatial affordances, and inference about hidden objects in cluttered settings. Zero-shot generalist manipulation policies evaluated on MolmoSpaces-CommonSense exhibit substantial commonsense failures: success rates in cluttered scenes decrease by over 50% as distractors increase, policies act on incorrect objects about 30% of the time in clutter, and affordance-sensitive grasping achieves correct object-part selection only about 50% of the time even with specific language prompts. Vision-language-model-based rollout success estimation is found to be unreliable in these challenging scenes, indicating that the difficulty extends beyond control to the broader perception-reasoning-action stack and that existing benchmarks may substantially overestimate zero-shot real-world readiness.Link
CausalWAM: Learning the Causes of Actions in World Action ModelsUC San DiegoThe paper identifies a theoretical limitation of existing world action models (WAMs), showing that when fitted to demonstrations they need not base action generation solely on action-relevant latent variables, as these can become entangled with nuisance variables. Under a sparsity constraint on the action-generation pathway, the authors prove that the latent variables used to generate actions in a WAM become an invertible function of the action-relevant latent variables alone, identifying the true action-relevant structure up to a permutation without requiring assumptions on temporal dependence or on how unobserved regimes evolve, and allowing statistical dependence between action-relevant and nuisance latents. They propose CWAM (Causal WAM), which adds a sparse routing path to the action head of a pretrained WAM while keeping the world-modeling objective unchanged, and report experiments on synthetic data and real-world robots that support the theory and show improved efficiency in closed-loop task success.Link
CuPIQP: A Batched and Differentiable QP Solver on GPUs for Robot Learning and ControlEPFL; ETH ZurichThe paper presents cuPIQP, a batched and differentiable quadratic program solver for GPUs based on a proximal interior-point method, supporting dense, sparse, and multistage QPs and exploiting parallelism across many independent problems while preserving per-problem numerical updates and stopping criteria. Benchmarking with batches of up to 16,384 QPs shows that cuPIQP achieves solve-time speedups of up to 18.8× over Moreau and 4.26× over an eight-core PIQP implementation. In an end-to-end robot learning task requiring differentiation through QP solutions, cuPIQP accelerates training by 4.6× relative to QPAX and 7.8× relative to Moreau, illustrating its utility as reusable infrastructure for large-scale model-based robot learning and control.Link
Proof-Carrying Evolution: Black-Box Search, White-Box FlightMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes proof-carrying evolution (PCE), a controller-design framework in which a search procedure, potentially guided by a language model, generates candidate controllers together with formal certificates of their properties; certificates are checked during search and only certified candidates can be returned, with the deployed artifact consisting of the control law plus its certificate. Across docking under a speed limit, pursuit of a maneuvering target, and attitude control on SO(3), experiments comparing four language models with classical search and synthesis report that on linear problems the strongest language models outperform standard classical baselines on every run, though a classical optimizer that co-designs certificates narrows this gap, while on SO(3) no method’s median performance surpasses a hand-tuned control law and CMA-ES remains highly competitive. The study finds that in a docking control setting with fixed objective and mutation operator, enforcing the rule that only certified candidates are retained yields new certified designs in every run, whereas removing this rule yields no certified designs, and that some purported champions for pursuit change ranking when moving from Euler to zero-order-hold dynamics, highlighting sensitivity to modeling assumptions.Link
Design and Validation of a Robotic Knee Exoskeleton with Musculoskeletal Simulation-Derived Biological Torque ControlCarnegie Mellon University; Stanford UniversityThe paper presents an open-source knee exoskeleton built from off-the-shelf components, together with a biological torque control strategy whose estimator is trained entirely in musculoskeletal simulation rather than on device-specific data, using a muscle-driven imitation policy that reproduces squatting motions from an open biomechanics dataset and distilling the resulting knee moments into an onboard-sensor-based estimator. In experiments with seven able-bodied participants performing squats, exoskeleton assistance using the simulation-derived biological torque controller reduces peak biological knee extension moment by 21.5% and peak vastus medialis muscle activation by 26.7% relative to squatting without the exoskeleton (p<0.05). The results indicate that a biological torque controller trained purely in simulation can transfer effectively to physical knee exoskeleton hardware without collecting new training data on the device, and that the combined release of hardware and training pipeline enables comparable controllers to be trained and evaluated across groups without repeated data collection.Link
RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent MemoryStanford UniversityThe paper introduces RoboArchitect, a system in which a coding model observes rollouts from a frozen robot policy, diagnoses failure modes, and iteratively edits the surrounding agent harness—including control logic, tool use, and memory—so that the harness, rather than the policy weights, is adapted to a new robot or benchmark. Across multiple robotic manipulation benchmarks and base policies, RoboArchitect improves average success on unseen scenes from 9.7% to 43.3% (4.5×) and on unseen tasks from 12.7% to 40.2% (3.2×) without any model weight updates, with especially large gains when the benchmark distribution differs substantially from the policy’s original training data. The evolved harnesses automatically discover intuitive control strategies (e.g., lifting a jammed arm or approaching horizontally when blocked from above) and implement persistent memory that carries experience such as effective grasp depths into later decisions, suggesting that significant performance headroom lies in automating harness design rather than further finetuning base policies.Link
MotorSense: A Video-EMG Dataset of Motoric Representations for Action UnderstandingCarnegie Mellon University; Massachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces MotorSense, a 100-hour egocentric dataset that pairs synchronized video with bimanual surface EMG, IMU, and audio during unscripted hand-object manipulation, positioning EMG as a dense, occlusion-free, and scalable alternative to manual annotation for capturing fine-grained hand configuration, finger movement, and interaction forces. Using cross-modal pre-training between video and EMG on MotorSense, the authors learn sensorimotor-aligned visual representations that produce a shared organization of action across visual and motoric spaces and transfer these representations to downstream tasks including action recognition, 3D hand reconstruction, and 3D contact reconstruction. Across four action-centric computer vision tasks and eight popular video datasets, models trained with sensorimotor alignment on MotorSense outperform conventional self-supervised learning baselines that use only video, and EMG-conditioned video diffusion demonstrates generative synthesis of fine-grained hand manipulation videos from motor signals.Link

From Teleoperation to Long-Horizon Dexterity: Evaluating Visuomotor Skills and Offline Proxies for Dexterous ManipulationAnalog Devices, Inc.The paper reports an empirical evaluation of 281 imitation-learning policies from seven policy families on about 1,700 real-robot trials of RJ45 connector grasping and insertion with a five-fingered robotic hand, finding that observation and action representations have a larger impact on real-world performance than optimizer or sampling choices. For point-cloud policies, specific representation choices—region-of-interest cropping, expressing points in the robot-base frame, and normalizing within the point encoder—are each reported to improve insertion success by roughly 70–100 percentage points, while for a vision-language-action policy, predicting absolute rather than relative actions improves insertion success by up to 70 percentage points. Out of 67 offline metrics examined, only five measures related to predicted-motion smoothness and directness correlate reliably with real-robot insertion success (correlation r = -0.70 to -0.80), and only within a single policy family, indicating that such offline proxies can shortlist policies but do not replace real-robot evaluation.Link
A Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot NavigationTexas A&M University; University of Illinois ChicagoThe paper proposes the Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter (CA-BRF), which constructs a capability-dependent backup-recoverable set in a shared Cartesian operational space by certifying pairwise-safe backup maneuvers that bring heterogeneous robots to rest while respecting each robot’s kinematic and actuation constraints. CA-BRF filters candidate navigation controls by selecting admissible interpolations between candidate and backup actions, coordinating across simultaneous multi-robot interactions so that a single executable control preserves backup recoverability and satisfies all pairwise recovery conditions. The authors establish recursive feasibility and safety guarantees for the discrete-time closed-loop system using CA-BRF even when nominal safety-constrained optimization is infeasible, and validate its effectiveness via simulations with up to 30 heterogeneous robots and hardware experiments involving five different kinematic classes.Link
Capability-Conditioned Graph Control Barrier Functions for Zero-Shot Safe Navigation of Heterogeneous Mobile Robot TeamsSogang University; Texas A&M UniversityThe paper introduces the Capability-Conditioned Heterogeneous Graph Control Barrier Function (CC-HGCBF), defined in a shared second-order reference-point operational space, to provide a common safety representation for heterogeneous robots while preserving their individual dynamics and control constraints. To enable zero-shot safe navigation across diverse and previously unseen robot models, the method encodes each robot’s state-dependent finite-horizon reachable velocity set as a fixed-dimensional Radial Capability Field (RCF), conditioning both the shared graph barrier and decentralized policy on this capability representation instead of predefined robot classes. Experiments on mixed robot teams containing both seen and unseen models, including physical-robot trials, demonstrate that CC-HGCBF with RCF achieves scalable, decentralized safe coordination and zero-shot transfer to new kinematic models and capability variations without retraining.Link
Learning from Physical Experience:Context-Space Adaptation for Robotic ManipulationThe Chinese University of Hong Kong; University of Electronic Science and Technology of China; xdof.aiThe paper empirically shows that a general-purpose multimodal reasoner, ChatGPT-6-Astra, can function as a context-responsive manipulation policy in real-world experiments, exhibiting effective fine manipulation and spatial reasoning when given a suitable action interface. The authors propose LEXP, a context-space adaptation harness that augments task-level planning with an experience module which interprets execution evidence, extracts lessons with applicability conditions, stores them, and injects relevant contextual feedback into subsequent action generation without changing model weights or the action interface. Across 12 real-world manipulation tasks, LEXP achieves 23/36 successful trials (63.9%), outperforming Astra with memory alone (19/36, 52.8%) and vanilla Astra (2/36, 5.6%), and ablations on three tasks show that removing planning or experience retention substantially reduces success rates (from 7/9 to 4/9 and 3/9, respectively).Link
Cross-Embodiment Motion Planning by Coupling Body-Agnostic World Model with Differentiable Ego-DynamicsNanyang Technological UniversityThe paper proposes Hourglass, a cross-embodiment motion-planning architecture that factorizes planning into a body-agnostic world model and robot-specific differentiable ego-dynamics, coupling them only via camera ego-motion so that a single parameter-conditioned policy can be trained across diverse robot embodiments and camera mounts. In simulation on seven robots spanning five locomotion types, the generalist Hourglass policy, trained via imagination rollouts through each robot’s ego-dynamics template, improves success rates by 3.8–10.6 percentage points over imitation-learning and diffusion-policy baselines using the same world model, and matches the performance of per-robot specialist policies without dedicated per-robot training while approaching a parameter-conditioned MPPI teacher within 1.4–2.8 percentage points. A model trained entirely on synthetic data transfers zero-shot to a real skid-steer robot unseen during training after approximately three minutes of teleoperated data collection for dynamics identification, enabling navigation in previously unseen indoor environments without an online map.Link
HandMimicX: Learning Human-Like Dexterous Manipulation via an Object-Centric Policy from Egocentric VideosMeiTuan; NVIDIA; The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen; TsingTec; Tsinghua UniversityThe paper presents HandMimicX, an object-centric reinforcement learning framework that learns dexterous manipulation skills from egocentric human RGB-D videos by optimizing an RL objective over object motion, thereby decoupling the learned skill from specific human hand trajectories. HandMimicX incorporates constraints on hand–object interaction states to encourage anatomically natural and physically plausible hand configurations during manipulation, rather than focusing solely on imitating hand poses. Simulation and real-robot experiments indicate that HandMimicX can efficiently acquire dexterous skills from human videos and generalize those skills across novel spatial configurations, though the abstract does not provide quantitative performance metrics.Link
DiffWBC: Unlocking Steerable General Humanoid Motion Tracking via One-Step Diffusion Whole-Body ControlShenzhen University of Advanced Technology; Southeast University; Southern University of Science and Technology; The Hong Kong Polytechnic UniversityThe paper introduces DiffWBC, a one-step diffusion-based whole-body controller that models distributions over future humanoid trajectories, enabling a single generative controller to perform general motion tracking, zero-shot test-time steering, and simulation-free failure discovery. DiffWBC can be distilled from a reinforcement-learning teacher or trained via model-based RL, operates at 50 Hz with a single forward pass per control step, achieves the lowest local tracking error among six general trackers on held-out benchmarks (20.1 mm on BONES), and is demonstrated on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid. By leveraging differentiable cost gradients and body-group masks for online modulation, DiffWBC substantially improves zero-shot test-time steering compared to kinematic reference editing (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.10 clean pass rate under a 1.25 m ceiling), and its trajectory-sampling dispersion identifies physical tracking failures with 91% precision in the top 2% highest-dispersion cases, where targeted fine-tuning resolves about 50% of these failures.Link
SAFARI: Semantic- And Frontier-driven Autonomous Risk InspectionSwiss Federal Institute of Technology (ETH Zürich)The paper proposes SAFARI, a semantic- and frontier-driven autonomous risk inspection framework that integrates object detections as a native probabilistic channel in a unified map, allowing geometric and semantic evidence to jointly guide exploration and motion planning in hazardous environments. In 11 quadruped-robot missions across three complex field sites, SAFARI achieves on average 98.7% coverage of the accessible mission area while operating without human intervention for 97.6% of mission time, and detects hazardous objects with characteristic dimensions as small as 15 cm. Ablation studies and behavioral analyses show that incorporating object-size awareness and safety-aware path planning is crucial for effective hazardous-object search within the SAFARI framework.Link
AC-SMPC: Differentiable Actor--Critic MPC with State Constraints for Safe and Fast Robot NavigationChinese Academy of Sciences Shanghai Institute of Microsystem and Information Technology; Soochow UniversityThe paper presents AC-SMPC, an actor–critic model predictive control framework that embeds a differentiable MPC layer with explicit state constraints for safe navigation, representing robots and obstacles with vertices and the workspace with half-spaces, and convexifying hyperplane-based collision-avoidance constraints within the MPC optimization. In AC-SMPC, the actor learns to adjust MPC objective parameters via closed-loop feedback to shape local motion behavior, while the critic evaluates long-term task performance, and the MPC layer is relaxed into an elastic quadratic program using bounded penalized slack variables to handle cases where local constraint approximations are infeasible. Simulation and hardware experiments on multi-robot dynamic collision avoidance in narrow environments indicate that AC-SMPC improves navigation efficiency and adaptively adjusts the learned policy while maintaining safety, compared to existing navigation and MPC-based approaches.Link
Direct Action-Manifold Modeling for Efficient and Robust Bimanual ManipulationTongji UniversityThe paper proposes the Direct Manifold Action Transformer (DMAT), which models observation-conditioned bimanual joint-space action chunks directly on an action manifold using stochastic interpolants, enabling single-step action generation without iterative denoising or numerical integration as in diffusion or flow-matching visuomotor policies. DMAT leverages local 3D scene geometry to anchor predictions and introduces State-Consistent Spectral-Time Refinement (STR) to reconcile predicted action chunks with proprioception and attenuate high-frequency residuals, improving temporal consistency and robustness. Across 567 standard and out-of-distribution trials per method, DMAT achieves higher aggregate success rates than Improved 3D Diffusion Policy (IDP3) and DiTFlow3D, with relative improvements of 129.1% and 95.7% respectively, generates action chunks 2.06× and 1.34× faster than these baselines, and remains robust under repeated 50 N external interventions.Link
