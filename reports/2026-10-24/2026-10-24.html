Executive Summary

  • Across diverse navigation domains, new traversability-aware controllers couple richer environment modeling with risk or preference reasoning: EBV-CAN uses a static external bird’s-eye image plus evolving endoscopic views and semantic queries to localize flexible endoscope tip/waypoints and predict bending/insertion commands, achieving 97.5% tip and 94.8% waypoint localization accuracy and 76.25% success in 1,920 physical trials while exposing a gap between low offline imitation error and real-world success; PATH-Net fuses visual/geometric bird’s-eye features and short-term vehicle history with inverse reinforcement learning to produce operator-preference-conditioned off-road cost maps and speed profiles that closely match 15 users’ teleoperated paths and reduce NASA-TLX workload scores from 34.7 to 22.2; ProTrav reconstructs probabilistic elevation fields with sparse variational GPs, propagates uncertainty into traversability costs, and embeds a risk-aware UCB term in an MPPI controller to achieve >90% simulated success vs <40% for a baseline and smoother attitudes; TRACE leverages vision-language–derived confidence maps with natural-language prompts to infer traversable path boundaries and embed them as soft constraints in NMPC, demonstrating smooth autonomous navigation in cluttered forests with deformable and visually ambiguous terrain. EBV-CAN: Vision--Language Grounding for Contact-Aided Endoscope NavigationStanford UniversityThe paper proposes EBV-CAN, a vision-language contact-aided navigation policy that uses a single static external bird’s-eye image plus evolving endoscopic views and motor states, with supervised semantic queries to localize the tip, waypoint, and target and to predict bending and insertion commands for flexible endoscope navigation. On the introduced VLCA dataset and 50 held-out episodes, EBV-CAN achieves 97.5% tip-coordinate localization accuracy and 94.8% waypoint localization accuracy on the global image, and in 1,920 physical trials of eight policies under a shared protocol it obtains 183/240 navigation successes (76.25%) under automatic blue-area scoring. The experiments show a discrepancy between offline imitation and real-world task success: GRU-based behavior cloning attains the lowest offline mean absolute error (0.056) yet only 1/240 physical successes, while EBV-CAN achieves substantially higher task completion, and instruction swapping in 17 dual-target episodes yields distinct target attention and motor outputs under identical observations.Link
Dispersive Tree Agreements for Multi-Robot CoordinationMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces a decentralized, real-time multi-robot planner, WarpWeaver, that extends dispersive tree search for differentially flat systems and uses local constraints plus decentralized column generation with space-rent negotiations to approximate joint-space optimal control as an anytime process. The authors show theoretically that inter-robot coupling can be replaced with suitable local constraints such that locally grown dispersive trees contain a near-optimal agreement, and that the negotiation protocol converges to an agreement whose near-optimality is certified when the process quiesces. In simulation, WarpWeaver reduces total travel time by up to 22% relative to centralized offline planners on the Dynobench suite and up to 37% relative to a decentralized real-time baseline, nearly doubles the success rate of a task-specific planner on graph connectivity tasks, and hardware experiments with six onboard-planning robots demonstrate real-world implementability.Link
TerraTransfer++: Demonstration-Free End-to-End PlannerUC BerkeleyThe paper presents TerraTransfer++, a demonstration-free end-to-end autonomous driving framework that first trains a single policy via high-throughput self-play in vectorized simulators, then distills its rollouts into a flow-matching trajectory teacher and a planner, and finally trains lightweight adapters on a frozen vision backbone using only paired (image, scene-state) frames without expert driving demonstrations. From one self-play teacher, the method yields two end-to-end students—a closed-loop policy and a planner—where the policy outperforms all published imitation-based end-to-end methods on the photorealistic 3D Gaussian splatting closed-loop benchmark HUGSim, according to the abstract. The planner trained in TerraTransfer++ attains 59.4 EPDMS on NAVSIM navhard, surpassing all published methods including DriveZero-Scale (57.1), and achieves 95.5 PDMS on navtest, reaching state-of-the-art performance on these benchmarks without human demonstration data.Link
Integrated Respiratory Sensing and Gated Control for Autonomous Needle Insertion Via CT Bore Robotic ArmUC San DiegoThe paper presents an integrated respiratory sensing and control system designed to perform autonomous, respiratory-gated needle insertions within a CT bore, eliminating the need for manual needle placement and patient breath-holding during CT-guided lung biopsy. Experiments with the proposed system demonstrate the ability to track and predict patient breathing cycles, execute autonomous respiratory-gated needle insertions, and detect anomalies in the breath cycle, suggesting feasibility of automated in-bore needle placement.Link
OpenTac: An Open Ecosystem for Customizable, Scalable, and Transferable 3D Tactile SensingMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces OpenTac, a scalable resistive tactile skin with a structured deformable overlay that converts tangential loading into spatially varying pressure, enabling decoding of local shear vectors from differential taxel responses and normal force from common-mode responses. The authors provide a full-stack OpenTac ecosystem, including a highly customizable CAD-based sensor design tool that can generate fabrication files from 2D outlines or developable 3D surfaces (including text-described geometries), a sensor simulator, cross-sensor transfer methods, and fast/adaptive data acquisition. Empirical characterization with a commercial high-precision force/torque sensor and experiments on four real-world robot embodiments show that OpenTac tactile skins support contact-rich feedback control and manipulation policy learning, and adaptive acquisition achieves kilohertz-rate partial readouts, up to 19.2× faster than full-array raster scanning at 16 sampled taxels per update for a 1024-taxel skin.Link
Live Thread Reconstruction for Autonomous ManipulationUC San DiegoThe paper proposes an image-to-manipulation pipeline that performs live 3D thread reconstruction while simultaneously generating safe and optimized sequential manipulation actions, targeting long-horizon autonomous suturing tasks in robotic surgery. The method is designed to handle the high-dimensional, deformable configuration space of surgical thread by continuously re-estimating the thread state from images and tracking past and current contact points, rather than assuming a fixed pose, thereby enabling more reliable manipulation during operations such as needle retrieval, detangling, and knot tightening.Link
Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action ModelsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces Progress Probes (P²), a lightweight runtime monitoring approach for Vision-Language-Action models that trains linear probes on internal policy activations from demonstrations to estimate task progress and flag executions that deviate from expected progress, without relying on external foundation models. Across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks using the open-source VLA π₀.₅, P² achieves task failure detection performance that is competitive with or better than monitors based on external vision-language models and methods requiring failure data for training, suggesting that internal activations suffice for effective runtime monitoring.Link

Motion Retargeting with Spatiotemporal Preferences for Humanoid Loco-ManipulationUnknown affiliationThe paper presents Spatiotemporal Retargeting (STR), a motion retargeting framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that uses a vision-language model to select key motion signals and body parts, identifies keyframes from these signals, and allocates extra optimization iterations and higher interaction weights to them to better preserve critical body–object relations at reduced computational cost. To enhance contact fidelity, the authors introduce Mechanics-Aware Contact Anchoring (MCA), which refines object-surface contact positions using an approximate object-wrench model while retaining desired contact surface directions, and propose Spatiotemporal Reinforcement Learning (STRL) that focuses sampling on key stages and uses a contact reward based on target proximity and contact forces for downstream control. Simulation studies on a Unitree G1 and real-world experiments on a Unitree R1 indicate that STR improves geometric contact coverage and continuity compared with uniform retargeting baselines, while the derived references and STRL improve downstream loco-manipulation task completion across diverse object-interaction motions.Link
OASIS: Online Anchor-centric Scene Inference System for Open-Vocabulary Retrieval and Navigation in Dynamic EnvironmentsBeihang University; Hangzhou Innovation Institute of Beihang UniversityThe paper proposes OASIS, an Online Anchor-centric Scene Inference System that represents environments using a sparse set of semantic anchors, each maintaining unified multimodal representations (semantic descriptions, visual features, geometric states) to support online mapping, open-vocabulary retrieval, reasoning, and navigation in dynamic settings. OASIS employs compact anchor-centric memory with on-demand evidence extraction and hierarchical retrieval to reduce redundant storage and improve retrieval reliability, and leverages transient observations gathered during navigation to relocalize objects that have moved due to scene changes. Experiments in simulation and real-world environments, including standard retrieval benchmarks and navigation tasks under static and dynamic conditions, show that OASIS can perform complex open-vocabulary retrieval and navigation and can successfully relocate displaced objects after scene changes.Link
PATH: Preference-Aware Traversability Estimation for Robotic Teleoperation in Off-Road NavigationBeijing Institute of Technology; China North Vehicle Research Institute; Hong Kong Polytechnic UniversityThe paper introduces PATH-Net, a Preference-Aware Traversability estimation Hierarchical Network that fuses visual and geometric bird’s-eye-view features with short-term vehicle state history to generate operator-preference-conditioned traversability cost maps and speed profiles for off-road teleoperation. PATH-Net uses maximum entropy inverse reinforcement learning with sparse speed supervision and an asymmetric feature aggregation mechanism that separates a risk-focused cost branch from a preference-adjusted speed branch, enabling alignment with different operator risk and speed preferences. In experiments with 15 operators over 225 teleoperated trajectories, PATH-Net’s recommendations achieve modified Hausdorff distances of 0.17 m (aggressive) and 0.15 m (conservative) relative to user trajectories, and questionnaire results show reduced subjective workload, with NASA-TLX scores dropping from 34.7 to 22.2 when using PATH-Net recommendations.Link
CMAR-search: Commonsense and Memory Augmented Reasoning for Object Search in Dynamic Interactive EnvironmentsBeihang University; Hangzhou Innovation Institute of Beihang UniversityThe paper proposes CMAR-search, a Commonsense and Memory Augmented Reasoning framework that builds a Functional 3D Scene Graph (F3DSG) from commonsense area functionalities, real-time perception, and accumulated memory to organize environments into functional areas and associated containers for hierarchical macro-area and micro-container action planning in dynamic object search tasks. CMAR-search enables dynamic object relocalization by continuously integrating environmental memory and perception, and in both simulation and real-world experiments it significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in success rate and search efficiency for object search in large-scale dynamic interactive environments.Link
Continuous-Time Motion Planning and Steering with Global ConvergenceSamsung SDS Research AmericaThe paper introduces GlobMP, a continuous-time Global Motion Planning method that formulates planning and steering as a single nonconvex program over piecewise-polynomial curves, using gradient projection with alternating cost reduction and feasibility restoration to enforce that polynomial inequality constraints hold over entire motion intervals while keeping trajectories close to learned proposals. Under mild assumptions and starting from a sufficiently fine grid, GlobMP is proven to have cost that approaches the global optimum as the number of polynomial pieces increases, while satisfying all constraints to arbitrary tolerance or exactly on stable instances. Experiments on real and synthetic benchmarks, including multi-robot teams of up to 200 robots, Robotarium multi-robot planning, maze navigation, and steering tactile-reactive bimanual humanoid policies, show that GlobMP achieves superior performance compared to strong baseline planners.Link
Adaptive Aerial Loco-Manipulation Policy Learning via LLM-Assisted Proactive Task GenerationNanjing University; National Key Laboratory of Novel Software Technology, Nanjing University; Northeastern UniversityThe paper presents ALMA, an aerial loco-manipulation framework that learns a contextual flight control policy which adapts from recent measurements and actions by using action-conditioned prediction of future measurement representations to retain response information relevant for flight control under external arm commands. ALMA incorporates an evolutionary training process in which a large language model analyzes observed control difficulties and proposes new arm motion structures, expanding training tasks to better cover challenging tracking scenarios. In simulation, ALMA reduces position RMSE by up to 24.7% relative to the strongest baseline, and the same policy transfers zero-shot to a real aerial manipulator where it reduces hover position RMSE by 79.6% during periodic arm motion compared to a real-world baseline controller.Link
ProTrav: Probabilistic Traversability with Elevation Uncertainty Propagation for Risk-aware Off-road NavigationTampere University; University of Tartu; Volvo Autonomous SolutionsThe paper proposes ProTrav, a probabilistic traversability estimation framework that uses a Sparse Variational Gaussian Process to reconstruct a probabilistic elevation field, then performs posterior function-space sampling to generate spatially correlated terrain realizations and propagate elevation uncertainty through geometric features into a distribution over traversability cost at arbitrary locations. ProTrav defines a risk term via a moment-based Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) on traversability cost and integrates it into a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) controller to enable risk-aware navigation on unstructured terrain. Simulation experiments show that ProTrav achieves navigation success rates above 90% on different terrains, compared with below 40% for a baseline and with reduced attitude variations, and real-world robot trials validate its applicability to off-road navigation.Link
LocoManiLab: A Unified Testbed for Data, Policies, Controllers, and Deployment in Humanoid Loco-ManipulationGamma Robotics; Institute of Artificial Intelligence (TeleAI), China Telecom; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; ShanghaiTech University; The University of Hong Kong; University of Science and Technology of China; Zhejiang UniversityThe paper introduces LocoManiLab, a unified testbed for humanoid loco-manipulation that provides a pipeline for data collection and generation, policy training, evaluation, and physical deployment across 234 atomic and compositional tasks in five household scene categories, supported by 51.5 hours of simulated teleoperation data. Using LocoManiLab, the authors evaluate twelve policies under a shared controller across task, scene, and visual perturbation splits, and two policies across five controllers, finding that success rates decline on unseen tasks and scenes and generally drop as visual perturbations increase, with notable differences in generalization and robustness across policies. LocoManiLab includes fine-tuning of a humanoid motion generation model for loco-manipulation to study alignment with task execution, and defines three simulated tasks closely matching real-world setups, where evaluation of three policies in both domains yields similar policy rankings, supporting the reliability of simulation-based assessment.Link
IORA: Irregular Object Immobilization under Restricted ActuationGwangju Institute of Science and Technology; Korea Electronics Technology Institute; Polygom; Yonsei UniversityThe paper presents IORA, a support-aware grasp synthesis framework for immobilizing irregular objects with low-DoF coupled robotic hands, which represents local surface regions of both hand and object as graphs and searches for kinematically feasible matchings to exploit contact with support surfaces. IORA refines candidate grasps by optimizing wrist pose and motor state for geometric contact feasibility, validates them through physical rollouts, and optionally performs task-guided local search around validated grasps to select configurations tailored to specified task conditions. Experiments on diverse objects indicate that IORA generates multiple grasp configurations with high grasp success and strong disturbance resistance, and real-world tests in a cooking scenario demonstrate physically executable grasps that achieve object immobilization using low-DoF hands and support contacts.Link
TRACE: Traversability-aware Reactive Navigation via VLM-driven Adaptive Constraints EstimationKorea Advanced Institute of Science & Technology (KAIST); Luleå University of TechnologyThe paper proposes TRACE, a traversability-aware reactive navigation framework that uses a vision-language model to generate confidence maps from positive and negative natural-language prompt sets, fuses them evidentially into a dense traversability map emphasizing signs of previously traversed paths, and extracts annotated points to estimate left and right path boundaries via percentile binning and spline or regularized polynomial fitting. TRACE incorporates the estimated traversable path boundaries as soft barrier constraints within a nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) formulation, enabling safe and adaptive navigation in unstructured forest environments with cluttered, deformable, and visually ambiguous terrain. Real-world experiments in forest environments show that TRACE enables smooth autonomous navigation under challenging visual and terrain conditions, demonstrating its effectiveness for traversability-aware reactive control of ground robots.Link
