## Executive Summary

- Humanoid and quadruped control advance with safer whole-body tracking, unified high-dynamic motion and recovery, and sub-kilogram continuous-rotation locomotion policies.
- Mobile navigation sees progress in construction-site blueprint-grounded routing, person-goal search using social context, and efficient geometry-aware VLN without runtime depth.
- Dexterous/contact-rich manipulation is accelerated by visuotactile diffusion policies, tactile skins and encoders, reflexive execution layers, and simple tactile integration into VLAs.
- World action models and VLAs evolve toward scalable data use and long-horizon reasoning via video/action priors, progressive subgoal planning, sparse imagination, and action-sketch conditioning.
- Safety, reliability, and efficiency are addressed through filter-aware humanoid fine-tuning with CBFs, RUL-aware motion planning, safe streaming flow planners, and calibration of high-level safety monitors.
- New simulation, benchmarking, and perception tools (Awomo-SimDataEngine, RoboChemGym, DexJoCo-X, ManiPhysicsBench, I2CD, CrowdOcc, RoboBridge) expand training and evaluation for manipulation and navigation in complex settings.

| Filter-Aware Fine-Tuning for Safe Humanoid Whole-Body Tracking | Stanford University | Analyzes mismatches between learned humanoid whole-body trackers and runtime safety filters such as CBFs, then introduces CoFiT, a filter-aware fine-tuning method that adapts pretrained policies to the presence of the filter. CoFiT sharply reduces constraint violations and required interventions in simulation and on Unitree G1 hardware, offering practical design principles for safe whole-body control in unstructured environments. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02341 |
| OpenRUA: Robot-Use Agents Are Zero-Shot Visuomotor Policies | Carnegie Mellon University | Shows that off-the-shelf coding agents given only terminal access to ROS 2 can function as zero-shot visuomotor policies without bespoke harnesses or task training. With a minimalist workspace, Claude Code (Opus 5) achieves near-perfect success on CaP-Bench and strong performance on LIBERO-PRO, while spontaneously building perception and closed-loop control programs from raw sensor interfaces. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02459 |
| Keep the Effect, Drop the Actor: Programmable Effect-to-Execution World-Action Models | New York University | Introduces PEWAM, a world-action model that compiles demonstrations into editable "effect programs" describing object keypoint trajectories and grasps, then re-solves execution for new scenes and embodiments. Effect programs edit cleanly, transfer across four robot arms without retraining, and substantially outperform goal-image or language conditioning on LIBERO and Meta-World tasks and real Franka manipulation. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02398 |
| NEEDLEWORK: Offline Rewriting of Robot Data with Verified Local Stitches | Stanford University | Presents NEEDLE, which augments high-dimensional robot demonstrations by inserting short, feasibility-verified action "bridges" between existing observations using only RGB, proprioception, and episode outcomes. A tailored sampling strategy incorporates these stitches during policy training without synthesizing images, yielding large success gains on real-robot manipulation tasks over prior trajectory-stitching baselines. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02339 |
| Bidirectional Voronoi-biased Exploration Curriculum for Reinforcement Learning | ETH Zurich | Proposes BVER, a bidirectional curriculum that grows start states from the goal and goals from the initial distribution, inspired by bidirectional RRTs, to tackle long-horizon sparse-reward tasks. Across mazes, quadruped box climbing, and ring-on-peg transfer, it learns faster and solves harder settings than prior reference-free curricula, approaching demonstration-based efficiency without expert data. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03395 |
