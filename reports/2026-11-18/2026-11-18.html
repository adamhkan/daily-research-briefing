Executive Summary

  • Several works advance safety-critical robot control: one distills a centralized control barrier function safety filter into a multi-agent reinforcement learning policy via implicit Q-learning and synthetic near-collision states, empirically yielding collision-free navigation in 1,800 test episodes without runtime safety filters and transferring a three-agent policy zero-shot to up to twenty simulated agents and to teams of up to five Crazyflie quadrotors; another augments an inverse-Jacobian PI visual servo controller with a residual Soft Actor-Critic policy and a delay-compensated Kalman filter to reduce conveyor tracking errors from 1.1–1.4 mm to 0.3–0.7 mm on a Franka arm; a third analyzes stability failures of Koopman controllers and introduces Tangent-Invariant Koopman to match the plant’s local linearization, achieving local stabilization on four unstable benchmarks and 88% constrained cart-pole swing-up success with 0.25 ms planning; and an extension of robust safe gradient flow RL handles trajectory-level and chance constraints, with theory bounding training-time constraint violations and experiments showing zero-shot sim-to-real deployment of whole-body humanoid control and collision-free Crazyflie waypoint navigation under probabilistic safety guarantees. (arXiv:2611.19190; arXiv:2611.19318; arXiv:2611.20402; arXiv:2611.19616)
  • New pipelines leverage limited or human-centric data for generalizable manipulation and humanoid control: AsymEEF transfers a small set of teleoperated bimanual demonstrations across heterogeneous two-, three-, and five-finger end-effector pairs via kinematic anchors, morphology-agnostic latent representations, and replay filtering, expanding replay-valid demonstrations by 16.6× in simulation and improving replay success on four simulated and six real bimanual tasks; Tactile-Driven Motor Adaptation structures humanoid manipulation policy learning around tactilely observed interactions with cone-geometry multi-finger contact and online adaptation, enabling a Unitree G1 with multi-fingered hands to open commercial round doorknobs in zero-shot sim-to-real tests and outperforming Rapid Motor Adaptation and reactive tactile baselines; and Ego2Sim2Humanoid converts egocentric RGB-D and head-motion recordings into physically verified humanoid training trajectories through scene reconstruction, contact-band prediction, motion adaptation, and RL policy training, allowing a GR00T-based visuomotor language model to perform autonomous loco-manipulation on a Unitree G1 using only the converted data without teleoperated robot demonstrations. (arXiv:2611.20557; arXiv:2611.18976; arXiv:2611.19196)
  • Several planning and optimization frameworks target long-horizon manipulation and motion generation: Lazy-MOSAIC performs lazy skill-sequence planning using a cheap learned skill-effect world model plus selective high-fidelity simulation validation and local repair, guaranteeing fully validated skill plans while cutting expensive simulator calls and planning time on cluttered-shelf retrieval in simulation and on hardware compared with MOSAIC; RoboKit offers a GPU-based toolkit for parallel differentiable optimization with multi-seed solvers and hybrid/coarse-to-fine scene representations, running 2–60× faster than task-specific optimizers on seven tasks, optimizing 4,096 grasps in 2.97 s, and distilling optimization into policies that execute 9–240× faster at similar accuracy while accelerating RL training; and multi-CONDOR learns motion primitives with multiple discrete attractors or continuous goal contours via imitation losses that align task dynamics to latent reference systems, with Lyapunov-based convergence analysis and experiments showing ~72% RMSE reduction vs. state-machine-selected single-goal primitives and 0% out-of-tolerance starts on multi-goal benchmarks, plus successful dual-handle grasping on a Franka arm. (arXiv:2611.21517; arXiv:2611.20185; arXiv:2611.21059)
  • Navigation and control for mobile robots and autonomous driving are improved by multiple systems: a controlled ProcTHOR evaluation isolates visual factors in ObjectNav and finds surface material changes dominate performance drops while clutter and lighting matter little, with a DINOv2-based model-based agent achieving 0.83 success yet remaining appearance-sensitive; a passage-focused hierarchical multi-robot framework uses a passage-induced convex graph, soft coordination in planning, and model predictive path integral control to achieve near-perfect success and superior makespan/flowtime in 1,200 simulations and collision-free operation of eight robots; a sampling-based whole-body MPC for supported reaching on a Unitree H1-2 extends reachable workspace by ~50 cm and completes reach-and-recovery in 81% of 54 trials; a depth-aware nursing assistant platform fuses RGB-D with rule-based avoidance and NavfnROS to boost navigation success by up to 40% and clearance by 35% over single-sensor baselines; EasyNav centralizes mapping, localization, planning, and control on a shared state to cut reaction latency from 377→33 ms (indoor laser) and 71→8 ms (GPS+LiDAR) vs. Nav2 while using less compute; a Room Probability Vector-based ObjectNav policy deployed on Spot and TurtleBot reaches 0.455 success over 66 trials with an 8.4-point drop from simulation traced to sensing and layout mismatches; AgenticJevNav couples planning, evidence memory, event-triggered verification, and bounded execution for agentic search, improving simulated success to 73.33% (a 21.67-point gain over an Agent-only baseline) and achieving 70% success on a quadruped robot; and VLN-Flash provides a training-free decoder that scores short-horizon action-prefix trees in one pass, preserving the original policy’s conditionals while delivering ~3× faster decoding and up to 2.7-point gains in navigation success/SPL, along with higher success and ~160 ms lower inter-action wait time on a Unitree GO2. (arXiv:2611.19621; arXiv:2611.18877; arXiv:2611.19100; arXiv:2611.19534; arXiv:2611.19894; arXiv:2611.19720; arXiv:2611.19912; arXiv:2611.19287)
  • Perception, mapping, and world modeling methods target efficiency and robustness: SuperGlance turns a decoder-only driving vision-language model into an encoder-style one-pass planner that scores 5,273 candidate trajectories from hidden states, supports reward-weighted and RL training, preserves VQA via multiple-choice and KL regularization, and matches or exceeds Qwen-Drive-1.0 planning performance on NAVSIM, PAI-AV, and WOD-E2E while running ~6× faster and achieving higher relative robustness when evaluation delay tracks GPU time; Polygraph incrementally fuses many drifting visual/visual-inertial odometry trajectories into a shared pose graph without a 3D map using cluster-based loop closures with learned image retrieval, metric depth, robust pose estimation, and a two-tier consensus gate, attaining comparable or better trajectory accuracy with fewer committed loop closures and faster runtime than prior work across diverse stereo, handheld, and legged datasets; Déjà-Events distills from a frozen RGB teacher into an event-only multi-view 3D model with sparsity-aware adaptive transformer gating, requiring no pose/depth ground truth and training ~1% of parameters, and achieves the lowest depth/pointmap and trajectory errors among evaluated event-based methods on EventScape and zero-shot MVSEC/DSEC, halving AbsRel on DSEC (0.15 vs. 0.30) with ~10× fewer parameters; and FlipTest introduces a three-level benchmark of visual minimal pairs, controls, and dense sweeps to test whether video world models flip predictions under small but causally critical image changes, finding 11 models perform poorly (best 18.3% pair accuracy, below 25% random) while a vision-language model and a measurement-based coding agent more accurately track the flip boundary, suggesting current pixel-prediction world models underutilize available causal state information. (arXiv:2611.19138; arXiv:2611.21163; arXiv:2611.21302; arXiv:2611.19822)
  • Additional control and navigation contributions focus on nursing, humanoids, and language-guided driving: a supported reaching MPC for humanoids uses local sensing and environmental bracing to extend reach and maintain heavily loaded postures for over four minutes before pose drift, reaching targets in 89% of 54 hardware trials; a depth-aware navigation stack for nursing robots integrates RGB-D perception with global planning and real-time control on a Husky+Kinova platform, improving simulated cluttered-environment success and minimum clearance over single-sensor approaches and validating efficient, safety-oriented operation in real hospitals; and a safe RL framework based on robust safe gradient flows is shown compatible with PPO-style estimators, offering high-probability bounds on cumulative trajectory-level constraint violations that decay with rollouts and enabling zero-shot deployment of whole-body humanoid control and collision-free drone waypoint navigation in sim-to-real tests. (arXiv:2611.19100; arXiv:2611.19534; arXiv:2611.19616)

Distilling Safety Filters for Filter-free Multi-agent RLUC BerkeleyThe paper proposes a reinforcement learning approach that learns only from executed safe actions via implicit Q-learning and distills a centralized control barrier function (CBF) safety filter into the policy at synthetic near-collision states, with theoretical guarantees that the distillation loss bounds constraint violations and per-step collision probability when distillation states cover deployed states. In simulation with multiple agents acting without any runtime safety filter and using only local observations, the distilled policies incur no collisions in 1,800 test episodes; a policy trained with three agents transfers zero-shot to teams of up to twenty agents with constant per-agent computation and remains collision-free with up to eight agents. On Crazyflie quadrotors, the distilled policies are demonstrated in hardware with up to five quadrotors flying simultaneously, indicating successful transfer of the filter-free multi-agent navigation policy to real robots.Link
AsymEEF: Bimanual Manipulation with Asymmetric End EffectorsUC San DiegoThe paper introduces AsymEEF, a framework that transfers a small set of teleoperated demonstrations across heterogeneous bimanual end-effector combinations (two-, three-, and five-fingered) using kinematic teacher anchors, sparse real-world X-Calib anchors, and a shared morphology-agnostic latent representation with a morphology-conditioned decoder, followed by replay filtering for executability. Using the learned representation, AsymEEF supports training a unified bimanual visuomotor policy across multiple end-effector pairs, enabling cross-end-effector reuse of demonstrations without additional teleoperation. Across four simulated and six real-world bimanual manipulation tasks, AsymEEF improves replay success over adapted transfer baselines and achieves a mean replay-valid demonstration expansion gain of 16.6× in simulation, and is further evaluated through downstream policy training on real robots.Link
Multi-CONDOR: Learning Multistable Motion Primitives from DemonstrationMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes multi-CONDOR, a CONDOR-based framework that learns dynamical-system motion primitives with either multiple discrete attractors or continuous attracting goal contours by combining imitation learning with losses that align learned task dynamics to latent reference systems encoding the desired attraction structure, backed by a Lyapunov-based convergence analysis. On simulated benchmarks with discrete goals, multi-CONDOR reduces trajectory RMSE by approximately 72% compared to separately learned single-goal primitives selected by a state machine, and for tasks with two and three goals reduces the fraction of initial states ending outside goal tolerance from 0.31% and 7.29% with StableNODE to 0% in both settings. Robotic experiments with a Franka Emika arm show that motion primitives learned with multi-CONDOR can successfully grasp a container by either of its two handles, demonstrating the ability to converge to alternative valid outcomes from demonstration data.Link
Lazy-MOSAIC: Accelerating Skill-Centric Manipulation Planning with Learned Skill-Effect World ModelsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper introduces Lazy-MOSAIC, a lazy planning framework for long-horizon manipulation that plans over hypothesized skill outcomes using a cheap learned skill-effect world model to predict each skill’s feasibility and resulting world state, and then selectively validates only promising candidates in a high-fidelity simulator while locally repairing the search when hypotheses are invalidated. Lazy-MOSAIC guarantees that every skill in a returned plan is ultimately validated in simulation, while significantly reducing the number of expensive simulator queries compared to prior skill-centric planning approaches such as MOSAIC. On cluttered-shelf retrieval tasks of increasing difficulty, both in simulation and on a physical robot, Lazy-MOSAIC plans substantially faster than baselines with fewer simulator calls, while matching or exceeding their success rates in all evaluated settings.Link
SuperGlance: Driving at a Glance with a Vision-Language ModelUniversity of WashingtonThe paper proposes SuperGlance, which converts a decoder-only vision-language model for autonomous driving into an encoder-style, one-pass driving policy by reading the model’s hidden state before decoding and using a linear head to score a fixed set of 5,273 candidate trajectories, enabling supervised reward-weighted training and reinforcement learning over these pre-enumerated trajectories. SuperGlance preserves the base model’s visual question-answering ability via multiple-choice training and a KL regularization term, so that the same one-pass representation can both plan and answer visual questions about driving scenes. On NAVSIM, PAI-AV, and WOD-E2E, SuperGlance plans at least as well as Qwen-Drive-1.0 while using 84 ms of GPU time (about 6× faster), and in AlpaSim closed-loop evaluation achieves comparable km per at-fault incident up to a 300 ms delay; when delay is tied to GPU time, its advantage over Qwen-Drive-1.0 increases from 2.8× to 3.9×, although at an imposed 1.2 s delay its performance falls below Qwen-Drive-1.0 operating at its own 518 ms.Link
Toward Planning Representations That Separate Task Structure from Visual ContentUniversity of WashingtonThe paper introduces a controlled evaluation of object-goal navigation in ProcTHOR houses where floor plans, object locations, goals, and start states are held fixed while individual visual factors (surface materials, clutter, lighting, and goal appearance) are varied, enabling isolation of how each visual change affects navigation performance. Across model-free RL, model-based RL, and agents using frozen pretrained visual encoders, the experiments show that surface material changes account for most of the average drop in success, whereas clutter and lighting changes have little effect, and even small global image statistics shifts—such as modifying only the goal object’s appearance—can significantly impair end-to-end agents. A model-based agent that plans over frozen DINOv2 image features achieves the highest success rate (0.83) under the full visual shift but still remains sensitive to surface appearance, underscoring the need for planning representations that decouple invariant spatial task structure from visual content.Link
Polygraph: Large-Scale Multi-Session Trajectory Optimization using Pose GraphsETH ZurichThe paper presents Polygraph, an online multi-session trajectory optimization pipeline that incrementally fuses many locally accurate but drifting visual/visual-inertial odometry trajectories into a shared pose graph without building a 3D map, processing data one keyframe and one spatial cluster at a time. Polygraph forms cross-session constraints by matching closed local clusters via learned image retrieval followed by sparse feature matching, monocular metric depth, robust pose estimation, and a two-tier consensus gate that only admits loop-closure edges once corroborated by independent evidence, while maintaining consistency through cheap incremental solves and periodic robust batch optimizations that prune outliers. On stereo outdoor driving, indoor HoloLens and iPad traversals, legged indoor locomotion with a Boston Dynamics Spot, and large-scale outdoor vehicle and handheld datasets, Polygraph runs faster than the closest prior work on the same GPU model, commits fewer loop-closure edges, and achieves comparable or better trajectory accuracy based on the prior work’s reported timings.Link
Residual Reinforcement Learning for Visual Servo Control with Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real TransferETH ZurichThe paper proposes a residual reinforcement learning approach for visual servo control in which a loop-shaped inverse-Jacobian PI controller is augmented by a residual policy learned with Soft Actor-Critic in a limited-fidelity digital twin, combined with a delay-compensated Kalman filter that fuses out-of-sequence camera measurements to produce latency-aware target position estimates. A frequency-domain sensitivity analysis provides a performance bound for a class of purely model-based controllers under uncertain kinematic mismatch, against which the learned residual controller is evaluated in both simulation and hardware. In experiments tracking moving objects on a conveyor with a Franka Emika Panda arm at conveyor speeds of 27.9–41.8 mm/s, the residual RL controller reduces mean tracking errors from 1.1–1.4 mm with the baseline inverse-Jacobian PI controller to 0.3–0.7 mm and drives errors below the derived model-based performance bound, demonstrating reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer despite modeling and measurement uncertainties.Link
RoboKit: Accelerating Parallel Differentiable Optimization for Robot LearningUC San DiegoThe paper introduces RoboKit, a general-purpose GPU-based toolkit for parallel differentiable optimization in robotics that combines multi-seed solvers, hybrid/coarse-to-fine scene representations for geometry, rendering, and physics, and differentiable cost functions to support both optimization-based planning and learning. RoboKit’s GPU-parallel multi-seed solvers run 2–60× faster than task-specific optimization baselines across seven evaluated tasks, and its hybrid/coarse-to-fine representations enable optimizing 4,096 grasp candidates in 2.97 s by accelerating collision checking, differentiable rendering, and simulation. By distilling optimization results into policies and using its parallel simulator for reinforcement learning, RoboKit produces policies that run 9–240× faster than the original optimizers at similar accuracy, and allows RL agents to reach the performance of a GPU-based baseline up to 2.1× sooner when trained on the same simulator.Link
Making Koopman Control Guarantees Hold on RobotsCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper analyzes why Koopman controllers that stabilize a learned latent-space linear model may fail to stabilize the underlying nonlinear plant, and shows that nominal closed-loop stability transfers when the learned closed-loop dynamics and the true plant dynamics agree to first order on the embedding’s tangent space at the target, with a derived robustness margin for approximate matching and a proof that a stable open-loop latent model cannot exactly represent an unstable equilibrium. It introduces Tangent-Invariant Koopman (TIK), which uses offline transitions to estimate a local plant linearization and constrains the learned latent dynamics to match this linearization to first order while employing a bilinear predictor to capture state-dependent actuation, thereby recovering the fitted LQR gain at the target. Across four simulated unstable benchmarks, all 40 Standard Koopman controllers learn latently stable closed loops but yield locally unstable nonlinear plants, whereas all 40 TIK controllers locally stabilize their respective nonlinear systems; an offline stability test using empirical curvature successfully identifies stable TIK controllers on two cart-pole tasks (though not under high noise), and under explicit model-error and curvature bounds, TIK achieves 88% constrained cart-pole swing-up success with a 0.25 ms planning budget and controls simulated seven-joint arms within 1–5 ms per step while guaranteeing constraint satisfaction after entering an invariant terminal set.Link
Déjà-Events: Multi-view 3D Geometry with Event Cameras via Sparsity-Aware Adaptive GatingETH ZurichThe paper proposes Déjà-Events, an event-only multi-view 3D model that predicts depth, camera pose, and pointmaps in a single forward pass by distilling token representations from a frozen RGB teacher (Déjà View), requiring no depth or pose ground truth and training only about 1% of its parameters. To address event sparsity, the method introduces sparsity-aware adaptive gating that adapts shared transformer block gates to event inputs and modulates them per patch by event density so that accuracy is preserved as more refinement steps are added. Trained solely on simulated data, Déjà-Events achieves the lowest pointmap and depth errors among evaluated event-based methods on EventScape and in zero-shot settings on MVSEC and DSEC, halving AbsRel error on DSEC compared with the strongest baseline (0.15 vs. 0.30) while using about one-tenth as many parameters, and also obtaining the lowest trajectory error on EventScape.Link
The Flip Test: Video World Models Fail to Learn the World StateUC BerkeleyThe paper introduces FlipTest, a three-level benchmark (visual minimal pairs, matched controls, and dense sweeps) that probes whether video world models correctly flip their predictions when small changes in an initial frame cause qualitatively different futures. Across 1,680 initial frames from 10 simulated and 15 real-world tasks, 11 evaluated video world models perform poorly on FlipTest, with the best model achieving only 18.3% pair accuracy—below the 25% accuracy of random guessing—and showing non-selective prediction changes that fail to track the true state boundary. On the same frames, a vision-language model and a coding agent that explicitly measures images more accurately track the flip boundary, suggesting that the necessary information is present in the visual input but current video prediction models fail to capture the causal state, motivating a rethinking of world modeling beyond pure next-frame pixel prediction.Link

Passage-Focused Soft Coordination for Hierarchical Multi-Robot Navigation in Bottleneck EnvironmentsNational Research University Higher School of Economics; Peking University; Saint-Petersburg UniversityThe paper proposes a passage-focused hierarchical multi-robot navigation framework that builds a passage-induced graph of convex sets and performs priority-ordered planning with two levels of soft coordination: passage-aware route selection that accounts for passage usage and traversal direction, and route-restricted continuous space-time planning that penalizes temporal conflicts. Local execution is handled by a model predictive path integral controller guided by the planned references, with adaptive temporal and geometric tracking plus selective replanning to accommodate execution deviations without rigid trajectory reservations while ensuring online collision avoidance. In 1,200 simulated instances across four environments with 5–30 robots, the framework achieves a near-perfect success rate and outperforms evaluated baselines in makespan and flowtime, and hardware experiments with eight robots complete all trials without collisions, indicating effective long-horizon coordination in bottleneck environments.Link
TMA: Sim-to-Real Forceful Humanoid Manipulation via Tactile-Driven Motor AdaptationAmazon; Rice University; The University of Texas at Austin; University of Maryland, College Park; University of MichiganThe paper presents Tactile-Driven Motor Adaptation (TMA), an integrated whole-body control framework for zero-shot sim-to-real forceful humanoid manipulation that structures policy learning around tactilely observed interactions, represents multi-finger contact with cone geometry, and adapts motor behavior online from tactile history. TMA jointly trains an upper-body manipulation policy with a pretrained general locomotion controller to coordinate dexterous multi-finger interaction, torque generation without slip, force-regulated pushing, and whole-body stability in the round-doorknob-opening task. Trained entirely in simulation and deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid with BrainCo Revo2 multi-fingered hands, TMA substantially outperforms Rapid Motor Adaptation and reactive tactile-policy baselines across four commercial round doorknobs in real-world evaluations.Link
Leaning Over to Reach Far-Away ObjectsUniversity of Colorado Boulder; University of Notre DameThe paper studies supported reaching for humanoid robots and proposes a sampling-based whole-body model predictive control (MPC) method that explicitly leverages environmental contacts for leaning, bracing, reaching, and recovery using only local sensing and fiducial markers. Running in real time at 34 Hz on a laptop and transferring from simulation to a Unitree H1-2 humanoid, the controller extends the robot’s reachable workspace by about 50 cm beyond unbraced standing reach and can maintain heavily loaded braced postures for over four minutes before pose estimation drifts. Across 54 hardware trials spanning six targets and three table heights, the system reaches the commanded target in 89% of trials and completes the full reach-and-recovery cycle in 81%, and analysis shows that temporal persistence of sampled actions and exploration scale are key to performance, with CEM yielding the broadest robustness to planning rate and model mismatch.Link
Ego2Sim2Humanoid: Agentic Real-to-Sim for Learning Loco-Manipulation VLAs without Robot DemonstrationsUniversity of Notre DameThe paper introduces Ego2Sim2Humanoid, a pipeline that converts egocentric RGB-D and head-motion recordings from glasses into physically verified robot training trajectories by reconstructing textured scenes, completing unseen surfaces when needed, and predicting target contact bands on manipulated objects. During motion adaptation, the method uses grasp planning to turn contact bands into feasible robot wrist and finger configurations, bilevel stance optimization for collision-free and reachable whole-body postures, and a motion generator plus two-stage motion correction to synthesize locomotion and out-of-view arm motions; it then trains a reinforcement-learning tracking policy in simulation with semantic rewards and renders synchronized robot-view data and actions. Fine-tuned solely on the converted dataset without any teleoperated robot demonstrations, a GR00T N1.7 visuomotor language action model performs autonomous loco-manipulation tasks on a Unitree G1 humanoid in real-world experiments.Link
VLN-Flash: Parallel Action Decoding with Adaptive Execution for Vision-and-Language NavigationBeihang University; Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications; Harbin Institute of Technology; Nanyang Technological University; National University of Defense Technology; University of Electronic Science and Technology of ChinaThe paper proposes VLN-Flash, a training-free decoder for vision-and-language navigation that scores the entire short-horizon action-prefix tree in a single forward pass using branch-specific attention and aligned token positions, exactly preserving the original policy’s conditional action distribution for each prefix. VLN-Flash computes at each depth the score margin between the stepwise-greedy prefix and its strongest alternative to adaptively choose how many actions to execute before re-observing, then executes the highest joint-score plan at the selected depth. On Uni-NaVid and StreamVLN, VLN-Flash yields nearly 3× faster action decoding than native autoregressive decoding and improves success rate and SPL on R2R-CE and RxR-CE by up to 2.7 percentage points, while on a Unitree GO2 it increases navigation success and reduces mean inter-action waiting time by about 160 ms after accounting for network latency.Link
Depth-Aware Path Planning and Collision Avoidance for Robotic Nursing Assistants Using Real-Time 3D VisionIndian Institute of Technology Kanpur; Indian Institute of Technology MandiThe paper presents a depth-aware navigation framework for robotic nursing assistants that fuses RGB-D depth sensing with rule-based obstacle avoidance and the NavfnROS global planner to handle cluttered environments with low-height and transparent obstacles that are difficult for LiDAR-only or vision-only systems. Implemented on a Husky A200 base with a Kinova Gen3 arm within a ROS-Gazebo multi-layer architecture for global planning, perception fusion, and real-time control, the system plans paths from start to goal while enforcing safety-aware navigation constraints. In simulated cluttered environments, the framework achieves up to a 40% higher navigation success rate and a 35% improvement in minimum clearance compared with single-sensor baselines, and real-world experiments further validate it as a computationally efficient and safety-oriented solution for healthcare robots.Link
Towards Probabilistically Safe Reinforcement Learning: Theory and Deployment on Humanoid and Aerial RobotsCalifornia Institute of TechnologyThe paper extends the robust safe gradient flow-based reinforcement learning (RSGF-RL) framework to handle general trajectory-level safety constraints, including chance constraints such as probabilistic collision avoidance, while allowing biased estimators of objectives and constraints and remaining compatible with algorithms like PPO. Theoretical analysis shows that, with high probability, the constraint violation during training under the proposed RSGF-RL scheme is bounded by the estimator bias plus a term that vanishes as the number of rollouts grows, decaying toward this level even from unsafe initial policies, and that the iterates converge to approximate KKT points of the original safe RL problem. Using PPO-style estimators within RSGF-RL, the authors demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real deployment of whole-body control policies on a Unitree G1 humanoid and collision-free waypoint navigation on a Crazyflie drone, indicating practical feasibility of the probabilistically safe RL approach.Link
Sim-to-Real Room-Mediated Object-Goal Navigation with ROS 2 and Nav2University of Technology SydneyThe paper deploys a Room-Mediated Co-occurrence ObjectNav policy based on Room Probability Vectors (RPVs) onto Boston Dynamics Spot and TurtleBot 4 via a robot-agnostic ROS 2 bridge, adapting sensing, localization, and action interfaces to transfer a simulation-trained semantic prior to real robots. In 66 real-world trials on Spot within a university research facility, searching for 11 object categories from two starting positions, the RPV-based policy achieves a raw Success Rate of 0.455. An analysis attributes the 8.4 percentage-point drop from the simulated Success Rate of 0.539 to factors including undetected glass walls, entry into off-limits areas, limited usable depth range, target size, and mismatches between the simulated room lexicon and the physical layout.Link
EasyNav: A Modular Architecture for Real-Time Navigation Across Heterogeneous Robot EnvironmentsUniversidad Rey Juan CarlosThe paper proposes EasyNav, a ROS 2 navigation architecture that centers all mapping, localization, planning, and control modules on an explicit shared navigation state, enabling modular, representation-agnostic pipelines under explicit compatibility and timing contracts. In real-robot indoor navigation experiments with laser sensing, EasyNav reduces reaction latency from 377 ms to 33 ms compared with Nav2 under matched conditions, while also using less CPU and memory. In large-scale indoor–outdoor navigation experiments using GPS and 3D LiDAR across multi-level routes, EasyNav reduces reaction latency from 71 ms to 8 ms compared with Nav2 under matched conditions, again with lower CPU and memory usage.Link
AgenticJevNav: Agentic Object Search and Navigation via Event-Driven VerificationUnknown affiliationThe paper introduces AgenticJevNav, an architecture that links goal-directed planning, evidence memory, event-triggered verification, and bounded execution for agentic object search and navigation, incorporating version, freshness, and once-only checks plus goal revision mechanisms to manage decisions and revoke obsolete actions. Across six simulated task families (60 trials), AgenticJevNav achieves a 73.33% success rate, improving aggregate success by 21.67 percentage points over a next-best Agent-only control variant under differing historical protocols. In a real-world deployment on a GENISOM L1 quadruped robot, AgenticJevNav attains a 70.0% success rate over 30 trials, including bounded search reporting, while remaining failures indicate limitations in grounding, approach planning, and runtime behavior.Link
