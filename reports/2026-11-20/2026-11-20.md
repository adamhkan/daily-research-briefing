## Executive Summary

- Several works advance locomotion control via learned neuromechanical or unified policies: Rat v2 with the Rat NCAP spinal-circuit-inspired architecture produces rat-like gaits at varying speeds in simulation, matches experimental locomotion and neural statistics, and shows through neuromechanical-interface analysis and in silico cell-type ablations that interface design shapes trainability, gait quality, and predicted graded gait perturbations; ReLoco trains a single proprioceptive humanoid controller with behavior-conditioned adversarial motion priors that, in simulation, achieves ~96.8% get-up and ~97.0% push-recovery success with command resumption and, on the 64.5-kg Tiangong 3.0 robot, executes walking, running, get-up, and post-fall resumption without controller switching or on-robot fine-tuning; WAM-Policy adapts a language-conditioned arm-centric world action model to quadrupedal loco-manipulation, then distills it via on-policy self-distillation and multi-teacher distillation to a fast student that maintains ~77% success in stage one, reaches 85% success after task-specific RL in stage two on QLM-Bench, and is deployed on a real Unitree Go2 Air with substantially reduced inference cost.  ([arXiv:2611.23364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23364); [arXiv:2611.23916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23916); [arXiv:2611.24011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24011))
- New models improve manipulation and deformable-object control by exploiting structure and rich sensing: Φ-CON models deformable linear object dynamics with a coupled oscillator network and a learned affine diffeomorphism, yielding a Lagrangian form that enables a model-based PD controller with closed-loop stability guarantees, NODE/LNN-comparable prediction using 90–97% fewer parameters, and 97.5–99.7% simulated endpoint error reduction plus 27.9% average error reduction in real bimanual DLO regulation versus model-free PD; 3DTouch builds a shared 3D visuo-tactile representation combining RGB-D, dense five-finger tactile forces, and explicit hand/object geometric relations to condition vision-language-action policies, raising average success by 42 percentage points over a VLA baseline and by 20 points over the strongest alternative on five dexterous manipulation tasks, with ablations attributing gains to tactile, geometric, and shared-spatial cues; Grana is a vision-language reward model that turns sparse subtask boundaries and action-quality annotations into dense progress targets for open-vocabulary subtasks, enabling zero-shot reward prediction on unseen short-horizon manipulations after pretraining on ~200 tasks and, after adaptation, improved progress estimation and policy performance on multi-minute long-horizon manipulation, including five evaluated long-horizon tasks.  ([arXiv:2611.23563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23563); [arXiv:2611.23961](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23961); [arXiv:2611.24926](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926))
- Safety and robustness are tackled through new theoretical concepts and sensing-aware filters: the recoverability framework replaces strict invariance with the requirement that a robot can reenter a certified recovery mode, with proofs that switching into a synthesized contraction-based tube guarantees exponential convergence despite disturbances and model error, and experiments on a simulated quadrotor and a full-scale IndyNXT racecar showing recoverability-based switching outperforms invariance safety filters and repeatedly recovers the car from aggressive, rain-induced limit-handling racelines; a radar-native control barrier function directly uses FMCW radar Doppler as the derivative of an ego-centered range CBF and incorporates a measurement margin from bounded range/Doppler/velocity/bearing errors to guarantee longitudinal-range safety under bounded errors and continuous detections, reducing buffer incursions to 2 versus 15 and 25 for constant-acceleration and constant-velocity Kalman filters across 508 encounters and stopping a Unitree Go1 within 0.777 ± 0.015 m of a moving obstacle versus a 0.758 m prediction while correctly reacting to a farther moving object over a nearer static return; the notion of overlap quantifies, for latent safety filters, the minimal fraction of safe transitions any cell-wise binary filter must reject to avoid known unsafe transitions and can be combined with conformal calibration to evaluate different latent designs, with experiments on visual navigation finding that a separate-encoder latent yields the lowest overlap and, at a fixed conformal level, rejects the fewest safe actions and attains the most goal reaches among three compared safety-filter architectures.  ([arXiv:2611.23589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23589); [arXiv:2611.23654](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23654); [arXiv:2611.23685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23685))
- World modeling and language-conditioned policies are refined for better counterfactual reasoning and control: JEPA Dots introduces a dual-latent world model that disentangles instruction-independent visual dynamics from language-conditioned task representations via masked-attention prediction and a flow-matching policy guided by task features, reaching 84.9% success on 40 LIBERO tasks with ~39.1M parameters, improving over a task-ID-conditioned baseline (76.0% overall, 67.1%→88.5% on LIBERO-Goal) and outperforming a larger NanoVLA-S model evaluated under a different protocol, while instruction-switching tests show dynamics latents remain invariant as task latents and policy outputs change with language; a separate study proves that world models trained solely on factual transitions cannot in general answer counterfactuals under the same hidden disturbance and, for systems where actions and unobserved disturbances act as separate forces with only second-order interactions, derives a counterfactual estimator whose error scales as O(h²), demonstrating in rendered simulators and an underwater vehicle that, using frozen visual encoders and JEPA-style world models, counterfactual R² improves from 38–46% to 86–96% (and 90–94% with 16× more data) and that hindsight planning with the estimator finds goal-reaching actions in 35–69% of events versus 14–23% for an oracle planner lacking outcome access.  ([arXiv:2611.24062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24062); [arXiv:2611.24778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778))
- Navigation and mapping are advanced with novel perception and planning architectures: ROMUX learns a cross-view transformer in simulation to map images from unknown, uncalibrated, and potentially multiple exocentric cameras directly to robot-centric metric obstacle maps without camera-pose inputs, then trains a PPO policy on these maps, achieving simulation success rates comparable to a robot-mounted-camera PPO and transferring zero-shot to real robots with differently placed exocentric cameras and varied room layouts; VLN-AR offers a hierarchical vision-language-navigation stack for UAVs that outputs structured 3D pose commands and intentions, uses an automated 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline and a three-stage training schedule to address 3D-data scarcity and domain gaps, and, with token sparsification and model-lightweighting, attains 7.1×–9.4× onboard inference speedups while maintaining strong navigation performance in simulation and real-world tests; BEV-Diffusion is a camera-only navigation model that encodes RGB sequences with a frozen DINOv2, lifts them via inverse perspective mapping and Bayesian fusion into a BEV memory, and uses a diffusion denoiser with uncertainty-aware guidance to jointly estimate graded traversability and paths, enabling a wheeled-legged robot to autonomously traverse 120 m in an unseen courtyard with 96% intervention-free completion (vs. 75–88% for baselines), retain 62–66% of out-of-view obstacles indoors versus ≤3% for baselines, achieve 67–100% task progress (vs. 20–40%), and adapt to a smaller legged robot by retraining ~3% of parameters in 2.3 minutes.  ([arXiv:2611.23894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23894); [arXiv:2611.23930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23930); [arXiv:2611.24155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24155))
- Multi-agent coordination, real-world benchmarks, and ego-to-robot transfer broaden evaluation and deployment: PIBT*ₜ extends priority-inheritance-based MAPF to include turn actions while preserving single-timestep planning and adding mechanisms for turn-induced dependencies, local deadlocks, livelock, and repeated pushing, solving up to 1000-agent instances within seconds on standard benchmarks and, in simulations and TurtleBot4/ADG-based manufacturing tests, reducing actual makespan versus turn-unaware MAPF; Flow-Oracle CBS (FOCBS) addresses MAPF with unassigned agents by combining conflict-based search over assigned agents with min-cost-flow routing and flow-conflict branching on min-cuts, guaranteeing termination and optimal assigned-agent service times on finite graphs, scaling well as unassigned agents increase, solving more instances than discarding non-completable paths, and cutting fleet travel by 40.8% relative to a parking-based baseline in simulation and robot experiments; RoboBuilder establishes a benchmark where coding agents co-design robot hardware and control from modular plus 3D-printed components, showing across 16,427 simulations and 135 built robots that agents can solve nearly all easy obstacles and discover mechanisms beyond search baselines but often fail on harder levels and in sim-to-real transfer (66% simulated passes succeed physically), while an active-learning loop that refines a simulator from real data and then redesigns robots eliminates 72% of level-1/2 real-world failures; Ego2X introduces the CORE contact-centric representation (surface anchors, relative forces, grasp modes, interaction states) to convert monocular egocentric human videos into robot-executable data, reducing in-contact hand depth error by 33.7% on HOT3D, more than doubling realized contacts over a HuRo baseline on EgoDex (62.9% vs. 27.9%), and, when used to train X-VLA solely on Ego2X-generated episodes in RoboTwin 2.0, boosting zero-shot success from 40.5% to 67.0% over 200 paired scenes.  ([arXiv:2611.23911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23911); [arXiv:2611.24108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24108); [arXiv:2611.23855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23855); [arXiv:2611.24138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24138))

| Neural Circuit Architectural Priors for Rat Locomotion | New York University | The paper proposes Rat v2, a flexible biomechanical rat body, and Rat NCAP, a mammalian-spinal-circuit-inspired neural architecture with cell-type-specific connectivity and a flexor-extensor neuromechanical interface, trained using GPU-accelerated embodied simulation infrastructure to produce locomotion at varying speeds and gaits without imitation learning. In simulation, Rat NCAP generates locomotion statistics, kinematics, and cell-type-specific neural activity consistent with experimental data, and analyses show that the neuromechanical interface strongly influences both trainability and gait quality. In silico cell-type ablations in Rat NCAP reproduce known gait phenotypes and predict graded gait changes under progressively stronger perturbations, suggesting that this intermediate-abstraction embodied model can yield mechanistic insights into locomotor circuits. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23364 |
| Learning the Dynamics of Deformable Linear Objects Through Coupled Oscillators for Bimanual Robotic Manipulation | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper introduces Φ-CON, a force-driven deformable linear object dynamics model combining a floating-base coupled oscillator network with a learned affine diffeomorphism between observed positions and latent coordinates, yielding shape dynamics that are stable by construction and admit explicit kinetic and potential energy expressions. Using the model’s Lagrangian structure, the authors design a model-based PD controller that compensates learned potential forces and provides closed-loop stability and convergence guarantees for DLO endpoint regulation. Across four simulated DLOs, Φ-CON attains prediction accuracy comparable to NODE and LNN baselines with 97.2% and 90.2% fewer parameters respectively, and its controller reduces mean endpoint regulation error by 97.5–99.7% in simulation and by an average of 27.9% per target in real bimanual robot experiments compared to a model-free PD baseline. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23563 |
| Recoverability, Not Invariance: Certified Safety for Robots at the Limits of Performance | Stanford University | The paper introduces recoverability, a contraction-theoretic safety concept that certifies a robot’s ability to reengage a trusted recovery mode instead of requiring forward invariance of the deployed controller. The authors prove that switching into a certified recovery mode within a synthesized tube guarantees exponential convergence despite disturbances and model error, and they formulate a switching-time program using a contraction insertion gradient in which feasibility alone ensures safety while optimality shapes performance. Sequential convex synthesis is used to grow certified tubes toward the viability kernel, and experiments on simulated quadrotor flight and a full-scale IndyNXT autonomous racecar show that recoverability outperforms invariance-based safety filters and repeatedly recovers the racecar from aggressive racelines at the limits of handling in rain. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23589 |
| Radar-Native Control Barrier Functions: Doppler Range Rate as a Measured Barrier Derivative | UC San Diego | The paper proposes a radar-native control barrier function approach that directly uses FMCW radar Doppler measurements as the derivative of an ego-centered range CBF, yielding a closed-form safety filter on the robot’s forward velocity without reconstructing range rate. A measurement margin derived from bounded errors in range, Doppler, ego velocity, and bearing provides a safety guarantee on longitudinal range under bounded measurement errors and continuously available detections. In 508 paired encounters, the direct-Doppler approach reduces buffer incursions to 2 versus 15 and 25 for constant-acceleration and constant-velocity Kalman filters, and on a Unitree Go1 it stops at 0.777 ± 0.015 m versus a predicted 0.758 m while reacting to a farther approaching object over a nearer static return, indicating reduced delay between obstacle motion changes and safety actions. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23654 |
| Measuring the Conservatism of Latent Safety Filters for World Models | UC San Diego | The paper defines overlap as the share of safe transitions that lie in latent–action cells containing at least one unsafe transition, and proves that this overlap equals the minimum fraction of safe transitions any cell-wise binary latent safety filter must reject to avoid accepting unsafe transitions in the data. The authors show that overlap and conformal calibration can be computed for any latent representation, and use them to compare three latent safety filter designs that attach safety labels to either a classifier on top of the world model, the world model itself, or a separate encoder. On a visual navigation task, the latent representation from a separate encoder yields the lowest overlap and, at a fixed conformal level, a safety filter that rejects the fewest safe actions and enables the planner to reach the most goals among the three designs. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23685 |
| JEPA Dots: Dual-Latent World Model for Language-Guided Robot Control | Stanford University | JEPA Dots introduces a dual-latent world model that separates instruction-independent visual dynamics from language-conditioned task representations, using masked attention for dynamics prediction and a flow-matching policy guided by task features for language-guided robot control. On 40 LIBERO tasks, JEPA Dots achieves 84.9% overall success with approximately 39.1M parameters, outperforming the reported 78.7% success of NanoVLA-S with 161.0M parameters under a different evaluation protocol, and improves success from 76.0% to 84.9% relative to a task-ID-conditioned adaptation baseline, including an increase from 67.1% to 88.5% on LIBERO-Goal. Instruction-switching experiments show that JEPA Dots’ task representations and policy outputs change with the language input while the dynamics representations and predicted world evolution remain invariant under fixed observations and actions, indicating successful decoupling of control from dynamics. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24062 |
| Grana: Compositional Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation | University of Washington | The paper presents Grana, a vision-language robot reward model that uses open-vocabulary subtask descriptions to identify unfinished subtasks and estimate their continuous progress, composing subtask predictions into long-horizon task progress estimates. The authors introduce an annotation protocol that converts sparse subtask boundaries and action-quality labels into dense progress targets without requiring annotators to assign explicit numeric progress scores, addressing issues with elapsed-time proxies such as variable subtask durations and failures that undo progress. Pretraining Grana on 200 diverse, mostly short-horizon manipulation tasks enables zero-shot reward prediction on unseen tasks and, after adaptation, improves progress estimation and downstream policy performance on multi-minute, long-horizon manipulation tasks, including five evaluated long-horizon tasks. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926 |
| Counterfactual World Models: JEPAs That Reason About "What If" | New York University | The paper shows theoretically that world models trained solely on factual transitions cannot, in general, answer counterfactual queries about outcomes under alternative actions with the same unobserved disturbance, regardless of model capacity or data. Leveraging systems where actions and hidden disturbances act as separate forces and interact only at second order in time, the authors derive a counterfactual estimator that, for transition duration h, approximates the counterfactual outcome as the observed outcome plus the difference in model predictions between alternative and factual actions, with an O(h²) error bound and a matching minimax lower bound under unpaired factual data. Using frozen visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2, MAE, DINOv3, SigLIP 2, CLIP, EVA-02) and JEPA world models on rendered simulators, the proposed counterfactual method improves counterfactual R² from 38–46% to 86–96% on four simulators and to 90–94% on an underwater vehicle with 16× more data, and in hindsight planning finds actions that would have reached the goal in 35–69% of events versus 14–23% for an oracle planner that knows the simulator but not the outcome. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24778 |
| RoboBuilder: Agentic Robot Co-Design with Real-world Video Feedback in the Loop | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper introduces RoboBuilder, a real-world benchmark where coding agents co-design robot hardware and control programs from standard components and a custom 3D-printed part, with every design that succeeds in simulation physically built and tested on a real obstacle course. Initial experiments across ten obstacles at three difficulty levels, involving four coding agents, 16,427 simulations of 12,774 distinct robots, and 135 physically built robots, show that agents can solve nearly all easy obstacles and discover mechanisms beyond search baselines, but often fail at harder levels, in multi-obstacle generalization, and in transferring simulated success to reality, where only 66% of simulated passes hold. By actively learning a simulator from requested measurements and videos of real runs, and then redesigning robots after observing real failures, an agent can correct aspects of its simulator and eliminate 72% of level-1 and level-2 real-world failures, demonstrating benefit from closing the sim-to-real loop. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23855 |

| Asymmetric Decomposition for Multi-Agent Path Finding with Unassigned Agents | Hefei National Laboratory; State Key Laboratory of Precision and Intelligent Chemistry; University of Science and Technology of China | The paper proposes Flow-Oracle Conflict-Based Search (FOCBS) for Multi-Agent Path Finding with Unassigned Agents (MAPF-UA), which performs conflict-based search over assigned agents while routing unassigned agents via a min-cost flow oracle, and introduces flow-conflict branching that converts min-cut blockers into constraints on assigned agents without sacrificing optimality. | Ego2X: Contact-aware Transfer from Egocentric Human Data to Any Robot | Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications; Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences; PowerTech(Mite.) | The paper introduces Ego2X, an object-centric ego-to-robot transfer framework that converts monocular egocentric human videos into physically executable robot data for both dexterous hands and parallel grippers by focusing on object motion and contact rather than just visual imitation.
