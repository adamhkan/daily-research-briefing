Executive Summary

  • Several works advance robot manipulation learning: AdaDexTrack combines a distilled generalist tracker with a reinforcement‑learned in‑loop modulator (reference, object‑latent, and positional‑target modulation) to improve language‑conditioned dexterous hand–object tracking, outperforming prior state of the art in simulated unseen‑trajectory and unseen‑object tests and enabling zero‑shot sim‑to‑real gains when adding the modulator on hardware; FLORE learns force‑reactive humanoid loco‑manipulation from robot‑free demonstrations using a vision/proprioception/wrench‑conditioned diffusion policy plus a hybrid compliance controller, achieving 80% success vs 26.7% for a position‑only policy across three contact‑rich tasks; EXE pretrains VLA models on egocentric human videos to factorize a latent action space into embodiment‑agnostic manipulation priors and embodiment‑specific motions, yielding consistent gains over non‑egocentric baselines on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa GR1, and real‑world tasks; and a nonlinear Gaussian‑kernel data‑enabled predictive controller with damped Gauss–Newton optimization and hybrid buffer management achieves real‑time trajectory tracking for a Franka arm pushing a block with unknown, changing friction in MuJoCo without an explicit dynamics model. (arXiv:2610.33852; arXiv:2610.35862; arXiv:2610.33685; arXiv:2610.35458)
  • New manipulation and autonomy benchmarks highlight capability gaps: PhysLadder defines 16 matched household tasks across three levels of contact and force complexity, where GPT‑6 Astra zero‑shot achieves 80.0%/47.6%/0.0% real‑world success on L1/L2/L3 with closely matched sim results, revealing sharp degradation with increasing interaction demands; RobotAgentBench builds 50 interactive household layouts and multi‑level metrics for open‑world autonomy, with experiments showing strong performance gaps between manipulation and full autonomy and advantages for agentic, recursively self‑improving architectures; BEHAVIOR‑Spatial couples 3,256 spatial QA queries and 24 tasks to show that a baseline policy’s manipulation success drops from 41.6% to 16.1% when only language instructions change, partially mitigated by oracle target cues, and that VLM QA/grounding accuracy only partially predicts VLA behavior; and RoboWorM proposes deployment‑aware KIVA and RIGIS metrics that improve alignment with human judgments of physical realism (e.g., 25.8% hallucination‑detection gain and 130% higher correlation) and expose how rollout acceleration can preserve appearance while degrading physics in robotic video world models. (arXiv:2610.34527; arXiv:2610.34649; arXiv:2610.34051; arXiv:2610.33807)
  • Several studies target safer, more interpretable vehicle and crowd navigation: Dream2Assist augments a recurrent state‑space world model with online intent inference and fictitious co‑play to yield intent‑conditioned assistive driving policies that improve torque alignment and reduce collisions over non‑intent baselines in N=30 racing and N=10 urban human‑subject studies, supported by an iterative deployment pipeline; a teleoperation field experiment with an N=18 sample and a full‑scale vehicle finds that a LiDAR+camera sensor‑fusion bird’s‑eye view significantly improves usability scores (SUS 77.9/100) and reduces cognitive load and completion time versus LiDAR‑only BEV and camera‑only views; a modular AV perception‑prediction‑planning stack that propagates structured uncertainty and constructs convex safe sets achieves 0% collisions and 0% solver infeasibility in Monte Carlo four‑way‑intersection simulations versus 36% collisions for deterministic and chance‑constrained baselines and runs in real time on Jackal UGVs; and Neuromorphic Decision Dynamics provides a decentralized, event‑based control law with analytical guarantees of collision‑free, deadlock‑free traversal and finite‑time traversal order formation under stated conditions, validated via large‑scale simulations and hardware tests in congested environments. (arXiv:2610.34650; arXiv:2610.35630; arXiv:2610.33186; arXiv:2610.33271)
  • New planning, safety, and monitoring methods improve robot robustness: a real‑time quadruped motion planner keeps a fully actuated SE(2) body model with anisotropic, heading‑coupled velocity bounds and composite Bernstein polynomial trajectories to guarantee continuous‑time collision avoidance and input limits in a nonconvex closed‑loop optimizer, recovering lateral and heading‑coupled maneuvers excluded by simplified kinematics in simulation and hardware; an active reachability‑learning monitor for humanoid fall risk alternates value learning with uncertainty‑guided exploration in a reach–avoid formulation, improving coverage and accuracy of the recoverability envelope on stochastic benchmarks and a 29‑DoF Unitree G1, with zero‑shot transfer to hardware; and a wheeled biped RAIWAY with a contact‑informed controller that infers latent multi‑contact events from proprioception alone traverses steep slopes, gravel, rocky terrain, and 15‑cm outdoor stairs more reliably than baselines lacking the contact‑informed mechanism. (arXiv:2610.35670; arXiv:2610.33317; arXiv:2610.33172)
  • Several works introduce new robotic platforms and agentic frameworks for skill adaptation: ORBIT is a multi‑finger hand with a rotating knuckle that reconfigures finger layouts in under 1 s while sustaining 1.57 rad/s finger speeds and 50 N root force, enabling tasks like cube‑face reorientation, lid threading, and sub‑centimeter pinching across anthropomorphic, isometric, and Barrett‑style layouts; MOTIO is an agentic skill‑engineering system that turns human videos, metric assets, and reusable skills into executable humanoid loco‑manipulation programs with physics‑based execution and multi‑frame feedback, demonstrating long‑horizon skills such as 101.75‑s bottle disposal and 76.0% screw‑tightening residual reduction and supporting distillation into a single reference policy that solves 18 simulated tasks; RoboDoctor pairs a fixed generalist robot policy with a language‑model agent that performs execution‑time diagnosis and method refinement using linked, reusable experience records, achieving a mean score of 57.10 and 49.44% native success over 54 RoboDojo‑Sim entries while leaving the isolated impact of cross‑episode memory as future work. (arXiv:2610.33241; arXiv:2610.33446; arXiv:2610.35604)

AdaDexTrack: Dynamic Modulation for Adaptive and Generalizable Dexterous Manipulation TrackingUC San DiegoThe paper proposes AdaDexTrack, a language-conditioned dexterous manipulation framework that combines a distilled generalist tracker with a reinforcement-learned modulator that performs in-loop reference modulation, object-latent modulation, and positional-target modulation for robust hand–object tracking from noisy text instructions. Across large-scale evaluations in simulation, AdaDexTrack consistently outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods on unseen-trajectory and unseen-object test sets in both average tracking error and success rate, indicating improved robustness and generalization for contact-rich manipulation tracking. The system demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on real hardware, where adding the modulator on top of the tracker-only variant yields substantial gains in manipulation performance, suggesting that in-loop modulation reduces drift from noisy language references during real-world execution.Link
Dream to Assist: Intent-Conditioned World Models for Shared Vehicle ControlMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper introduces Dream2Assist, a model-based reinforcement learning framework that augments a recurrent state-space world model with online intent inference and fictitious co-play training to enable proactive yet legible shared control for assistive driving without overriding human agency. Through three human-subjects studies and a preliminary on-vehicle deployment in high-speed racing (N=30) and urban driving (N=10), intent-conditioned policies show measurable improvements in torque alignment and collision rate compared to non-intent-conditioned baselines. An iterative deployment pipeline combining policy down-selection, reward restructuring, and human–machine interface design is reported to mitigate the sim-to-real-human gap for learning-based assistive agents in safety-critical, real-time vehicle control.Link
Revisiting Direct Control: A Field Evaluation of Sensor Fusion Efficacy in Teleoperating Full-Scale Autonomous Vehicles via Bird's Eye ViewMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper conducts a field experiment with a full-scale, car-sized vehicle and N=18 participants to compare three teleoperation interfaces: a standard camera feed, a bird’s-eye-view (BEV) from raw LiDAR, and a sensor-fusion BEV combining LiDAR and camera data. Contrary to prior passive-observation studies, the sensor-fusion BEV interface significantly outperforms the LiDAR-only BEV and camera-only views in perceived usability (SUS 77.9/100; p≤0.0001 vs LiDAR, p=0.043 vs camera), reduces cognitive task load (NASA-TLX 34.58/100, p=0.01 vs LiDAR), and achieves statistically significant reductions in task completion time (p=0.0461). The results indicate that BEV sensor fusion improves safety margins and reduces operator mental demand during active remote driving, challenging existing teleoperation conventions that favor simpler visualizations.Link
Continuous-Time Safe Planning and Control for Agile QuadrupedsMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper presents a real-time motion planning framework for quadrupeds that retains a fully actuated SE(2) body model with anisotropic, heading-coupled velocity bounds, and parameterizes trajectories as composite Bernstein polynomials to certify collision avoidance and input bounds continuously over the planning horizon. By accepting the resulting nonconvex optimization and solving it in closed loop at online replanning rates, the method directly commands body velocities to the locomotion controller while guaranteeing continuous-time safety with respect to moving obstacles, unlike planners that certify safety only at discrete samples. Simulation and hardware experiments on a quadruped navigating among unknown moving obstacles show that the approach recovers lateral and heading-coupled motions that are excluded by simplified kinematic models, demonstrating improved exploitation of the robot’s true dynamic capabilities.Link
Learning Force-Reactive Humanoid Loco-ManipulationCarnegie Mellon University; New York UniversityThe paper proposes FLORE, a framework for learning force-reactive humanoid loco-manipulation that uses robot-free demonstrations from force-sensing handheld grippers and wearable body trackers to train a vision-, proprioception-, and wrench-conditioned diffusion policy that outputs actions and per-hand force commands. A hybrid compliance whole-body controller with force tracking executes policy outputs by integrating force error from real F/T sensors per hand, achieving accurate tracking along commanded force directions while maintaining compliance along other axes with minimal loss in whole-body motion accuracy in simulation. Across three contact-rich tasks on a humanoid robot, the force-reactive policy achieves an 80% success rate, substantially outperforming a position-only policy trained on the same demonstrations and executed with the same controller, which attains 26.7% success.Link

PhysLadder: A Hierarchical, Matched Sim-Real Benchmark for Physical InteractionCarnegie Mellon University; Stanford UniversityThe paper introduces PhysLadder, a hierarchical Sim-Real benchmark of 16 matched household manipulation tasks structured into three levels: L1 (contact selection and geometric alignment), L2 (force regulation in sustained contact), and L3 (coordinated contact reconfiguration plus force regulation), with shared task definitions and success criteria across simulation and real-world setups. Evaluating GPT-6 Astra zero-shot over three trials per task, the model attains real-world success rates of 80.0% on L1, 47.6% on L2, and 0.0% on L3, with corresponding simulation success rates of 66.7%, 47.6%, and 0.0%, revealing a sharp performance drop as interaction demands increase and complete failure on the most demanding level. The study finds that for Astra, 10 of 16 tasks show identical success counts between sim and real and the remaining 6 differ by only one success out of three trials, supporting matched simulation as a scalable testbed for probing the spatial reasoning, contact selection, force regulation, and coordination limitations that currently bound reliable manipulation.Link
TRIAGE: Efficient Vision-Language-Action Inference Needs Both Reuse and PruningCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper analyzes token-level behavior in vision-language-action backbones and finds that whether to prune or reuse cached key–value states for a visual token should depend on its representation drift, with low-drift tokens performing no worse under reuse than pruning, while high-drift tokens can incur up to 4.4× higher action error under reuse compared to pruning. Building on this diagnostic, the authors propose TRIAGE, a training-free accelerator that, under a fixed token recomputation budget, treats caches as valid only if drift is below a calibrated backbone-specific threshold and not too old, then prioritizes recomputation of important invalid tokens, reuses valid tokens, and prunes the rest, with prune-only and reuse-only as special cases. On LIBERO and SimplerEnv robotic manipulation benchmarks running on a Jetson AGX Orin, TRIAGE maintains average success rates within 3 percentage points of full recomputation while reducing prefill latency by up to 48%, and when recomputing only 7% of visual tokens, it outperforms the strongest baseline by 24.4 percentage points on LIBERO-Object.Link
RobotAgentBench: Towards Open-world Robot AutonomyUC San DiegoThe paper introduces RobotAgentBench (RAB), a benchmark for open-world robot autonomy that constructs 50 diverse, interactive household layouts via an agent-assisted human-in-the-loop real-to-sim pipeline, supporting 90 atomic tasks, 18 short-horizon tasks, 8 long-horizon tasks, and 4 open-ended scenarios. RAB provides multi-level evaluation: task-level assessments decompose performance into task discovery, context reasoning, manipulation, failure recovery, and robustness, while scenario-level assessments measure how robot agents integrate these abilities to autonomously fulfill high-level user needs in realistic household environments. Experiments using standalone VLA/WAM models and varied agent architectures under a unified planning–execution–verification framework reveal substantial performance gaps between manipulation and full autonomy, highlight advantages for agentic architectures with recursive self-improvement, and underscore remaining challenges for open-world robot autonomy.Link
RoKON: Efficient and Adaptive Data-Enabled Predictive Control for Robotic ManipulationCarnegie Mellon UniversityThe paper extends data-enabled predictive control for robotics by using a nonlinear Gaussian-kernel-based predictor to model input–output relationships and by developing a damped Gauss–Newton optimization method that exploits the least-squares structure to achieve real-time control performance. A hybrid buffer management strategy is introduced that selectively incorporates informative online data while retaining relevant offline data, enabling the predictor to adapt to changes in the system dynamics over time. In MuJoCo simulations of a Franka Research 3 arm pushing a block with unknown, changing friction, the proposed controller demonstrates effective trajectory tracking and computational suitability for real-time manipulation without requiring an explicit dynamics model.Link
Behavior Spatial Benchmark: Evaluating Spatial Understanding for RoboticsStanford UniversityThe paper presents BEHAVIOR-Spatial, a benchmark derived from BEHAVIOR-1K that evaluates spatial understanding for robotics across three components: 3,256 spatial QA queries over 49 activities, 1,240 point-based target grounding queries in 14 manipulation tasks, and action evaluations for those 14 manipulation plus 10 navigation tasks. Under a manipulation protocol that tests both held-out configurations and instruction changes within a fixed layout, a baseline policy reaches 41.6% success on held-out configurations but only 16.1% when instructions change, while providing oracle visual target cues increases these rates to 60.3% and 38.0%, respectively, revealing substantial spatial instruction-following limitations. Analyses show policy-dependent correlations between VLM question answering/grounding accuracy and VLA manipulation performance, motivating joint evaluation of spatial perception metrics and robot behavior rather than relying solely on task completion as evidence of correct spatial understanding.Link
RoboWorM: Evaluating Movement Realism, Consistency and Acceleration Affects in Embodied Video World ModelsStanford UniversityThe paper proposes RoboWorM, a deployment-aware benchmark for robotic Video World Models that uses hardware priors (robot kinematic trees, fixed camera setups) and two deterministic, VLM-free metrics—KIVA for intra-view kinematic stability and RIGIS for multi-view geometric consistency—to systematically evaluate structural and spatiotemporal fidelity across 1,605 episodes spanning multiple robot morphologies, tasks, and view configurations. Using robotic invariants, KIVA improves hallucination detection alignment with human annotations by 25.8% relative to the strongest frontier VLMs, and RIGIS achieves a 130% higher Spearman correlation with human preferences for physical consistency evaluation. In a large-scale audit of quality–efficiency trade-offs over more than 29,000 generated rollouts, evaluating 9 acceleration techniques on 3 leading world models, the paper shows that acceleration methods can preserve superficial visual quality while degrading physical realism, and demonstrates up to 2.3× inference speed-up with minimal quality loss when guided by the proposed metrics.Link
Thermal-Headroom Routing for Efficient Robot Perception in Extreme HeatMassachusetts Institute of TechnologyThe paper proposes Thermal-Headroom Routing (THR), a lightweight, predictive model-selection policy that uses a closed-form RC thermal model to forecast processor temperature and exclude detector configurations expected to trigger thermal throttling for robot perception workloads. In simulations with four YOLO11 configurations on seven real-world traces, eight ambient temperatures, and 20 randomized seeds, THR eliminates throttling at 45–55 °C with tight inference deadlines, reduces deadline misses by 3.9–7.5 percentage points, and lowers energy per on-time frame by approximately 12–13% relative to a latency-belief router; a separate deadline-paced experiment yields 6.2–9.5 percentage-point miss-rate reductions. Experiments indicate that well-tuned thermostats or duty caps can match THR only at fixed operating points, while predictive routing is more advantageous when ambient conditions or thermal parameters differ from those used to tune static policies; measurements on an Apple M5 support the single-node heating approximation but do not confirm actual hardware throttling behavior.Link
RoboDoctor: Training-Free Recursive Self-Improvement for Robot PoliciesNew York UniversityThe paper introduces RoboDoctor, a training-free framework that combines a fixed-weight generalist robot policy with a language-model agent to perform execution-time adaptation via diagnosis, grounded action, and persistent cross-episode experience aimed at revising three targets: control allocation between policy and agent, intervention timing, and refinement of physical methods. RoboDoctor maintains linked experience records that capture the rationale, method, outcome, and reuse conditions for decisions, enabling strategy changes, selective corrections, and grasp revisions during task execution without additional policy training. On a curated archive of 54 RoboDojo-Sim task entries, RoboDoctor achieves a five-dimension mean score of 57.10 with a native success rate of 49.44%, illustrating its execution-time adaptation behavior, while the authors note that controlled trials are still required to isolate the specific contribution of cross-episode experience.Link

RAIWAY: A Large-Wheeled Bipedal Robot with Contact-Informed Blind LocomotionUnknown affiliationThe paper presents RAIWAY, a wheeled bipedal robot with 20-inch wheels and a contact-informed control framework for rolling locomotion over irregular and discontinuous terrain, where multi-contact wheel–terrain interactions dominate dynamics. The proposed controller is trained in simulation to compress multi-contact events into a latent representation and, at deployment, infers this representation online from proprioceptive signals alone, removing the need for explicit contact sensing. Real-world experiments show that on the RAIWAY platform, the contact-informed controller enables traversal of steep slopes, gravel beds, studded surfaces, rocky terrain, and continuous ascent of outdoor staircases with riser heights up to 15 cm, outperforming baselines without the contact-informed mechanism in challenging indoor and outdoor environments.Link
Interpretable Collision Avoidance and Feasibility Guarantees through an Uncertainty Aware PipelineUnknown affiliationThe paper proposes a modular perception–prediction–planning pipeline for autonomous vehicles that propagates structured, interpretable uncertainty between modules, computes collision probabilities upstream, and constructs convex safe sets for the planner, thereby decoupling collision-avoidance guarantees from optimization feasibility. In Monte Carlo simulations of a four-way unsignalized intersection, the proposed pipeline achieves 0% collisions and 0% solver infeasibility with a planning time of 12.7 ms per cycle, compared to 36% collision and nonzero infeasibility rates for deterministic and chance-constrained baselines (36% and 10% collision rates, respectively). An ablation study supports the necessity of each modeled uncertainty source, and hardware experiments on Clearpath Jackal UGVs demonstrate that the pipeline can run in real time on physical platforms while maintaining the described safety and feasibility guarantees.Link
ORBIT: A Modular Multi-finger Robotic Hand Platform with Rotational Finger ReconfigurationUnknown affiliationThe paper introduces ORBIT, a modular multi-finger robotic hand platform featuring a rotating knuckle mechanism in which independent fingers move around a cylindrical palm via a belt drive, enabling sub-1-second reconfiguration of finger layouts without disassembly. ORBIT exhibits robustness metrics including an average finger angular velocity of 1.57 rad/s and a root finger force resistance of 50 N, supporting its use in dexterous manipulation experiments. Using a three-finger ORBIT instantiation, the authors conduct a pilot grasping study comparing anthropomorphic, isometric, and Barrett-style layouts across eleven varied objects and demonstrate that ORBIT’s orbital degree of freedom enables tasks that are difficult for fixed-knuckle designs, such as cube-face reorientation, lid threading, and precision pinching of sub-centimeter objects.Link
Neuromorphic Decision Dynamics for Deadlock-free Safe Navigation in Congested EnvironmentsUnknown affiliationThe paper introduces Neuromorphic Decision Dynamics (NDD), a decentralized, event-based control framework for safe navigation in congested environments, where each agent couples internal decision and attention states with local motion observations to modulate speed along a prescribed path without communication or knowledge of others’ objectives or group size. NDD is analytically shown to guarantee collision avoidance, deadlock- and livelock-free traversal, and finite-time formation of traversal order for finite heterogeneous agent populations under explicit conditions on sensing range, feedback gains, response time scales, and actuation limits. Large-scale simulations, comparisons with existing methods, and hardware experiments demonstrate that NDD enables scalable, coordinated navigation in complex congested scenarios, with computational cost scaling only with the number of locally sensed interactions.Link
Active Reachability Learning for Humanoid Recoverability MonitoringUnknown affiliationThe paper formulates humanoid fall-risk monitoring as learning the stochastic finite-horizon recoverability envelope of a given fallback policy and proposes an active reachability learning framework that iteratively alternates between value learning and active exploration to better characterize this envelope. Data exploration is cast as a reach–avoid problem in which an uncertainty-guided explorer seeks unseen states while avoiding confidently unrecoverable regions, supported by a combined Temporal Difference and Monte Carlo objective that aims to improve in-distribution value accuracy and uncertainty estimation, with high-uncertainty regions conservatively treated as unrecoverable to mitigate optimistic out-of-distribution hallucinations. On stochastic benchmarks and a 29-DoF Unitree G1 humanoid, the proposed method substantially improves monitor coverage and accuracy compared to prior approaches and transfers zero-shot to real-world hardware for recoverability monitoring.Link
MOTIO: Agentic Skill Engineering for Reality-Grounded Humanoid Loco-ManipulationUnknown affiliationThe paper presents MOTIO, an agentic skill-engineering system that converts human-action videos, metric 3D assets, motion references, and reusable robot skills into executable humanoid loco-manipulation programs via in-context task and contact reasoning, physics-based execution, multi-frame feedback, and human validation, without requiring task-specific teleoperation or new robot demonstrations. MOTIO uses simulation-side layout and object variation to prepare long-horizon skills and demonstrates, among others, a bottle-disposal skill that executes continuously for 101.75 seconds on a physical Unitree G1 in a scanned office, as well as a screw-tightening skill where physical refinement reduces thread residual by 76.0%. Across 33 tasks reaching first approval, MOTIO reports a median agent-turn time of 97.0 minutes and a 2.80-hour request-to-approval duration, and shows that agent-generated skills can supervise BC/DAgger distillation into a single reference policy that completes 18 simulated tasks under one checkpoint without inference-time expert assistance.Link
EXE: Egocentric Cross-Embodiment Pretraining via Factorized Latent Actions for VLA ModelsUnknown affiliationThe paper proposes EXE, an egocentric cross-embodiment pretraining framework for Vision-Language-Action models that learns a factorized latent action space from human egocentric manipulation videos, separating embodiment-agnostic manipulation priors (e.g., reaching, grasping, placing) from embodiment-specific motion patterns. By leveraging the shared latent action representation, robot policies trained with EXE can acquire generalizable manipulation semantics from diverse human demonstrations while reducing bias from human-specific kinematics, enabling transfer across embodiments with different kinematic structures such as parallel grippers and dexterous hands. Experiments show that EXE consistently improves performance over baselines without egocentric pretraining on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa GR1, and real-world manipulation tasks, indicating the effectiveness of the factorized cross-embodiment representation.Link
Open-World Law-to-Action: Vision-Language Priors and Touch for Parts with Hidden PhysicsUnknown affiliationThe paper proposes Open-World Law-to-Action (L2A), in which a vision-language model predicts candidate motion types, parametric laws of motion, and potential locking mechanisms for each movable part, and a robot refines these priors online via Bayesian linear regression over touch feedback, including handling single- and two-degree-of-freedom motions and maintaining a hypothesis that unseen parts or motions may lock the target. Theoretical analysis shows that touch alone cannot disprove the hypothesis that an unseen part locks the target, characterizes when acquiring an additional visual observation is worth its cost, and proves that constant load and friction are indistinguishable from touch data while a part moves in only one way. In simulation, using VLM-proposed physics enables the robot in new kitchens to complete 80% of tasks versus 98% when all parts are known, increases success on objects requiring new motion terms from 353 to 378–383 of 400 episodes, and using a second visual look raises performance in mixed scenes from 284 to 329 of 366 tasks; a mobile robot that does not use simulator information is reported to complete 70 of 100 requests in new scenes versus 62 of 100 when given simulator-identified parts, after one change between held-out tests.Link
One Scan is Enough: Demonstration-Free Adaptation for Language-Guided NavigationPeking University; PrimeBotThe paper introduces Scan2Instruction, which converts a single brief iPhone scan of a new building into a full training environment for language-guided navigation by automatically generating executable navigation episodes with grounded instructions, eliminating the need for on-site teleoperated demonstrations. The PRPT-KS method adapts a language-guided navigation policy using a closed-form Pareto utility derived directly from the reconstructed scene, without a learned critic or human labels, enabling demonstration-free scene adaptation. Across five real environments, combining Scan2Instruction and PRPT-KS yields an 85% success rate for the RealNav robot compared with 49% for a RealNav baseline post-trained on human demonstrations, and achieves 82% success in unscanned regions of the same buildings compared with 41% for the same baseline.Link
Real-Time Human Trajectory Prediction from RGB-D for Mobile Robots Using Bi-MambaOtto von Guericke UniversityThe paper presents TrajMamba, a lightweight bidirectional state-space model for short-term human trajectory prediction from RGB-D, which uses a single unified processing pipeline for training and deployment to align pose extraction, depth back-projection, and smoothing, encodes short windows of hip-center velocities with stacked bidirectional Mamba blocks, and decodes future velocities via a GRU head. On the ETH/UCY benchmark, TrajMamba achieves an average ADE of 0.29 m and FDE of 0.51 m for human trajectory prediction. With 262K parameters, TrajMamba runs in real time on modest hardware and is deployed on a TIAGo mobile robot with an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin for RGB-D–based human trajectory prediction in real environments.Link
