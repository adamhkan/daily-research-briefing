## Executive Summary

- Institution-aligned work spans fast deformable material identification for manipulation, hierarchical language-guided navigation control, and multiple advances in VLA robustness, memory, and policy adaptation.
- Several papers push test-time adaptation: from dynamics and actuator degradation compensation to memory formation and self-compensation under execution errors.
- Humanoid and whole-body control see new kernels for physical interaction, SIM(3)-equivariant loco-manipulation encoders, spectral skill representations, and human-to-humanoid transfer and retargeting.
- A dense cluster of VLA/WAM contributions tackle long-horizon manipulation, force-aware and contact-rich control, acceleration and pruning, domain robustness, and representation design.
- Navigation-focused work introduces social navigation from internet videos, audio-guided navigation, safety certificates, RGB-only safety filters, and structured map-based multimodal reasoning.
- Multiple agentic and in-context frameworks target scalable data generation, skill acquisition and reuse, autonomous policy improvement, and systematic evaluation/benchmarks for manipulation and navigation.

| FORM: Robot Manipulation through Direct Material Law Identification | UC Berkeley | FORM identifies deformable-material laws online from a single interaction using a weak-form momentum formulation matching the forward simulator’s discretization, turning identification into fast linear least-squares. It cuts identification time to seconds while maintaining accurate prediction, and reuses the learned model for planning manipulation of rods, elastoplastic shaping, and precise pouring both in simulation and on hardware. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38105 |
| WayFinder: Hierarchical Visual-Language-Action for Zero-Shot Waypoint Generation and Low-Level Kinematic Control | UC San Diego | WayFinder factors navigation into a zero-shot multimodal LLM that generates waypoints from language and maps, and a lightweight onboard controller that executes continuous kinematic control. By querying the high-level policy only upon failure, it avoids fine-tuning, reduces expensive MLLM calls, and boosts navigation success in AirSim by up to 27.45% over low-level baselines. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37922 |
| BlenDAgger: Blended Shared Control for Interactive Imitation Learning | Carnegie Mellon University | BlenDAgger blends human and policy actions during interventions instead of fully handing over control, generating smoother corrections and trajectories closer to the training distribution. Across five manipulation tasks, it yields over 30 percentage-point gains in autonomous success versus HG-DAgger and speeds up real-world data collection without worsening user experience. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37599 |
| T$^2$Mem: Learning Test-Time Memory for Robotics | Stanford University | T$^2$Mem endows a frozen VLA manipulation policy with test-time-learned fast-weight memory, encoding observation history through online self-supervision without external memory labels. On 16 RoboMME tasks it lifts success from 17.93% to 56.83% and outperforms recurrent-memory baselines while being at least 3× faster than explicit history processing. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36720 |
| LIBERO-MAX: Do Robot Policies Adapt When the World Changes? | Carnegie Mellon University; New York University; Stanford University | LIBERO-MAX is a paired benchmark that holds policy, initial state, and pre-change actions fixed while injecting mid-task scene changes, isolating robustness to temporal perturbations. Evaluating 14 policies shows consistent 11–25.7 percentage-point drops in success after events and shared failure modes for geometry and observation shifts, establishing a controlled testbed for adaptation under changing worlds. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36518 |
| One from Infinity: Actualizing Futures from Pretrained World Models into Robot Actions | UC Berkeley | RoboActualizer treats a pretrained video world model as a prior over possible futures and learns a small ‘actualizer’ that selects task-relevant futures and corresponding actions via flow matching, keeping the world model frozen. With ~60M trainable parameters—up to 100× fewer than typical WAMs/VLAs—it achieves strong performance on LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0 and real robots with ~39 ms control latency. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36413 |
| KPI: A Promptable Kernel for Physical Interaction on Humanoids | Carnegie Mellon University | KPI inserts a ‘contract’ layer between trajectory sources and a generic whole-body tracker, allowing per-direction prompts such as track, comply, or hold-force-range. It adapts arm impedance and references at contact rate from tracking error and wrench estimates, enabling instruction-driven tasks like winch cranking, door opening, and box transport without task-specific low-level controllers. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36151 |
| Scouting the Dynamics Gap: Test-Time Policy Adaptation via Action-Outcome Feedback | Carnegie Mellon University | SCOUT meta-learns a shared latent ‘belief’ space linking a dynamics model and policy, updating this latent online by minimizing prediction error between expected and observed action outcomes. This enables rapid sim-to-real and dynamics adaptation for manipulation without reward signals, as the policy continuously revises its internal dynamics beliefs during deployment. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36107 |
| doPlan: A Variable-Horizon Dataset for Multi-Stage Language-Conditioned Planning in Autonomous Driving | UC San Diego | doPlan augments nuPlan with over 5,000 human-written passenger instructions aligned to up to ~8.5 minutes of driving, capturing immediate, deferred, event-conditioned, and multi-stage goals. Evaluations of language-conditioned driving models show that while they respond to language, they often fail to realize requested maneuvers, underscoring the challenge of connecting persistent intent to successive plans. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38028 |
| Comparing Utility of Inertial, Occupancy, Semantic, and Intent Information in Human Motion Prediction During Daily Tasks | Stanford University | Using a diffusion model trained on Meta Aria egocentric data, this work quantifies how inertial cues, scene occupancy/semantics, gaze, and explicit intent affect indoor human motion prediction. Including body motion, environment structure, and particularly explicit intent significantly cuts prediction error over constant-velocity baselines, highlighting that intent estimation or input is key for fine-grained motion forecasting. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37971 |

It yields accurate kinematic playback in simulation and real signing and reach-to-grasp motions, enabling upper-body manipulation transfer from plain videos. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37776 |
