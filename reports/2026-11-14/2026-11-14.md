## Executive Summary

- Several works advance robot planning and control under sensing or dynamical constraints: a visibility-aware task and motion planner interleaves active sensing with deterministic rearrangement plans using persistent object-level memory to outperform baselines on heavily occluded tabletop tasks; a generative inverse-kinematics method samples configuration distributions that handle mobile bases, multi-end-effector targets, scene awareness, and preference conditioning while improving solve rate and runtime over existing IK baselines; a diffeomorphic vector-field reshaping scheme aligns nonholonomic robot heading with nominal guidance fields to preserve obstacle avoidance and convergence, achieving collision-free convergence in both simulations and ground-vehicle experiments; a perception-aware diffusion planner for UAVs conditions trajectory generation on pixel-level sensor reliability and uses CHOMP guidance to halve collision rates versus the learned prior in zero-shot real flights; and an analytic policy-gradient planner exploits differential flatness to build differentiable episode rollouts, matching or exceeding baseline performance in simulation after ~30 minutes of training and reaching 11 m/s in real quadrotor flights and skid-steer robot tests. ([arXiv:2611.16213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16213); [arXiv:2611.15565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15565); [arXiv:2611.14918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14918); [arXiv:2611.15277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15277); [arXiv:2611.15296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15296))
- New frameworks target cross-embodiment and whole-body skill transfer: an egocentric cross-embodiment transformer with modality-specific tokenizers, action experts, and an embodiment-dreaming objective boosts average normalized task progress from 0.63 to 0.80 across 13 task–embodiment pairs and improves out-of-distribution performance from 0.53 to 0.77; a hierarchical whole-body controller for quadrupedal mobile manipulators uses morphology conditioning and residual-wrench feedback to convert short-horizon end-effector trajectories into coordinated arm–base motion, achieving 93.95% completion with 2.30 cm / 4.81° errors on held-out simulated arms and large error reductions versus a strong baseline, and demonstrating extended-reach language-conditioned pick-and-place on real hardware. ([arXiv:2611.16728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16728); [arXiv:2611.15117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15117))
- Humanoid motion generation and interaction are addressed by a pipeline that reconstructs scene geometry from a single RGB image, synthesizes human–scene interaction, and retargets to humanoids with two refinement stages, yielding a 5.5× speedup over a prior method (480 s → 87 s) and success rates of 89% in simulation and 70% over 50 Unitree G1 trials; and by an interface-level adapter trained in simulation to adjust motion references from observation histories, which can be plugged into existing humanoid trackers to enable compliant, sensor-free guidance, reducing guidance-target error by 63.6–75.4% while maintaining nominal tracking in simulation and hardware tests. ([arXiv:2611.14981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14981); [arXiv:2611.14985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14985))
- Vision-language(-action) and intent-prediction systems are explored across several settings: a hindsight-driven framework trains a compact action verifier from autonomous rollouts of a frozen VLA model, then filters its proposed action chunks, achieving 82.3% average success on LIBERO-10 tasks and a 22.6-point success increase on three real-world manipulation tasks versus the frozen baseline; a teleoperation-intent benchmark and model jointly infer atomic actions and missions from video, where a fine-tuned Vision-Intention Model substantially outperforms large proprietary VLMs (e.g., action Sentence-BERT similarity 0.825 vs. 0.563–0.581) and reduces human teleoperation time by ~35–40% while cutting prediction latency by ~70%; an ObjectNav framework uses an LLM-derived target–furniture prior and a diffusion generator to produce a single pre-mission global search plan that outperforms a strong online baseline under matched priors and transfers from ScanNet++ training to Gibson and real homes; and a bimanual LLM–planner hybrid converts language into PDDL and uses temporal partial-order planning to enforce spatial/temporal constraints, yielding higher success, faster planning, and more efficient long-horizon bimanual plans than LLM-only baselines, with an end-to-end demonstration on a real NICOL robot. ([arXiv:2611.14834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14834); [arXiv:2611.16419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16419); [arXiv:2611.15093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15093); [arXiv:2611.15317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15317))
- Memory, representation, and reliability are studied at the system level: a critic-gated event memory records events that cause large critic-estimated changes in success probability, reaching 88.0% success in a ten-subtask diagnostic environment where observation-window and kinematic-memory baselines score 0.0%, and showing that removing negative-outcome records collapses success to 1.7%; an evaluation of visual tokenizers for tactile tasks finds that three 16× discrete tokenizers induce 6.5–9.7 point accuracy drops on ObjectFolder Real while a Stable Diffusion VAE incurs only a 0.3-point drop and better preserves object identity, motivating SD-VAE latents for a photo-to-touch generator and showing that retraining classifiers on reconstructions can mitigate discrete-tokenizer degradation; and a conceptual review outlines how systems can adapt reliance on fallible LLM components using decision-theoretic and online-learning tools to reduce error impact and bound the risk of rare catastrophic failures, noting limited but encouraging empirical evidence for such adaptive reliance strategies. ([arXiv:2611.16386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16386); [arXiv:2611.15834](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15834); [arXiv:2611.17020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17020))
- New robotic platforms and end-effectors broaden application domains: a continuum "Helping Hand" grasper for endoscopic submucosal dissection fits a 3.7 mm channel while sharing the lumen with an electrosurgical knife, shows sufficient reachable workspace and traction forces in bench tests, and enables ex vivo tissue isolation and removal in chicken thigh experiments; a unified learning framework for high-speed trail navigation encodes terrain semantics into an egocentric elevation map and directly learns control, allowing a bicycle robot to autonomously race a kilometer-long unstructured trail at average 3.26 m/s (peaks >5 m/s) and to fail up to an order of magnitude less often than hierarchical baselines in simulation; and an accessible mobile bimanual platform combining two low-cost arms with a TurtleBot 4 base and 250 toilet-cleaning demonstrations shows that among several VLAs, π₀ attains 60% strict and 70% relaxed autonomous success (90% with limited human help), while simple progress and memory augmentations do not improve baselines, underscoring both feasibility and remaining challenges for low-cost long-horizon mobile bimanual manipulation. ([arXiv:2611.15638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15638); [arXiv:2611.15280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15280); [arXiv:2611.15381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15381))

| Visibility-Aware Task and Motion Planning with Task-Centric Perception | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper proposes \ourmethod{}, a visibility-aware task and motion planning algorithm that extends deterministic TAMP solvers to partially observable object rearrangement tasks by interleaving sensing actions into a full-observability plan skeleton and maintaining a persistent object-based memory that fuses observations into object mesh hypotheses and an observed occupancy map. | EgoX: Egocentric Cross-Embodiment Manipulation with Embodiment Dreaming | Carnegie Mellon University; UC Berkeley | The paper introduces EgoX, an egocentric cross-embodiment learning framework that pretrains a Modularized Cross-Embodiment Transformer with Embodiment Dreaming (MXT+) on heterogeneous demonstrations by using modality-specific tokenizers and action experts with a shared Transformer trunk, and a training-only objective that predicts future visual and proprioceptive latents to encourage embodiment-agnostic dynamics modeling. Across 13 task-embodiment pairs over five tasks and six robot embodiments (five physical robots and one simulated humanoid), cross-embodiment pretraining with EgoX increases MXT+ average normalized task progress from 0.63 to 0.80 compared to training from scratch, with out-of-distribution task progress improving from 0.53 to 0.77. Ablation studies show that broader pretraining mixtures, modular observation/action interfaces, and the embodiment-dreaming objective each contribute to performance, with dreaming leading to more similar latent geometries across embodiments. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16728 |
| Generalized Inverse Kinematics via Conditional Flow | Massachusetts Institute of Technology | The paper proposes GenGenIK, a generative inverse kinematics approach that produces distributions over robot configurations and supports mobile bases, multiple end-effectors and targets, scene awareness, and preference-conditioned behavior within a unified interface. Across tasks including bimanual lift and flip-up, GenGenIK experimentally outperforms existing IK methods in both solve rate and runtime, while allowing controllable sampling and remaining effective under partial scene observations. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15565 |
| Remember What Changes the Odds: Critic-Gated Event Memory for Long-Horizon Manipulation | Stanford University | The paper introduces CGEM, a critic-gated event memory that selects and encodes events based on large critic-estimated changes in eventual success probability, representing positive changes as progress records and negative changes as failure records, with failure records discarded after their associated subtasks are completed. In a ten-subtask diagnostic environment with exactly computed success probabilities, CGEM achieves 88.0% task success, whereas both a 36-step observation window and a kinematic event memory baselines achieve 0.0% success under the same conditions. Removing failure records from CGEM drastically degrades performance, reducing success from 88.0% to 1.7%, indicating that explicitly remembering negative-outcome events is critical for resolving decision aliasing in the tested long-horizon setting. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16386 |
| Helping Hand: A Continuum Robotic Grasper for Traction in Single-Channel Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection | UC San Diego | The paper presents the Helping Hand, a two-segment continuum robotic grasper designed for endoscopic submucosal dissection that incorporates a base slot to allow an electrosurgical knife to extend through the same 3.7 mm working channel, enabling traction and cutting via a single instrument lumen. Experimental evaluations characterize the Helping Hand’s reachable workspace and its ability to generate traction forces from different initial configurations, and an ex vivo chicken thigh demonstration shows that it can isolate and remove tissue, suggesting potential to support ESD workflows. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15638 |
| Learning Low-Latency Action Verification for Frozen Vision-Language-Action Models | UC Berkeley | The paper proposes HDH, a three-stage, low-latency framework that leverages hindsight from autonomous rollouts of a frozen vision-language-action (VLA) model to train a compact action verifier, which then scores and selects among the frozen model’s candidate action chunks during deployment. On four LIBERO-10 manipulation tasks, HDH achieves an average success rate of 82.3%, and on three real-world manipulation tasks it improves average success by 22.6 percentage points over the baseline frozen VLA policy. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14834 |
| Where to Generate Touch: Do Image Latents Preserve Material Recognition? | Carnegie Mellon University; Stanford University; University of Washington | The paper evaluates whether image-model latent spaces preserve tactile material information by training material classifiers on real GelSight presses and measuring performance on encode-decode reconstructions from several pretrained image tokenizers and the Stable Diffusion VAE (SD-VAE), finding that three 16× discrete tokenizers cause 6.5–9.7 point losses in accuracy on ObjectFolder Real while SD-VAE incurs only a 0.3-point drop. Across ObjectFolder Real, Touch and Go, and DIGIT object recognition (YCB-Slide), the 16× discrete tokenizers degrade recognition substantially more than SD-VAE; SD-VAE reconstructions preserve object identity with 76.7% object-matching accuracy versus 78.9% for real touch and 67.0% for MAGVIT-v2. Based on these comparisons, the authors select SD-VAE latents for a photo-to-touch generator and show that retraining classifiers on reconstructions, without additional labels, can mitigate the performance gap for discrete tokenizers. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15834 |
| I See What You Want: Decoding Hierarchical Operator Intent from Visual Teleoperation History | ETH Zurich | The paper formulates hierarchical intent prediction from visual teleoperation history, jointly inferring an operator’s ongoing atomic action and higher-level mission from short video segments, and introduces VI AMO, a dataset of 6,600 episodes from eleven robot sources with 13,653 action and 9,702 mission annotations to study this problem. Large proprietary vision-language models perform poorly on VI AMO, with GPT-4o achieving Sentence-BERT similarity scores of 0.563 (action) and 0.470 (mission) and GPT-5.6 Luna scoring 0.581 and 0.554, whereas the proposed fine-tuned Vision-Intention Model (VIM) reaches 0.825 and 0.821 overall and 0.726 and 0.717 on the 35.4% of test actions whose labels are absent from training. In a real-world downstream setting where a vision-language-action policy takes over mid-task using predicted missions, VIM reduces required human teleoperation time by 34.6% relative to GPT-4o and 39.8% relative to GPT-5.6 Luna, while cutting intent-prediction latency by about 70% on average. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16419 |
| Maximizing LLM Utility While Managing LLM Fallibility | Carnegie Mellon University | The paper reviews how systems can maximize the utility of large language models despite their fallibility by adapting reliance on the LLM as empirical evidence about its performance accumulates, drawing parallels to established methods for handling noisy or faulty components in traditional systems. It argues that appropriate forms of reliance adaptation depend on the LLM’s role and contribution type, requiring tailored reliability estimates that classical tools from decision theory, estimation, and online learning can convert into reliance decisions that reduce the impact of LLM errors and can bound the risk of rare but critical failures when no independent safeguard exists. The review notes that empirical studies on adapting reliance on LLMs are still limited, but available comparisons suggest that such adaptation can improve overall system performance. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17020 |

| DIVAL: Diffeomorphic Vector-Field Alignment for Nonholonomic Motion Planning | Unknown affiliation | The paper proposes DIVAL, a diffeomorphic vector-field alignment method that locally reshapes a nominal vector field in the workspace so that, at the robot’s initial position, the reshaped field aligns with the nonholonomic robot’s heading while matching the nominal field outside the deformed region, thereby enabling heading-consistent tracking from the start. DIVAL’s alignment allows the robot to preserve the nominal vector field’s desirable properties, including obstacle avoidance, convergence to target, and smooth control, by maintaining heading alignment throughout motion. Numerical simulations, in which nominal vector-field-tracking baselines are augmented with DIVAL reshaping, achieve collision-free convergence in all trials and avoid control discontinuities compared to the original baselines, and hardware experiments on a ground vehicle show collision-free convergence across varied targets and initial conditions under real-world disturbances. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14918 |
| From a Single RGB Image to Robot Execution: Metric Scene Alignment and Contact-Guided Retargeting | Beijing University of Chemical Technology; National University of Defense Technology; Noetix Robotics | The paper proposes a unified pipeline that reconstructs scene geometry from a single RGB image, generates a human-scene interaction, and then retargets it to a humanoid robot, with two refinement stages (Joint Metric Interaction Refinement and Contact Guided Residual Retargeting) to maintain metric scene alignment and correct robot-specific support errors. Compared with GRAIL, the proposed refinement process reduces motion refinement time from 480 s to 87 s (a 5.5× speedup) while producing motion references that can be tracked by multiple pretrained humanoid humanoid trackers without scene-specific policy fine-tuning. In evaluation, the complete pipeline achieves an 89% success rate in simulation and a 70% success rate across 50 physical trials on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot for executing motions derived from single RGB images. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14981 |
| ILA: A Transferable Interface-Level Adapter for Compliant Humanoid Motion Tracking | Harbin Institute of Technology, Shenzhen, China; National University of Singapore, Singapore | The paper introduces ILA, an interface-level adapter that modifies humanoid motion references based on the robot’s observation history to enable compliant motion tracking and responsiveness to physical guidance, without requiring dedicated interaction sensors or retraining of existing motion trackers. Trained entirely in simulation to predict reference corrections induced by physical interaction, ILA can be plugged into different pretrained humanoid motion trackers, functioning as a reusable, sensor-free extension for compliant guidance. Across three humanoid motion trackers, ILA reduces guidance-target error by 63.6–75.4% relative to the original policies, and both simulation and hardware experiments indicate improved responsiveness to human guidance while preserving nominal motion-tracking performance. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14985 |
| Decide Before You Move: Diffusion-Generated Search Reference for Zero-Shot Open-Vocabulary Object Navigation in Semi-Static Environments | Lamarr Institute; ShanghaiTech University; University of Bonn | The paper presents PreNav, an ObjectNav framework for semi-static indoor environments that retains a static map for path planning but generates a single pre-mission global search reference using an LLM-derived target–furniture co-occurrence prior and a conditional diffusion model that outputs an ordered search centerline and band. PreNav avoids repeated online vision–language calls by deciding where to search before movement, then approaches the target once detected during execution, assuming large structural elements (walls, furniture) remain unchanged while smaller objects may move or vanish. Experiments on a Gibson ObjectNav benchmark show that PreNav outperforms a strong online baseline under matched structural priors, using a generator trained only on room-level ScanNet++ scenes and deployed on floor-level Gibson without fine-tuning, and real-world tests with object relocation and open-vocabulary queries further validate its effectiveness. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15093 |

PolyMorphWBC uses a trajectory-conditioned coordinator for base motion, arm-specific IK for end-effector tracking, and a frozen locomotion backend, together with URDF-derived morphology conditioning and residual-wrench feedback, and is trained on procedurally generated arms and structured trajectories within and beyond fixed-base reach. In IsaacLab, PolyMorphWBC attains 93.95% task completion with 2.30 cm mean position error and 4.81° orientation error on held-out trajectories and arm morphologies; in MuJoCo, it reduces end-effector position error by 69–72% and orientation error by 91–97% relative to UMI-on-Legs on four trajectories, and is demonstrated on a physical Go2–ARX5 system for language-conditioned extended-reach pick-and-place using pi_0-generated trajectories without VLA base commands. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15117 |
| DroneDiffusion: Uncertainty-Guided Diffusion Path Planner for UAV Navigation | Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology | The paper introduces DroneDiffusion, a perception-aware diffusion-based local 3D trajectory planner for UAVs that conditions a denoising diffusion probabilistic model on a pixel-aligned sensor reliability map, onboard RGB image, goal point, and initial velocity to avoid regions of degraded sensing such as lens flare, overexposure, and reflections. A vision–language perceptual safety estimator produces the reliability map, and training-free CHOMP guidance over a 3D Euclidean signed distance field is used at inference time to enforce obstacle avoidance without retraining the diffusion model. Trained entirely in simulation and deployed zero-shot on a real drone, DroneDiffusion increases mean perceptual reliability along generated trajectories to 0.71 versus 0.63 for a geometric baseline, and CHOMP guidance halves the collision rate of the learned prior from 14.7% to 7.8%, showing that perceptual safety conditioning and geometric guidance are complementary. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15277 |
| Autonomous Trail Racing with a Bicycle Robot | Unknown affiliation | The paper presents a unified learning framework for autonomous high-speed trail navigation with a bicycle robot that encodes the trail as a semantic terrain layer and jointly optimizes local navigation and locomotion, avoiding the traditional twist/pose command hierarchy by directly learning control from terrain semantics and episode length with fixed reward weights. | APG-Planner: Learning Vision-Based High-Speed Trajectory Planning via Analytic Policy Gradient | Unknown affiliation | The paper proposes APG-Planner, a vision-based high-speed trajectory planner trained using an analytic policy gradient (APG) framework that exploits differential flatness to reconstruct vehicle states from planned trajectories, enabling differentiable trajectory-level state transitions without an explicit low-level dynamics model. By iteratively composing trajectory planning with differentiable state transitions, the method forms a differentiable episode rollout and computes analytic policy gradients from episode-level losses, reducing gradient noise and improving training efficiency compared to sample-based model-free policy gradient methods. In simulation, APG-Planner achieves performance comparable to or better than existing baselines after about 30 minutes of training on a single GPU, reaches up to 11 m/s in real-world quadrotor flight experiments, and is further validated on a skid-steer ground robot, indicating applicability to different differentially flat robotic systems. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15296 |
| BiPOP: Partial-Order Planning for Long-Horizon Bimanual Robotic Manipulation | University of Hamburg | The paper introduces BiPOP, a framework that combines large language models with temporal partial-order planning to generate long-horizon bimanual manipulation plans by explicitly modeling workspace partitioning, arm reachability, inter-arm dependencies, arm assignment, synchronization requirements, and opportunities for concurrent execution. In BiPOP, an LLM converts natural-language task descriptions into a PDDL specification, and a temporal planner enforces spatio-temporal constraints and schedules actions, producing symbolically consistent plans that leverage parallelism between the two arms. On simulated long-horizon bimanual tasks of varying complexity, BiPOP achieves higher success rates, faster planning times, and more efficient plans than both open-loop LLM planning and closed-loop ReAct-style LLM planning, and the complete pipeline is demonstrated end to end on the real bimanual NICOL robot; failure analysis shows LLM-only approaches often violate spatial and temporal coordination constraints, motivating the symbolic planner integration. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15317 |
